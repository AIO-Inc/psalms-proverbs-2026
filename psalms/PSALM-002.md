# PSALM 2 — The Kings Are Laughing, and So Is He

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 2:1–12.*

---

Why are the nations in an uproar?

Look around. The powers of this world — the governments, the empires, the men with the microphones — they're all in a huddle, plotting the same thing they've always plotted: how to get out from under anything bigger than themselves. "Let's cut the ropes," they say. "Let's throw off the yoke. Nobody tells us what to do."

And you know what the Source does?

He laughs.

Not a cruel laugh. The laugh of a father watching a toddler announce he's moving out. The One who sits above it all looks down at the whole conspiracy of self-important men and finds it — honestly — a little funny. Because He already knows how this ends.

He's already set His King on the hill. Already made the decree. Already said the words: *"You are my son. Today I have become your father."*

Think about that. The answer to all the plotting and scheming of the powerful isn't a bigger army. It's a family. It's a Father saying to a Son, "Ask me. Ask me for the nations and I'll hand them to you. Ask me for the ends of the earth and they're yours."

The kingdoms that are built on pride? They're pottery. Clay pots. One tap from an iron rod and they shatter into pieces nobody bothers to pick up.

So here's the advice — and it's addressed straight to the kings, the rulers, the people who think they're in charge:

Get smart. Get educated. Serve with awe — real awe, the kind that makes your knees go weak when you finally see what you're standing in front of. Don't fear Him. *Feel* Him. Rejoice, and tremble, because both are the correct response to something that big.

Come honest. Come humble. Come make peace with the Son before you make war on Him — because you cannot win that war, and He'd rather welcome you than defeat you.

And the last line — the whole point:

**Happy is everyone who takes refuge in Him.**

Not everyone who obeys perfectly. Not everyone who never rebels. Everyone who *runs to Him*. That's the door. It's still open. It was never closed.

The nations can rage. Let them. You know where the shelter is.

---

*Note on translation: v. 11 — "serve the LORD with fear" is* yirah *= awe/reverence, not dread. "Rejoice with trembling" — both at once: joy and overwhelmed-ness are the same response to real greatness. "Kiss the Son" (v. 12) = make peace, pay homage, come home. "Blessed" (*ashrei*) = happy, flourishing.*
