# PSALM 64 — The Secret Arrows

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 64:1–10. About people who attack from hiding — and what happens to them.*

---

**"Hear my voice, God, in my complaint. Guard my life from the dread of the enemy."**

*The dread of the enemy.* Not just the enemy — the *dread*. The fear itself is the thing he wants guarding from. Because sometimes the fear of the attack does more damage than the attack.

**"Hide me from the secret plots of the wicked — from the mob of workers of harm."**

*Secret plots.* The Hebrew is *sod* — the same word Psalm 25 used for God's *intimate counsel*, the secret He shares with those in awe. Here it's the enemies' *sod* — their private conspiracy, their back room. Two secret councils. One is a Father telling His kids the plan. The other is a gang deciding who to hurt.

**"They've sharpened their tongue like a sword. They've aimed their arrow — a bitter word — to shoot from hiding at the blameless. Suddenly they shoot — and don't fear."**

*A bitter word — as an arrow.* Words as projectiles. Fired from cover. You never see the archer. You just feel the hit — the rumor, the review, the anonymous complaint, the thing said in a room you weren't in.

*From hiding.* That's the tell. Honest opponents face you. These ones need the bushes.

*And don't fear.* No awe. No sense that anyone's watching. They think the hiding place is *complete* — that if the target can't see them, nobody can.

**"They strengthen themselves in an evil plan. They talk about laying snares in secret. They say: 'Who will see them?'"**

*Who will see?* Same question as Psalm 59 — *who hears?* It's the working assumption of every hidden attack: *nobody's watching.*

**"They search out wrongdoing. They've finished a careful search. The inside of man — the heart — is deep."**

*The heart is deep.* Even David admits it. What's inside a person goes down further than anyone can see. The schemers think that depth is their protection — nobody can find the bottom of what they're planning.

But — verse 7:

**"But God will shoot them with an arrow. Suddenly — they're wounded."**

*Suddenly.* Same word they used. They shot *suddenly* from hiding. God shoots *suddenly* from heaven. The one who thought he was the archer finds out he's the target — and the shot comes from the one direction he wasn't covering.

**"He'll make them stumble — their own tongue against them. Everyone who sees them will shake their heads."**

*Their own tongue against them.* The weapon turns around. The bitter word they fired comes back with their name on it. The rumor gets traced. The anonymous thing gets un-anonymized. And the people who watched — *shake their heads*. Not at the victim. At the archer.

**"Then all people will be in awe. They'll declare the work of God. They'll understand what He's done."**

*In awe.* Here's the *yirah* — and look where it lands. Not on the wicked. On *everyone who watched*. When the hidden archer gets hit by the arrow nobody saw coming — the whole crowd goes quiet and says *God did that.* The wonder spreads.

**"The one who does right will rejoice in the Source and take refuge in Him. All the upright in heart will glory."**

*Take refuge.* The blameless man was *hiding* too — from the arrows. But he was hiding in the *right place*. Two hiders. One in the bushes, one under the wings. Only one of them stays hidden.

Ten verses. Here's the shape: *They sharpen the tongue. They aim the word. They shoot from hiding and say who'll see. But the heart is deep — and God sees the bottom. He shoots back. Their own tongue turns on them. Everyone watching stands in awe. And the one who took refuge in the right place — glories.*

When the arrows come from somewhere you can't see: don't go looking for the archer. Get under the wings. God's got the return shot.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* mi-pachad oyev *= from the dread of the enemy —* pachad*, human terror. v. 2 —* sod m're'im *= the secret council of evildoers — same word as Ps 25:14 (God's* sod*). v. 4 —* pit'om yoruhu v'lo yira'u *= "suddenly they shoot him and do not fear" — no reverence. v. 5 —* mi yir'eh-lamo *= "who will see them?" v. 7 —* pit'om *= suddenly — deliberate echo of v. 4. v. 9 —* va-yir'u kol-adam *=* yirah *= "and all people will stand in awe" — reverent astonishment at divine justice.*
