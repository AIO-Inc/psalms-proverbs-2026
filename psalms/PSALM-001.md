# PSALM 1 — Two Roads, One Life

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 1:1–6.*

---

Listen up. Here's the first thing the whole book wants you to know.

You are going to be happy — really happy, deep-in-your-bones happy — when you stop taking your cues from people who don't have your back. You know the ones. The ones who scheme instead of build. The ones who stand around in the circle of cynics, mocking everything and making nothing. You don't walk with them. You don't sit down with them. You don't take their advice as your GPS.

Instead? You find delight in the way things actually work — the teaching, the instruction, the design. Not as a rulebook. As a love letter. You turn it over in your mind when the day starts and when the day ends. You chew on it. You let it get into your blood.

And here's what happens to a person like that.

You become a tree. Not a weed, not a tumbleweed — a tree. Planted on purpose, right next to running water. Your roots go down where the drought can't reach. When your season comes, you produce. Your leaves don't wither. Everything you put your hand to comes to something.

Now the other road.

The people who've made a lifestyle out of missing the mark — they're not trees. They're chaff. The dry husk the farmer throws in the air, and the wind takes it, and nobody ever sees it again. No roots. No fruit. No weight. Nothing to hold them down when the wind picks up.

So when the moment of truth comes, they can't stand. They've got nothing to stand on. They don't belong in the room where the rooted people gather, because they never planted anything.

Here's the bottom line. The Source knows the road of the person who does right. Knows it personally. Walks it with them. But the road of the one who keeps missing the mark? It just runs out. It ends in nothing, because it was built on nothing.

Two roads. One life. You're standing at the fork right now — you always are.

Plant yourself. Go find the water. Put your roots down today.

---

*Note on translation: "blessed" (Hebrew* ashrei*) = happy, flourishing, on the right track. "Wicked" (*rasha*) = one who habitually misses the mark, not a monster. "The LORD knows" (*yada*) = intimate, personal knowledge — not surveillance.*
