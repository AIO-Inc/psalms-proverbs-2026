# PSALM 104 — You Open Your Hand

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 104:1–35. Genesis 1 as a song. Thirty-five verses walking through creation day by day — with lions, ships, and a sea monster God made to play with.*

---

Psalm 103 blessed God for what He does for *people*. Psalm 104 blesses Him for *everything else*. Same opening line. Different subject.

**"Bless the Source, O my soul. Source my God — You are very great. You are clothed with splendor and majesty."**

*Clothed.* Psalm 93 — *robed in majesty*. And then — the wardrobe gets specific:

**"He wraps Himself in light as with a garment. He stretches out the heavens like a tent — and lays the beams of His upper chambers on their waters. He makes the clouds His chariot — and rides on the wings of the wind. He makes winds His messengers — flames of fire His servants."**

*Light as a garment.* Day one — *let there be light*. He *wore* it.

*The heavens like a tent.* Day two — the sky as a canvas He *pitched*. The upper chambers on the *waters* — the ancient picture of the sky as a roof over the deep.

*Clouds His chariot. Wind His wings.* Psalm 18 — *He rode on a cherub and flew*. The weather is His *transportation*.

**"He set the earth on its foundations. It can never be moved."**

Day three begins. *Never moved.* Psalm 93, 96 — the ground holds.

**"You covered it with the watery depths as with a garment. The waters stood above the mountains. But at Your rebuke the waters fled. At the sound of Your thunder they took to flight. They flowed over the mountains — they went down into the valleys — to the place You assigned for them. You set a boundary they cannot cross. Never again will they cover the earth."**

*The waters fled.* The chaos — running. *At Your rebuke.* One word and the sea *retreats* to where it's told. *A boundary they cannot cross.* Job 38 — *who shut up the sea behind doors?* This One.

Now — the springs, and this is where the psalm gets *tender*:

**"He makes springs pour water into the ravines. It flows between the mountains. They give water to all the beasts of the field. The wild donkeys quench their thirst."**

*Wild donkeys.* Not the useful animals. The *wild* ones. The ones nobody owns. God's making sure *they* get a drink.

**"The birds of the sky nest by the waters. They sing among the branches."**

Psalm 84's sparrow — but *everywhere*. Every stream's got a nest and a song.

**"He waters the mountains from His upper chambers. The land is satisfied by the fruit of His work. He makes grass grow for the cattle — and plants for people to cultivate — bringing forth food from the earth:**
**wine that gladdens human hearts — oil to make their faces shine — and bread that sustains their hearts."**

*Wine. Oil. Bread.* The three staples. And look at the *verbs*: wine *gladdens*. Oil makes faces *shine*. Bread *sustains*. God didn't just make food. He made food that makes you *happy*. Wine wasn't necessary. He added it. For *gladness*.

**"The trees of the Source are well watered — the cedars of Lebanon that He planted. There the birds make their nests. The stork has its home in the junipers. The high mountains belong to the wild goats. The crags are a refuge for the rock badgers."**

*Rock badgers.* The hyrax. A small, furry, unimportant animal that lives in cracks. And God built the *crags* for them. *A refuge* — Psalm 46's word — for *badgers*. If He made a shelter for the hyrax, He made one for you.

**"He made the moon to mark the seasons — and the sun knows when to go down."**

Day four. *The sun knows.* Like it's got a schedule and keeps it.

**"You bring darkness — it becomes night — and all the beasts of the forest prowl. The lions roar for their prey — and seek their food from God."**

*The lions seek their food from God.* Read that. The apex predator — the thing at the top of every food chain — *praying for dinner*. Not consciously. But the psalm says that's what the roar *is*. A request. And God hears it.

**"The sun rises — and they steal away. They return and lie down in their dens. Then people go out to their work — to their labor until evening."**

Night shift. Day shift. Lions clock out; humans clock in. God runs the whole schedule.

**"How many are Your works, Source! In wisdom You made them all. The earth is full of Your creatures."**

*Full.* Psalm 33 — *full of loyal-love*. Here — *full of creatures*. Teeming.

Now — the sea:

**"There is the sea — vast and spacious — teeming with creatures beyond number — living things both large and small. There the ships go to and fro — and Leviathan — which You formed to frolic there."**

*Leviathan.* Psalm 74 — *You crushed the heads of Leviathan*. Job 41 — the terror nobody can hook. And here — *You formed him to FROLIC.*

The Hebrew is *l'sachek* — to play. To *sport*. God made the sea monster — the symbol of chaos, the thing every ancient culture feared — *as a toy*. For *fun*. His own. Or Leviathan's. Either way — the monster's *playing*.

That's the most joyful verse in the psalm. The thing you were scared of is *God's pet*.

**"All creatures look to You — to give them their food at the proper time. When You give it to them — they gather it up. When You open Your hand — they are satisfied with good things."**

*You open Your hand.* Picture it. Every living thing on earth — donkeys, storks, badgers, lions, Leviathan — *looking up*. Waiting. And God *opens His hand*. And they eat.

That's the economy of the whole planet. One open hand.

**"When You hide Your face — they are terrified. When You take away their breath — they die and return to the dust. When You send Your Spirit — they are created — and You renew the face of the ground."**

*Your breath. Your Spirit.* Same word — *ruach*. Every breath every creature takes is *His* on loan. He gives it — they live. He takes it — dust. He sends it again — *new*. The whole cycle of life and death is God *breathing*.

**"May the glory of the Source endure forever. May the Source rejoice in His works."**

*May the Source REJOICE in His works.* God — *happy* about what He made. Genesis 1 — *it was good*. Psalm 104 — *He's still glad about it*.

**"He who looks at the earth — and it trembles. Who touches the mountains — and they smoke."**

**"I will sing to the Source all my life. I will sing praise to my God as long as I live. May my meditation be pleasing to Him — as I rejoice in the Source."**

*As I rejoice.* God rejoices in His works. The psalmist rejoices in God. Mutual delight.

**"But may sinners vanish from the earth — and the wicked be no more."**

One line. The only shadow in thirty-five verses. Because the creation's *good* — and the thing that wrecks it doesn't belong.

**"Bless the Source, O my soul. Praise the Source!"**

*Praise the Source.* *Hallelu-Yah.* The first *hallelujah* in the Psalms. Right here. After Leviathan frolicking and the lions praying for dinner and God opening His hand.

Thirty-five verses. Here's the shape: *He wears light. The sky's His tent. The sea ran when He spoke. Springs for the wild donkeys. Wine to make you glad. Crags for the badgers. Lions asking God for dinner. Leviathan — made to PLAY. You open Your hand — everyone eats. Your breath goes out — life. Comes back — dust. Goes out again — new. May He rejoice in what He made. Hallelujah.*

Go outside. Everything you see is looking up at an open hand. So are you.
