# PSALM 117 — All You Nations

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 117:1–2. Two verses. The shortest psalm — and the shortest chapter in the Bible. The middle chapter of the Bible, by count. And it's addressed to everyone.*

---

Two verses. Seventeen Hebrew words. Right in the middle of the Passover Hallel — right in the middle of the *Bible* — the smallest psalm says the biggest thing.

**"Praise the Source — all you nations! Extol Him — all you peoples!"**

*All you nations.* *Goyim.* The Gentiles. The outsiders. The Egyptians who enslaved them. The Babylonians who burned the temple. The Philistines, the Moabites, the Edomites — Psalm 83's ten-nation coalition. *All of them.*

*Praise Him.*

This is *Passover*. The night Israel remembers being rescued *from* the nations. And they stop — in the middle of it — and *invite the nations in*. Psalm 87 — *born in Zion*. Psalm 96 — *sing, all the earth*. Psalm 67 — *let ALL the peoples thank You*. Here it's the *whole psalm*. Nothing *but* the invitation.

Paul quoted this — Romans 15:11 — to prove the Gentiles were *always* in the plan. *Praise the Lord, all you Gentiles; let all the peoples extol Him.* He pulled it from the *Passover* liturgy. The most *Jewish* night of the year — and it contains the door for *everyone*.

**"Because great is His loyal-love toward us — and the faithfulness of the Source endures forever."**

*Toward us.* Wait — *us*. Israel. He's telling the nations: *praise Him because of what He did for US.* Not for you — for *us*. And that's the point. The nations are supposed to look at *Israel's* rescue — the sea, the manna, the return from exile — and go: *if He did that for THEM — He's worth praising by ANYONE.*

The Jewish story is the *evidence*. The whole world's invited to *read* it.

*Great is His loyal-love.* The Hebrew is *gavar aleinu chasdo* — His *chesed* has *prevailed* over us. *Overpowered.* Not "been nice to." *Won.* Love as a *conquering* force.

*Faithfulness forever.* *Emet l'olam.* Psalm 100's ending. Psalm 89's seven. The word that doesn't move.

**"Praise the Source!"**

*Hallelujah.*

Two verses. Here's the shape: *ALL nations — praise Him. Because look what He did for us. His love overpowered us. His faithfulness is forever. Hallelujah.*

Here's the thing about the shortest psalm: there's nothing *in* it but the invitation and the reason. No lament. No enemy. No *how long*. Just — *everyone, come. Here's why.*

Jesus sang this the night before He died. Two verses. *All you nations.* The next day He'd open the door for them — with His arms spread wide enough to hold the whole list.

You're a *nation*. You're a *people*. You're invited. It's the middle of the Bible — and it says *come*.
