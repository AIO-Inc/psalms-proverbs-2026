# PSALM 132 — I Will Not Give Sleep to My Eyes

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 132:1–18. Thirteenth Song of Ascent — the longest of the fifteen. Eighteen verses. Two oaths: David swears to find God a house; God swears to keep David's line. The pilgrims are almost at the Temple — and this is the song of how it got there.*

---

Twelve songs in, the road's been about *you* — your tent in Kedar, your foot on the path, your soul like a weaned child. Psalm 132 pulls the camera back. *Why is there a Temple at the top of this hill at all?*

Because a man made a vow and couldn't sleep until he kept it.

**"Source — remember David, and all his hardships."**

*Remember David.* The pilgrims are asking God to *remember* — Psalm 105 — *He remembered*. Psalm 106 — *they forgot*. Here — *remember the man*. Not for his sake. For the *promise* attached to him.

Here's the vow:

**"He swore an oath to the Source — he made a vow to the Mighty One of Jacob: 'I will not enter my house — or go to my bed. I will not give sleep to my eyes — or slumber to my eyelids — until I find a place for the Source — a dwelling for the Mighty One of Jacob.'"**

*I will not give sleep to my eyes.* Listen to that. The king's got a palace, a bed, everything. And he's saying *I'm not sleeping in it until God has a place too.* 2 Samuel 7 — David looks at his cedar house and says *the Ark of God sits in a tent.* It bothered him. It bothered him enough to lose sleep.

*Until I find a place.* The Ark had been *lost*. Captured by the Philistines. Then parked at a farmhouse — Kiriath-Jearim — for decades. Israel had *forgotten where God was*. And David went looking.

**"We heard it in Ephrathah — we found it in the fields of Jaar."**

*The fields of Jaar* — that's Kiriath-Jearim, the farmhouse. *We found it.* Like finding something in the attic you'd stopped looking for. God had been sitting in a barn for twenty years because nobody bothered to bring Him home.

You ever lose track of where God is? Not lose faith — just… stop knowing where He *sits* in your life? Psalm 132 says — *go find the Ark. Bring it back. Lose sleep over it if you have to.*

**"Let us go to His dwelling place — let us worship at His footstool. Arise, Source, and come to Your resting place — You and the Ark of Your might."**

*Arise, Source.* That's Numbers 10 — the words Moses said every time the Ark moved: *Arise, Source, let Your enemies be scattered.* Psalm 68 opened with it too. The pilgrims are singing the Ark's *traveling song*. Because they're traveling *to* it.

*Your resting place.* The Ark had been *wandering* since Sinai. Tent to tent. Battle to barn. David says — *rest here.*

**"May Your priests be clothed with right-standing — may Your loyal ones sing for joy. For the sake of Your servant David — do not reject Your anointed one."**

That's David's half. Now — the turn. God *answers* the oath with an oath:

**"The Source swore an oath to David — a sure oath He will not revoke: 'One of your own descendants I will place on your throne. If your sons keep My covenant and the statutes I teach them — then their sons will sit on your throne for ever and ever.'"**

*A sure oath He will not revoke.* Psalm 89 — the *same* oath, and Psalm 89 ended with *where is it? the crown's in the dirt.* Psalm 132 — the pilgrims are singing it *again*, after the exile, after the crown's been in the dirt for generations. Not because they've seen it kept. Because *He swore.*

*If your sons keep My covenant.* There's the condition. And the sons didn't. So how does the oath survive? Read on.

**"For the Source has chosen Zion — He has desired it for His dwelling: 'This is My resting place for ever and ever. Here I will sit enthroned — for I have desired it.'"**

*I have desired it.* *Ivvitiha.* God *wanted* this hill. Not needed. *Wanted.* Psalm 68 — *why do you gaze in envy, you rugged mountains? The Source desired Zion.* Psalm 87 — *the Source loves the gates of Zion.* He picked it. Out of all the mountains — *this one*.

**"I will bless her with abundant provisions — her poor I will satisfy with food. I will clothe her priests with salvation — and her loyal ones will ever sing for joy."**

Watch the echo. David asked *clothe the priests with right-standing*. God says *I'll clothe them with SALVATION.* David asked *let the loyal ones sing*. God says *they will EVER sing.* Every request — answered *bigger*. That's Ephesians 3 — *more than we ask or imagine.*

*Her poor I will satisfy with food.* The Temple as *soup kitchen*. Psalm 22 — *the poor will eat and be satisfied*. God's house has a table for the ones who can't pay.

And the last two verses — the answer to Psalm 89:

**"Here I will make a horn grow for David — and set up a lamp for My anointed one. I will clothe his enemies with shame — but his head will be adorned with a radiant crown."**

*A horn grow.* *Tzemach* — a *sprout*, a *branch*. Jeremiah 23 — *I will raise up for David a righteous Branch*. Zechariah 3 — *My servant the Branch*. Luke 1 — Zechariah the priest, at the Temple, singing: *He has raised up a horn of salvation for us in the house of His servant David.* The pilgrims sang Psalm 132 for five hundred years not knowing the sprout would be a carpenter's son.

*A lamp for My anointed.* 1 Kings 11 — *so that David My servant may always have a lamp before Me in Jerusalem.* The lamp never went out. It just looked like it had.

*His head adorned with a radiant crown.* Psalm 89 — *You've defiled his crown in the dust.* Psalm 132 — *his crown will shine.* Same crown. Different day. *Yatzitz* — it will *blossom*. Like the horn *sprouts*. Dead stumps grow.

Eighteen verses. Here's the shape: *Remember David — he couldn't sleep until God had a home. The Ark was lost in a barn — we found it. Arise, Source — rest here. Clothe the priests, let the loyal sing, keep Your anointed. — And God swears back: your line on the throne. I CHOSE Zion — I wanted it. I'll feed her poor, clothe her priests with salvation, make them sing FOREVER. A horn will sprout. A lamp will burn. The crown will blossom.*

Here's what to carry. Two oaths. Yours — *I won't rest until God has His place in my life.* His — *I won't revoke what I swore.* Yours can fail. His can't. And the whole Temple at the top of this road is proof that a lost-sleep vow and an unbreakable promise can meet on the same hill.

What have you let sit in the barn? Go get it.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 — *unnoto* = his afflictions/hardships (Ketiv); Qere *anvato* = his humility — both readings preserved. v. 2, 5 — *avir Ya'akov* = "the Mighty One of Jacob" (Gen 49:24). v. 3–5 — the oath; cf. 2 Sam 7:1–2; 1 Chr 15. v. 6 — *Efratah* = Bethlehem region / Ephraim (Shiloh); *s'dei-ya'ar* = "fields of the wood/Jaar" = Kiriath-Jearim (1 Sam 7:1–2; 2 Sam 6). v. 8 — *kumah YHWH* = "Arise, Source" — Num 10:35; cf. 68:1; Solomon quotes v. 8–10 at the Temple dedication (2 Chr 6:41–42). v. 11 — *emet lo-yashuv mimennah* = "a truth He will not turn back from" — cf. 89:35, 49. v. 12 — the conditional (*im*); cf. 89:30–33. v. 13–14 — *ivvah* = desire/long for (×2); cf. 68:16; 87:2. v. 17 — *atzmiach keren* = "I will cause a horn to sprout" — *tzemach*/*tzamach* is the Branch vocabulary (Jer 23:5; 33:15; Zech 3:8; 6:12); quoted Luke 1:69; *ner* = lamp (1 Kgs 11:36; 15:4). v. 18 — *yatzitz nizro* = "his crown will blossom/shine" — *tzitz* = flower/gleam; direct reversal of 89:39. No *yirah* in this psalm.*
