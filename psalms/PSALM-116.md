# PSALM 116 — I Will Lift Up the Cup of Salvation

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 116:1–19. Fourth Hallel psalm. One person's testimony in the middle of the national celebration — "I was dying, I called, He heard." And a cup.*

---

The Hallel's been *we* — the nation, the Exodus, the idols. Psalm 116 is *I*. One voice at the Passover table saying *let me tell you what He did for ME.*

**"I love the Source — because He heard my voice. He heard my cry for mercy."**

*I love the Source.* The Hebrew starts with *ahavti* — *I love*. Not "I trust." Not "I obey." *Love.* And the reason — *because He HEARD.* Psalm 115's idols have ears that don't work. Psalm 116 opens with a God whose ears *do*.

**"Because He turned His ear to me — I will call on Him as long as I live."**

*Turned His ear.* Psalm 40 — *He bent down to me.* Psalm 113 — *He stoops.* The ear *inclined*. Like leaning in to hear a whisper.

*As long as I live.* The rest of my life — *calling*. Because the first call *worked*.

Now — what happened:

**"The cords of death entangled me. The anguish of the grave came over me. I was overcome by distress and sorrow."**

*Cords of death.* Psalm 18 — *the ropes of death wrapped around me.* He was *tied up*. The grave had a *rope* on him. Not near death — *caught* by it.

**"Then I called on the name of the Source: 'Source — save me!'"**

*Three words.* *Save me.* No theology. No formula. Psalm 107's *then they cried*. Here's what the cry *sounded like*. Three words.

**"The Source is gracious and right. Our God is full of compassion."**

Exodus 34 again. Psalm 103, 111, 112. The Passover table keeps coming back to the self-description.

**"The Source protects the unwary. When I was brought low — He saved me."**

*The unwary.* The Hebrew is *p'ta'im* — the simple, the naive, the ones who didn't see it coming. Not the strategists. The ones who *walked into it*. God protects *those* people. That's most of us.

And then — the line that's on every recovery wall:

**"Return to your rest, my soul — because the Source has been good to you."**

*Return to your rest.* He's talking to his soul — Psalm 42, 103. *Soul — you can stand down.* The ropes are off. *Go back to resting.* Because — *He's been good to you.* Not "will be." *Has been.*

**"Because You, Source, have delivered me from death — my eyes from tears — my feet from stumbling —"**

*Death. Tears. Stumbling.* Three rescues. The big one — and the two small ones. He didn't just survive. His *eyes* got dried and his *feet* got steadied.

**"— that I may walk before the Source in the land of the living."**

*The land of the living.* Psalm 27 — *I'll see the goodness of the Source in the land of the living.* He's *here*. Still. Walking.

**"I trusted in the Source when I said: 'I am greatly afflicted.'"**

Read the order. *I trusted — when I said — I'm afflicted.* Saying *I'm in trouble* IS trust. Not the absence of it. The complaint went *to God* — that's the trust.

**"In my alarm I said: 'Everyone is a liar.'"**

*Everyone is a liar.* He said that. In the Bible. In *alarm* — the Hebrew is *b'chofzi*, in my panic. Psalm 31:22 — *I said in my panic: I've been cut off.* Psalm 116 — *I said in my panic: nobody can be trusted.* Both are in the book. Both were *wrong*. Both were *honest*. He records what he said when he was scared — and doesn't edit it.

Now — the question. And it's the question of every rescued person:

**"What shall I return to the Source — for all His goodness to me?"**

*What can I give back?* The ropes are off, the eyes are dry, the feet are steady — *what do I DO?* And here's the answer. And it's not what you'd guess:

**"I will lift up the cup of salvation — and call on the name of the Source."**

*Lift up the cup.*

Not "I'll sacrifice a bull." Not "I'll build a shrine." *I'll raise a glass.* The Hebrew is *kos-y'shu'ot* — the cup of *salvations*, plural. The Passover cup. The one on the table right now. He *lifts it* — and *calls on the name*.

That's what you give back for rescue. You *drink to it*. You *toast*. You name Him out loud with the cup in your hand.

And — Jesus. The night before the cross. The Passover table. Psalm 116 on His lips. *He took the cup — gave thanks — and said: this is My blood.* Luke 22. He *lifted the cup of salvation*. And *called on the name*. Then He went and *became* the salvation the cup was for.

**"I will fulfill my vows to the Source — in the presence of all His people."**

*In the presence of all.* Publicly. The vow made in the ropes — paid at the table.

**"Precious in the sight of the Source is the death of His faithful servants."**

*Precious.* The Hebrew is *yakar* — Psalm 36, 72. *Weighty. Costly.* When one of His loyal ones dies — God doesn't shrug. It *costs* Him. He *feels the weight* of every one. Nobody's death is cheap to Him.

**"Truly I am Your servant, Source. I serve You just as my mother did. You have freed me from my chains."**

*As my mother did.* Psalm 86 — *the son of Your servant woman*. Hereditary loyalty. She served — so I do.

*Freed me from my chains.* The ropes of verse 3. *Off.*

**"I will sacrifice a thank offering to You — and call on the name of the Source. I will fulfill my vows to the Source — in the presence of all His people — in the courts of the house of the Source — in your midst, Jerusalem."**

*In your midst, Jerusalem.* He's *there*. The place he almost didn't live to see.

**"Praise the Source!"**

Nineteen verses. Here's the shape: *I LOVE Him — because He heard. Death had a rope on me. I said three words — save me. He protects the naive. Soul — return to your rest. Death, tears, stumbling — all handled. I said everyone's a liar — in my panic — and He saved me anyway. What do I give back? — I lift the cup. I call on the name. My death is PRECIOUS to Him. He freed me from the chains. I'm in Jerusalem.*

Jesus sang this. Then He lifted the cup. Then He paid the vow *in the presence of all people* — on a hill outside the city.

You've got a cup. Lift it. Call on the name. That's what you give back.
