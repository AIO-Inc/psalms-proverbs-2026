# PSALM 57 — Awake, My Glory

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 57:1–11. Written in the cave — David hiding from Saul (1 Samuel 22 or 24).*

---

He's in a cave. Literally. Dark, cold, damp, with Saul's army somewhere outside. And this is the song that came out.

**"Be gracious to me, God — be gracious to me. Because my soul takes refuge in You. In the shadow of Your wings I take refuge — until the destruction passes by."**

*Until it passes.* He knows it's going to pass. That's the assumption baked into the sentence. Not *if*. *Until.* Storms have durations. This one does too.

*Shadow of Your wings* — Psalm 17, Psalm 36. The mother bird over the nest. He's in a *cave* — and he says he's under *wings*. Same darkness, different name for it.

**"I call to God Most High — to God who fulfills His purpose for me."**

*Who fulfills His purpose for me.* The Hebrew is *gomer alai* — "who completes for me," "who brings it to an end on my behalf." He's in a cave, and he's naming God as *the One who finishes what He started with me.* You don't get to a cave by accident when God's finishing something. The cave is part of the finishing.

"He'll send from heaven and save me. He'll shame the one trampling me. God will send His loyal-love and His truth."

Psalm 43 — *send out Your light and Your truth*. Same two agents. God dispatches His character to come get you.

**"My soul is among lions. I lie down among the ones breathing fire — the children of Adam whose teeth are spears and arrows, whose tongue is a sharp sword."**

*I lie down among lions.* He's trying to *sleep* — in a cave — with predators around. And the predators are *men*. Teeth like spears. Tongues like swords. Psalm 52's razor tongue again.

And then — the refrain. He'll say it twice, and it's bigger than the whole cave:

**"Be exalted above the heavens, God! Let Your glory be over all the earth!"**

He's in a hole in the ground — and he's talking about *above the heavens*. The smaller his space, the bigger his God. That's not denial. That's *perspective*. The cave is a few hundred square feet. The glory is *over all the earth*. He picks which one to look at.

"They've spread a net for my steps. My soul is bowed down. They dug a pit in front of me — **they fell into the middle of it.**"

Psalm 7 — the pit-digger falls in. It keeps happening because it keeps being *true*.

And now — the turn. And it's one of the most beautiful stretches in the Psalms:

**"My heart is steadfast, God. My heart is steadfast. I will sing. I will make music."**

*Steadfast.* Twice. The Hebrew is *nachon* — fixed, established, prepared. Like a foundation stone that's been set and isn't moving. His *circumstances* are chaos. His *heart* is set. Those two things are independent, and he's just proved it.

**"Awake, my glory! Awake, harp and lyre! I will awaken the dawn!"**

*I will awaken the dawn.* Read that again. He's not going to wait for the sun to come up and *then* sing. He's going to sing *first* — and *wake the sun up*. Reverse the order. Most people let the morning determine their mood. David's going to let his song *start* the morning.

In a cave. Surrounded by lions. *I will awaken the dawn.*

That's the most defiant line in the first sixty psalms. It says: *I'm not a passenger. I'm not waiting for conditions to improve so I can worship. I'm going to worship — and the conditions can catch up.*

**"I will thank You among the peoples, Master. I will sing to You among the nations. Because Your loyal-love is great — up to the heavens. Your truth — to the clouds."**

*Among the nations.* He's in a cave — and he's planning a world tour. Because the song's too big for the cave.

And the refrain, second time:

**"Be exalted above the heavens, God! Let Your glory be over all the earth!"**

Eleven verses. Cave to cosmos. *Be gracious — I'm under Your wings until it passes — I'm among lions — but You're above the heavens — they dug the pit and fell in — my heart is STEADFAST — wake up, harp — I'll wake the dawn — I'll sing among the nations — You're over all the earth.*

Whatever cave you're in: your heart can be steadfast while everything else shakes. Sing first. Wake the dawn. Don't wait for the sun.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* ad-ya'avor havot *= "until the destruction passes over" — cf. Isaiah 26:20. v. 2 —* El gomer alai *= "God who completes/perfects for me." v. 7 —* nachon libi *= my heart is fixed/established (x2). v. 8 —* a'irah shachar *= "I will awaken the dawn" — active, causative; some render "I will awake early" but the Hebrew has David as the one waking the dawn, not the reverse. Paul & Silas sang in prison at midnight (Acts 16:25) — same spirit. No* yirah*.*
