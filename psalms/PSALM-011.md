# PSALM 11 — Don't Fly Away

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 11:1–7.*

---

Everybody's telling you to run.

"In the Source I take refuge. So how can you say to me, 'Fly away like a bird to your mountain'?"

That's the advice he's getting. *Get out. Hide. Disappear until this blows over.* Well-meaning people telling him to bail. And he pushes back: *I already have a refuge. Why would I leave it?*

He's not denying the danger:

"Look — the mark-missers are bending the bow. They've got the arrow on the string. They're going to shoot from the shadows at the upright in heart. When the foundations are being torn down — what can the one who does right even do?"

*When the foundations are torn down.* That's not a personal problem. That's the whole structure of things coming apart. Institutions failing. Trust gone. The stuff you thought was solid turning out to be hollow. What do you *do* with that?

Here's what he does. He looks up:

**"The Source is in His holy temple. The Source — His throne is in the heavens."**

The foundations down here are shaking. The throne up there is not. That's the whole answer. You don't fly to the mountain — you remember Who's above the mountain.

"His eyes watch. His gaze examines the children of Adam."

Watching. Not glancing — *examining*. Testing. Taking the measure.

"The Source tests the one who does right — and the mark-misser, and the lover of violence, His soul hates."

Two kinds of testing. The upright get *tested* — refined, stretched, proven, like gold in fire. It's not comfortable, but it's not rejection. The lover of violence gets *rejected* — not the person, the *love of harm*. God's soul cannot abide the thing that tears people apart.

"He'll rain coals on the mark-missers — fire and sulfur and a scorching wind will be the portion in their cup."

The cup. Everybody gets a cup. What's in yours is what you chose to pour.

**"Because the Source is right, He loves right actions. The upright will see His face."**

That's the finish. Not "the upright will escape to the mountain." *The upright will see His face.* You stay planted, you stay in the refuge, and the reward isn't safety — it's *sight*. You get to look at Him.

The bird that flies away never gets that. It's safe on the mountain, alone.

Don't fly. Stay in the refuge. The foundations are shaking; the throne isn't. And you're going to see His face.

---

*Note on translation: v. 5 — "the LORD trieth the righteous" =* bachan *= to test, assay, refine (metallurgy term). The test is proof of value, not punishment. "His soul hateth" = rejection of the way of violence, not of the person. v. 7 — "His countenance doth behold the upright" (KJV) — Hebrew reads "the upright shall behold His face" — the direction of the seeing was reversed in translation. No* yirah*.*
