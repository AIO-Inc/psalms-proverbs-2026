# PSALM 69 — The Waters Have Come Up to My Neck

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 69:1–36. After Psalm 22, the psalm most quoted about the crucifixion — the vinegar, the zeal for the house, the hatred without cause.*

---

Psalm 68 was the parade. Psalm 69 is the man drowning while the parade goes by.

**"Save me, God — because the waters have come up to my neck."**

*To my neck.* One more inch. He's not describing a hard time. He's describing the last breath before the water covers the mouth.

**"I'm sinking in deep mud — there's nowhere to stand. I've come into deep water. The flood sweeps over me."**

Psalm 40's mud — but this time he's not out of it yet. *Nowhere to stand.* The feet reach down and find nothing.

**"I'm worn out from calling. My throat is dry. My eyes fail — from waiting for my God."**

*My throat is dry* — from *praying*. He's prayed himself hoarse. *Eyes fail from waiting.* You can wear your eyes out looking for someone who isn't showing up.

**"The ones who hate me for no reason outnumber the hairs on my head. My enemies are mighty — the ones destroying me with lies. What I didn't steal — I have to return."**

*Hate me for no reason.* John 15:25 — Jesus quotes this. *They hated Me without cause.* And *what I didn't steal, I have to return* — paying for a crime you didn't commit. That's the whole crucifixion in one line.

**"God — You know my foolishness. My guilt isn't hidden from You."**

He's not perfect. He says so. Which makes the *hated without cause* land harder — he has real faults, and they're still attacking him for the *fake* ones.

**"Don't let the ones who wait for You be ashamed because of me, Master — Source of Armies. Don't let the ones who seek You be disgraced because of me, God of Israel."**

Look at this. He's drowning — and he's worried about *other people's faith*. *If I go down, will they stop believing?* The man at his lowest is thinking about the ones watching.

**"Because for Your sake I've carried reproach. Shame covers my face. I've become a stranger to my brothers — a foreigner to my mother's sons."**

*A stranger to my mother's sons.* His own family doesn't claim him. John 7:5 — *even His brothers did not believe in Him.*

And then — the verse:

**"Because zeal for Your house has eaten me up. The insults aimed at You have fallen on me."**

*Zeal for Your house has eaten me up.* John 2:17 — when Jesus flipped the tables in the temple, the disciples remembered *this line*. The passion for God's house *consumed* Him. It's not a hobby. It *eats* you.

*The insults aimed at You have fallen on me.* He's standing where God stands, so what's thrown at God hits him. That's what it costs to represent Someone people hate.

"When I wept and fasted — it became a reproach against me. When I wore sackcloth — I became a proverb to them. The ones sitting at the gate talk about me. I'm the song of the drunks."

*The song of the drunks.* He's a *joke* in the bar. They've got a tune about him.

**"But I — my prayer is to You, Source. In the time of Your favor, God — in the abundance of Your loyal-love — answer me with Your faithful rescue."**

*But I.* Psalm 55's two words. They do their thing. *I* pray.

"Deliver me from the mud — don't let me sink. Rescue me from the ones who hate me — from the deep waters. Don't let the flood sweep over me. Don't let the deep swallow me. Don't let the pit close its mouth over me."

*Don't let the pit close its mouth.* The grave is a *mouth*. And it's *closing*.

**"Answer me, Source — because Your loyal-love is good. In the abundance of Your compassion — turn to me. Don't hide Your face from Your servant. I'm in trouble — hurry, answer me."**

"Come near my soul. Redeem it. Ransom me because of my enemies. **You know my reproach — my shame and my disgrace. All my enemies are in front of You.**"

**"Reproach has broken my heart. I'm sick. I looked for sympathy — there was none. For comforters — I found none."**

*Broken my heart.* Not the enemies' swords. *The reproach.* The words did it.

*I looked for comforters — none.* Nobody came. Not one.

And then — the verse everyone knows without knowing where it's from:

**"They gave me poison for food. For my thirst — they gave me vinegar to drink."**

Matthew 27:34, 48. John 19:29. On the cross — *they offered Him wine mixed with gall. They lifted a sponge full of vinegar to His mouth.* A thousand years before, David wrote down what it would taste like.

Now — the imprecatory section. And it's long, and it's brutal:

"Let their table be a trap before them. Let their feasts become a snare. Let their eyes go dark so they can't see. Make their loins shake continually. Pour out Your anger on them. Let Your burning wrath overtake them. Let their camp be desolate. Let no one live in their tents. **Because they persecuted the one You struck — and talk about the pain of the one You wounded.**"

*They persecuted the one You struck.* He was already down — from God's hand — and they *piled on*. Kicked the man God was disciplining. That's the crime: not opposing him, but *enjoying* his suffering.

"Add guilt to their guilt. Don't let them come into Your right-standing. Let them be blotted from the book of life. Don't let them be written with the ones who do right."

Peter quotes verse 25 in Acts 1:20 — about *Judas*. *Let his camp be desolate.* The imprecations landed on someone.

**"But I — I'm afflicted and in pain. Let Your rescue, God, set me high."**

*But I.* Second time.

**"I will praise the name of God with a song. I'll magnify Him with thanksgiving. And that will please the Source more than an ox — more than a bull with horns and hooves."**

Psalm 50, Psalm 51 — the song beats the bull.

**"The humble will see — and be glad. You who seek God — let your heart live!"**

*Let your heart live.* An imperative. *Heart — LIVE.* Said by the man with water at his neck, to the ones watching him.

**"Because the Source hears the needy. He doesn't despise His captive people."**

*Doesn't despise.* Psalm 51 — the broken heart He won't despise. Psalm 22 — the affliction of the afflicted He didn't despise. It's the same promise every time: *He doesn't look away from the ones at the bottom.*

**"Let heaven and earth praise Him — the seas and everything moving in them. Because God will save Zion. He'll rebuild the cities of Judah. They'll settle there and possess it. The seed of His servants will inherit it. The ones who love His name will live there."**

Thirty-six verses. From *water at my neck* to *heaven and earth praise Him.* From *no comforters* to *let your heart live.* From *vinegar* to *the cities rebuilt.*

Here's the shape, and it's the shape of the cross: *He's drowning. Hated without cause. Zeal ate Him. His family disowned Him. They gave Him vinegar. Nobody came. And He said: heart — live. He doesn't despise the ones at the bottom.*

If the water's at your neck — this is your psalm. Say *but I*. Say *let your heart live*. He doesn't despise you. He rebuilds cities.
