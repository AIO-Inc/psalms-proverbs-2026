# PSALM 143 — Do Not Bring Your Servant Into Judgment

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 143:1–12. Twelve verses. Attributed to David. The seventh and last of the penitential psalms (6, 32, 38, 51, 102, 130, 143). Paul builds Romans 3 and Galatians 2 on verse two. A man who's stopped arguing his case and started asking for the Spirit.*

---

Seven penitential psalms. Psalm 6 — *I'm worn out from groaning*. Psalm 32 — *when I kept silent my bones wasted*. Psalm 38 — *my wounds fester*. Psalm 51 — *create in me a clean heart*. Psalm 102 — *my days vanish like smoke*. Psalm 130 — *out of the depths*. And now Psalm 143 — the last one. And it's the *quietest*. No sackcloth. No bones. Just a man who knows he can't win the argument and doesn't try.

**"Source — hear my prayer. Listen to my cry for mercy. In Your faithfulness and right-standing — come to my relief."**

*In Your faithfulness and right-standing.* *Be-emunat'cha b'tzidkat'cha.* He's appealing to *God's* character. Not *I've been good*. *YOU are faithful. YOU do right.* That's the whole basis. It has to be — because of verse two.

**"Do not bring Your servant into judgment — for no one living is righteous before You."**

*Do not bring me into judgment.* *Al-tavo v'mishpat.* *Don't take me to court.* Because if You do — I lose. Everyone does.

*No one living is righteous before You.* *Lo-yitzdak l'fanecha chol-chai.* Not one. Psalm 14 — *there is no one who does good, not even one*. Psalm 130 — *if You kept a record of sins, who could stand?* Psalm 143 — *nobody. Not me. Not anybody breathing.*

Paul took this verse and made it the *hinge* of the gospel. Romans 3:20 — *no one will be declared righteous by the works of the law*. Galatians 2:16 — *by works of the law no one will be justified*. He's quoting Psalm 143:2. David said it first — in a pit, with no theology degree — *if this is a trial, I lose. So don't make it a trial.*

That's the penitential heart. Not *I'm sorry — now I've earned it back*. It's *I can't earn it. Don't grade me. Be faithful instead.*

**"The enemy pursues me — he crushes me to the ground. He makes me dwell in the darkness — like those long dead."**

*Crushes me to the ground.* Psalm 7 — *lest he trample my life to the ground*. Psalm 143 — *he already did*. Past tense. Face in the dirt. Psalm 44 — *our bodies cling to the ground*.

*In the darkness like those long dead.* *K'metei olam.* Like the *dead of long ago*. Psalm 88 — *counted among the dead… in the darkest depths*. He's alive but feels *buried*. Not recently dead — *ancient* dead. Forgotten dead. That's the depression that says *I've been gone so long nobody remembers I was here*.

**"So my spirit grows faint within me — my heart within me is dismayed."**

*My spirit grows faint.* Psalm 142 — *same phrase*. *B'hit'atef alai ruchi.* Two psalms in a row — *the spirit curling up*. The editors put 142 and 143 side by side because they're the *same man on two consecutive nights*.

*My heart is dismayed.* *Yishtomem.* *Appalled.* *Stunned.* Not sad — *numb*. Ezekiel 3 — *I sat there among them for seven days, stunned*. The word for when there's nothing left to feel.

Now — the turn. And watch what he does — he *remembers*:

**"I remember the days of long ago. I meditate on all Your works — and consider what Your hands have done."**

*I remember.* *Zacharti.* Psalm 77 — *I will remember the deeds of the Source*. Psalm 143 — *same move*. When the heart's numb — you go to the *archive*. Not to *feel* something. To *know* something. *What did He do before? He did that. He's the same.*

*What Your hands have done.* Psalm 138 — *don't abandon the work of Your hands*. Psalm 139 — *Your hands knit me*. Psalm 143 — *I'm looking at Your hands' résumé*.

**"I spread out my hands to You. My soul thirsts for You — like a parched land."**

*Spread out my hands.* Psalm 141 — *the lifting of my hands as the evening sacrifice*. Psalm 143 — *hands OPEN*. Palms up. Empty. The posture of somebody who's got nothing to hand over and everything to receive.

*Like a parched land.* *K'eretz ayefah.* A *weary* land. Psalm 42 — *as the deer pants*. Psalm 63 — *in a dry and weary land*. Psalm 143 — *I AM the dry land*. Not thirsty *in* it. *It.* Cracked. Waiting for rain that isn't coming.

*A breath.*

**"Answer me quickly, Source — my spirit fails. Do not hide Your face from me — or I will be like those who go down to the pit."**

*My spirit fails.* *Kal'tah ruchi.* It's *finishing*. Running out. Psalm 84 — *my soul yearns, even faints*. Psalm 143 — *it's almost gone*.

*Do not hide Your face.* Psalm 13, 27, 30, 69, 88, 102 — the *hidden face*. Psalm 143 — *if You hide it — I'm in the pit*. Not *I'll feel sad*. *I'll be DEAD*. The face is *oxygen*.

**"Let the morning bring me word of Your loyal-love — for I have put my trust in You. Show me the way I should go — for to You I lift up my soul."**

*Let the morning bring me word.* *Hashmi'eni va-boker chasd'cha.* *Make me HEAR in the morning Your chesed.* Psalm 30 — *joy comes in the morning*. Psalm 90 — *satisfy us in the morning with Your loyal-love*. Psalm 143 — *I need to HEAR it. First thing.* Before the day gets a chance to say anything else.

*Show me the way I should go.* *Hodi'eni derech.* Psalm 25 — *show me Your ways*. Psalm 142 — *You know my way*. Psalm 143 — *then tell ME*. He's asking for *direction*, not just rescue. *I don't know which way to walk.*

*To You I lift up my soul.* *Elecha nasati nafshi.* *Nasa* — the *lift-off* word. The scapegoat verb — Leviticus 16. He *lifts* his soul *up* — the way sin gets lifted off. *Take it.*

**"Rescue me from my enemies, Source — for I hide myself in You. Teach me to do Your will — for You are my God. May Your good Spirit lead me on level ground."**

*Teach me to do Your will.* *Lam'deni la'asot r'tzonecha.* Not *tell me*. *TEACH me* — like a skill. Psalm 51 — *create in me a clean heart*. Psalm 143 — *and then TRAIN it*.

*May Your good Spirit lead me.* *Ruchacha tovah tanchéni.* This is rare. Psalm 51 — *take not Your Holy Spirit from me*. Psalm 139 — *where can I go from Your Spirit?* Psalm 143 — *let Your Spirit LEAD me*. The *guide* isn't a map. It's *God's own breath* walking in front of you. Nehemiah 9 — *You gave Your good Spirit to instruct them*. Romans 8 — *led by the Spirit*. Paul's Spirit-led life has its seed *here*, in the seventh penitential psalm.

*On level ground.* *B'eretz mishor.* Psalm 26 — *my feet stand on level ground*. Psalm 27 — *lead me in a straight path*. After the parched land and the pit — *flat, open, walkable*. Not the mountaintop. Just — *ground I won't trip on*.

**"For Your name's sake, Source — preserve my life. In Your right-standing — bring me out of trouble. In Your loyal-love — silence my enemies. Destroy all my foes — for I am Your servant."**

*For Your name's sake.* Not mine. Psalm 23, 25, 31, 79, 109 — *for Your name's sake*. The reason God should act is *God*. David's out of his own reasons.

*I am Your servant.* *Ani avdecha.* First verse — *Your servant*. Last verse — *Your servant*. That's the frame. Not *Your righteous one* — verse two killed that. *Your SERVANT.* Somebody who belongs to You. And you take care of what's yours.

Twelve verses. Here's the shape: *Hear me — because YOU'RE faithful. Don't take me to court — nobody wins that. I'm crushed. In the dark like the long dead. My spirit's curling. My heart's numb. — I remember what You did. Hands open. I'm the dry land. Hurry. Don't hide Your face. — Let the MORNING bring Your loyal-love. Show me the way. Teach me. Let Your Spirit lead — on flat ground. For Your name. I'm Your servant.*

Here's your practice. Tomorrow morning — before the phone, before the news — say verse eight. *Let the morning bring me word of Your loyal-love.* Then verse ten — *let Your good Spirit lead me on level ground.* You don't need the whole map. You need the next flat step and the breath in front of you.

Seven penitential psalms. Not one of them says *I've fixed it*. Every one says *You do*. That's why there are seven.

---

*Note on translation: Seventh penitential psalm (6, 32, 38, 51, 102, 130, 143). v. 1 — *be-emunat'cha b'tzidkat'cha* = "in Your faithfulness, in Your righteousness/right-standing." **v. 2 — *al-tavo v'mishpat et-avdecha* = "do not enter into judgment with Your servant"; *ki lo-yitzdak l'fanecha chol-chai* = "for no living one is righteous before You" — quoted Rom 3:20; Gal 2:16 (with "by works of law" added by Paul); cf. 130:3; 14:3; Job 9:2; 25:4.** v. 3 — *ba-machashakkim k'metei olam* = "in dark places like the dead of long ago/forever" (cf. 88:5–6; Lam 3:6). v. 4 — *va-tit'atef alai ruchi* — identical to 142:3; *yishtomem libbi* = "my heart is appalled/stunned" (cf. Ezek 3:15). v. 5 — *zacharti yamim mi-kedem* = "I remember days from before" (cf. 77:5, 11). v. 6 — *k'eretz-ayefah* = "like a weary land" (cf. 63:1). Selah after v. 6 — rendered as breath. v. 7 — *kal'tah ruchi* = "my spirit is finished/spent"; *al-taster panecha* = hidden face (cf. 13:1; 27:9; 88:14; 102:2). v. 8 — *hashmi'eni va-boker chasd'cha* = "cause me to hear in the morning Your loyal-love" (cf. 30:5; 90:14); *nasati nafshi* = "I lift up my soul" — *nasa* (cf. 25:1; 86:4). v. 10 — *lam'deni la'asot r'tzonecha* = "teach me to do Your will/pleasure"; ***ruchacha tovah tanchéni b'eretz mishor* = "Your good Spirit — may it lead me in level land" — one of few OT references to the Spirit as personal guide (cf. Neh 9:20; Isa 63:10–14; Rom 8:14; Gal 5:18);** some MSS/LXX read "in Your good Spirit"; both preserved. v. 11 — *l'ma'an-shimcha* = for Your name's sake. v. 12 — *ani avdecha* = "I am Your servant" — frames the psalm (v. 2, 12). No *yirah* in this psalm.*
