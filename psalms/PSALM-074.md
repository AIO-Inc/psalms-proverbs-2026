# PSALM 74 — They Burned Your House

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 74:1–23. Asaph. The temple has been destroyed — most likely 586 BC, the Babylonians. A lament over the ashes.*

---

The building's gone. Not damaged — *gone*. The place where God lived, where every psalm in this book was sung — smoke and rubble. Asaph is standing in it.

**"Why, God, have You rejected us forever? Why does Your anger smoke against the sheep of Your pasture?"**

*Your sheep.* Psalm 23 — *the Source is my shepherd.* Now the shepherd's anger is *smoking* against the flock. He's not accusing God of being someone else. He's accusing the shepherd of turning on his own sheep — and asking *why*.

**"Remember the congregation You purchased long ago — the tribe You redeemed as Your inheritance — Mount Zion, where You lived."**

*Remember.* He's asking God to *remember*. As if God forgot. That's the language of someone who feels forgotten — you say it because you need to hear yourself say it.

*Where You lived.* Past tense.

Now — the description. And it's a war crime report:

**"Turn Your steps toward the everlasting ruins — all this destruction the enemy has brought on the sanctuary."**

*Everlasting ruins.* It doesn't feel temporary. It feels *permanent*.

**"Your enemies roared in the middle of Your meeting place. They set up their standards as signs. They acted like men wielding axes to cut through a thicket of trees. They smashed all the carved paneling with their axes and hatchets."**

*Axes.* On the carved cedar. The paneling Solomon's craftsmen spent years on — reduced to firewood by men who didn't know what they were swinging at.

**"They burned Your sanctuary to the ground. They defiled the dwelling place of Your name."**

*To the ground.*

**"They said in their hearts: 'We'll crush them completely!' They burned every place where God was worshiped in the land."**

*Every* place. Not just the temple. Every shrine, every high place, every corner where someone prayed. Scorched earth on the *idea* of God.

And now — the line that lands hardest:

**"We're given no signs from God. No prophets are left. And none of us knows how long this will be."**

*No prophets are left.* No one to say *this is what it means.* No one to say *it ends on this date.* Just silence. And the question — *how long* — with nobody to answer it.

If you've been in a season where God went quiet and *everyone* who used to explain Him went quiet too — this is your verse.

**"How long will the enemy mock You, God? Will the foe revile Your name forever?"**

Notice — *mock YOU.* Not us. Asaph's making it God's problem. *They're not just insulting us. They're insulting You. Are You going to let that stand?*

**"Why do You hold back Your hand — Your right hand? Take it from the folds of Your garment and destroy them!"**

*Your hand's in Your pocket.* That's the image. God standing there with His hands *tucked away* while the temple burns. *Take it out.*

Now — Asaph does what Psalm 44 did. He goes to the record:

**"But You, God — You are my King from of old. You bring rescue on the earth."**

*From of old.* Before the temple. Before Israel.

**"It was You who split open the sea by Your power. You broke the heads of the monster in the waters. It was You who crushed the heads of Leviathan — and gave him as food to the creatures of the desert. It was You who opened up springs and streams. You dried up the ever-flowing rivers."**

*You. You. You. You. You.* Five times. The Exodus, the creation, the taming of chaos — *You did that.* He's reminding God of God's own résumé. Because if the One who broke Leviathan's heads is standing here with His hand in His pocket — the pocket isn't the problem. The *timing* is.

**"The day is Yours — and the night is Yours too. You established the sun and moon. It was You who set all the boundaries of the earth. You made both summer and winter."**

*The night is Yours too.* That's the line. The dark — the *ruins*, the silence, the no-prophets — *Yours*. Not the enemy's. Not the void's. If the night belongs to God, then the night is *going somewhere*.

**"Remember how the enemy has mocked You, Source — how foolish people have reviled Your name."**

*Remember.* Second time.

**"Don't hand over the life of Your dove to wild beasts. Don't forget the lives of Your afflicted people forever."**

*Your dove.* The gentlest bird. That's how he sees Israel now — not an army, not a nation. A *dove* in the jaws of something.

**"Have regard for Your covenant — because haunts of violence fill the dark places of the land."**

*Your covenant.* Psalm 44's last move. Not "we deserve." *You promised.*

**"Don't let the oppressed retreat in disgrace. May the poor and needy praise Your name."**

*May the poor praise.* He wants them to have a reason to.

And the ending — no resolution:

**"Rise up, God — defend Your cause. Remember how fools mock You all day long. Don't ignore the clamor of Your adversaries — the uproar of Your enemies, which rises continually."**

*Rise up.* The temple's still ash when he stops writing.

Twenty-three verses. Here's the shape: *The building's gone. They took axes to it. There are no prophets. How long? — But You split the sea. You broke Leviathan. The night is Yours too. Remember Your dove. Remember Your covenant. Rise up.*

When the sacred thing burns: name what they did. Say there are no prophets. Then go to the record — the sea, the monster, the *night is Yours too* — and say *remember*. Twice if you need to.
