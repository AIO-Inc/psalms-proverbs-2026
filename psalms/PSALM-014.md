# PSALM 14 — The Fool Says in His Heart

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 14:1–7. Nearly identical to Psalm 53.*

---

"The fool says in his heart, 'There is no God.'"

Stop. Before anyone weaponizes that line — read the next word. *Heart.* Not mouth. Not "the fool argues in a debate." The fool *says in his heart*. This isn't about atheists. It's about the practical assumption underneath how a person lives. Plenty of church people say "there is no God" with their heart every day while their mouth says the opposite.

The Hebrew word for fool here — *nabal* — isn't "stupid." It's *morally* hollow. Someone who's decided consequences don't apply. Someone who lives as if nobody's watching.

"They're corrupt. Their actions are disgusting. There's not one who does good."

Now watch — the camera pulls back:

"The Source looks down from heaven on the children of Adam, to see if there's anyone who understands, anyone who seeks God."

He's *looking*. Searching. Scanning the whole human race for one person who gets it.

**"They've all turned aside. Together they've gone bad. There is no one who does good — not even one."**

*Not even one.* This is the line Paul quotes in Romans 3 to make the point that nobody gets to stand on their own record. Not the fool, not the religious. Everybody's turned aside. Everybody's on the wrong road.

That's not condemnation. That's *diagnosis*. You can't fix what you won't name. And the good news needs the bad news first, or it isn't news.

Then — here's what the fool actually *does*:

"Don't they know anything — all these workers of harm, who eat up My people like they're eating bread, and never call on the Source?"

*Eat up My people like bread.* Casually. Like it's lunch. That's what a life built on "there is no God" produces — you consume people, because if nobody's watching, why not?

Then the turn:

**"There — they were terrified. Because God is with the generation of the one who does right."**

The fool built everything on "nobody's there." And then he finds out Somebody's there — standing *with* the people he was eating. That's the terror: not punishment. The *realization*.

"You'd shame the plans of the poor — but the Source is his refuge."

The poor guy you were consuming? He had a refuge the whole time. You just couldn't see it.

Then the cry at the end — and this is the whole book of Psalms in one line:

**"Oh, that rescue for Israel would come from Zion! When the Source brings His people back — Jacob will celebrate, Israel will be glad."**

Bring us back. That's the prayer. Not "punish the fools." *Bring us home.* All of us — the ones who turned aside, which is everyone.

Check your heart, not your mouth. What does it actually say? Then remember: He's looking for the one who seeks. Be the one He finds.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* nabal *= morally senseless, hollow — not intellectually deficient. "In his heart" =* b'libo *= the inner operating assumption. v. 3 — quoted in Romans 3:10–12. v. 5 —* pachad *= dread/terror — legitimate human fear at a sudden realization, correctly rendered (NOT* yirah*). v. 7 — "captivity" (KJV) is not in the Hebrew; the phrase is* b'shuv *= "when He returns/restores." The restoration is unconditional.*
