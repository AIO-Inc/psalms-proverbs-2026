# PSALM 48 — Walk Around the City

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 48:1–14. A song about Jerusalem — but really about what it means to have a place where God lives. Sons of Korah.*

---

**"Great is the Source — and greatly to be praised — in the city of our God, His holy mountain."**

The greatness and the *place*. He's not just great in the abstract. He's great *here*, in this city, on this hill. There's an address.

**"Beautiful in height — the joy of the whole earth — Mount Zion, in the far north, the city of the great King."**

*The joy of the whole earth.* One small hill in one small country — and it's the joy of the *planet*. Because of Who lives there. Any place God dwells becomes the center of the map.

**"God is in her citadels. He's made Himself known as a fortress."**

*Made Himself known.* Not "is technically present." *Known.* Recognized. People who lived there could tell you: *the fortress isn't the walls. The fortress is Him.*

Now — the story. Something happened:

"Look — the kings assembled. They advanced together. **They saw — and they were stunned. They panicked. They fled.** Trembling seized them there — pain like a woman in labor."

*They saw — and they were stunned.* Kings. Plural. Armies coming to take the city. And then they *saw* something — the text doesn't say what — and it broke them. Not a battle. Not a siege. They *looked* — and ran.

That's what happens when you come against a place where God actually lives. You don't get defeated. You get *undone*. You see something you didn't expect, and your knees go.

"Like the east wind that shatters the ships of Tarshish."

The biggest merchant ships in the ancient world — splintered by wind. Not by another ship. By *weather*. By something nobody can fight.

**"As we have heard — so we have seen — in the city of the Source of Armies, in the city of our God. God will establish her forever."**

*As we heard — so we saw.* This is the line. They grew up on the stories — the Exodus, the wilderness, David. *Heard.* And then something happened in their own lifetime and they *saw* it. The stories became eyewitness.

That's what every generation needs. Not just the record — the *repeat*. Something you saw yourself.

**"We've thought about Your loyal-love, God — inside Your temple."**

*Thought about.* The Hebrew is *dimminu* — to compare, to consider, to imagine. They sat inside the building and *worked it through*. What He'd done. Why. Who He is. Worship isn't just noise — sometimes it's sitting inside and thinking.

**"As Your name is, God — so is Your praise, to the ends of the earth. Your right hand is full of right-standing."**

*As Your name, so Your praise.* The praise *matches* the name. He's exactly as good as people say — and better. No gap between the reputation and the reality.

"Let Mount Zion be glad. Let the daughters of Judah rejoice — because of Your judgments."

And now — the instruction. This is the part to *do*:

**"Walk around Zion. Go all the way around her. Count her towers. Consider her ramparts. Go through her citadels — so that you can tell the next generation."**

*Walk around.* Physically. Take the tour. *Count* the towers. *Look* at the walls. Not so you'll be impressed by architecture — so you'll have *specifics* when you tell your kids. Not "God is faithful." *"There were twelve towers, and the north wall was thirty feet, and I put my hand on it."*

Vague faith doesn't transfer. *Details* do. Go count the towers.

And the last verse — the whole reason for the tour:

**"Because this God is our God forever and ever. He will guide us — until death."**

*This God.* Not "a god." *This one* — the one who lives in this city, who stunned the kings, whose name matches His praise. *Ours.* And *He will guide us* — the same word as a shepherd leading. All the way. *Until death* — and the Hebrew has a hint of *beyond* death. He walks you to the edge, and then keeps walking.

So — walk around the city. Count what He's built in your life. Put your hand on the walls. Get the details. Then tell the next generation: *as we heard — so we saw.*
