# PSALM 102 — But You Remain the Same

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 102:1–28. "A prayer of an afflicted person who has grown weak and pours out a lament before the Source." No author. The fifth penitential psalm — and the one that ends with the universe wearing out while God doesn't.*

---

The heading tells you everything: *an afflicted person who has grown weak.* Not a king. Not a prophet. Just someone worn down — pouring it out.

**"Hear my prayer, Source. Let my cry for help come to You. Don't hide Your face from me when I'm in distress. Turn Your ear to me. When I call — answer me quickly."**

*Quickly.* Psalm 70's *hurry*. He doesn't have time for slow.

Now — the body. And it's Psalm 38's honesty:

**"My days vanish like smoke. My bones burn like glowing embers. My heart is blighted and withered like grass. I forget to eat my food."**

*I forget to eat.* Not "I can't eat." *Forget.* The body's needs stop registering. If you've been in depression — you know. The meals just don't happen.

**"In my distress I groan aloud — and am reduced to skin and bones."**

**"I am like a desert owl — like an owl among the ruins. I lie awake. I have become like a bird alone on a roof."**

Three birds. *Desert owl* — in the empty places. *Owl in the ruins* — Psalm 74's rubble. *A bird alone on a roof* — the sparrow that *didn't* find the nest by the altar. Psalm 84 envied the bird that got in. Psalm 102 is the bird that didn't.

*I lie awake.* Psalm 77's insomnia. Psalm 63's night watches. Alone. On the roof. Eyes open.

**"All day long my enemies taunt me. Those who rail against me use my name as a curse."**

*Use my name as a curse.* His *name* has become the *insult*. "You're a real ___." That's how far down it's gone.

**"I eat ashes as my food — and mingle my drink with tears."**

*Ashes for food.* Psalm 42's tears as bread — now it's ashes. What you eat when there's nothing left.

**"— because of Your great wrath. You have taken me up and thrown me aside."**

*Thrown me aside.* Picked up — and tossed. Like something you don't need.

**"My days are like the evening shadow. I wither away like grass."**

*Evening shadow.* Long and thin and about to disappear into the dark. *Grass.* Psalm 90, 92, 103 — the grass again. He's *dying*, and he knows it.

Eleven verses of that. And then — verse 12. The word that changes every lament:

**"But You, Source — sit enthroned forever. Your renown endures through all generations."**

*But You.* Not *but I* — that was David's. This one's *but YOU*. The afflicted man doesn't have a *but I*. He's got nothing. So he turns the whole sentence around and puts *God* in the subject position. *I'm smoke. YOU'RE forever.*

**"You will arise and have compassion on Zion — because it is time to show favor to her. The appointed time has come."**

*The appointed time.* Psalm 75 — *when I choose the time*. The afflicted man says: *it's HERE.* Not because he feels it. Because he's decided to *say* it.

**"Because her stones are dear to Your servants. Her very dust moves them to pity."**

*Her dust.* The rubble of Jerusalem. Psalm 74, 79. And the exiles *love the dust*. They'd kiss the ground if they could get to it. That's what it means to belong to a place — you love it broken.

**"The nations will stand in awe of the name of the Source. All the kings of the earth will revere Your glory. Because the Source will rebuild Zion — and appear in His glory."**

*Rebuild.* Not "restore the mood." *Rebuild the stones.* Psalm 51 — *build the walls of Jerusalem*. It's a *construction* promise.

*Stand in awe.* *Yirah.* The nations — in wonder.

**"He will respond to the prayer of the destitute. He will not despise their plea."**

*Not despise.* Psalm 22, 51, 69 — the broken thing He won't turn away from.

**"Let this be written for a future generation — that a people not yet created may praise the Source."**

*A people not yet created.* He's writing for *you*. Sitting on the roof, forgetting to eat, eating ashes — and he says *write this down for the people who don't exist yet.* Psalm 22's *a people not yet born*. Psalm 78's chain. The afflicted man knows his suffering is *material* for someone he'll never meet.

**"'The Source looked down from His sanctuary on high — from heaven He viewed the earth — to hear the groans of the prisoners — and release those condemned to death.'"**

*The groans of the prisoners.* Psalm 79. He *heard* them. And *released*.

**"So the name of the Source will be declared in Zion — and His praise in Jerusalem — when the peoples and the kingdoms assemble to worship the Source."**

Psalm 87 — the nations *inside*.

Now — back to the man. And it's brutal for one more verse:

**"In the course of my life He broke my strength. He cut short my days."**

*Cut short.* He's not going to get his seventy.

**"So I said: 'Don't take me away, my God, in the midst of my days —'"**

And then — the turn *out of himself* entirely. The last six verses. Hebrews 1 quotes them about Christ:

**"'— Your years go on through all generations. In the beginning You laid the foundations of the earth — and the heavens are the work of Your hands.'"**

*In the beginning.* Genesis 1. The man who's dying goes back to the *first* thing.

**"'They will perish — but You remain. They will all wear out like a garment. Like clothing You will change them — and they will be discarded.'"**

*They will wear out.* The *heavens*. The *earth*. The oldest, most permanent things anyone can see — *clothes*. Getting threadbare. Going in the bin. God *changes them* like a shirt.

Think about what he's doing. He started with *my days vanish like smoke*. He ends with *the universe wears out like a garment*. He's put his own dying in the *same category* as the stars burning out. Everything's temporary. *Everything.* The heavens *and* him.

**"'But You remain the same — and Your years will never end.'"**

*But You.* Second time. *The same.* The Hebrew is *atah-hu* — *You are He.* Unchanging. The one fixed point in a universe that's wearing out.

Hebrews 1:10–12 quotes this — about *Jesus*. *You, Lord, laid the foundations of the earth... they will perish, but You remain... You are the same.* The dying man on the roof was describing the Son.

**"'The children of Your servants will live in Your presence. Their descendants will be established before You.'"**

*Their descendants — established.* The man won't get his seventy. But his *kids* — Psalm 90's *establish the work of our hands* — they'll be *in the presence*. The line goes on. His doesn't. Theirs does. And that's enough for him to stop asking.

Twenty-eight verses. Here's the shape: *I'm smoke. I forget to eat. I'm a bird alone on a roof. Ashes for food. You threw me aside. — BUT YOU sit enthroned forever. It's time to rebuild Zion. Write this down for people not born yet. — He cut my days short. — But You laid the foundations. The heavens will wear out like a shirt. You'll fold them up. But YOU remain the same. And the kids will be established.*

You're smoke? So are the stars. And there's One who isn't. Put *Him* in the subject position and see what happens to the sentence.
