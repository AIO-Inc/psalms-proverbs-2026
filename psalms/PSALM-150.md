# PSALM 150 — Let Everything That Has Breath

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 150:1–6. Six verses. The last psalm. The fifth and final Hallelujah — and the doxology that closes Book Five and the whole Psalter, the way 41, 72, 89, and 106 closed the first four. No petition. No lament. No enemy. Thirteen commands to praise, one reason, every instrument, and the last word is breath.*

---

One hundred forty-nine psalms. Curses, caves, pits, plagues, the crown in the dirt, the rivers of Babylon, the weaned child, the open hand. Every register a human voice has. And the book ends with — *six verses*. And *not one request*.

Psalm 1 opened — *happy is the one*. Psalm 150 closes — *Hallelujah*. The book started with a *person* and ends with *everything*.

**"Hallelujah! Praise God in His sanctuary. Praise Him in His mighty heavens."**

*In His sanctuary.* *B'kodsho.* The holy place. Psalm 134 — *stand by night in the house*. Psalm 150 — *praise in it*. Down here.

*In His mighty heavens.* *Bi-r'kia uzzo.* The *firmament of His strength*. Genesis 1:6 — the *raqia*. The dome. Up there. Psalm 148 — *from the heavens, from the earth*. Psalm 150 — *both, in one verse*. Where do you praise? *Everywhere there is.*

**"Praise Him for His acts of power. Praise Him for His surpassing greatness."**

*For His acts of power.* *Bi-g'vurotav.* The *mighty deeds*. Egypt. The sea. Sihon and Og. The exile. The return. Psalm 106, 145 — the *g'vurot*. Psalm 150 — *that's WHY*. One reason in the whole psalm — and it's *what He did*. Psalm 136 — twenty-six verses of *what He did*. Psalm 150 — *all of it, in one word*.

*His surpassing greatness.* *K'rov gudlo.* *According to the abundance of His greatness.* Praise *proportional* to the size. Which means — *there's no ceiling*. Psalm 145 — *His greatness no one can fathom*. Psalm 150 — *then don't stop*.

Now — the *how*. And it's every instrument in the Temple orchestra:

**"Praise Him with the sounding of the trumpet."**

*Shofar.* The *ram's horn*. Not a brass trumpet — a *horn*. Psalm 47, 81, 98 — *the shofar*. The sound that brought down Jericho. The sound at Sinai. The sound on Yom Kippur. Raw. Unmusical. A *blast*. Psalm 150 — *start there*.

**"Praise Him with the harp and lyre."**

*Nevel v'chinnor.* The *strings*. David's instrument — 1 Samuel 16 — the *kinnor* that calmed Saul. Psalm 137 — *we hung our harps in the trees*. Psalm 150 — *they're DOWN*. In hand. Playing.

**"Praise Him with tambourine and dancing."**

*B'tof u-machol.* Psalm 149 — *dancing with tambourine*. Psalm 150 — *again*. Miriam's instrument — Exodus 15. The *women's* instrument. The *body* instrument — you hit it and you *move*. Psalm 30 — *You turned my wailing into dancing*. Psalm 150 — *the dancing made the final cut*.

**"Praise Him with the strings and pipe."**

*B'minnim v'ugav.* *Minnim* — strings, generally. *Ugav* — the *flute*, the *pipe*. Genesis 4 — Jubal, the first musician — *the harp and the pipe*. Psalm 150 — *the oldest instruments there are*. Since the fourth chapter of Genesis, somebody's been blowing into a reed. Psalm 150 — *aim it at Him*.

**"Praise Him with the clash of cymbals. Praise Him with resounding cymbals."**

*Tzil'tz'lei-shama. Tzil'tz'lei t'ruah.* Two kinds of cymbals. The *hearing* cymbals — the clear ring. The *shout* cymbals — the *crash*. *T'ruah* — the *battle shout*, the *alarm*. Psalm 47 — *shout to God with cries of joy*. Psalm 150 — *the cymbals SHOUT*. Loud. Not tasteful. *Loud.*

Count them. *Shofar. Harp. Lyre. Tambourine. Dance. Strings. Pipe. Cymbals. Cymbals.* Wind, string, percussion, body. Everything that makes a noise. Psalm 150 — *bring it all*. There's no instrument that's too crude, too loud, too old, too *worldly*. If it makes sound — it's *drafted*.

And the last verse. The last verse of the *Psalter*:

**"Let everything that has breath praise the Source. Hallelujah!"**

*Everything that has breath.* *Kol ha-n'shamah.* *All the breath.* Not *all the people*. Not *all the faithful*. *All the BREATH.* Genesis 2:7 — *He breathed into his nostrils the breath of life* — *nishmat chayyim*. Same word. The breath He *gave* — Psalm 150 — *give it BACK*. As praise.

That's the whole book in one line. Psalm 6 — *who praises You from the grave?* Psalm 30 — *can dust praise You?* Psalm 88 — *the dead don't*. Psalm 115 — *the dead don't praise; WE do*. Psalm 146 — *as long as I live*. Psalm 150 — *if you've got BREATH — that's the qualification*. Not righteousness. Not understanding. Not being *in the sanctuary*. *Breath.*

Which means — *right now* — if you're reading this — you qualify. You're *in*. The last verse of the Psalms doesn't check your résumé. It checks your *pulse*.

And — *n'shamah* is *breath*, not *ruach* — *spirit*. Psalm 150 could've said *every spirit*. It said *every BREATH*. The physical thing. The in-and-out. The thing you're doing right now without thinking. Psalm 150 says — *THAT. That's the instrument.* Before the shofar, before the harp, before the cymbals — the *breath* is the first instrument, and the last, and the one everyone owns.

*Hallelujah.*

Thirteen times in six verses — *praise*. *Hallelu.* And the last word — *Hallelu-Yah*. The book's Hebrew name is *Tehillim* — *Praises*. A hundred fifty psalms — most of them *not* praise. Laments. Curses. Questions. *How long? Why have You forsaken me? By the rivers of Babylon.* And the book's called *Praises*. Because — this is where it *ends up*. Not where it starts. Where it *arrives*.

Six verses. Here's the shape — but the shape is the *whole book*: *Praise Him — down here, up there. For what He DID. With everything that makes noise — the horn, the strings, the drum, the dance, the pipe, the crash. And everything — EVERYTHING — with breath. Hallelujah.*

Here's what to do. One thing. Take a breath. A real one — all the way in. That's the instrument. Now — let it out with His name on it. *Hallelujah.* Out loud. You've just done Psalm 150. You've just done what the whole book was *for*.

Then go read Psalm 1. It says *happy is the one*. You know who now.

---

*Note on translation: Closing doxology of Book Five and of the Psalter (cf. 41:13; 72:18–19; 89:52; 106:48). v. 1 — *hal'lu-Yah* opens and closes; *b'kodsho* = "in His holy place/sanctuary"; *bi-r'kia uzzo* = "in the firmament of His strength" (*raqia* — Gen 1:6–8). v. 2 — *bi-g'vurotav* = "for His mighty acts" (cf. 106:2; 145:4, 12); *k'rov gudlo* = "according to the abundance of His greatness" (cf. 145:3). v. 3 — *b'teka shofar* = "with the blast of the ram's horn" — *shofar*, not brass *chatzotz'rah* (cf. 47:5; 81:3; 98:6; Josh 6; Exod 19:16); *nevel v'chinnor* = harp and lyre (1 Sam 16:23; 137:2). v. 4 — *b'tof u-machol* = with tambourine and dance (Exod 15:20; 30:11; 149:3); *b'minnim v'ugav* = "with strings and pipe" — *ugav* (Gen 4:21; Job 21:12; 30:31). v. 5 — *tzil'tz'lei-shama* = "cymbals of hearing/sound"; *tzil'tz'lei t'ruah* = "cymbals of shout/alarm" — *t'ruah* is the battle/festival shout (cf. 47:1; Num 10:5–6; 29:1). **v. 6 — *kol ha-n'shamah t'hallel Yah* = "all the breath — let it praise Yah" — *n'shamah* = breath (Gen 2:7; 7:22; Isa 42:5), distinct from *ruach* (spirit/wind); the qualification for praise is breathing, not status.** Thirteen occurrences of *hallel* in six verses. Book title *Tehillim* (Praises) derives from *tehillah* (145 title) and this closing. No *yirah* in this psalm.*
