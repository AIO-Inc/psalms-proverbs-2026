# PSALM 107 — Let the Redeemed Say So

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 107:1–43. Book Five opens. Four rescues, one refrain — "then they cried out, and He delivered them." The psalm for anyone who's been in a ditch and got out.*

---

Book Four closed with eight failures and one *yet*. Book Five opens with four ditches and one *then they cried*.

**"Give thanks to the Source — because He is good. His loyal-love endures forever."**

Psalm 106's refrain — carried across the book break. Same words. New book. *Good. Forever.*

**"Let the redeemed of the Source say so — those He redeemed from the hand of the foe — those He gathered from the lands — from east and west, from north and south."**

*Say so.* The Hebrew is *yom'ru* — *let them SAY*. Not think it. Not feel it. *Say it out loud.* If you've been redeemed — you've got a *job*: tell it. Psalm 66 — *come and hear what He did for me*. Psalm 107 makes it a command.

*From east and west, north and south.* The exiles — Psalm 106's *gather us* — *gathered*. From every direction.

Now — the four rescues. Each one has the same shape: *trouble → they cried → He delivered → thank Him.* Watch the pattern. It's *your* pattern.

**One — the lost.**

**"Some wandered in desert wastelands — finding no way to a city where they could settle. They were hungry and thirsty — and their lives ebbed away."**

*No way to a city.* Lost. Not in a dramatic way — just *nowhere*. Walking and walking and no destination appearing. Psalm 63's desert. If you've been *directionless* — this is you.

**"Then they cried out to the Source in their trouble — and He delivered them from their distress. He led them by a straight way — to a city where they could settle."**

*Then they cried.* The first *then*. *A straight way.* Not a shortcut. *Straight.* And — *a city*. A place. An address.

**"Let them give thanks to the Source for His loyal-love — and His wonderful deeds for mankind — because He satisfies the thirsty — and fills the hungry with good things."**

*Satisfies. Fills.* Psalm 63, 103, 104 — the *sated* words. The lost get *fed*.

**Two — the imprisoned.**

**"Some sat in darkness — in utter darkness — prisoners suffering in iron chains — because they rebelled against God's commands — and despised the plans of the Most High. So He subjected them to bitter labor. They stumbled — and there was no one to help."**

*Because they rebelled.* This group's in the ditch *because of what they did*. The psalm says so. No pretending. *Iron chains.* *Darkness.* *No one to help.* Psalm 88's pit. Addiction. The consequence you earned.

**"Then they cried out to the Source in their trouble — and He saved them from their distress. He brought them out of darkness — utter darkness — and broke away their chains."**

*Then they cried.* Second *then*. Same words — even for the ones who *deserved* the chains. God doesn't check the cause before He breaks the iron.

**"Let them give thanks to the Source for His loyal-love — and His wonderful deeds for mankind — because He breaks down gates of bronze — and cuts through bars of iron."**

*Gates of bronze. Bars of iron.* Isaiah 45. The prison's not locked to Him.

**Three — the sick.**

**"Some became fools through their rebellious ways — and suffered affliction because of their wrongdoing. They loathed all food — and drew near the gates of death."**

*Loathed all food.* Psalm 102 — *I forget to eat*. Sick from what they did to themselves. *Gates of death* — the door's right there.

**"Then they cried out to the Source in their trouble — and He saved them from their distress. He sent out His word and healed them. He rescued them from the grave."**

*Then they cried.* Third *then*. *He sent out His word.* Psalm 33 — *He spoke and it was*. One word — *healed*. Matthew 8 — *just say the word and my servant will be healed.* The centurion knew this psalm.

**"Let them give thanks to the Source for His loyal-love — and His wonderful deeds for mankind. Let them sacrifice thank offerings — and tell of His works with songs of joy."**

*Tell. Songs.* The healed *sing about it*.

**Four — the storm.**

**"Some went out on the sea in ships. They were merchants on the mighty waters. They saw the works of the Source — His wonderful deeds in the deep."**

Sailors. Not lost, not chained, not sick — *working*. Doing their job. And the sea turned.

**"He spoke and stirred up a tempest — that lifted high the waves. They mounted up to the heavens — and went down to the depths. In their peril their courage melted away. They reeled and staggered like drunkards. They were at their wits' end."**

*At their wits' end.* The Hebrew is *kol-chochmatam titbala* — *all their wisdom was swallowed*. Every skill they had — useless. The experts — helpless. If you've been *competent* and hit something competence can't fix — this is you.

**"Then they cried out to the Source in their trouble — and He brought them out of their distress. He stilled the storm to a whisper. The waves of the sea were hushed."**

*Then they cried.* Fourth *then*. *Stilled the storm to a whisper.* Mark 4 — *Peace, be still. And the wind died down and it was completely calm.* The disciples were sailors. They'd sung this psalm. And then they *watched it happen*.

**"They were glad when it grew calm — and He guided them to their desired haven."**

*Their desired haven.* The harbor they were *aiming for*. Not a random shore. The place they *wanted*.

**"Let them give thanks to the Source for His loyal-love — and His wonderful deeds for mankind. Let them exalt Him in the assembly of the people — and praise Him in the council of the elders."**

*In the assembly. In the council.* Publicly. The sailors tell it in front of everyone.

Four ditches. Four *thens*. Four *let them thank*.

Now — the epilogue. God as the One who *reverses*:

**"He turned rivers into a desert — flowing springs into thirsty ground — and fruitful land into a salt waste — because of the wickedness of those who lived there. He turned the desert into pools of water — and the parched ground into flowing springs. There He brought the hungry to live — and they founded a city where they could settle."**

*Rivers to desert. Desert to pools.* Both directions. He can dry up the proud. He can *water* the empty. Psalm 65's overflow — for the *hungry*.

**"They sowed fields and planted vineyards — that yielded a fruitful harvest. He blessed them — and their numbers greatly increased — and He did not let their herds diminish."**

**"Then their numbers decreased — and they were humbled by oppression, calamity, and sorrow. He who pours contempt on nobles — made them wander in a trackless waste. But He lifted the needy out of their affliction — and increased their families like flocks."**

*Pours contempt on nobles. Lifts the needy.* Psalm 75 — *He brings one down, He lifts another.* Psalm 113. Mary's song.

**"The upright see and rejoice — but all the wicked shut their mouths."**

*Shut their mouths.* Nothing to say. The pattern's too obvious.

And the last verse — the instruction:

**"Let the one who is wise heed these things — and ponder the loyal-love of the Source."**

*Ponder.* The Hebrew is *yitbon'nu* — *understand deeply*. Look at the four rescues. Look at the pattern. *Trouble — cry — delivered.* Every time. That's not luck. That's *chesed*. And the wise person *studies* it.

Forty-three verses. Here's the shape: *Give thanks — He's good. Redeemed? SAY SO. The lost cried — He led them to a city. The chained cried — He broke the iron. The sick cried — He sent a word. The sailors cried — He whispered the storm down. Four ditches — four cries — four rescues. He dries rivers and waters deserts. Wise? Study the pattern.*

You've been in one of the four. Maybe all of them. And you cried. And you're reading this — so you got out.

*Let the redeemed say so.* Say it.
