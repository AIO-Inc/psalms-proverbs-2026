# PSALM 70 — Hurry

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 70:1–5. Five verses. Nearly identical to Psalm 40:13–17 — pulled out and made to stand alone, because sometimes you only have time for the short version.*

---

Psalm 40 was seventeen verses — the mud, the rock, the new song, the trouble coming back. Psalm 70 is its last five verses, cut loose and set on their own. No testimony first. No build-up. Just the emergency.

That's the point. Sometimes you don't have seventeen verses in you. You have five. Here they are.

**"God — to rescue me! Source — to help me — hurry!"**

The Hebrew has no verb in the first line. Literally: *God — to rescue me.* Not even "come." Just the direction and the need. It's what comes out when there's no time for grammar.

*Hurry.* The word is *chushah* — quick, fast, *now*. The whole psalm is built on it.

**"Let the ones seeking my life be ashamed and disgraced. Let the ones wanting my harm be turned back and humiliated. Let the ones saying 'Ha! Ha!' turn back because of their shame."**

*Ha! Ha!* The mockers. The ones who saw you go down and laughed. David's got the exact sound of it — *he'ach* — a snort. He wants the snort turned around.

**"Let all who seek You rejoice and be glad in You. Let those who love Your rescue say continually: 'God is great!'"**

Two groups. The *ha-ha* crowd and the *God is great* crowd. Same event — someone in trouble. One laughs. One turns to God. David's asking for the second group to win — and to keep saying it. *Continually.*

And the last verse — the one worth memorizing:

**"But I am poor and needy. God — hurry to me. You are my help and my deliverer. Source — don't delay."**

*Poor and needy.* No pretense. Not "I've been faithful." Not "I deserve." Just — *I've got nothing and I need You.* That's the whole argument. It's the same argument as Psalm 40, Psalm 51, Psalm 69 — and it's the only one that works every time, because it's the only one that's always true.

*Hurry to me.* Second time. Bookends. The psalm opens with *hurry* and closes with *hurry*.

*Don't delay.* The last two words. Not a request — a *plea*. The kind of thing you say when the delay itself would be the disaster.

Five verses. Here's the whole thing: *God — help — NOW. Turn the mockers around. Let the seekers say You're great. I've got nothing. Hurry. Don't delay.*

Why is this in the book twice? Because Psalm 40 is for when you have time to tell the whole story. Psalm 70 is for when you don't. When the water's at your neck (Psalm 69) and you can't get seventeen verses out — you get five. And they're enough.

Memorize it. All five. So that when you can't think — you can still pray.

*God — hurry. Don't delay.*
