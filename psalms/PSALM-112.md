# PSALM 112 — He Will Not Be Afraid of Bad News

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 112:1–10. The twin of Psalm 111 — same acrostic form, same length. But 111 was about God. 112 is about the person who's in awe of Him. What that person is like.*

---

Psalm 111 ended: *the awe of the Source is the beginning of wisdom.* Psalm 112 opens by describing the person who *has* it.

**"Praise the Source! Happy is the one who stands in awe of the Source — who finds great delight in His commands."**

*Hallelujah.* Then — *happy*. *Ashrei.* Psalm 1's word. And *who stands in awe* — *yirah*. The 111:10 person. What does their life look like?

*Great delight in His commands.* Psalm 1 — *delights in the instruction*. Psalm 19 — *sweeter than honey*. The awe-person doesn't *tolerate* the rules. They *enjoy* them. Because the rules came from the One they're amazed by.

**"Their children will be mighty in the land. The generation of the upright will be blessed."**

*Their children.* Psalm 78's chain. Psalm 103's *children's children*. The awe *transfers*.

**"Wealth and riches are in their houses — and their right-standing endures forever."**

*Wealth.* Yes, it says that. Not as a formula — Psalm 73 already destroyed the "righteous get rich" formula. As a *description*: the awe-person tends to have *enough*. Because they don't waste it on Psalm 62's vapor.

**"Even in darkness light dawns for the upright — for the one who is gracious and compassionate and right."**

*Even in darkness.* Psalm 97 — *light is sown for the righteous*. Here it *dawns*. Not "the upright never see darkness." *Even IN it* — light comes.

*Gracious and compassionate.* Stop. Those are *God's* words. Exodus 34. Psalm 103:8, 111:4 — *the Source is gracious and compassionate*. And now — the *person in awe* is described with the *same two words*. That's the point of the twin psalms. Psalm 111: *God is gracious and compassionate.* Psalm 112: *the one in awe of Him becomes gracious and compassionate.* You turn into what you're amazed by.

**"Good will come to those who are generous and lend freely — who conduct their affairs with justice."**

*Generous. Lend freely.* Psalm 37 — *the righteous is generous and gives*. The awe-person's *hands are open* — because they've watched God's hand open (Psalm 104).

And then — the verse. The one that matters most for actual life:

**"Surely the righteous will never be shaken. They will be remembered forever. They will have no fear of bad news. Their heart is steadfast — trusting in the Source."**

*Never be shaken.* The word — Psalm 15, 16, 21, 30, 46, 55, 62. Eight times now. *Not moved.*

*No fear of bad news.* Read that slow. Not "no bad news comes." *No FEAR of it.* The phone rings at 2 a.m. The test results come back. The email arrives. And the awe-person — *isn't afraid*. Not because they're numb. Because their heart is *steadfast* — Psalm 57, 108's *nachon*. Fixed. Already anchored to something the bad news can't move.

This is the practical payoff of *awe* over *fear*. The person who's *afraid of God* is afraid of *everything* — because they've been trained to expect the worst from the top. The person who's *in awe of God* has looked at the thing bigger than any bad news — and the bad news shrinks.

**"Their heart is secure. They will have no fear. In the end they will look in triumph on their foes."**

*Secure.* The Hebrew is *samuch* — supported, leaned-on. Like a wall you can put your back against.

**"They have freely scattered their gifts to the poor. Their right-standing endures forever. Their horn will be lifted high in honor."**

*Scattered.* Psalm 97's *sown*. They *throw* it — to the poor. Not carefully budgeted charity. *Scattered.* 2 Corinthians 9:9 — Paul quotes *this verse* about generous giving.

*Their horn lifted.* Psalm 75, 89, 92 — the horn of the humble, raised.

And the last verse — the contrast:

**"The wicked will see and be vexed. They will gnash their teeth and waste away. The longings of the wicked will come to nothing."**

*Gnash their teeth.* The wicked *watching* the awe-person — steady, generous, unafraid of the phone — and *grinding their molars*. Because they've got everything the awe-person doesn't chase, and none of the *steadiness*.

*Come to nothing.* Psalm 1 — *the way of the wicked will perish*. The twin psalms end where Psalm 1 did.

Ten verses. Here's the shape — and it's the *portrait* of 111:10's wisdom: *Happy is the one in awe — they DELIGHT in the commands. Their kids are strong. They have enough. Light dawns for them even in the dark. They're gracious and compassionate — GOD'S words. They give freely. They're never shaken. NO FEAR OF BAD NEWS — heart steadfast. They scatter to the poor. The wicked watch and grind their teeth.*

Here's the test. What happens in your chest when the phone rings late? If it's dread — you've been trained in fear. If it's steadiness — you've been trained in awe.

Psalm 112 says you can *switch*. Start with the gasp. The steadiness follows.

---

*Note on translation: Acrostic — 22 half-lines, twin of Psalm 111. v. 1 —* ashrei-ish yare et-YHWH *= "happy [is] the man in awe of YHWH" —* yirah*. v. 4 —* chanun v'rachum v'tzadik *= "gracious and compassionate and righteous" — the divine attributes of Exod 34:6 / Ps 111:4 applied to the human. v. 6 —* l'olam lo-yimot *= "forever he will not be moved." v. 7 —* mi-sh'mu'ah ra'ah lo yira *= "of bad news he will not fear" — ordinary human dread (*yare*), correctly rendered as fear; the point is its ABSENCE in the one who has* yirah *of God. v. 8 —* samuch libo *= supported/secure [is] his heart. v. 9 —* pizar natan la-evyonim *= "he scattered, he gave to the needy" — 2 Cor 9:9.*
