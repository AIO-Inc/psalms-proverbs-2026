# PSALM 111 — The Beginning of Wisdom

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 111:1–10. An acrostic — twenty-two lines, one per Hebrew letter. About God. Its twin, Psalm 112, is about the person who lives like God is real. And it ends with the most mistranslated sentence in the Bible.*

---

Ten verses. Twenty-two lines. Alphabetical. This is a psalm built to be *memorized* — A to Z, what God is like.

**"Praise the Source! I will extol the Source with all my heart — in the council of the upright and in the assembly."**

*Hallelujah.* Then — *with all my heart*. Psalm 86 asked for an *undivided* heart. Here it's *all* of it, in public. *In the council. In the assembly.* Not private devotion. *Out loud, in the room.*

**"Great are the works of the Source. They are pondered by all who delight in them."**

*Pondered.* The Hebrew is *d'rushim* — sought out, studied, investigated. God's works aren't just admired. They're *researched* — by people who *delight* in doing it. Faith with a magnifying glass.

**"Glorious and majestic are His deeds. His right-standing endures forever."**

**"He has caused His wonders to be remembered. The Source is gracious and compassionate."**

*Caused to be remembered.* Psalm 105 — God's memory. Here — He makes *you* remember. Passover. Sabbath. The festivals. He *built* memory aids into the calendar so the wonders wouldn't slip.

*Gracious and compassionate.* Exodus 34 — the self-description. Psalm 103's words.

**"He provides food for those who stand in awe of Him. He remembers His covenant forever."**

*Provides food.* Psalm 104 — *You open Your hand.* For the ones in *awe* — *yirah*. Wonder gets fed.

*Remembers His covenant forever.* Psalm 105:8. Same words. The acrostic's building the same portrait.

**"He has shown His people the power of His works — giving them the lands of other nations."**

**"The works of His hands are faithful and just. All His precepts are trustworthy. They are established forever and ever — enacted in faithfulness and uprightness."**

*Trustworthy.* The Hebrew is *ne'emanim* — reliable, faithful. His *rules* are as reliable as His *works*. You can lean on both.

**"He provided redemption for His people. He ordained His covenant forever — holy and awesome is His name."**

*Awesome.* *Nora.* *Yirah* again — as a *name*. Psalm 99 — *great and awesome name*.

And now — the last verse. The one you've heard wrong your whole life:

**"The awe of the Source is the beginning of wisdom. All who follow His precepts have good understanding. To Him belongs eternal praise."**

*The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom.* That's how it's been printed for four hundred years. Proverbs 1:7, Proverbs 9:10, Job 28:28 — same sentence. And every kid who heard it in Sunday school learned that being *afraid of God* is where smart starts.

It's not. The Hebrew is *yirat YHWH* — *yirah*. The word this whole project has been correcting since Psalm 2. *Awe.* Wonder. The gasp at the Grand Canyon. The thing that makes you go quiet in front of something too big.

*The AWE of the Source is the beginning of wisdom.*

Think about what that changes. Wisdom doesn't start with *dread*. It starts with *wonder*. With looking up at the sky — Psalm 8 — and asking *what is man?* With watching the sea run when He speaks — Psalm 104 — and going *oh*. With standing in front of the ear He built — Psalm 94 — and realizing *He hears*. The person who's *amazed* by God is the one who starts getting *smart*. The person who's *scared* of God just gets *small*.

Every culture has a version of the scared-of-God person. Hiding. Bargaining. Performing. That's not wisdom. That's *anxiety with a religious label*. The wise one is the one who's *stopped* being afraid — because they've seen enough to be *astonished* instead.

*Beginning.* The Hebrew is *reshit* — the Genesis 1:1 word. *In the beginning.* Awe is where wisdom *starts*. The first thing. Everything else — the study, the precepts, the understanding — grows from the gasp.

*All who follow His precepts have good understanding.* The awe leads to the *doing*. Psalm 34 — *I will teach you awe: keep your tongue from evil, do good, seek peace.* Same sequence. Wonder → action → understanding.

*To Him belongs eternal praise.* The last line. The alphabet ends where it should — in praise.

Ten verses. Twenty-two lines. Here's the shape: *Hallelujah — all my heart, in public. His works are STUDIED by people who delight in them. He built memory into the calendar. He feeds the ones in awe. His rules are as reliable as His works. His name is awesome. — The AWE of the Source is the beginning of wisdom.*

Not fear. Never fear. *Awe.* Go stand in front of something too big to understand — and start getting wise.
