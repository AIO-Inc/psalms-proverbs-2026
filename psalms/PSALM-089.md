# PSALM 89 — Where Is the Promise?

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 89:1–52. Ethan the Ezrahite. Fifty-two verses. The last psalm of Book Three. Starts as the most confident hymn about God's promise to David — and ends asking where that promise went.*

---

Psalm 88 was one man in the dark. Psalm 89 is the whole *covenant* in the dark. The promise to David — the throne forever — and the throne is *empty*.

But Ethan doesn't start there. He starts with thirty-seven verses of *how good the promise was*.

**"I will sing of the Source's loyal-love forever. With my mouth I will make Your faithfulness known through all generations."**

*Forever. All generations.* Confident. The word *chesed* — loyal-love — shows up *seven times* in this psalm. And *emunah* — faithfulness — *seven* more. He's hammering the two things God can't fail at.

**"I will declare that Your loyal-love stands firm forever — that You've established Your faithfulness in heaven itself."**

Then — the promise. God speaking:

**"'I have made a covenant with My chosen one. I have sworn to David My servant: I will establish your line forever — and make your throne firm through all generations.'"**

*Forever. All generations.* Same words. God's promise using the same timeline as Ethan's praise.

Now — a hymn to God's power. Long, and gorgeous:

**"The heavens praise Your wonders, Source — Your faithfulness too, in the assembly of the holy ones. Who in the skies above can compare with the Source? Who is like the Source among the heavenly beings? In the council of the holy ones God is greatly held in awe. He is more awesome than all who surround Him."**

*Awe. Awesome.* *Yirah* twice. The angels themselves — *in awe*. Not cowering. *Amazed*.

**"Source God of Armies — who is like You? You are mighty, Source — and Your faithfulness surrounds You. You rule over the surging sea. When its waves mount up — You still them. You crushed Rahab like one of the slain. With Your strong arm You scattered Your enemies."**

*Rahab.* Not the woman — the *chaos monster*. Psalm 74's Leviathan. God crushed the thing that threatened creation.

**"The heavens are Yours — and Yours also the earth. You founded the world and all that is in it. You created the north and the south. Tabor and Hermon sing for joy at Your name."**

*The mountains sing.* Psalm 65 — the hills wear joy.

**"Your arm is endowed with power. Your hand is strong. Your right hand exalted. Right-standing and justice are the foundation of Your throne. Loyal-love and faithfulness go before You."**

Psalm 85's four virtues — *loyal-love and faithfulness* walking ahead of God like heralds.

**"Happy are those who have learned to acclaim You — who walk in the light of Your presence, Source. They rejoice in Your name all day long. They exult in Your right-standing. Because You are their glory and strength — and by Your favor You exalt our horn."**

*You exalt our horn.* Psalm 75 — the horns of the righteous lifted up.

Now — the covenant with David. In full. God speaking, at length:

**"'I have found David My servant. With My sacred oil I have anointed him. My hand will sustain him. Surely My arm will strengthen him. The enemy will not get the better of him. The wicked will not oppress him. I will crush his foes before him — and strike down his adversaries. My faithful love will be with him — and through My name his horn will be exalted.'"**

**"'I will set his hand over the sea — his right hand over the rivers. He will call out to Me: You are my Father — my God — the Rock my Savior. And I will appoint him My firstborn — the most exalted of the kings of the earth.'"**

*My Father.* *My firstborn.* The king as God's *son*. Psalm 2 — *You are My son.* Hebrews 1 picks this up for Christ.

**"'I will maintain My love to him forever — and My covenant with him will never fail. I will establish his line forever — his throne as long as the heavens endure.'"**

*Never fail. Forever. As long as the heavens.*

And then — God anticipates the *problem*:

**"'If his sons forsake My law and do not follow My statutes — if they violate My decrees and fail to keep My commands — I will punish their sin with the rod — their wrongdoing with flogging —'"**

*If they fail — I'll discipline.* God saw it coming. The sons *would* blow it.

**"'— but I will not take My love from him — nor will I ever betray My faithfulness. I will not violate My covenant — or alter what My lips have uttered.'"**

*But I will NOT take My love.* Even if they fail. *The covenant holds.* Discipline — yes. *Abandonment — never.*

**"'Once for all I have sworn by My holiness — and I will not lie to David — that his line will continue forever — and his throne endure before Me like the sun. It will be established forever like the moon — the faithful witness in the sky.'"**

*Like the sun. Like the moon.* Same as Psalm 72. *I will not lie to David.*

Thirty-seven verses. That's the promise. Unbreakable. Sworn on God's holiness. Sun and moon.

And then — verse 38:

**"But You have rejected. You have spurned. You have been very angry with Your anointed one."**

*But.* The word that's been the *turn toward hope* in fifty psalms — is now the turn toward *catastrophe*.

**"You have renounced the covenant with Your servant. You have defiled his crown in the dust."**

*Renounced the covenant.* Ethan says it. The thing God just swore He'd never do — *You did it.* The crown's in the *dirt*.

**"You have broken through all his walls — reduced his strongholds to ruins. All who pass by have plundered him. He has become the scorn of his neighbors."**

Psalm 80's vine with the walls broken down — but now it's the *king*.

**"You have exalted the right hand of his foes. You have made all his enemies rejoice. Indeed — You have turned back the edge of his sword — and have not supported him in battle."**

*You turned his sword.* God — fighting on the *other* side.

**"You have put an end to his splendor — and cast his throne to the ground. You have cut short the days of his youth. You have covered him with a mantle of shame."**

*Cast his throne to the ground.* The throne that was supposed to be *like the sun*.

This is 586 BC. Jerusalem burned. The last Davidic king — Zedekiah — blinded, chained, hauled to Babylon. His sons killed in front of him. The line that was supposed to last *forever* — apparently *over*.

Now — the questions:

**"How long, Source? Will You hide Yourself forever? How long will Your wrath burn like fire?"**

*How long.* Psalm 13, 74, 79, 80, 82 — it's the question of Books Two and Three. Here it's about the *covenant itself*.

**"Remember how fleeting is my life. For what futility You have created all humanity! Who can live and not see death — or who can escape the power of the grave?"**

Psalm 39, Psalm 90 — *we're vapor*. He's saying: *we don't have forever to wait. If the promise is going to come true — it needs to be in a human lifetime.*

**"Master — where is Your former loyal-love — which in Your faithfulness You swore to David?"**

*Where is it.* Not "does it exist." *Where did it GO?* He believes in it. He just can't *find* it.

**"Remember, Master — how Your servant has been mocked — how I bear in my heart the taunts of all the nations — the taunts with which Your enemies, Source, have mocked — with which they have mocked every step of Your anointed one."**

*Every step.* They followed the king to Babylon — jeering the whole way.

And then — the psalm stops. And the editors add the Book Three doxology:

**"Praise be to the Source forever! Amen and amen."**

*Praise be to the Source forever.* Right after *where is Your loyal-love?* No answer. Just — the blessing. Because that's what you say at the end of a book. Even a book that ends in the dark.

Fifty-two verses. Here's the shape: *I'll sing of Your loyal-love forever. You promised David — forever, sun and moon, I will not lie. Even if his sons fail — I won't take My love. — BUT You've thrown the crown in the dirt. You turned his sword. Where is the promise You swore? How long? — Praise be to the Source forever. Amen.*

Here's what Ethan couldn't see: the promise didn't fail. It went *underground*. The throne stayed empty for six hundred years — and then a descendant of David was born in a stable in David's town. *I will not lie to David.* He didn't. It just took longer than a human lifetime — which was Ethan's exact fear.

So — when the promise looks broken and the crown's in the dirt: say *where is it*. Say *how long*. Then say *amen* anyway. The line isn't over. It's just not visible yet.

Book Three ends here. Book Four opens with Moses — before David, before the throne, before any of it — reminding you that God was home before there was a kingdom to lose.
