# PSALM 101 — I Will Not Set Before My Eyes

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 101:1–8. David. A king's resolutions — what he will and won't allow in his house. Eight verses. Seven "I wills."*

---

After the enthronement psalms — a human king sitting down to decide how *he'll* rule under the real King. This is the job interview.

**"I will sing of Your loyal-love and justice. To You, Source, I will sing praise."**

*Loyal-love and justice.* The two things Psalm 89 and 99 said hold up God's throne. David wants them holding up *his*.

**"I will be careful to lead a blameless life. When will You come to me?"**

*When will You come to me?* A question in the middle of the resolutions. He's saying: *I'll clean the house — and I'm waiting for the guest.* The whole psalm is getting ready for a visit.

Now — the resolutions. And the first one is about *himself*:

**"I will conduct the affairs of my house with a blameless heart."**

*My house.* Start there. Not the kingdom. Not the courts. *Home.* The place nobody sees. If it's not straight there, it's not straight anywhere.

**"I will not set before my eyes anything that is worthless."**

*Before my eyes.* The screen. The feed. The thing you stare at when nobody's watching. David's deciding what gets to be *in front of him*. Not what he does — what he *looks at*. Because what's in front of the eyes ends up in the heart. Psalm 119 will say *turn my eyes from worthless things*. Here it's a *decision*.

The Hebrew is *d'var b'liya'al* — a *worthless* thing. Belial. The stuff that's *nothing* dressed up as something. He won't look at it.

**"I hate what faithless people do. I will have no part in it."**

*No part.* Not "I'll be careful around it." *Zero.*

**"The perverse of heart shall be far from me. I will have nothing to do with what is evil."**

*Far from me.* Distance. Psalm 1 — *don't walk with them, don't sit with them.* Psalm 26 — *I don't sit with the hidden ones.* He's choosing his *radius*.

Now — the house staff:

**"Whoever slanders their neighbor in secret — I will put to silence. Whoever has haughty eyes and a proud heart — I will not tolerate."**

*Slanders in secret.* Psalm 15 — *doesn't pick up a reproach against his friend.* The whisperer doesn't get a seat at David's table.

*Haughty eyes.* The look-down. The one who's already decided he's above the room. *Not tolerated.* Not because pride's a sin in a list — because a proud man *can't serve*. He's too busy being served.

**"My eyes will be on the faithful in the land — that they may dwell with me. The one whose walk is blameless will minister to me."**

*My eyes will be on.* Verse 3 — *I won't set worthless things before my eyes.* Verse 6 — *my eyes will be on the faithful.* He's not just *removing* — he's *replacing*. Take the trash out of the line of sight. Put *faithful people* there instead.

*They may dwell with me.* Live in the house. The faithful get *proximity*. That's the reward for integrity in this psalm — not money, not rank. *Closeness to the king.*

**"No one who practices deceit will dwell in my house. No one who speaks falsely will stand in my presence."**

*Speaks falsely.* Psalm 12's smooth lips. Psalm 52's razor tongue. Psalm 55's butter-smooth betrayer. David's been *burned* by every one of these. Now he's king — and the liar doesn't get past the door.

And the last verse:

**"Every morning I will put to silence all the wicked in the land. I will cut off every evildoer from the city of the Source."**

*Every morning.* Daily. Not a one-time purge. A *routine*. Every dawn — check the house, check the court, check who's whispering. The Hebrew is *la-b'karim* — *in the mornings*. Plural. It never stops.

Eight verses. Seven *I wills*. Here's the shape: *I'll sing of loyal-love and justice. Start with my own house. Nothing worthless in front of my eyes. Nothing to do with the crooked. No slanderers, no proud eyes. My eyes on the FAITHFUL — they live with me. No liars past the door. Every morning — clean it again.*

Here's what to do with it: you've got a house. Maybe not a kingdom — but a house. What's in front of your eyes? Who's at your table? Who whispers in your ear? David made a list. Make one.

And ask his question: *when will You come to me?* Then get the house ready.
