# PSALM 68 — Father of the Fatherless

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 68:1–35. A processional — God marching from Sinai to Zion, with the whole history of Israel in the parade. One of the hardest psalms in the book to translate, and one of the richest.*

---

This is a *march*. God moving out — through the wilderness, through battle, up the mountain, into the sanctuary. Thirty-five verses of motion. Don't try to hold it all. Catch the moments.

**"Let God rise up! Let His enemies scatter! Let the ones who hate Him flee from His face!"**

These are Moses' words — Numbers 10:35, what he said every time the Ark moved out: *Rise up, Source! Let Your enemies scatter!* The psalm opens with the marching orders.

"Like smoke is driven away — drive them away. Like wax melts before fire — let the wicked perish before God. **But let the ones who do right be glad. Let them exult before God. Let them rejoice with gladness.**"

Smoke and wax for the enemies. *Glad, exult, rejoice* — three words — for the righteous. The parade's got two kinds of people watching, and they're having very different days.

**"Sing to God! Sing praises to His name! Lift up a song to the One who rides through the deserts — the Source is His name — and exult before Him!"**

*Rides through the deserts.* Not enthroned in a palace. *Riding.* Through the dry places. Where His people are.

And then — the verse. The one that defines the whole march:

**"Father of the fatherless. Defender of widows. God — in His holy dwelling."**

*Father of the fatherless.* Stop. This is the God who rides through deserts and scatters enemies like smoke — and the *first title* He takes in this psalm is *Father to the kid with no dad.* Not King. Not Warrior. *Father of orphans.*

If your father wasn't there — if he left, or died, or was there and shouldn't have been — this is your verse. The One with the whole army behind Him stops and says *I'm yours.* Psalm 27 said *even if my father and mother abandon me, He'll gather me in.* Psalm 68 says who does the gathering.

*Defender of widows.* The woman with no man to stand for her in court — He stands.

**"God sets the lonely in families. He brings out prisoners into prosperity. But the rebellious live in a parched land."**

*Sets the lonely in families.* The Hebrew is *yechidim* — the solitary ones, the *onlys*. Psalm 25's word — *I am alone*. God takes the *only* and puts him at a *table*. Gives him a home he didn't have. That's what He does with the parade — He picks up the ones standing alone on the sidewalk and puts them in the march.

Now — the history, fast:

"God — when You went out before Your people, when You marched through the wilderness — the earth shook. The heavens poured — before God, the One of Sinai. Before God, the God of Israel."

Sinai. The mountain smoking. The ground shaking.

**"You sent abundant rain, God. When Your inheritance was weary — You restored it. Your people settled in it. In Your goodness, God, You provided for the poor."**

*When Your inheritance was weary.* Tired people. Worn-out land. And He *rained*. That's the answer to weariness in this psalm — not a lecture. *Rain.*

Then a battle — kings scattering, women dividing the spoil, "the wings of a dove covered with silver" — the war's over so fast the plunder's already being sorted.

**"The mountain of God is a mighty mountain. Why do you look with envy, you many-peaked mountains? This is the mountain God desired for His dwelling. Yes — the Source will live there forever."**

The other mountains are *jealous*. Bashan, Hermon — bigger, higher. And God picked *Zion* — a modest hill. Because He doesn't choose by size. He chooses by *desire*. *This is the one I want.*

**"The chariots of God are tens of thousands — thousands upon thousands. The Master is among them. Sinai is in the sanctuary."**

*Sinai is in the sanctuary.* The mountain moved. What happened at Sinai — the fire, the voice, the presence — it's *in Jerusalem now*. The whole terrifying power of the wilderness mountain — living in a building in the city.

**"You ascended on high. You led captives in Your train. You received gifts from among men — even from the rebellious — so that You, Source God, might dwell there."**

Paul quotes this in Ephesians 4:8 — about Christ ascending and giving gifts. *Even from the rebellious.* The ones who fought Him end up in the parade — not as prisoners. As *gift-bearers*.

**"Blessed be the Master — day by day He carries our load. God is our rescue."**

*Day by day He carries our load.* The Hebrew is *ya'amas lanu* — He loads Himself up for us. Like a pack animal. Every day, He picks up the weight. The God with tens of thousands of chariots — *carrying your bags*.

**"Our God is a God of rescues. To the Source — the Master — belong the exits from death."**

*The exits from death.* The Hebrew is *totza'ot* — the ways out. Death has *exits* — and He holds the keys to every one.

Then a flash of the imprecatory — enemies' heads shattered, blood, dogs — the war psalm hasn't forgotten it's a war psalm.

**"They've seen Your procession, God — the procession of my God, my King, into the sanctuary. Singers in front. Musicians behind. Between them — young women playing tambourines."**

*Here's* the parade. Singers first. Then the girls with tambourines. Then the band. It's *loud*. It's *joyful*. It's a *party moving up a hill*.

"Bless God in the great assembly! Bless the Source, you descendants of Israel! There's little Benjamin — leading them. The princes of Judah in their crowd. The princes of Zebulun. The princes of Naphtali."

*Little Benjamin — leading.* The smallest tribe, up front. Because in God's parade, the small ones lead.

**"Summon Your power, God. Show Your strength, God — as You've done for us before."**

Now — the nations:

"From Your temple above Jerusalem — kings bring You gifts. Rebuke the beast in the reeds — the herd of bulls among the calves of the peoples. Let them come humbled, bringing silver. Scatter the nations that delight in war. **Envoys will come from Egypt. Cush will hurry to stretch out its hands to God."**

*Egypt.* The old enemy. The oppressor. *Bringing gifts.* *Cush* — Ethiopia, the far south — *hurrying* to reach out. The parade doesn't end at Zion's gate. It picks up *the whole world* on the way.

**"Sing to God, kingdoms of the earth! Sing praise to the Master! To the One who rides in the ancient heavens — listen — He sends out His voice, a mighty voice."**

**"Ascribe power to God! Over Israel is His majesty. His power is in the skies. You are awesome, God, from Your sanctuary. The God of Israel gives power and strength to His people.**
**Blessed be God!"**

*Awesome.* The last *yirah* in the psalm. Not "terrible." *Awesome.* The one you look at from the sidewalk as the parade goes by, tambourines and chariots and little Benjamin up front, and you can't say anything but *wow*.

Thirty-five verses. Here's what to carry: *He rides through the deserts. He's Father to the fatherless. He puts the lonely at a table. He rains on the weary. He carries your load day by day. He holds the exits from death. And the parade picks up Egypt on the way home.*

You're not on the sidewalk. Get in the march.
