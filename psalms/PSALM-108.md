# PSALM 108 — A Song Stitched from Two Others

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 108:1–13. David. Verses 1–5 are Psalm 57:7–11. Verses 6–13 are Psalm 60:5–12. Two old songs — sewn into a new one. On purpose.*

---

You've already read this psalm. Twice. In two different places. And the editors put it back together here for a reason.

Psalm 57 was David in the cave — *my heart is steadfast, I will awaken the dawn.* Psalm 60 was David losing a battle at home while winning one up north — *through God we will do valiantly.* Psalm 108 takes the *ending* of each and makes them one thing.

Here's the first half — the cave song's finish:

**"My heart, God, is steadfast. I will sing and make music with all my soul."**

*Steadfast.* The Hebrew is *nachon* — fixed, established. Psalm 57's word. David said it in a cave with Saul's army outside. Now it's said *from the throne*. Same steadfastness. Different room.

**"Awake, harp and lyre! I will awaken the dawn!"**

*I will awaken the dawn.* Sing first — sun follows. The most defiant line in Book Two, now the opening of a Book Five psalm.

**"I will praise You, Source, among the nations. I will sing of You among the peoples. Because great is Your love — higher than the heavens. Your faithfulness reaches to the skies."**

*Among the nations.* Book Two's theme. Book Five keeps it.

**"Be exalted, God, above the heavens. Let Your glory be over all the earth."**

Psalm 57's refrain.

Now — the seam. And the second half — Psalm 60's ending:

**"Save us and help us with Your right hand — that those You love may be delivered."**

**"God has spoken from His sanctuary: 'In triumph I will parcel out Shechem — and measure off the Valley of Succoth. Gilead is Mine. Manasseh is Mine. Ephraim is My helmet. Judah is My scepter. Moab is My washbasin. On Edom I toss My sandal. Over Philistia I shout in triumph.'"**

God walking the property. *Mine, Mine, Mine.* Ephraim's His *hat*. Moab's His *foot-bath*. The whole map — inventoried and owned.

**"Who will bring me to the fortified city? Who will lead me to Edom? Is it not You, God — You who have rejected us — and no longer go out with our armies?"**

*You who have rejected us.* David keeps the hard line in. Even in the stitched-together victory song — the honesty stays. *You rejected us. And You're the only One who can fix it.*

**"Give us aid against the enemy — because human help is worthless."**

*Worthless.* *Shav.* Vapor. Psalm 33's horse.

**"With God we will gain the victory — and He will trample down our enemies."**

*With God.* The last line of Psalm 60 — *through God we will do valiantly.*

So — why stitch them?

Because the two halves are the two halves of *faith*. Psalm 57's half is *personal* — *my* heart is steadfast, *I* will wake the dawn. Psalm 60's half is *national* — *us*, *our* armies, *we* will do valiantly. The editors put them together to say: *the steadfast heart in the cave and the army marching on Edom are the same thing.* You don't get one without the other. Private worship — public courage. The dawn you woke alone — becomes the battle you fight together.

And notice what got *left out*. Psalm 57's first six verses — the lions, the net, the pit. Psalm 60's first four — *You've broken us, You've made us stagger.* Gone. Psalm 108 keeps only the *turn* from each. Not because the trouble wasn't real. Because *this* is the song for *after*. When you've been through both — and you want to sing just the part where it held.

That's allowed. Sometimes you sing the whole lament. Sometimes — later — you sing only the *yet*.

Thirteen verses. Here's the shape: *My heart is steadfast — I'll wake the dawn — among the nations. — God owns the map. You rejected us — You're our only help. Human help is vapor. With God — victory.*

Two old songs. One new one. Take the endings of your hardest seasons and *sew them together*. That's a song too.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1–5 = Psalm 57:7–11 (with minor variants). v. 6–13 = Psalm 60:5–12. v. 1 —* nachon libi *= my heart is fixed/steadfast. v. 2 —* a'irah shachar *= "I will awaken the dawn." v. 7–9 — divine speech, the land-survey. v. 12 —* shav t'shu'at adam *= "vain [is] the salvation of man." v. 13 —* b'Elohim na'aseh-chayil *= "in/with God we will do valiantly." The compilation is deliberate — Book Five's editors reuse Book Two material to show continuity across the exile. No* yirah*.*
