# PSALM 81 — Open Your Mouth Wide

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 81:1–16. Asaph. A festival psalm — probably Tabernacles — where the celebration stops halfway and God takes the microphone.*

---

It starts as a party. Then God interrupts.

**"Sing for joy to God our strength! Shout aloud to the God of Jacob! Begin the music! Strike the tambourine! Play the melodious harp and lyre!"**

Loud. Tambourine, harp, lyre. A festival in full swing.

**"Sound the ram's horn at the New Moon — and when the moon is full — on the day of our festival. This is a decree for Israel — an ordinance of the God of Jacob. He established it as a statute for Joseph — when He went out against Egypt."**

*A decree.* The party isn't optional. God *commanded* celebration. Put it on the calendar. Made it *law*. Think about that — a God who legislates *joy*.

And then — mid-festival — the voice changes:

**"I heard an unknown voice say:"**

*An unknown voice.* Asaph, in the middle of the music, hears something he doesn't recognize. And it's God. Speaking directly. The rest of the psalm is God talking.

**"'I removed the burden from their shoulders. Their hands were set free from the basket.'"**

*The basket.* Egypt — carrying bricks. The load on the shoulder, the basket in the hand. *I took it off.* That's how God introduces Himself: *I'm the One who took the basket out of your hand.*

**"'In your distress you called — and I rescued you. I answered you out of a thundercloud. I tested you at the waters of Meribah.'"**

*You called — I rescued.* The pattern. Every time.

Now — the commandments, restated as a *relationship*:

**"'Hear, My people — and I will warn you. If you would only listen to Me, Israel! You shall have no foreign god among you. You shall not bow down to an alien god.'"**

*If you would only listen.* That's not a command. That's a *plea*. God with His hands open.

**"'I am the Source your God — who brought you up out of Egypt.'"**

The first commandment's preamble. *Before I tell you what to do — remember what I did.*

And then — the verse the whole psalm is built for:

**"'Open your mouth wide — and I will fill it.'"**

*Open your mouth wide.* Like a baby bird. Like a kid at the dinner table. The posture of *expecting* to be fed. God's saying: *you don't have to hunt. You don't have to earn. You have to OPEN.* Wide. And He fills it.

That's the deal He's offering. The whole covenant in six words. *Open — I fill.*

Now — the heartbreak:

**"'But My people would not listen to Me. Israel would not submit to Me.'"**

*Would not.* Not "couldn't." *Wouldn't.* The mouth stayed shut. They wanted to feed themselves.

**"'So I gave them over to their stubborn hearts — to follow their own devices.'"**

*I gave them over.* Read that carefully. Not "I punished them." *I let them have what they wanted.* The most terrifying judgment in the Bible isn't fire. It's God saying *fine — your way.* Romans 1 — *God gave them over.* Psalm 78 — *He gave them what they craved, till they were gorged.*

The stubborn heart *gets to be stubborn*. That's the sentence.

**"'If My people would only listen to Me — if Israel would only follow My ways — how quickly I would subdue their enemies — and turn My hand against their foes!'"**

*If only.* Twice. *How quickly.* God's describing what He *wants* to do — and what's stopping Him isn't His will. It's a closed mouth.

**"'Those who hate the Source would cringe before Him — and their punishment would last forever.'"**

And the last line — God still holding out the food:

**"'But you would be fed with the finest of wheat. With honey from the rock I would satisfy you.'"**

*The finest wheat.* Not manna — the boring bread they complained about. *The finest.* And *honey from the rock* — Psalm 78's rock that gave water. Now it gives *honey*. He's upgrading the offer. *You wanted meat in the wilderness? I've got honey. From a rock. Just open your mouth.*

The psalm ends there. God with the honey in His hand. The mouth — still the question.

Sixteen verses. Here's the shape: *Party — it's commanded. Then a voice: I took the basket out of your hand. Open your mouth wide and I'll fill it. But you wouldn't. So I gave you your own way. If only — how quickly. I've got the finest wheat. Honey from the rock. Open.*

You're not the one who has to fill it. You're the one who has to *open*. Wide. That's the whole job.
