# PSALM 36 — In Your Light We See Light

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 36:1–12. Half diagnosis, half doxology.*

---

This psalm has two halves that don't match — on purpose. The first half is a clinical description of a man without God. The second is one of the most beautiful passages in Scripture. The gap between them is the point.

**Half One: The Man.**

"Rebellion speaks to the mark-misser deep inside. **There is no awe of God before his eyes.**"

That's the diagnosis. Not "he doesn't believe." *No awe.* He looks at the sky, the ocean, a newborn, and feels *nothing*. That's not a theological position — it's a *disability*. He's lost the capacity to be amazed, and everything else follows from that.

"Because he flatters himself in his own eyes — about finding his own guilt, about hating it."

*Flatters himself.* He's told himself a story where he's fine. He's not going to find his own fault — he's arranged not to. And he's certainly not going to *hate* it. He's made peace with it.

"The words of his mouth are harm and deceit. He's stopped being wise. He's stopped doing good."

*Stopped.* He used to. Something was there once. And he let it go.

"He plans harm on his bed. He sets himself on a road that isn't good. **He doesn't reject evil.**"

*Plans harm on his bed.* Lying there at night, working it out. Psalm 4 said *have it out with your own heart on your bed and be still.* This man's on the same bed doing the opposite.

*Doesn't reject evil.* Not "loves evil" — he doesn't *turn it down*. When it's offered, he takes it. That's how far the slide went. No awe → self-flattery → stopped being good → doesn't say no.

That's the whole man. Five verses. And then —

**Half Two: The God.**

The psalm doesn't transition. It just *turns*. Like walking out of a basement into the sun:

**"Source — Your loyal-love is in the heavens. Your faithfulness reaches the clouds.**
**Your right-standing is like the mighty mountains. Your judgments are the great deep."**

Sky. Clouds. Mountains. Ocean. He's measuring God's character against the biggest things there are — and they come up short. His loyal-love goes *past* the sky. His faithfulness *reaches* the clouds. His right-standing is *mountain-sized*. His judgments are *deep as the sea*.

**"Man and beast You save, Source."**

*And beast.* The animals too. Your dog's included. His rescue isn't just for the ones who can pray.

**"How precious is Your loyal-love, God! The children of Adam take refuge in the shadow of Your wings."**

*Precious.* The Hebrew is *yakar* — rare, costly, weighty. Not "nice." *Priceless.*

And the wings — Psalm 17's image. Under the feathers. Warm. Dark. Safe.

**"They feast on the abundance of Your house. You give them drink from the river of Your delights."**

*The river of Your delights.* The Hebrew word is *Eden*. Literally — *the river of Your Edens*. God's pleasures. Not His tolerance, not His patience — His *joy*. And He's pouring it out for you to drink. God's own happiness, on tap.

That's what the man in Half One is missing. Not rules. *This.*

And then — the line:

**"Because with You is the fountain of life. In Your light we see light."**

*The fountain.* The source. Where life comes from before it comes from anywhere else. And *in Your light we see light* — you can't even see the sun without light already in your eyes. He's the light that lets you see any light at all. Every good thing you've ever noticed — you noticed it *by Him*.

The man in Half One had no awe. This is why. He was trying to see without the light. Everything went gray.

"Keep extending Your loyal-love to those who know You — Your right-standing to the upright in heart. **Don't let the foot of pride come against me. Don't let the hand of the mark-missers shake me.**"

*The foot of pride.* Being stepped on by someone who thinks he's above you. And *shake me* — the word from Psalm 15, 16, 21 — *don't let me be moved.*

"There — the workers of harm have fallen. They've been thrown down — they can't get up."

Half One's man ends on the ground. Half Two's God is still pouring the river.

So: check for awe. It's the first thing to go. If the sky's stopped doing anything to you — you're in Half One, whether you believe or not. The way back is *In Your light we see light.* Get in the light. The sight comes back.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* ein-pachad Elohim *— note this is* pachad *(dread) not* yirah*; the wicked man has no* dread *of God either — he's lost every form of reverence. Rendered "awe" for the contrast the psalm is drawing. v. 4 —* ra lo yim'as *= he does not reject evil. v. 7 —* yakar *= precious, rare, weighty. v. 8 —* nachal adanecha *= "the river of Your Edens/delights." v. 9 —* m'kor chayim *= fountain/source of life;* b'orcha nir'eh-or *= "in Your light we see light."*
