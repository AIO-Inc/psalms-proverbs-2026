# PSALM 109 — I Am Prayer

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 109:1–31. David. The fiercest imprecatory psalm in the book. Thirty-one verses. Peter quoted it about Judas. Read it as what it is — a man handing God the rage instead of acting on it.*

---

This is the hardest psalm to read out loud. And it's in the Bible for exactly that reason.

**"My God, whom I praise — do not remain silent."**

*Whom I praise.* He starts with worship. Then he says everything else.

**"Because people who are wicked and deceitful have opened their mouths against me. They speak against me with lying tongues. With words of hatred they surround me. They attack me without cause."**

*Without cause.* Psalm 35, 69 — unprovoked. He didn't start this.

**"In return for my friendship they accuse me — but I am a man of prayer."**

The Hebrew is stark: *va-ani t'fillah* — *and I — prayer.* Not "I pray." *I AM prayer.* That's all he's got. They've got lawyers and lies and a mob. He's got — *prayer*. He's become it.

**"They repay me evil for good — and hatred for my friendship."**

Psalm 35 again — *I sat with them when they were sick.* Same wound.

Now — the curses. Twenty verses of them. And they're *brutal*. Some read these as David quoting what his *enemies* said about *him* — because verse 20 says *this is what my accusers say*. Some read them as David's own words. Either way — they're *in the book*. Here's the first stretch:

**"Appoint someone evil to oppose my enemy. Let an accuser stand at his right hand. When he is tried — let him be found guilty. May his prayers condemn him."**

*May his prayers condemn him.* The prayers themselves — evidence against him.

**"May his days be few. May another take his place of leadership."**

Acts 1:20 — Peter quoted *this line* about Judas. *Let another take his office.* And they chose Matthias.

**"May his children be fatherless — and his wife a widow. May his children be wandering beggars. May they be driven from their ruined homes. May a creditor seize all he has. May strangers plunder the fruits of his labor. May no one extend kindness to him — or take pity on his fatherless children. May his descendants be cut off. May their names be blotted out from the next generation."**

Stop. Sit with how bad that is. *His children fatherless. Wandering beggars. No pity for the orphans. Blotted out.* David's asking for the *family line* to end.

Why is this in Scripture?

Because *somebody's felt it*. If you've been destroyed by a liar — if someone you fed turned and took everything — there's a place in you where these words *live*. And you've got two choices: act on them, or *pray* them. David *prays* them. He doesn't send the assassin. He doesn't burn the house. He *writes it down and hands it to God*.

That's the whole function of imprecatory psalms. Psalm 58 — a *container*. Psalm 109 — the biggest one. You bring the ugliest thing in you *here* — *to God* — instead of to the person.

**"May the wrongdoing of his fathers be remembered before the Source. May the sin of his mother never be blotted out. May their sins always remain before the Source — that He may blot out their name from the earth."**

**"Because he never thought of doing a kindness — but hounded to death the poor and the needy and the brokenhearted. He loved to pronounce a curse — may it come back on him. He found no pleasure in blessing — may it be far from him. He wore cursing as his garment. It entered into his body like water — into his bones like oil. May it be like a cloak wrapped about him — like a belt tied forever around him."**

*He hounded the brokenhearted.* Psalm 34 — *the Source is close to the brokenhearted*. This man *hunted* them. That's the crime under all the curses — he went after the ones God protects.

*Cursing as his garment.* He *wore* it. And David asks that it *stick*.

**"May this be the Source's payment to my accusers — to those who speak evil of me."**

*This is what my accusers say* — some translations. Either way — David's done. Verse 20. The curses stop.

And now — the turn. And after twenty verses of that, it's *gentle*:

**"But You, Master — my Source — deal well with me for Your name's sake. Out of the goodness of Your love — deliver me."**

*But You.* Psalm 102's move. God in the subject position.

**"Because I am poor and needy — and my heart is wounded within me."**

*Wounded.* The Hebrew is *chalal* — pierced. Same word as the slain on a battlefield. His *heart* has been *run through*. The curses weren't strength. They were a *pierced man screaming*.

**"I fade away like an evening shadow. I am shaken off like a locust. My knees give way from fasting. My body is thin and gaunt."**

*Shaken off like a locust.* Brushed off a sleeve. Nothing.

**"I am an object of scorn to my accusers. When they see me — they shake their heads."**

**"Help me, Source my God. Save me according to Your loyal-love. Let them know that it is Your hand — that You, Source, have done it."**

*Let them know it's Your hand.* Not mine. He wants the vindication to have *God's* fingerprints — not his.

**"May my accusers be clothed with disgrace — and wrapped in shame as in a cloak."**

One last echo of the curses. Then — done.

**"With my mouth I will greatly extol the Source. In the great throng of worshipers I will praise Him."**

*In the throng.* Publicly. The pierced man — *singing* in a crowd.

And the last verse:

**"Because He stands at the right hand of the needy — to save their lives from those who would condemn them."**

*At the right hand of the needy.* Verse 6 asked for an *accuser* at the enemy's right hand. Verse 31 — *God* stands at the *needy man's* right hand. The place of the prosecutor — filled by the *Defender*. Romans 8:34 — *who is he that condemns? Christ is at the right hand of God, interceding.*

Thirty-one verses. Here's the shape: *I am prayer. They repaid friendship with hatred. — Twenty verses of the worst thing a human can wish on another. — But You. I'm poor. My heart is PIERCED. I'm a locust brushed off a sleeve. Save me — let them know it's Your hand. I'll praise You in the crowd. He stands at the right hand of the needy.*

Here's what to do with Psalm 109: when the rage is that big — bring it *here*. Say every word. To *God*. Then stop at verse 21. And let the One standing at your right hand handle the rest.

You don't have to be the accuser. That seat's taken — and not by you.

*Note on translation: v. 4 —* va-ani t'fillah *= "and I — prayer" — verbless; "I am [a man of] prayer." v. 6–19 — the imprecations; some scholars (and the NIV footnote) read v. 6–19 as the enemies' words quoted, based on v. 20; the traditional reading is David's own. Both are defensible; the psalm's function as container for rage holds either way. v. 8 —* p'kudato yikach acher *= "his office let another take" — Acts 1:20. v. 16 —* nichei levav *= brokenhearted. v. 22 —* libi chalal b'kirbi *= "my heart is pierced within me" —* chalal *= slain, pierced through. v. 23 —* ka-arbeh *= like a locust. v. 31 —* ya'amod li-y'min evyon *= "He stands at the right hand of the needy" — inversion of v. 6.
