# PSALM 43 — Send Out Your Light

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 43:1–5. The conclusion of Psalm 42 — the third and final refrain.*

---

Same singer. Same exile. But the panting deer has caught its breath enough to make a *demand*.

**"Vindicate me, God. Plead my case against a nation without loyalty. Rescue me from the deceitful and unjust man."**

*Plead my case.* Legal language. He's not asking for comfort anymore. He wants a *verdict*. He's been taunted for two psalms — *where is your God?* — and he's done absorbing it. *Take this to court. Argue it. Win it.*

**"Because You are the God of my stronghold. Why have You rejected me? Why do I go around in mourning, oppressed by the enemy?"**

*God of my stronghold — why have You rejected me.* Same breath. He calls God his fortress and asks why the fortress threw him out. Both things. No apology for the contradiction. That's what real faith sounds like when it's in pain — it holds the truth and the wound at the same time.

Then — the request. And it's not "bring me home." It's smaller and bigger than that:

**"Send out Your light and Your truth. Let them lead me. Let them bring me to Your holy mountain — to the place where You live."**

*Send out Your light and Your truth.* Not an army. Not a miracle. Two *guides*. Light — so I can see the road. Truth — so I know which road it is. He's asking for a way to *walk back*. Step by step. From wherever he is.

And this is the answer to the exile question. He asked in 42:2 — *when can I come and see the face of God?* Here's how: *You send the light. The light leads. I follow it to Your mountain.* He doesn't have to find his own way back. He needs the two guides to come get him.

**"Then I will come to the altar of God — to God, my exceeding joy. And I'll praise You with the harp, God — my God."**

*My exceeding joy.* The Hebrew is *simchat gili* — "the joy of my rejoicing." Joy on top of joy. Not "my duty." Not "my obligation." The thing that makes him *glad past gladness*. That's what God is to the temple singer.

*With the harp.* He's going to play again. The instrument that's been silent in exile — he's already hearing it.

And the refrain. Third time. Last time:

**"Why are you bowed down, my soul? Why are you in turmoil inside me?**
**Wait for God — because I will praise Him again — my rescue and my God."**

Same words as 42:11. But it lands different now. In 42:5 it was a man talking himself off a ledge. In 42:11 it was a man tightening his grip. Here — after *plead my case*, after *send Your light*, after *my exceeding joy* — it's a man who's already halfway home. The refrain's the same. The one saying it isn't.

That's how repetition works in prayer. You say the same thing three times, and each time *you've* changed, so it means something new. The words are a stairway. You're higher every time you step on them.

So — three refrains across two psalms. Here's the shape: *I'm panting. I'm eating tears. Why, soul? Wait. — I'm drowning in Your waves. Why, soul? Wait. — Plead my case. Send Your light. I'll play the harp again. Why, soul? Wait.*

You don't get out of exile in one prayer. You get out in three. Or thirty. Same words. Higher every time.

Send out Your light. Let it lead.
