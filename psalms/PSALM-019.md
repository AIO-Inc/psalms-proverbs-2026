# PSALM 19 — Two Books, Same Author

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 19:1–14. C.S. Lewis called it "the greatest poem in the Psalter and one of the greatest lyrics in the world."*

---

God wrote two books. This psalm reads both.

**Book One: The Sky.**

"The heavens are telling the glory of God. The dome of the sky declares the work of His hands. Day after day pours out speech. Night after night makes it known."

*Pours out speech.* The sky is *talking*. Every sunrise is a sentence. Every night sky is a paragraph. It's been running nonstop since the beginning, and it never repeats itself.

"There's no speech, no words — their voice isn't heard. But their line goes out through all the earth, their words to the ends of the world."

No words — and yet everyone hears it. Every language, every century, every tribe. You don't need a translator for a sunset. It's the one sermon no one can claim they never heard.

"In the heavens He set a tent for the sun. It comes out like a groom from his room — glad, like a champion about to run his race. It rises from one end of the sky and circles to the other. Nothing hides from its heat."

The sun as a *groom*. Coming out of the bedroom on his wedding morning, grinning. Like an athlete at the start line, bouncing on his toes. That's how David sees the sunrise — not a physics event. *Joy*. Something that's glad to be doing exactly what it was made to do.

Nothing hides from its heat. Book One reaches everybody.

**Book Two: The Word.**

Now David switches — from the sky to the scroll. And he does something clever: six lines, six names for the Word, six things it does.

**"The instruction of the Source is complete — it restores the soul."** Complete. Whole. Nothing missing. And it *brings you back* — the same word as "return." The Word is a homing beacon for the part of you that wandered.

**"The testimony of the Source is reliable — it makes the simple wise."** You don't have to be smart. You have to be *simple* — open, teachable — and it'll make you wise.

**"The rules of the Source are straight — they make the heart glad."** *Glad.* Not guilty. Not burdened. Straight lines make a heart happy the way a clean room does.

**"The command of the Source is pure — it lights up the eyes."** Same phrase as Psalm 13 — *light up my eyes*. The Word does what David begged for.

**"The awe of the Source is clean — it stands forever."** *Awe.* Here's *yirah* — and look at what it does: it *stands forever*. Awe is the one thing that doesn't wear out. You get bored of everything eventually — except wonder.

**"The judgments of the Source are true — every one of them right."**

Then the value statement:

**"More desirable than gold — than heaps of pure gold. Sweeter than honey — than honey dripping from the comb."**

Not "you should read it." *Sweeter than honey.* He *wants* it. Craves it. Because he's tasted what it does.

"By them Your servant is warned. In keeping them there's great reward."

*Warned.* The Word is a guardrail before it's anything else. It tells you where the cliff is.

**Book Three: The Heart.**

Now David turns the light on himself. He's read the sky. He's read the scroll. Now he reads *him*:

"Who can discern his own errors? Clear me from hidden faults."

*Hidden* faults. The ones you don't know you have. The blind spots. You can't confess what you can't see — so he asks God to handle the ones he's missing.

"Keep Your servant from willful sins. Don't let them rule over me. Then I'll be blameless — clean from great transgression."

*Don't let them rule over me.* He knows the danger isn't the one slip. It's the slip that becomes a master. Every addict knows exactly what he means.

And then the last line — the prayer people have said before sermons for three thousand years:

**"Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable in Your sight, Source — my Rock and my Redeemer."**

Mouth and heart. What comes out and what stays in. Both of them — *acceptable*. Not perfect. Acceptable. Welcome.

*My Rock and my Redeemer.* The last word is *goel* — the kinsman who buys you back. The relative who pays your debt when you can't. He ends the greatest lyric in the world with the word for *the One who bought me back*.

Two books. Same Author. Read the sky in the morning. Read the Word at night. Then let Him read you — and clean out what you couldn't see.

---

*Note on translation: v. 7 —* torah *= instruction/teaching, not "law" in a legal sense;* meshivat nefesh *= restores/returns the soul. v. 9 — "the fear of the LORD is clean" =* yirat YHWH *=* yirah *= awe/reverence, NOT fear. The whole point is that awe is* clean *and* eternal *— dread is neither. v. 12 —* nistarot *= hidden things, secret faults. v. 14 —* goel *= kinsman-redeemer (see Ruth 4, Job 19:25).*
