# PSALM 142 — No One Cares for My Soul

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 142:1–7. Seven verses. Superscription: "A maskil of David. When he was in the cave. A prayer." The last psalm in the book with a historical setting. Adullam or En Gedi — 1 Samuel 22 or 24. A man hiding in the dark with nobody left.*

---

Here's the story. David's running from Saul. He's got no army yet — or he's got four hundred misfits who followed him into a cave at Adullam. Or it's later — En Gedi — and Saul's *right outside*, close enough that David could cut the hem of his robe. Either way — a cave. Wet stone. Dark. And a king who wants him dead somewhere above.

Psalm 57 was written in a cave too — *I will awaken the dawn*. That one had confidence. Psalm 142 doesn't. This is the *other* cave psalm. The one before the dawn showed up.

**"I cry aloud to the Source — I lift up my voice to the Source for mercy."**

*Cry aloud.* *Ez'ak.* Not whisper. *Scream.* In a cave — where sound *carries*. He doesn't care who hears. Psalm 141 — *hear my voice*. Psalm 142 — *I'm making sure You can*.

**"I pour out my complaint before Him — before Him I tell my trouble."**

*Pour out my complaint.* *Eshpoch sichi.* *Sichi* — my *musing*, my *brooding*, the stuff going around in my head. *Poured out.* Psalm 62 — *pour out your hearts before Him*. Psalm 102 — *a prayer of one who pours out his complaint*. Psalm 142 — *dumped on the cave floor in front of God*.

*Before Him.* *L'fanav.* Twice in one verse. *Before His FACE.* Psalm 140 — *the upright dwell with His face*. Psalm 142 — *I'm pouring the whole mess out right in front of it*. That's the confidence hidden inside the despair — he still thinks God's *close enough* to pour on.

**"When my spirit grows faint within me — it is You who know my way. In the path where I walk — people have hidden a snare for me."**

*When my spirit grows faint.* *B'hit'atef alai ruchi.* When my spirit *wraps itself up* — folds in — collapses. Psalm 77 — *my spirit grew faint*. Psalm 143 will say it again next. It's the exile's phrase, the depressive's phrase — *the thing inside me is curling up*.

*It is You who know my way.* *Attah yadata n'tivati.* Psalm 139 — *You know my sitting and rising, You know my way*. Psalm 142 — *and I need You to*. Because *I* don't. He's in a cave. There's no *way*. Just rock. *But You know it.*

*A snare in my path.* Psalm 140, 141 — traps. Three psalms in a row. The road's been mined.

Now — the verse that makes this psalm what it is:

**"Look to my right — and see. No one is concerned for me. I have no refuge. No one cares for my soul."**

*Look to my right.* The *right hand* — Psalm 16, 109, 110, 121 — that's where the *defender* stands. Your right-hand man. Your second. David says — *look there. Nobody.* The spot's empty.

*No one cares for my soul.* *Ein doresh l'nafshi.* Literally — *there's no one SEEKING my soul*. Nobody's looking for me. Nobody's asking where I am. Four hundred men in the cave with him — and he says *no one*. Because loneliness isn't about the headcount.

If you've ever been in a full room and felt this verse — you know it's not a contradiction. The people around you can be *there* and still not *seeking your soul*. David gets that. He said it in a crowded cave.

*I have no refuge.* *Avad manos mimenni.* The escape *perished*. Psalm 59 — *You are my refuge*. Psalm 142 — *the refuge is GONE*. He's in a hole in the rock — the literal refuge — and says *I have none*. Because the rock doesn't *care* about him either.

That's the bottom. Verse four. Read it, and don't rush past it. The canon kept a verse where David says *nobody cares if I live*. That's *permission*.

Now — the turn. And it's not a trumpet. It's a man saying the *same words* differently:

**"I cry to You, Source. I say: 'You are my refuge — my portion in the land of the living.'"**

*You are my refuge.* *Attah machsi.* Verse four — *I have no refuge*. Verse five — *You ARE my refuge*. Same word-family, one verse apart. He wasn't lying in verse four. There *was* no one. And then he turned his head — and said it *to God* — and the *no one* became *You*.

*My portion in the land of the living.* *Chelki b'eretz ha-chayyim.* Psalm 16 — *You are my portion*. Psalm 73 — *my portion forever*. Psalm 119 — *You are my portion, Source*. Psalm 142 — *in the LAND OF THE LIVING*. He's in a cave. Underground. Halfway to Sheol. And he claims his inheritance *among the living*. That's not describing where he is. That's *deciding* where he belongs.

**"Listen to my cry — for I am in desperate need. Rescue me from those who pursue me — for they are too strong for me."**

*Too strong for me.* *Am'tzu mimenni.* He admits it. Saul's got the army, the crown, the country. David's got a cave. *They're stronger.* Psalm 18 — *He rescued me from my strong enemy — they were too strong for me* — same phrase, but 18 is *after*. 142 is *during*.

**"Set me free from my prison — that I may praise Your name. Then the righteous will gather about me — because of Your goodness to me."**

*From my prison.* *Mi-masger.* The *shut place*. The cave is a prison with no bars — you're free to leave and die. *Bring me OUT.*

*That I may praise Your name.* The reason he asks. Not *so I can be king*. *So I can PRAISE*. Psalm 6, 30, 88 — *who praises You from the grave?* Psalm 142 — *get me out and I'll do it*.

*Then the righteous will gather about me.* *Bi yachtiru tzaddikim.* They'll *crown* around me — *surround* me. Verse four — *look to my right — no one*. Verse seven — *the righteous will GATHER*. The empty right hand — *filled*. Not yet. *Then.* But he can see it.

And that's how it ends. *Because of Your goodness to me.* *Ki tigmol alai.* Because You'll *deal bountifully* with me. Future tense. He's still in the cave. The psalm doesn't get him out. But he *knows* what it'll look like when he's out — *a crowd of the righteous, and him in the middle, and everyone saying LOOK what He did*.

He got out. 1 Samuel 22 — *his brothers and his father's household came down to him*. 1 Samuel 23 — six hundred men. The right hand filled up. Verse seven came true. But *the psalm* — the *psalm* was written in the dark, before, with *no one*.

Seven verses. Here's the shape: *I scream to the Source. I pour it all out in front of Him. My spirit's curling up — but You know the way. Look right — NO ONE. No refuge. No one cares. — I cry: YOU are my refuge. My portion — among the living. They're stronger than me. Get me out — so I can praise. Then they'll gather around me. Because of what You did.*

Here's your practice. If you're in the cave — say verse four. Out loud. *No one cares for my soul.* Don't clean it up. Then — same breath — say verse five. *You are my refuge.* Both. One after the other. That's the whole turn. Not from despair to confidence. From *no one* — to *You*.

The cave doesn't have to change. The direction of your face does.

*Note on translation: Superscription — *maskil l'David bi-h'yoto va-m'arah t'fillah* = "a maskil of David, when he was in the cave — a prayer." Cave: Adullam (1 Sam 22:1–2) or En Gedi (1 Sam 24:1–7); cf. Ps 57 (also "in the cave"). v. 1 — *ez'ak* = I cry out/scream. v. 2 — *eshpoch l'fanav sichi* = "I pour out before Him my complaint/musing" — *siach* = brooding thought (cf. 55:2; 64:1; 102 title); *l'fanav* = "before His face" ×2. v. 3 — *b'hit'atef alai ruchi* = "when my spirit wraps itself/faints upon me" (cf. 77:3; 143:4; Lam 2:11); *attah yadata n'tivati* = "You know my path" (cf. 139:3). v. 4 — *habbet yamin u-r'eh* = "look right and see"; *v'ein-li makkir* = "and there is no one recognizing/acknowledging me"; *avad manos mimenni* = "refuge has perished from me"; ***ein doresh l'nafshi* = "there is no one seeking my soul"** — cf. Jer 30:17 "no one cares for you." v. 5 — *attah machsi* = "You are my refuge" — deliberate reversal of v. 4; *chelki b'eretz ha-chayyim* = "my portion in the land of the living" (cf. 16:5; 27:13; 73:26; 119:57). v. 6 — *am'tzu mimenni* = "they are stronger than I" (cf. 18:17). v. 7 — *mi-masger* = from the prison/enclosure; *bi yachtiru tzaddikim* = "around me the righteous will encircle/crown" (*katar* — cf. 22:12 negatively); *ki tigmol alai* = "for You will deal bountifully with me" (cf. 13:6; 116:7; 119:17). No *yirah* in this psalm.*
