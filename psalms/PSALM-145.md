# PSALM 145 — You Open Your Hand

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 145:1–21. Twenty-one verses. The last psalm attributed to David in the book. An acrostic — every verse starts with the next Hebrew letter, alef to tav — with one letter missing in the standard text and restored from the Dead Sea Scrolls. The only psalm titled "a praise" (tehillah) — the word the whole book is named after. Jews say it three times a day. This is David's last word.*

---

Seventy-three psalms carry David's name. This is the last. After the cave, the pit, the war, the adultery, the dead son, the crown in the dirt — the old man sits down and writes one more. An *acrostic* — A to Z. Because he wants to say *everything*, in order, and leave nothing out.

**"I will exalt You, my God the King. I will praise Your name for ever and ever. Every day I will praise You — and extol Your name for ever and ever."**

*My God the King.* *Elohai ha-melech.* David — the *king* — calls God *the King*. He's spent his life on a throne and he knows whose it is.

*Every day.* *B'chol-yom.* Not festival days. *Every.* The praise isn't for occasions. It's *dailies*. Psalm 119 — *seven times a day*. Psalm 145 — *every day, forever*.

**"Great is the Source and most worthy of praise. His greatness — no one can fathom."**

*No one can fathom.* *Ein cheker.* No *searching it out*. Psalm 139 — *You searched me*. Psalm 145 — *I can't search YOU*. He knows the bottom of me. There's no bottom to Him.

**"One generation commends Your works to another. They tell of Your mighty acts. They speak of the glorious splendor of Your majesty — and I will meditate on Your wonderful works. They tell of the power of Your awesome works — and I will proclaim Your great deeds."**

*One generation to another.* *Dor l'dor.* Psalm 78 — *tell the next generation*. Psalm 102 — *written for a generation to come*. Psalm 145 — David's *doing* it. This psalm *is* the handoff. The old king telling the kids.

*Your awesome works.* *Nor'otecha.* The *yirah* root. Not *terrible* — KJV — *awesome*. Psalm 139 — *awesomely made*. Psalm 145 — *awesome works*. The things that stop your breath. Handed down.

**"The Source is gracious and compassionate — slow to anger and rich in loyal-love."**

There it is. *Exodus 34.* God's *own* self-description to Moses on Sinai — *the Source, the Source, gracious and compassionate God, slow to anger, abounding in loyal-love*. Psalm 86, 103, 111, 112 — quoted it. Psalm 145 — David's *last* psalm — quotes it *again*. Because when you've got one verse to leave — you leave *that* one.

*Slow to anger.* *Erech appayim.* *Long of nostrils*. The breath takes a *long* time to come out hot. Psalm 7:11's KJV — *God is angry every day* — was wrong. Psalm 145 — *slow*. David had a lifetime to find out.

**"The Source is good to all. He has compassion on all He has made."**

*Good to ALL.* *Tov-YHWH la-kol.* Not to Israel. *All.* Not to the righteous. *All.* Psalm 136 — *food to all flesh*. Psalm 145 — *compassion on ALL He made*. If He made it — He *feels* for it. That's the widest sentence in the psalm.

**"All Your works praise You, Source — and Your loyal ones extol You. They tell of the glory of Your kingdom — and speak of Your might — so that all people may know of Your mighty acts — and the glorious splendor of Your kingdom. Your kingdom is an everlasting kingdom — and Your dominion endures through all generations."**

*Your kingdom.* *Malchut'cha.* Four times in four verses. The old *king* — talking about *the* kingdom. Psalm 2 — *the kings of the earth*. Psalm 93, 96–99 — *the Source reigns*. Psalm 145 — *everlasting*. His crown got put in the dirt — Psalm 89. *This* one doesn't.

Daniel 4 — *His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom* — Nebuchadnezzar said it after seven years as a beast. Same words. The Babylonian king learned what the Israelite king already knew.

Now — the *missing* verse. The Hebrew alphabet has twenty-two letters. The standard Hebrew text has twenty-one verses — *nun* is missing. For a thousand years people wondered. Then the Dead Sea Scrolls — Cave 11 — had it. And so did the Greek. Here it is:

**"The Source is faithful in all His words — and loyal in all He does."**

*Faithful in all His words.* *Ne'eman YHWH b'chol-d'varav.* The *nun* verse. *Ne'eman* — faithful, trustworthy — starts with *nun*. It was *always* there. Somebody dropped it. And the Scrolls put it back. That's this whole project in one verse — *a lost line about God's faithfulness, restored*.

**"The Source upholds all who fall — and lifts up all who are bowed down."**

*Upholds all who fall.* *Somech YHWH l'chol-ha-nof'lim.* Not *keeps from falling*. *Upholds the FALLING*. The ones *mid-fall*. Psalm 37 — *though he stumble, he will not fall, for the Source upholds him*. Psalm 145 — *ALL who fall*. Psalm 143's man — *crushed to the ground* — *lifted*.

*Bowed down.* *K'fufim.* *Bent over.* Psalm 146 will say it again. The ones who can't straighten up. He does it *for* them.

And now — the verse the Jewish tradition built the mealtime blessing around:

**"The eyes of all look to You — and You give them their food at the proper time. You open Your hand — and satisfy the desire of every living thing."**

*The eyes of all look to You.* *Einei-chol elecha y'sabberu.* Every eye. Psalm 104 — *they all look to You*. Psalm 123 — *as the eyes of servants look to the master's hand*. Psalm 145 — *every living thing — looking at Your hand*.

*You open Your hand.* *Poteach et-yadecha.* This is the verse. Three words. *You. Open. Hand.* Psalm 104 — *when You open Your hand, they are satisfied*. Psalm 145 — *the hand opens — and every living thing gets what it wants*. Not needs. *Wants.* *Ratzon.* The *desire*. He doesn't ration. He *opens*.

That's why Jews say it before eating — three times a day. *You open Your hand.* The bread on the table came out of an open palm. Not earned. *Opened.*

**"The Source is righteous in all His ways — and loyal toward all He has made. The Source is near to all who call on Him — to all who call on Him in truth."**

*Near.* *Karov.* Psalm 34 — *near to the brokenhearted*. Psalm 119 — *You are near*. Psalm 145 — *near to ALL who call*. Deuteronomy 4 — *what nation has a god so NEAR?* He's not far and getting closer. He's *near* — the whole time.

*In truth.* *Ve-emet.* Not perfectly. *Honestly.* Psalm 142 — *I pour out my complaint*. That's calling in truth. The mess is the truth.

**"He fulfills the desires of those who stand in awe of Him. He hears their cry and saves them."**

*Those in awe of Him.* *Y're'av.* The *yirah* word — *awe*, not fear. And look what happens to the awe-people: *He fulfills their DESIRES*. *R'tzon y're'av ya'aseh.* Same word as verse sixteen — *ratzon*. The open hand that satisfies every living thing — and the awe-people get their *ratzon* done. You don't get your desires by grabbing. You get them by *standing in awe* and opening your hands like He does.

*He hears their cry.* Psalm 142 — *I cry aloud*. Psalm 145 — *heard*.

**"The Source watches over all who love Him — but all the wicked He will destroy."**

*Watches over.* *Shomer.* Psalm 121 — six times. Psalm 145 — *the keeper keeps the lovers*. And the mark-missers — *destroyed*. One hard line in twenty-one verses. Because David's not writing a greeting card. He's writing the truth. Psalm 1 — *the way of the wicked perishes*. Psalm 145 — *same*. The open hand and the closed fist — for different people.

And the last verse — David's *last* verse in the whole book:

**"My mouth will speak in praise of the Source. Let every creature praise His holy name — for ever and ever."**

*My mouth.* *Pi.* Psalm 141 — *guard my mouth*. Psalm 145 — *my mouth will PRAISE*. That's what the guard was for.

*Every creature.* *Kol-basar.* *All flesh.* Psalm 136 — *food to all flesh*. Psalm 145 — *praise from all flesh*. Everything He feeds — *sings back*. That's David's last line. Not *I*. *Everyone.*

And then — five more psalms. All *Hallelujah*. David hands off — and the whole book stands up.

Twenty-one verses — twenty-two with the one they found. Here's the shape: *I'll exalt You, my King, every day, forever. Great — no bottom. Generation to generation. Your awesome works. Gracious, compassionate, slow to anger, rich in loyal-love. Good to ALL. Your kingdom forever. Faithful in every word. You uphold the falling — lift the bent. Every eye's on Your hand — and You OPEN it. Near to all who call — honestly. The awe-people get their desires. You keep the lovers. My mouth — and all flesh — forever.*

Here's what to do. Before your next meal — say verse sixteen. *You open Your hand and satisfy the desire of every living thing.* That's it. Then eat. You'll taste it differently.

David's done. Alef to tav. Nothing left out. The book he started with *happy is the one* ends his voice with *all flesh, forever*.

---

*Note on translation: Title: *tehillah l'David* — the only psalm titled *tehillah* ("praise"), from which the book's Hebrew name *Tehillim* derives. Acrostic: 22 letters; MT lacks the *nun* verse (between v. 13 and 14). **Restored from 11QPs^a (Dead Sea Scrolls, Cave 11), LXX, Syriac, and one medieval Hebrew MS: *ne'eman Elohim bi-d'varav v'chasid b'chol-ma'asav* = "Faithful is God in His words and loyal in all His works" — rendered with "the Source" for consistency (11QPs^a reads YHWH).** Talmud (Berakhot 4b) notes the missing *nun* and links it to Amos 5:2 (*naf'lah*, "fallen"), with v. 14 (*nof'lim*) as the answer. v. 1 — *Elohai ha-melech* = "my God the King." v. 3 — *ein cheker* = unsearchable (cf. Isa 40:28). v. 4 — *dor l'dor* = generation to generation. **v. 6 — *nor'otecha* = "Your awesome [deeds]" — *yirah* root; KJV "terrible acts" → awesome.** v. 8 — Exod 34:6 formula (cf. 86:15; 103:8; 111:4; 112:4); *erech appayim* = "long of nostrils/slow to anger." v. 9 — *tov-YHWH la-kol* = "good is the Source to all"; *rachamav al-kol-ma'asav* = "His compassions on all His works." v. 11–13 — *malchut* ×4; v. 13a cf. Dan 4:3, 34; 7:27. v. 14 — *somech l'chol-ha-nof'lim* = "upholding all the falling"; *k'fufim* = bent/bowed (cf. 146:8). v. 15–16 — *einei-chol elecha y'sabberu* = "the eyes of all hope toward You" (cf. 104:27); *poteach et-yadecha* = "opening Your hand"; *ratzon* = desire/favor — used in Jewish grace after meals (*Birkat Hamazon*) and in *Ashrei* (145 recited thrice daily). v. 17 — *chasid* = loyal (of God). v. 18 — *karov* = near (Deut 4:7; cf. 34:18; 119:151); *ve-emet* = in truth/faithfully. **v. 19 — *r'tzon y're'av ya'aseh* = "the desire of those in AWE of Him He will do" — *yirah* correction; KJV "them that fear him" → those in awe; note *ratzon* repeats from v. 16.** v. 20 — *shomer* = keeps/watches (cf. 121); *r'sha'im* = mark-missers/wicked. v. 21 — *kol-basar* = all flesh (cf. 136:25). Last Davidic psalm; 146–150 follow as the closing Hallel. ***Yirah* in v. 6 (*nor'otecha*) and v. 19 (*y're'av*) — both corrected to awe.***
