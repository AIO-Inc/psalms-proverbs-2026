# PSALM 77 — Has His Right Hand Changed?

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 77:1–20. Asaph. A night of insomnia that turns into a Bible study — and the Bible study saves him.*

---

This is a psalm about what to do at 3 a.m. when you can't feel God and you're starting to wonder if He's changed.

**"I cried out to God for help. I cried out to God to hear me. When I was in distress I sought the Master. At night I stretched out untiring hands — and I would not be comforted."**

*Would not be comforted.* Not "couldn't." *Wouldn't.* He's refusing the easy answers. The cheap comfort. If you've been there — where people say the nice thing and you push it away because it's not *enough* — he's there too.

**"I remembered You, God — and I groaned. I meditated — and my spirit grew faint."**

Here's the terrible part: *remembering God made it worse.* Thinking about God — the God who used to show up — and comparing it to now. The memory of the good becomes the weight.

**"You kept my eyes from closing. I was too troubled to speak."**

*You kept my eyes from closing.* He blames the insomnia on God. *You're doing this.* And — *too troubled to speak*. The prayers stopped forming. Just the ceiling and the silence.

**"I thought about the former days — the years of long ago. I remembered my songs in the night."**

*My songs in the night.* He used to *sing* in the dark. Psalm 42 — *His song is with me at night.* Now the night's silent and he's remembering when it wasn't.

Then — the questions. And they're the questions nobody's supposed to say out loud:

**"Will the Master reject forever? Will He never show His favor again? Has His loyal-love vanished forever? Has His promise failed for all time? Has God forgotten to be merciful? Has He in anger withheld His compassion?"**

Six questions. *Forever. Never. Vanished. Failed. Forgotten. Withheld.* Every one aimed at the *character* of God. Not "why is this happening" — *has He CHANGED?* Is the loyal-love gone? Did the promise break?

He asks all six. In the Bible. And nobody edited them out.

Now — the turn. And it's not a feeling. It's a *decision*:

**"Then I thought: 'To this I will appeal — the years when the Most High stretched out His right hand.'"**

*To this I will appeal.* Legal language. *I'm going to make a case. And here's my evidence.* Not his feelings — they're useless right now. Not his circumstances — they're the problem. *The years when His right hand was out.* The *history*.

**"I will remember the deeds of the Source. Yes — I will remember Your miracles of long ago. I will consider all Your works and meditate on all Your mighty deeds."**

*I will. I will. I will.* Three times. He's *choosing* to remember. At 3 a.m., when the remembering was making it worse — he changes *what* he remembers. Not "how good it used to feel." *What God actually DID.*

And here's what he pulls up:

**"Your ways, God, are holy. What god is as great as our God? You are the God who performs miracles. You display Your power among the peoples. With Your mighty arm You redeemed Your people — the descendants of Jacob and Joseph."**

The Exodus. He goes to the Exodus. Same as Psalm 74 — the one thing that can't be argued with.

**"The waters saw You, God. The waters saw You and writhed. The very depths were convulsed. The clouds poured down water. The skies resounded with thunder. Your arrows flashed back and forth. Your thunder was heard in the whirlwind. Your lightning lit up the world. The earth trembled and quaked."**

The Red Sea — but from the *water's* point of view. *The waters saw You — and writhed.* The sea *panicked*. The deep *convulsed*. That's what happened when the God he's wondering about showed up at the shore.

**"Your path led through the sea — Your way through the mighty waters — though Your footprints were not seen."**

*Your footprints were not seen.* Here's the answer to the six questions. God walked *through the sea* — through the chaos, through the thing that was killing them — and *nobody saw His feet.* The people saw the water part. They didn't see *Him*. He was there — invisible — doing the impossible.

So at 3 a.m. when you can't see Him — *that's not new.* They couldn't see Him at the Red Sea either. The footprints were never the evidence. The parted water was.

**"You led Your people like a flock — by the hand of Moses and Aaron."**

*Like a flock.* Psalm 23. The shepherd. Through the sea, invisible, footprints unseen — still the shepherd.

And that's where it ends. No "and then I felt better." No resolution to the six questions. Just — the Red Sea, the writhing waters, the unseen footprints, the flock.

Because that *is* the resolution. He didn't get his feelings back. He got his *facts* back. And he went to sleep on the facts.

Twenty verses. Here's what to do at 3 a.m.: *Cry out. Refuse the cheap comfort. Ask all six questions — has He changed? Then say "to this I will appeal" — and go to the record. Not how it felt. What He DID. The waters writhed. His footprints were unseen. He was there anyway.*

Has His right hand changed? Go check the sea.
