# PSALM 55 — Cast Your Burden

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 55:1–23. Betrayal by a close friend — most likely Ahithophel, David's counselor, who joined Absalom's rebellion.*

---

Psalm 41 was *the friend who ate my bread*. Psalm 55 is the full version. This is what it feels like from the inside.

"Hear my prayer, God. Don't hide from my plea. Pay attention to me — answer me. **I'm restless in my complaint. I moan.**"

*Restless.* The Hebrew is *arid* — to wander, to roam. He can't sit still. Pacing. The prayer's coming out in pieces because he can't settle long enough to say it straight.

"At the voice of the enemy — at the pressure of the wicked — they bring trouble down on me. In anger they hate me."

**"My heart writhes inside me. The terrors of death have fallen on me. Fear and trembling come on me. Horror overwhelms me."**

Four words for panic. *Writhes. Terrors. Trembling. Horror.* He's stacking them because one won't do it. This is a panic attack in the Bible. If you've had one — the heart doing something wrong, the shaking, the sense the floor's about to go — David had one too. It's in here.

And here's what he wants:

**"I said: 'If only I had wings like a dove — I'd fly away and rest. Look — I'd run far away. I'd stay in the wilderness. I'd hurry to my shelter — away from the raging wind and the storm.'"**

*Wings like a dove.* He wants *out*. Not to fight. Not to win. To *leave*. To be a bird and go somewhere nobody knows his name.

You've wanted that. The fantasy of the plane ticket, the cabin, the disappearing. David's got it too. And he says it out loud to God — which is the right place to say it.

"Confuse them, Master — divide their speech. Because I see violence and strife in the city. Day and night they go around on its walls. Harm and trouble are inside it. Destruction is in the middle of it. Oppression and deceit don't leave its streets."

The city's rotting. His *own* city. Jerusalem — and it's full of the people who turned.

Now — the wound. The reason for all of it:

**"Because it's not an enemy who insults me — I could bear that. It's not someone who hates me rising against me — I could hide from him.**
**But it's you — a man my equal — my companion — my close friend.**
**We had sweet fellowship together. We walked in the house of God with the crowd."**

*I could bear that.* If it were an enemy — fine. Enemies do enemy things. You expect it. You brace for it.

*But it's you.* The one who sat at the table. The one who gave counsel. The one who *walked into the temple beside him*. Shared worship. Shared secrets. *Sweet* fellowship — the word means intimate, close, the kind of friendship where you don't have to explain yourself.

And *that's* who went to Absalom.

There's no armor for this. You can't brace for the friend. The whole point of the friend is that you *don't* have to brace.

"Let death seize them. Let them go down to Sheol alive — because evil is in their home, in the middle of them."

Ugly. Honest. He wants them gone. The psalm lets him say it.

And then — the turn:

**"But I — I call to God. And the Source will save me."**

*But I.* The friend did what he did. *I* do this.

**"Evening, morning, and noon — I complain and moan. And He hears my voice."**

*Evening, morning, noon.* Three times a day. Not one heroic prayer — a *rhythm*. He's built a schedule of complaining to God, and God's showing up every time.

"He'll redeem my life in peace from the battle against me — because there are many against me. God will hear and answer them — the One enthroned from ancient times — because they don't change. They have no awe of God."

*They don't change.* That's the diagnosis of the betrayer. Not that he did a bad thing — that he *won't turn*. No awe, no turning.

"My companion stretched out his hand against his friends. He violated his covenant. **His mouth was smoother than butter — but war was in his heart. His words were softer than oil — but they were drawn swords.**"

*Smoother than butter. Softer than oil.* He was *kind*. That's what made it work. The betrayer isn't harsh. He's *warm*. Right up until the sword comes out.

And then — the verse. The one to memorize:

**"Cast your burden on the Source — and He will sustain you. He will never let the one who does right be shaken."**

*Cast.* The Hebrew is *hashlech* — to throw. Hurl. Not "hand over gently." *Throw it.* The weight of the betrayed friendship, the panic, the wanting-to-fly-away — *hurl it at Him.*

*He will sustain you.* The word is *kul* — to hold up, to carry, to nourish. Like a mother carrying a child. He picks up what you threw *and* He picks up *you*.

*Never be shaken.* The word again. Fifth or sixth time in the Psalms. The betrayed man — *not moved*.

**"But You, God — You'll bring them down to the pit of destruction. Men of blood and deceit won't live out half their days.**
**But I — I trust in You."**

*But I.* Second time. Same two words. *They* do what they do. *I* trust.

Peter quoted verse 22 — *cast all your anxiety on Him, because He cares for you.* He knew what David was talking about. He'd denied the closest Friend anyone ever had. He knew both sides of this psalm.

So — when it's not an enemy, when it's *you*: pace if you have to. Want the wings. Say the ugly prayer. Then — three times a day — *throw it*. And say *but I*. He carries what you throw. And He carries you.

"Sheol" v. 15 = the grave.*
