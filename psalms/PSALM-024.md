# PSALM 24 — Lift Up Your Heads, You Gates

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 24:1–10. A processional — sung when the Ark was carried up into Jerusalem.*

---

Picture a crowd carrying the Ark up the hill. This is what they sang.

**"The earth is the Source's — and everything in it. The world, and everyone who lives in it."**

Everything. Every acre, every ocean, every person. Yours, mine, the billionaire's, the dictator's — all of it His. This is the first line, and it means nobody owns anything. We're all tenants. That should humble the rich and comfort the poor in exactly equal measure.

"Because He founded it on the seas. He established it on the waters."

The ancient world thought of the deep as chaos — the thing that could swallow everything. And God set the land right on top of it. Solid ground on chaos. That's the whole planet — and it holds.

Now the question, and it's the same one as Psalm 15:

**"Who can climb the hill of the Source? Who can stand in His holy place?"**

Who gets to go up? Who gets in?

And the answer — again, not a word about ritual:

**"The one with clean hands and a pure heart. Who hasn't lifted his soul to what's false. Who hasn't sworn deceitfully."**

Clean hands — what you *do*. Pure heart — what you *want*. Both. You can have clean hands and a rotten heart (you didn't do it, but you wanted to). You can have a pure heart and dirty hands (you meant well, but look what you did). He wants both.

*Hasn't lifted his soul to what's false.* Literally: hasn't *raised* his inner self toward emptiness. Hasn't given his deepest longing to something that can't hold it. That's idolatry in one line — not bowing to a statue, but *aiming your soul* at a hollow thing.

"He'll receive blessing from the Source. Right-standing from the God who saves him."

*From the God who saves him.* Wait — if he had to have clean hands to get up the hill, why does he need saving? Because nobody's hands are clean enough on their own. The right-standing is *received*, not earned. You climb the hill; He does the cleaning.

"This is the generation of the ones who seek Him — who seek Your face, Jacob."

*Seek Your face.* Again. David's obsession. Not seeking blessings — seeking the *face*.

Now the crowd reaches the gates. And the song becomes a shout:

**"Lift up your heads, you gates! Be lifted up, you ancient doors! And the King of Glory will come in!"**

He's talking to the *gates*. Commanding the doors. *Get taller. Open wider. Somebody's coming who's too big for you.*

Then a voice from inside the walls — the gatekeeper, the skeptic, the one who's been guarding this door for a long time:

**"Who is this King of Glory?"**

And the answer comes back:

**"The Source — strong and mighty! The Source — mighty in battle!"**

Again — it's call and response, the crowd pushing forward:

**"Lift up your heads, you gates! Lift them up, you ancient doors! And the King of Glory will come in!"**

**"Who is He — this King of Glory?"**

**"The Source of Armies — He is the King of Glory!"**

That's the whole psalm. The One who owns everything, who set the earth on chaos — He wants to come *in*. Into the city. Into the temple. Into the gate that's been shut for a long time.

Your heart is a gate. It's got a keeper, and the keeper's suspicious, and he's been asking "who is this?" for years.

Here's your answer. The Source. Strong and mighty. The King of Glory.

Lift up your head. Let Him in.
