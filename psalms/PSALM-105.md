# PSALM 105 — He Remembered His Covenant

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 105:1–45. Forty-five verses of history — Abraham to the Promised Land — with one hero: God's memory. The first half of a pair; Psalm 106 tells the same story from the other side.*

---

Psalm 78 told the history as a *warning* — here's how they failed. Psalm 105 tells it as a *celebration* — here's how God *didn't*. Same events. Different hero.

**"Give thanks to the Source. Call on His name. Make known among the nations what He has done. Sing to Him. Sing praise to Him. Tell of all His wonderful acts."**

Five commands. *Thank. Call. Make known. Sing. Tell.* Then two more:

**"Glory in His holy name. Let the hearts of those who seek the Source rejoice. Look to the Source and His strength. Seek His face always."**

*Seek His face always.* David's obsession — Psalm 27. *Always.*

**"Remember the wonders He has done — His miracles — and the judgments He pronounced — you descendants of Abraham His servant — you children of Jacob, His chosen ones."**

*Remember.* You remember. And then — the whole psalm — *He* remembers:

**"He is the Source our God. His judgments are in all the earth. He remembers His covenant forever — the promise He made — for a thousand generations."**

*He remembers His covenant.* *Zachar.* Psalm 98 — *He remembered His loyal-love*. Psalm 78 — *He remembered they were flesh*. Here's the whole thing: *forever. A thousand generations.* The people forget. God's memory is the only thing that holds.

**"— the covenant He made with Abraham — the oath He swore to Isaac. He confirmed it to Jacob as a decree — to Israel as an everlasting covenant: 'To you I will give the land of Canaan — as the portion you will inherit.'"**

Abraham. Isaac. Jacob. Three generations. One promise. *The land.*

**"When they were but few in number — few indeed — and strangers in it — they wandered from nation to nation — from one kingdom to another."**

*Few. Strangers. Wandering.* That's how it started. Not an army. A *family*. Homeless.

**"He allowed no one to oppress them. For their sake He rebuked kings: 'Do not touch My anointed ones. Do My prophets no harm.'"**

*Do not touch.* Pharaoh with Sarah. Abimelech. God stepping in front of the wandering family and saying *hands off.*

Now — Joseph:

**"He called down famine on the land — and destroyed all their supplies of food. And He sent a man before them — Joseph — sold as a slave."**

*He sent a man before them.* Read that carefully. Joseph was *sold*. Betrayed by his brothers. Thrown in a pit. Sold to traders. And the psalm says — *God SENT him.* The betrayal was the *delivery method*. Genesis 50:20 — *you meant it for evil; God meant it for good.*

**"They bruised his feet with shackles. His neck was put in irons — till what he foretold came to pass — till the word of the Source proved him true."**

*Till.* Psalm 94's *till*. There's always a *till*. Joseph in chains — *till* the word proved true.

**"The king sent and released him. The ruler of peoples set him free. He made him master of his household — ruler over all he possessed — to instruct his princes as he pleased — and teach his elders wisdom."**

From the pit to the palace. The slave teaching the *elders*.

**"Then Israel entered Egypt. Jacob resided as a foreigner in the land of Ham. The Source made His people very fruitful. He made them too numerous for their foes — whose hearts He turned to hate His people — to conspire against His servants."**

*Whose hearts He turned.* Even the *hatred* — inside the plan. The oppression that came next wasn't God losing control. It was the *setup*.

**"He sent Moses His servant — and Aaron, whom He had chosen. They performed His signs among them — His wonders in the land of Ham."**

Now — the plagues. Fast:

**"He sent darkness and made the land dark — for had they not rebelled against His words? He turned their waters into blood — causing their fish to die. Their land teemed with frogs — which went up into the bedrooms of their rulers. He spoke — and there came swarms of flies — and gnats throughout their country. He turned their rain into hail — with lightning throughout their land. He struck down their vines and fig trees — and shattered the trees of their country. He spoke — and the locusts came — grasshoppers without number. They ate up every green thing in their land — ate up the produce of their soil. Then He struck down all the firstborn in their land — the firstfruits of all their manhood."**

*He spoke — and.* Twice. Frogs in the *bedrooms* of the rulers. God's not subtle when He's making a point.

**"He brought out Israel — laden with silver and gold — and from among their tribes no one faltered."**

*No one faltered.* Not one person stumbled on the way out. The slaves walked out of the superpower *carrying its treasure*.

**"Egypt was glad when they left — because dread of Israel had fallen on them."**

*Glad when they left.* The oppressor *relieved* to see them go.

**"He spread out a cloud as a covering — and a fire to give light at night. They asked — and He brought them quail. He fed them well with the bread of heaven. He opened the rock — and water gushed out. It flowed like a river in the desert."**

Cloud. Fire. Quail. Manna. Water from rock. Psalm 78 told these as *complaints*. Psalm 105 tells them as *gifts*. Same events. This time — no grumbling in the text. Just *He gave*.

And the reason for all of it:

**"Because He remembered His holy promise — given to His servant Abraham."**

*He remembered.* Second time. The plagues, the exodus, the manna — all of it because God *didn't forget* a conversation He had with a childless old man in a tent five hundred years earlier.

**"He brought out His people with rejoicing — His chosen ones with shouts of joy. He gave them the lands of the nations — and they fell heir to what others had toiled for —"**

*What others had toiled for.* They walked into houses they didn't build. Deuteronomy 6.

**"— that they might keep His precepts — and observe His laws.**
**Praise the Source!"**

*That they might keep.* The whole rescue had a *purpose*. Not just freedom *from*. Freedom *for*. And then — *hallelujah*. Second one in the Psalms.

Forty-five verses. Here's the shape: *Thank, call, tell, sing. He REMEMBERS His covenant — a thousand generations. Abraham, Isaac, Jacob — few and wandering. Hands off My anointed. Joseph — SENT by being sold. Chains till the word proved true. Egypt — frogs in the bedrooms. Out with the gold — nobody stumbled. Cloud, fire, quail, bread, water. Because He REMEMBERED Abraham. Into houses they didn't build. So they'd keep His word. Hallelujah.*

The hero of this psalm isn't Abraham or Joseph or Moses. It's God's *memory*. You'll forget. He won't. That's the covenant.
