# PSALM 59 — The Dogs Come Back at Night

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 59:1–17. Written when Saul sent men to watch David's house and kill him in the morning (1 Samuel 19).*

---

Saul's men are outside David's house. Right now. Waiting for dawn so they can kill him. His wife's going to lower him out a window to escape. This is what he prayed while they waited.

**"Rescue me from my enemies, my God. Set me high above the ones rising against me. Rescue me from the workers of harm. Save me from men of blood."**

Four verbs. *Rescue. Set high. Rescue. Save.* He's not being elegant. He's *repeating* — because that's what you do when men with swords are outside.

**"Look — they lie in wait for my life. Strong men gather against me — not for my rebellion, not for my missing-the-mark, Source. For no fault of mine they run and get ready."**

*For no fault of mine.* Like Psalm 44 — innocent. He hasn't done anything. Saul's just decided he has to die.

"Wake up! Come meet me! Look! You — Source of Armies, God of Israel — wake up to punish all the nations. Show no favor to any deceitful evildoer."

*Wake up.* Psalm 44 said it. David says it. It's *allowed*.

Now — the dogs. This is the image the psalm's built on:

**"They come back at evening. They howl like dogs. They prowl around the city."**

*They come back at evening.* Every night. The same threat, returning at the same hour. Not one attack — a *pattern*. If you've had a fear that shows up every night at the same time — the 3 a.m. dread, the evening panic — you know exactly what David means. *The dogs come back.*

"Look — they spew from their mouths. Swords are in their lips. 'Who hears?' they say."

*Who hears?* They think nobody's listening. They think the dark covers them.

**"But You, Source — You laugh at them. You mock all the nations."**

Third time we've heard God laugh — Psalm 2, Psalm 37, now here. The men with swords outside think they're terrifying. God finds them *funny*.

**"My strength — I will watch for You. Because God is my stronghold."**

*I will watch for You.* The dogs are watching *him*. He's watching for *God*. Everyone's watching — but the direction of the watching decides everything.

**"My God of loyal-love will come to meet me. God will let me look on my enemies."**

*Come to meet me.* Not "I'll find Him." *He'll come out.* Like Psalm 21 — God coming out to where you are.

"Kill them? No — my God, don't kill them — or my people will forget. Make them wander by Your power. Bring them down, Master, our shield."

Interesting. David *doesn't* want them dead — yet. He wants them *wandering*. Alive, as a lesson. *If they just die, everyone forgets. Let them stumble around so people remember what happened to the ones who hunted the innocent.*

"For the missing-the-mark of their mouth, the words of their lips — let them be caught in their pride. For the curses and lies they tell — finish them in Your anger. Finish them — until they're gone. Let people know to the ends of the earth that God rules in Jacob."

And the dogs again — second time:

**"They come back at evening. They howl like dogs. They prowl around the city. They wander looking for food. If they're not satisfied — they growl."**

Same refrain. The threat's still there. Still nightly.

But — the last two verses:

**"But I — I will sing of Your strength. In the morning I'll shout for joy in Your loyal-love. Because You've been my stronghold — my refuge in the day of my trouble."**

*In the morning.* The dogs come at *evening*. David sings in the *morning*. They own the night. He owns the dawn. And morning always comes after evening — that's the order that never changes.

**"My strength — to You I'll sing. Because God is my stronghold — my God of loyal-love."**

*My God of loyal-love.* The last words. Not "my God of vengeance." Not "my God of power." *Loyal-love.* The men outside are men of *blood*. The One inside with him is the God of *chesed*.

So — when the dogs come back at evening, same time every night: know they're watching, and watch for Him instead. Know He's laughing. And know the morning is yours. They get the dark. You get the song.
