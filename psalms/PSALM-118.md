# PSALM 118 — The Stone the Builders Rejected

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 118:1–29. The last Hallel psalm. The last song Jesus sang with His disciples before Gethsemane. Luther's favorite. Contains the verse the crowd shouted on Palm Sunday — and the one Jesus turned on the priests.*

---

This is the last thing He sang. Passover night. The cup lifted. The bread broken. And then — *this*. Twenty-nine verses. Then out the door to the garden.

**"Give thanks to the Source — because He is good. His loyal-love endures forever."**

Psalm 106, 107's refrain. And then — three groups, same words:

**"Let Israel say: 'His loyal-love endures forever.' Let the house of Aaron say: 'His loyal-love endures forever.' Let those who stand in awe of the Source say: 'His loyal-love endures forever.'"**

*Israel. Aaron. Those in awe.* Psalm 115's three. The people, the priests, and *whoever's amazed*. All saying the same four words. *Forever.*

Now — the testimony:

**"When hard pressed — I cried to the Source. He brought me into a spacious place."**

*Hard pressed.* The Hebrew is *min-ha-metzar* — from the *narrow place*. Squeezed. Walls closing in. And — *a spacious place*. Psalm 18 — *He brought me out into a wide-open space*. From the squeeze to the open.

**"The Source is with me. I will not be afraid. What can mere mortals do to me?"**

Psalm 56 — *what can flesh do?* Hebrews 13:6 quotes *this*. *The Lord is my helper — I will not fear — what can man do to me?*

**"The Source is with me. He is my helper. I look in triumph on my enemies."**

**"It is better to take refuge in the Source — than to trust in humans. It is better to take refuge in the Source — than to trust in princes."**

*Better.* Twice. Psalm 84 — *better is one day*. Psalm 118 — *better to trust Him than the powerful*. Because Psalm 82's princes fail. He doesn't.

Now — the battle. And it's *surrounded*:

**"All the nations surrounded me — but in the name of the Source I cut them down. They surrounded me on every side — but in the name of the Source I cut them down. They swarmed around me like bees — but they were consumed as quickly as burning thorns. In the name of the Source I cut them down."**

*Surrounded.* Three times. *Every side. Like bees.* And — *in the name of the Source* — three times. He didn't fight in his own name. He fought in *the Name*. And the swarm — *gone like burning thorns*. Psalm 58 — before the pot's warm.

**"I was pushed back and about to fall — but the Source helped me."**

*About to fall.* Psalm 73 — *my feet almost slipped*. Psalm 94 — *my foot is slipping*. The edge. And — *helped*.

**"The Source is my strength and my defense. He has become my salvation."**

Exodus 15:2. Moses' song at the Red Sea. *The Lord is my strength and my song; He has become my salvation.* The Passover table singing the *first* Passover's song.

**"Shouts of joy and victory resound in the tents of the righteous: 'The Source's right hand has done mighty things! The Source's right hand is lifted high! The Source's right hand has done mighty things!'"**

*Right hand.* Three times. Psalm 110 — *sit at My right hand*. Here — the *right hand did it*.

And then — the verse. The one that should make you stop:

**"I will not die — but live — and will proclaim what the Source has done."**

*I will not die but live.* Sung by Jesus. The night before He died. Read it again knowing that. *I will not die — but LIVE.* He knew what was coming. He sang it anyway. Because He knew what was *after*.

**"The Source has chastened me severely — but He has not given me over to death."**

*Chastened severely.* The Hebrew is *yasor yisrani* — disciplined, disciplined me. Doubled. Hard correction. *But not given over to death.* Psalm 30 — *You kept me from going down to the pit*.

Now — the gates. Psalm 24's gates:

**"Open for me the gates of righteousness. I will enter and give thanks to the Source."**

*Open for me.* He's outside. Asking in.

**"This is the gate of the Source — through which the righteous may enter."**

*The gate of the Source.* One gate. John 10 — *I am the gate*. Jesus sang about the gate — and then *became* it.

**"I will give You thanks — because You answered me. You have become my salvation."**

And now — the verse Jesus turned on the priests. Matthew 21:42. Right after the parable of the vineyard — the tenants who killed the owner's son. He looked at them and said: *have you never read —*

**"The stone the builders rejected — has become the cornerstone."**

*The builders rejected it.* The experts. The ones whose *job* was to know good stone. They looked at it and said *no. Wrong shape. Doesn't fit. Throw it out.*

*And it became the cornerstone.* The stone the *whole building* rests on. The one that sets every angle. The one you *can't* build without.

Peter preached it — Acts 4:11. *This is the stone you builders rejected — which has become the cornerstone.* To the *same priests*. Who'd done exactly that. Paul — Ephesians 2:20. Peter again — 1 Peter 2:7. The early church couldn't stop quoting it. Because it's the *whole gospel* in one image: *the One you threw out is the One holding everything up.*

If you've been rejected by the builders — the experts, the gatekeepers, the ones who decide who fits — this verse. *Rejected* isn't the last word. *Cornerstone* is.

**"The Source has done this — and it is marvelous in our eyes."**

*The Source did it.* Not the builders. They rejected. *He* placed.

**"This is the day the Source has made. Let us rejoice and be glad in it."**

*The day the Source has made.* You've heard this as a morning motivational. It's not. In context — it's *the day the rejected stone became the cornerstone*. The day of reversal. The day the builders were wrong. *That* day. Rejoice in *that*.

And then — Palm Sunday:

**"Source — save us! Source — grant us success!"**

*Save us.* The Hebrew is *hoshi'ah na* — *Hosanna*. The crowd shouted it as Jesus rode in. Matthew 21:9. They were quoting *this verse*. *Hosanna! Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord!* Straight from Psalm 118.

**"Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Source. From the house of the Source we bless you."**

*Blessed is he who comes.* The Palm Sunday line. And — Luke 13:35 — Jesus told Jerusalem: *you will not see Me again until you say: Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord.* He was waiting for them to sing *Psalm 118*. And five days before the cross — they did.

**"The Source is God — and He has made His light shine on us. With boughs in hand — join in the festal procession — up to the horns of the altar."**

*Boughs in hand.* Palm branches. The Hebrew is *isru-chag ba-avotim* — bind the festival with branches. They *literally* waved branches singing this. Palm Sunday was *Psalm 118 enacted*.

*Up to the altar.* The procession ends at the place of sacrifice.

**"You are my God — and I will praise You. You are my God — and I will exalt You."**

*My God.* Twice. Personal.

**"Give thanks to the Source — because He is good. His loyal-love endures forever."**

Bookend. The refrain. The last line of the last song Jesus sang.

Twenty-nine verses. Here's the shape: *He's good — loyal-love forever — Israel, Aaron, everyone in awe, say it. From the narrow place to the open. Better than princes. Surrounded three times — cut them down in the Name. About to fall — helped. I WILL NOT DIE BUT LIVE. Open the gates. The stone the builders REJECTED — cornerstone. This is THE day. Hosanna. Blessed is He who comes. Branches. Altar. He's good. Forever.*

Then He stood up. Walked out. Crossed the Kidron. Into the garden. Where the builders' servants came with torches.

*The stone the builders rejected.* Three days later — *cornerstone*.

Sing it. Then walk out.
