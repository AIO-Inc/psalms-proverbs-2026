# PSALM 125 — Like Mount Zion

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 125:1–5. Sixth Song of Ascent. Five verses. Two images of what a trusting person is like — a mountain and a city — and a warning about the crooked road.*

---

Psalm 124 — *we got out of the net*. Psalm 125 — *now what are we like?*

**"Those who trust in the Source are like Mount Zion — which cannot be shaken — but endures forever."**

*Like Mount Zion.* The pilgrims are *looking at it*. They've climbed the road — Psalm 122, feet in the gate — and there's the hill. And the psalm says: *that's you.*

*Cannot be shaken.* *Lo-yimot.* The word. Psalm 15, 16, 21, 30, 46, 55, 62, 112. Nine times now. *Not moved.* And the *image* for it — a *mountain*. Not a wall — walls fall. Not a tower — towers get knocked down. A *mountain*. You don't push a mountain over. It was there before you and it'll be there after.

*But endures forever.* The Hebrew is *l'olam yeshev* — *forever it sits*. Psalm 102 — *You sit enthroned forever*. The mountain *sits* the way God sits. Settled. Not going anywhere.

That's what trust does to a person. Not "makes them feel calm." Makes them *geologically stable*.

**"As the mountains surround Jerusalem — so the Source surrounds His people — both now and forevermore."**

*As the mountains surround Jerusalem.* Second image. Jerusalem's in a *bowl*. Hills on every side — Olives to the east, Scopus to the north, the ridges to the south and west. You stand in the city and look *up* in every direction. *Ringed.*

*So the Source surrounds His people.* Psalm 32 — *You surround me with shouts of rescue.* Psalm 34 — *the angel camps around.* Psalm 125 — *surrounded like Jerusalem is surrounded.* You can't approach the city without crossing a hill. You can't approach *them* without crossing *Him*.

So — two images. *You're the mountain that can't be moved. And you're the city the mountains protect.* Both. Stable *and* surrounded.

Now — the warning:

**"The scepter of the wicked will not remain over the land allotted to the righteous."**

*Will not remain.* The Hebrew is *lo yaniach* — *will not rest*. The oppressor's rod is *over* the land right now — the psalm doesn't deny it. But it won't *stay*. Psalm 37 — *a little while, and the wicked are gone*. Psalm 125 — the scepter *lifts*.

And here's the reason the warning matters:

**"— otherwise the righteous might use their hands to do evil."**

Read that carefully. *If the oppression went on forever — the righteous might give up and go crooked.* God *ends* the wicked's rule — not just for justice — but *so the good people don't break*. He knows how long a person can hold out. He's not going to test it past the limit. 1 Corinthians 10:13 — *He won't let you be tested beyond what you can bear.*

**"Do good, Source, to those who are good — to those who are upright in heart."**

**"But those who turn to crooked ways — the Source will banish."**

*Crooked ways.* The Hebrew is *akalkalot* — *twisting, winding*. Psalm 18 — *to the crooked You show Yourself shrewd*. The road that *bends*. Not the sinner who slipped — Psalm 119's lost sheep. The one who *chose the winding road* and kept walking it.

*Banish.* The Hebrew is *yolichem* — *lead them away*. Same verb as *lead* — Psalm 23, *He leads me*. The straight get *led home*. The crooked get *led off*. Same shepherd. Different destination — because of the *road they picked*.

And the last line — the blessing:

**"Peace be on Israel."**

*Shalom al-Yisrael.* Psalm 122 — *pray for the peace of Jerusalem*. Psalm 125 — *peace on the people*. The Ascents keep landing on *shalom*. Because that's what the road's *for*.

Five verses. Here's the shape: *Trust — and you're Mount Zion. Can't be moved. Forever. He surrounds you like the hills surround the city. The wicked's rod won't stay — because He knows how long you can hold. Do good to the good. The ones who chose the crooked road — He leads them off. Peace on Israel.*

Here's what to take: you're standing at the bottom of a hill that's been there for four thousand years — and the psalm says *that's what trust makes you*. Not soft. Not fragile. A *mountain*. And *around* you — more mountains.

You can't be moved. And you can't be reached without crossing Him.
