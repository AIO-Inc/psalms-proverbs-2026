# PSALM 78 — So the Next Generation Would Know

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 78:1–72. Asaph. Seventy-two verses of Israel's history — told as a warning, and as a love story that wouldn't quit.*

---

This is the longest history lesson in the Psalms. Asaph sits the kids down and tells them everything — the good, the bad, the *repeatedly* bad — and why it matters that they hear it.

**"My people — hear my teaching. Listen to the words of my mouth. I'll open my mouth with a parable. I'll utter hidden things — things from long ago."**

*A parable.* He's calling history a *parable*. Because the events aren't just events — they *mean* something. And you have to be told what.

**"Things we've heard and known — things our fathers told us. We won't hide them from their descendants. We'll tell the next generation the praiseworthy deeds of the Source — His power and the wonders He's done."**

*We won't hide them.* The whole psalm is one long refusal to let the story die.

**"He decreed statutes for Jacob — established the law in Israel — which He commanded our fathers to teach their children — so the next generation would know them — even the children yet to be born — and they in turn would tell their children."**

Four generations in one sentence. *Fathers → children → next generation → children yet to be born → THEIR children.* That's the chain. Psalm 71's old man, Psalm 48's *walk around the city* — this is the *why*. If one link breaks, the whole thing goes dark.

**"Then they would put their trust in God — and not forget His deeds — but keep His commands. They would not be like their fathers — a stubborn and rebellious generation — whose hearts were not loyal to God — whose spirits were not faithful to Him."**

*Not be like their fathers.* He's about to tell the kids exactly how their ancestors blew it — so they *don't*.

Here's the first failure:

**"The men of Ephraim — though armed with bows — turned back on the day of battle. They didn't keep God's covenant. They refused to live by His law. They forgot what He'd done — the wonders He'd shown them."**

*Forgot.* There's the word. Psalm 9, Psalm 10, Psalm 50 — *forgetting* is the root sin. Not rejecting. *Forgetting.* And Ephraim — the biggest tribe — forgot with *bows in their hands*.

Now — the Exodus, compressed:

**"He did miracles in the sight of their fathers — in the land of Egypt. He split the sea and led them through. He made the water stand up like a wall. He guided them with a cloud by day — and with light from a fire all night. He split rocks in the wilderness — gave them water as abundant as the seas. He brought streams out of a rocky crag — made water flow down like rivers."**

Sea split. Water standing up. Cloud. Fire. Rock cracked open and *rivers* pouring out. That's what they saw. *With their eyes.*

**"But they continued to sin against Him — rebelling in the wilderness against the Most High. They willfully put God to the test — demanding the food they craved."**

*The food they craved.* They had bread from heaven — *manna* — and they wanted *meat*. Because manna was boring. That's the rebellion: *God, what You're giving isn't what I WANT.*

**"They spoke against God: 'Can God really spread a table in the wilderness? True — He struck the rock and water gushed out — but can He also give us bread? Can He supply meat for His people?'"**

*True — He did that. But can He do THIS?* Every miracle raised the bar for the next one. They watched the sea split and wondered if He could handle *lunch*.

**"When the Source heard them — He was furious. His fire broke out against Jacob. His wrath rose against Israel — because they didn't believe in God or trust in His rescue."**

*Didn't believe.* After the sea. After the rock. That's what unbelief actually is — not "I've never seen evidence." *I've seen it and I still don't trust You for the next thing.*

**"Yet He gave a command to the skies above — opened the doors of the heavens. He rained down manna for the people to eat. He gave them the grain of heaven. Men ate the bread of angels. He sent them all the food they could eat."**

*Yet.* He gave it anyway. *Bread of angels.* Complaining people, eating angel food.

**"He let loose the east wind from the heavens — led out the south wind by His power. He rained meat down on them like dust — flying birds like sand on the seashore. He made them come down inside their camp — all around their tents. They ate till they were gorged — He'd given them what they craved."**

*Gorged.* He gave them the meat. So much they were sick of it. Because sometimes the answer to *I want what I want* is — *here. All of it.* And then you find out wanting it was the problem.

**"But before they turned from the food they craved — even while it was still in their mouths — God's anger rose against them. He put to death the sturdiest among them — cutting down the young men of Israel."**

*Still in their mouths.* Judgment with the food half-chewed.

**"In spite of all this — they kept on sinning. In spite of His wonders — they didn't believe. So He ended their days in futility — their years in terror."**

*In spite of.* Twice. The wonders didn't work. That's the horrible truth of this section — *miracles don't produce faith*. They watched the impossible for forty years and died unbelieving.

Now — the cycle. And it's the shape of every human life:

**"Whenever God slew them — they would seek Him. They eagerly turned to Him again. They remembered that God was their Rock — that God Most High was their Redeemer."**

*Whenever He slew them — they sought Him.* Pain made them look up. Every time.

**"But then they would flatter Him with their mouths — lying to Him with their tongues. Their hearts were not loyal to Him. They were not faithful to His covenant."**

*Flatter Him.* They said the right things — *when it hurt*. Then it stopped hurting and so did the saying.

And here's the verse — the one that makes the whole seventy-two verses worth reading:

**"Yet He was merciful. He forgave their wrongdoing and didn't destroy them. Time after time He held back His anger — and didn't stir up His full wrath.**
**He remembered that they were only flesh — a passing breeze that doesn't return."**

*He remembered they were only flesh.* The people who *forgot* Him — He *remembered* what they were. Dust. Breath. A breeze that goes by once. And He *held back*. Time after time.

That's the psalm in one line. They forgot Him. He remembered them.

**"How often they rebelled against Him in the wilderness — grieved Him in the wasteland! Again and again they put God to the test. They vexed the Holy One of Israel. They didn't remember His power — the day He redeemed them from the oppressor."**

Then — the plagues, retold: water to blood, flies, frogs, locusts, hail, livestock, *the firstborn of Egypt*. All of it. So the kids know what God did to get their great-grandparents out.

**"But He brought His people out like a flock. He led them like sheep through the wilderness. He guided them safely — so they were unafraid. But the sea engulfed their enemies. And so He brought them to the border of His holy land — to the hill country His right hand had taken. He drove out nations before them — allotted their lands as an inheritance. He settled the tribes of Israel in their homes."**

*Like a flock.* Psalm 77's ending. Psalm 23. He *shepherded* them into the land.

**"But they put God to the test — rebelled against the Most High. They didn't keep His statutes. Like their fathers — they were disloyal and faithless — as unreliable as a faulty bow."**

*A faulty bow.* You pull it back, aim, release — and the arrow goes somewhere else. Not because you aimed wrong. Because the *bow's* wrong. That's Israel — pointed at God, firing off in every other direction.

**"They angered Him with their high places. They aroused His jealousy with their idols. When God heard them — He was furious. He rejected Israel completely."**

And then — the unthinkable:

**"He abandoned the tabernacle of Shiloh — the tent He'd set up among humans. He sent the Ark of His might into captivity — His splendor into the hands of the enemy. He gave His people over to the sword. He was furious with His inheritance."**

*He sent the Ark into captivity.* 1 Samuel 4 — the Philistines took it. God let the box He lived in get *carried off*. Because the people had made it a good-luck charm and forgotten Who was in it.

**"Fire consumed their young men. Their young women had no wedding songs. Their priests were put to the sword. Their widows could not weep."**

*Could not weep.* Too many dead to mourn.

Now — the turn. And it's a strange image:

**"Then the Master awoke — as from sleep — as a warrior wakes from the stupor of wine. He beat back His enemies. He put them to everlasting shame."**

*As from sleep.* Psalm 44 — *wake up!* Here He *does*. Like a man who was out cold and suddenly sits up swinging.

**"Then He rejected the tents of Joseph. He did not choose the tribe of Ephraim."**

Ephraim — the big tribe, the one that turned back on battle day with bows in hand — *not chosen*.

**"But He chose the tribe of Judah — Mount Zion, which He loved. He built His sanctuary like the heights — like the earth He established forever."**

*Which He loved.* Not the biggest. Not the strongest. *Loved.* Psalm 68 — *this is the mountain God desired.*

And the ending — after seventy-one verses of failure and mercy — it comes down to one man:

**"He chose David His servant — and took him from the sheep pens. From tending the sheep He brought him — to be the shepherd of His people Jacob — of Israel His inheritance.**
**And David shepherded them with integrity of heart. With skillful hands he led them."**

*From the sheep pens.* The whole history — sea, manna, quail, plagues, Shiloh, Ark, fire — ends with a *kid in a field with sheep*. God went and got a shepherd to shepherd the people who kept wandering off.

And behind David — the Shepherd who'd say *I am the good shepherd.* The psalm's pointing further than it knows.

Seventy-two verses. Here's the shape: *Tell the kids everything. Their ancestors saw the sea split and asked "but can He do lunch?" They forgot Him — He remembered they were dust. They fired like a faulty bow. He let the Ark get taken. Then He woke up. He chose the small tribe He loved. And He picked a shepherd boy.*

Don't hide it from the next generation. All of it. The forgetting *and* the remembering. Especially the remembering.
