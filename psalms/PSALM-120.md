# PSALM 120 — I Am for Peace

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 120:1–7. The first of fifteen Songs of Ascent (120–134) — pilgrim songs sung on the road up to Jerusalem. Seven verses. It starts far from home, among people who love a fight.*

---

Fifteen psalms. One road. Up to Jerusalem — you always go *up*, it's on a hill — for the festivals. These are the songs the pilgrims sang walking. Short. Memorable. Built for feet.

And the first one starts *far away*. In the wrong place.

**"I call on the Source in my distress — and He answers me."**

*He answers.* Present tense. Not "answered once." *Answers.* It's how the road starts — with a God who picks up.

**"Save me, Source — from lying lips and deceitful tongues."**

*Lying lips.* Psalm 12, 52, 55, 101, 109 — the tongue again. The pilgrim's first problem isn't distance. It's *the people he's been living with*. Liars.

**"What will He do to you — and what more besides — you deceitful tongue?"**

He's talking to the *tongue*. Not the person — the *tongue*. Like it's a separate thing. Because it *is* — the thing that talks isn't always the thing that means.

**"He will punish you with a warrior's sharp arrows — with burning coals of the broom bush."**

*Broom bush coals.* The hottest-burning wood in the desert. Psalm 140 uses it too. The tongue that burned others — *burned*.

Now — where he *is*. And it's the reason for the whole journey:

**"Woe to me — that I dwell in Meshek — that I live among the tents of Kedar!"**

*Meshek. Kedar.* Two places nobody at the Passover table had been. Meshek — far north, near the Black Sea. Kedar — Arabian desert tribes. *Opposite directions.* He can't literally be in both. So — they're *code*. For *far*. For *foreign*. For *not home*. For living among people whose ways aren't yours.

If you've been the believer in a room full of people who aren't — the sober one at the party, the honest one in the office, the one who doesn't laugh at the joke — you've lived in Meshek. You know the tents of Kedar.

**"Too long have I lived among those who hate peace."**

*Too long.* He's been *counting*. And the number's gone past what he can take.

*Those who hate peace.* Not "disagree with." *Hate.* People who *want* the conflict. Who feed on it. Who'd rather have the fight than the fix.

And the last verse — the reason he's packing his bag:

**"I am for peace — but when I speak — they are for war."**

*I am for peace.* The Hebrew is stark — *ani shalom*. Literally: *I — peace.* Not "I want peace." *I AM peace.* Psalm 109 — *I am prayer*. Psalm 120 — *I am peace.* It's what he *is*. Not a position. An *identity*.

*When I speak — they are for war.* He opens his mouth — and the room goes hostile. Not because of what he said. Because of *what he is*. Peace walks into a room that hates peace — and the room *attacks*.

That's the sign it's time to go up. Not because you've failed. Because you've *stayed too long in the wrong tent*.

Seven verses. Here's the shape: *I call — He answers. Save me from the liars. Tongue — you'll burn. I've been living in Meshek — in Kedar — far from home — too long — among people who want the fight. I AM peace. They're for war.*

That's Song One. You're not in Jerusalem. You're in the tents of Kedar. And the first step of the fifteen is *naming where you are* — and deciding you've been there long enough.

Fourteen more songs. Each one a step closer.

*Note on translation: Songs of Ascent —* shir ha-ma'alot *— Psalms 120–134; sung by pilgrims ascending to Jerusalem for the three festivals, and possibly on the fifteen steps of the Temple (Mishnah Sukkah 5:4). v. 1 —* va-ya'aneni *= "and He answers me." v. 4 —* gachalei r'tamim *= coals of broom (Retama raetam — burns hottest). v. 5 —* Meshech *= far north (Gen 10:2);* Kedar *= Arabian nomads (Gen 25:13) — geographically incompatible; symbolic of alienation among hostile foreigners. v. 6 —* rabbat *= too long/much. v. 7 —* ani-shalom *= "I [am] peace" — verbless, identity statement. No* yirah*.*
