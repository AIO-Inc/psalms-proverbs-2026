# PSALM 27 — One Thing

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 27:1–14.*

---

**"The Source is my light and my rescue — who will I fear? The Source is the stronghold of my life — who will I dread?"**

Two questions. Both rhetorical. He's not asking for a list. He's saying the list is empty.

*Light* — so I can see what's actually there instead of what the dark is making up. *Rescue* — so when what's there is bad, I've got an exit. *Stronghold* — so while I'm waiting for the exit, I've got walls.

"When the harmful ones came at me to eat my flesh — my enemies and my foes — *they* stumbled and fell."

They came to eat him. And they tripped. Not because David swung. Because the ground under people who hunt the innocent has a way of giving out.

**"Even if an army camps against me, my heart won't dread. Even if war rises against me — even then I'm confident."**

*Even then.* He's not saying the army won't come. He's saying the army coming doesn't change the math.

Now the center of the psalm. The line that makes it different from every other lament in the book:

**"One thing I've asked of the Source. This one thing I'll seek after: to dwell in the house of the Source all the days of my life. To gaze on the beauty of the Source. To inquire in His temple."**

*One thing.* Not safety. Not victory. Not the army going away. *To be where He is — and to look at Him.*

*Gaze on the beauty.* Not study. Not analyze. *Gaze.* The way you look at a sunset, a face you love, the ocean. He wants to be in the room with God and just — *look*.

Most people's "one thing" is a problem they want solved. David's "one thing" is a Person he wants to see. When that's your one thing, the army camping outside becomes background noise.

"Because in the day of trouble He'll hide me in His shelter. He'll conceal me in the secret of His tent. He'll set me high on a rock."

*The secret of His tent.* The inner room. The place the stranger doesn't get to. You're not hiding in the hallway — you're behind the curtain.

"Now my head is lifted up above my enemies all around. I'll sacrifice in His tent with shouts of joy. I'll sing — I'll make music to the Source."

Then — the mood shifts. The confident man gets quiet:

"Hear my voice when I call, Source. Be gracious to me. Answer me."

**"My heart said of You: 'Seek My face.' Your face, Source, I will seek."**

Listen to how that works. God speaks *inside* David's heart — *seek My face*. And David answers *out loud* — *I will*. It's a conversation. The desire to look for God is God, first, calling you to look.

"Don't hide Your face from me. Don't turn Your servant away in anger. You've been my help. Don't leave me. Don't abandon me, God of my rescue."

And then the line that stops people cold:

**"Even if my father and mother abandon me — the Source will gather me in."**

*Gather me in.* The word for taking in a stray. Pulling a child into your arms. The deepest human bond — parents — can fail. And even then there's Someone who picks you up.

If your parents failed you — and for a lot of people reading this, they did — this is the verse. He wrote it for you.

"Teach me Your way, Source. Lead me on a level path — because of the ones watching me. Don't hand me over to my enemies' desire. False witnesses have risen against me. They're breathing violence."

Then — David talks to *himself*. Says the thing he needs to hear:

**"If I hadn't believed I'd see the goodness of the Source in the land of the living —"**

He doesn't finish the sentence. He can't. The alternative is unthinkable. *If I hadn't believed that — I don't know what I'd have done.*

*In the land of the living.* Not just in heaven. *Here.* He expects to see God's goodness on this side of the grave. That expectation is what kept him standing.

And the last line — the instruction:

**"Wait for the Source. Be strong. Let your heart be courageous. Wait for the Source."**

*Wait* — twice. Bookending *be strong, be courageous*. Because waiting isn't passive. Waiting is the *hardest* strength there is. Anybody can act. Standing still while the army camps outside, refusing to run, refusing to panic, eyes on the one thing — *that's* courage.

Light. Rescue. Stronghold. One thing. Gather me in. Wait.

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* mimi ira *= "whom shall I fear" and* mimi efchad *= "whom shall I dread" — both ordinary human fear words (*yare* in its plain sense,* pachad*), correctly rendered as fear/dread. NOT* yirah *of God. v. 4 —* achat *= one thing;* lachazot b'noam *= to gaze on the beauty/pleasantness. v. 10 —* ya'asfeni *= gather me in, take me up. v. 14 —* kaveh *= wait, hope, twist together like a rope — expectant waiting.*
