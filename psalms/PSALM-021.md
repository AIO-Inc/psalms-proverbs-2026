# PSALM 21 — You Gave Him What He Asked

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 21:1–13. The answer to Psalm 20 — the king came back.*

---

Psalm 20 was the blessing before the battle. This is the party after.

"Source, the king celebrates in Your strength. How he rejoices in Your rescue!"

*Your* strength. Not his. He's back, he won, and the first thing out of his mouth is *it wasn't me.*

**"You gave him the desire of his heart. You didn't withhold the request of his lips."**

Remember Psalm 20:4 — *may He give you the desire of your heart*? Here's the receipt. They asked. He gave. Prayer isn't a lottery ticket — sometimes you get to stand up and say *He did the exact thing we asked for.*

Say that out loud when it happens. Don't let answered prayers go unannounced.

"You came to meet him with good blessings. You set a crown of pure gold on his head."

*Came to meet him.* God didn't wait at the finish line. He came *out* to where the king was.

"He asked You for life — You gave it. Length of days, forever and ever."

*Forever.* Bigger than the king asked for. He wanted to survive the battle. He got a dynasty that runs to the Messiah. God rounds *up*.

"His glory is great in Your rescue. You've laid honor and majesty on him. You've made him blessed forever. You've made him glad with the joy of Your face."

*The joy of Your face.* Same thing David keeps circling back to — Psalm 4, Psalm 16, Psalm 17. Not the crown. Not the victory. The *face*. That's what made him glad.

**"Because the king trusts in the Source — through the loyal-love of the Most High, he will not be shaken."**

Not shaken. Fourth time we've hit that word. It's becoming the theme of the first book of Psalms: the people who trust don't get knocked down.

Now the warrior half — this is the part about the enemies:

"Your hand will find all Your enemies. Your right hand will find the ones who hate You. You'll make them like a furnace when You show Your face. The Source will swallow them in His anger. Fire will consume them. You'll destroy their offspring from the earth, their descendants from among the children of Adam."

It's fierce. It's a war song, and war songs don't apologize. But notice — these are *God's* enemies, the ones who hate *Him*. And the reason:

"Because they intended evil against You. They devised a scheme they couldn't pull off."

*Couldn't pull off.* The plan against God always fails. Always. The enemies in this psalm aren't people who disagree — they're people who set out to destroy what God was building, and found out you can't.

"You'll make them turn their backs. You'll aim Your bow at their faces."

And then the close — the same shout as Psalm 20:

**"Be exalted, Source, in Your strength! We will sing and praise Your power."**

Here's how to use this psalm: when the prayer gets answered — when the thing you begged for in Psalm 20 actually happens — *come back and say so*. Don't pocket the win and move on. Stand up. Say "You gave him what he asked." The people who were praying with you need to hear that it worked.

Every Psalm 20 deserves a Psalm 21.
