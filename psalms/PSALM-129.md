# PSALM 129 — Plowers Plowed My Back

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 129:1–8. Tenth Song of Ascent. Eight verses. Israel's history as a body that's been beaten — and survived. And grass on a roof.*

---

Psalm 128 was the table. Psalm 129 is the *scars* on the people sitting at it.

**"They have greatly oppressed me from my youth — let Israel say —"**

*From my youth.* Israel speaking as one *person*. Psalm 88 — *from my youth I have suffered*. Here it's the *nation* — Egypt was the *childhood*. Slavery was the *first memory*.

*Let Israel say.* Psalm 124's call-and-response. The leader says it. The crowd says it back. Because *every* generation walking up this hill has a version.

**"— they have greatly oppressed me from my youth — but they have not gained the victory over me."**

*Have NOT.* The Hebrew is *gam lo-yachlu li* — *yet they have not prevailed against me*. That's the whole psalm. Beaten — *not beaten*. Egypt, Philistia, Assyria, Babylon. Every one of them — *gone*. Israel — *still walking up the hill*.

And then — the image. It's brutal:

**"Plowers have plowed my back — and made their furrows long."**

*Plowed my back.*

Picture it. A man face-down. And someone drags a *plow* across his back — the iron blade cutting *rows*. Long ones. The Hebrew is *he'erichu l'ma'anitam* — *they lengthened their furrows*. They took their time. They went the whole length.

That's the whipping post. That's the slave's back. That's every stripe on Israel's history — Egypt's overseers, Babylon's soldiers, every occupier who *cut* them. The nation as a *body* — with a back that's been *farmed*.

Isaiah 50:6 — *I offered My back to those who beat Me.* Isaiah 53:5 — *by His wounds we are healed.* The Servant's back — plowed. Jesus at the pillar — John 19:1. The furrows were *long*.

**"But the Source is righteous. He has cut me free from the cords of the wicked."**

*Cut me free.* The Hebrew is *kitzetz* — *severed*. The ropes that held him to the post — *cut*. Psalm 116 — *You freed me from my chains*. Psalm 124 — *the snare is broken*. The plowing happened. The ropes are *gone*.

Now — the other side. What happens to the plowers:

**"May all who hate Zion be turned back in shame."**

**"May they be like grass on the roof — which withers before it can grow —"**

*Grass on the roof.* Ancient roofs were flat — mud and thatch. Seeds blew up there. Grass sprouted. And it looked *fine* — green, growing — for about a week. No depth. No root. The first hot day — *gone*. Psalm 1 — *like chaff*. Psalm 37 — *like grass they wither*. Psalm 129 — *roof grass*. The most rootless thing there is. It's not even *in* the ground.

That's the oppressor. He looks like he's *growing*. He's on the *roof* — above everyone. And there's *nothing under him*.

**"— with it the reaper cannot fill his hands — nor the one who gathers fill his arms."**

*Can't fill his hands.* You go up to harvest the roof grass and there's — nothing. A few blades. Not enough for a handful. Not enough for an armful. Psalm 126's *sheaves* — the righteous come back with *arms full*. The wicked's harvest — *can't fill a hand*.

**"May those who pass by not say to them: 'The blessing of the Source be on you!'"**

In harvest season, people walking past a field would call out the blessing — Ruth 2:4 — *the Source be with you!* — and the reapers would call back. It was the sound of a *good harvest*. And the psalm says — for the roof grass — *nobody says it*. No one walking by sees anything worth blessing.

And then — the last line. Which is *odd*. Because after all that — the psalm ends by saying the blessing *anyway*:

**"We bless you in the name of the Source."**

*We bless you.* Who's *you*? Some read it as the pilgrims blessing *each other* — the reapers in the real field, not the roof. Some read it as a *closing benediction* to whoever's listening. Either way — the psalm about a plowed back and rootless enemies *ends with a blessing*. Because that's what the beaten people *do*. They got plowed. They got cut free. And they *bless*.

Eight verses. Here's the shape: *Oppressed since childhood — say it back — but they didn't WIN. Plowers plowed my back — long furrows. He cut the ropes. The ones who hate Zion — roof grass. Looks green. No root. Withers in a week. Can't fill a hand. Nobody blesses that harvest. — We bless you in the name of the Source.*

Here's what to take: your back's got furrows. Somebody plowed it. Maybe more than one somebody. And you're *still walking*. The ones who did it? Check their roots. They're on the roof. Give it a week.

And then — do the strange thing. *Bless.* In the name of the Source. That's how the plowed people end their psalm.
