# PSALM 122 — Our Feet Are Standing in Your Gates

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 122:1–9. Third Song of Ascent. David. The pilgrim arrives. Nine verses about a city — and a command to pray for its peace.*

---

Psalm 120 — *I'm far away.* Psalm 121 — *I'm walking.* Psalm 122 — *I'm HERE.*

**"I rejoiced with those who said to me: 'Let us go to the house of the Source.'"**

*I rejoiced.* At the *invitation*. Not at arriving — at being *asked*. Somebody said *let's go* — and his heart jumped. That's the beginning of every pilgrimage. Someone says *come with me* and you say *yes*.

**"Our feet are standing in your gates, Jerusalem."**

*Standing.* Present tense. He's *in the gate*. Psalm 24 — *lift up your heads, you gates*. Psalm 84 — *I'd rather be a doorkeeper*. Psalm 87 — *He loves the gates of Zion*. Psalm 100 — *enter His gates with thanksgiving*. And now — *feet in the gate*. Stopped. Looking around. Taking it in.

If you've ever arrived somewhere you'd wanted to be for years — and just *stood* there for a second — that's this verse.

**"Jerusalem is built like a city that is closely compacted together."**

*Closely compacted.* The Hebrew is *chub'rah-lah yachdav* — *joined together as one*. Tight. The buildings touching. No gaps. That's what he sees first — not the temple. The *unity* of the place. Everything *fitted*.

**"That is where the tribes go up — the tribes of the Source — to praise the name of the Source according to the statute given to Israel."**

*The tribes go up.* All of them. Twelve tribes — one destination. The thing that scattered across the land *converges* here. Three times a year — *everybody*.

**"There stand the thrones for judgment — the thrones of the house of David."**

*Thrones for judgment.* The courts. Psalm 82's failed judges — *here's* where the real ones sit. Jerusalem's not just worship. It's *justice*. The two are in the same city.

Now — the turn. From describing to *praying*:

**"Pray for the peace of Jerusalem: 'May those who love you be secure. May there be peace within your walls — and security within your citadels.'"**

*Pray for the peace.* *Sha'alu sh'lom Y'rushalayim.* Say it in Hebrew and hear it — *sha'alu SHALOM Y'ruSHALAyim*. The city's *name* has *shalom* in it. *Yeru-shalem* — "foundation of peace." He's asking for the city to *be what it's called*.

*Those who love you be secure.* The Hebrew is *yishlayu* — be at ease, prosper, be *tranquil*. Not just protected — *at ease*.

**"For the sake of my family and friends — I will say: 'Peace be within you.'"**

*For my family and friends.* He's not praying for Jerusalem as an abstraction. He's praying for it because *people he loves are in it*. His brothers. His companions. The peace of the city is the peace of *their* homes.

*Peace be within you.* Talking to the city. Like it's a person. Psalm 114 interviewed the sea. Psalm 122 blesses a *town*.

**"For the sake of the house of the Source our God — I will seek your prosperity."**

*I will seek your good.* The Hebrew is *avakshah tov lach*. Active. Not just wishing. *Seeking.* Working for it. Because the *house of God* is in it.

Nine verses. Here's the shape: *I rejoiced when they said "let's go." Our feet are STANDING in your gates. It's all joined together. Everyone comes up here. The courts are here. — Pray for the peace of the city whose name means peace. For the sake of my people in it. For the sake of His house in it. I'll seek your good.*

Here's what to do with Psalm 122: you've got a Jerusalem. A place that holds the people you love and the God you worship. Your church. Your town. Your family's kitchen. *Pray for its peace.* Out loud. By name. *Peace be within you.* Then — *seek its good*. Don't just wish. *Work.*

And when your feet are standing in the gate — stop. Take it in. You wanted this for years.
