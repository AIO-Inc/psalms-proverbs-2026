# PSALM 44 — Wake Up! Why Are You Sleeping?

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 44:1–26. A national lament — the whole people lost a battle they did nothing to deserve losing. Sons of Korah.*

---

This is the psalm for when you did everything right and still lost.

It opens with history — the good kind:

"God — with our ears we've heard. Our fathers told us what You did in their days, in days long ago. **You — with Your hand — drove out the nations and planted our fathers. You crushed the peoples and made them spread out.** It wasn't by their sword they took the land. It wasn't their arm that saved them. It was Your right hand. Your arm. The light of Your face — because You delighted in them."

*Because You delighted in them.* Same reason as Psalm 18. Not merit. *Delight.* You rescued them because You *liked* them.

**"You are my King, God. Command rescue for Jacob! Through You we push back our enemies. Through Your name we trample the ones rising against us. I don't trust in my bow. My sword doesn't save me. You've saved us from our enemies. You've shamed the ones who hate us. In God we boast all day long. We praise Your name forever."**

That's the theology. Fully correct. Not trusting the bow — Psalm 20 all over again. They've *learned the lesson*. They believe the right thing.

And then — verse 9. The floor drops out:

**"But You've rejected us. You've humiliated us. You don't go out with our armies anymore."**

*Anymore.* You used to. You stopped.

"You make us retreat from the enemy. The ones who hate us plunder us freely. You've handed us over like sheep to be eaten. You've scattered us among the nations. **You sold Your people for nothing. You didn't even set a high price.**"

*Sold us for nothing.* Not even a good deal. That's the bitterness — *if You were going to hand us over, You could at least have gotten something for us.*

"You've made us a disgrace to our neighbors — a mockery, a joke to everyone around us. You've made us a proverb among the nations. People shake their heads at us. All day long my humiliation is in front of me. Shame covers my face — at the voice of the taunter and the blasphemer, at the face of the enemy and the avenger."

Then — the verse that makes this psalm different from every other lament so far:

**"All this has come on us — and we haven't forgotten You. We haven't been false to Your covenant. Our heart hasn't turned back. Our steps haven't left Your path."**

*We didn't do anything wrong.* Read it again. This isn't Psalm 32 or 38 — no confession, no "because of my sin." They kept the covenant. They stayed on the road. And they got crushed *anyway*.

The Bible has a psalm for that. Innocent suffering. National-scale. *We did it right and lost.* It's in here so you'd know God isn't afraid of the accusation.

"Yet You've crushed us in a place of jackals. You've covered us with deep darkness. If we'd forgotten the name of our God, or spread our hands to a foreign god — **wouldn't God have found it out? He knows the secrets of the heart.**"

*You'd know if we'd cheated.* They're inviting the audit — like Psalm 7, like Psalm 26. *Check us. You'll find we're clean. So — why?*

**"But because of You we're killed all day long. We're counted as sheep for the slaughter."**

Paul quotes this — Romans 8:36. Right before *nothing can separate us from the love of God*. He picked the bleakest verse in the psalm to set up the strongest promise in his letter. *Sheep for the slaughter — AND nothing separates us.* Both.

And then — the line. The one people don't expect to find in the Bible:

**"Wake up! Why are You sleeping, Master? Rouse Yourself! Don't reject us forever!"**

*Why are You sleeping.* Said *to God*. The whole congregation, standing, shouting at heaven: *WAKE UP.*

Nobody edited this out. The priests kept it. The rabbis kept it. The church kept it. Three thousand years of God's people preserving a psalm that yells at God to *wake up* — because sometimes that's the only honest prayer, and God would rather have your honest shout than your polite silence.

"Why do You hide Your face? Why do You forget our affliction and our oppression? **Our soul is bowed down to the dust. Our belly clings to the ground.**"

Face down. Not kneeling — *flat*. Belly in the dirt.

And the end — no resolution, no "and then God answered":

**"Rise up! Help us! Redeem us — for the sake of Your loyal-love."**

*For the sake of Your loyal-love.* They stop arguing their own innocence. The last appeal isn't *we deserve it*. It's *You promised.*

So — when you did it right and lost: here's your psalm. Tell Him the history. Tell Him you didn't cheat. Tell Him to wake up. Get your belly in the dirt. And then — not *we deserve* — *You promised.* That's the one that holds.
