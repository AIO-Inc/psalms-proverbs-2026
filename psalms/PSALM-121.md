# PSALM 121 — He Who Keeps You Does Not Sleep

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 121:1–8. Second Song of Ascent. Eight verses. "Keep" appears six times. The traveler's psalm — for anyone leaving home, going to war, or walking into a hospital.*

---

Psalm 120 was *I'm in the wrong tent.* Psalm 121 is *I've started walking.* And the road's dangerous.

**"I lift up my eyes to the hills — where does my help come from?"**

*The hills.* Two readings — both true. The hills where Jerusalem sits — *up there, that's where I'm going*. Or the hills full of bandits, where the road gets dangerous — *up there, that's where the threat is*. Either way — the eyes go *up*. And the question follows: *where's my help?*

Not from the hills. From past them.

**"My help comes from the Source — the Maker of heaven and earth."**

*The Maker.* Psalm 115 — *the idols were made by hands; the Source made the hands*. Your help isn't from a landmark. It's from the One who *made* the landmarks.

Now — the *keeping*. Six times. Watch:

**"He will not let your foot slip — He who keeps you will not slumber."**

*Not let your foot slip.* Psalm 73, 94 — *my foot was slipping*. On a mountain road — that's the danger. One wrong step. And — *He who KEEPS you*. The Hebrew is *shomer* — a watchman. A guard on the wall. Someone whose *job* is to stay awake and watch *you*.

**"Indeed — He who keeps Israel will neither slumber nor sleep."**

*Neither slumber nor sleep.* Not even a doze. Psalm 44 said *wake up!* Psalm 78 had God *waking as from sleep*. Psalm 121 says — *He never went under.* The watchman doesn't nod off at 3 a.m. Which is exactly when *you* need Him to be awake.

The idols of Psalm 115 have eyes that can't see. This One has eyes that *never close*.

**"The Source keeps you. The Source is your shade at your right hand."**

*Shade.* On a desert road, at noon — shade is *survival*. Not comfort. *Life.* Psalm 91's *shadow of the Almighty*. And *at your right hand* — Psalm 16, 110 — close. Within reach. Walking beside you on the sun side.

**"The sun will not harm you by day — nor the moon by night."**

*Sun by day. Moon by night.* The whole twenty-four hours. Psalm 91 — *terror of night, arrow by day*. Psalm 121 — *sun and moon*. The ancients thought the moon caused madness — "lunatic." Whatever the night does to your mind — *kept*.

**"The Source will keep you from all harm. He will keep your life."**

*From all harm.* Not "from most." *Keep your life.* The Hebrew is *nefesh* — your soul, your whole self. Not just the body on the road. *You.*

And the last verse — the widest one:

**"The Source will keep your going out and your coming in — both now and forevermore."**

*Going out and coming in.* Every departure. Every return. The morning you leave for work. The night you get home. The trip to the hospital. The trip back. The day you're born — *going out*. The day you die — *coming in*. All of it. *Kept.*

*Now and forevermore.* No expiration.

Eight verses. Six *keeps*. Here's the shape: *Eyes up — where's help? From the Maker. He won't let your foot slip. He doesn't sleep — not even a doze. He's your shade on the sun side. Day and night — covered. All harm. Your whole self. Every going out, every coming in. Forever.*

Jewish parents say this over kids leaving for school. Soldiers carry it. It's read at bedsides and gravesides and airports.

Here's why: it's the psalm for *not being able to watch*. You can't stay awake over the ones you love. You can't walk every road with them. So you say: *He who keeps you does not sleep.* And you hand the watch to the One who can hold it.

Lift your eyes. Then walk.

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* esa einai el-he-harim *= "I lift my eyes to the mountains" — ambiguous: destination or danger; the question mark on "where does my help come from" is implied by the answer in v. 2. v. 3, 4, 5, 7 (x2), 8 —* shamar *= keep/guard/watch — six occurrences;* shomer *= keeper, watchman. v. 4 —* lo-yanum v'lo yishan *= "neither slumbers nor sleeps" — two words for sleep, both negated. v. 5 —* tzil'cha *= your shade. v. 7 —* nafshecha *= your soul/life/self. v. 8 —* tzet'cha u-vo'echa *= "your going out and your coming in" — merism for all activity, all of life. No* yirah*.*
