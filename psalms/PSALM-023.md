# PSALM 23 — The Shepherd

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 23:1–6. The most-known poem in human history — and most people have never actually heard it.*

---

You know this one. Or you think you do. You've heard it at funerals, on greeting cards, in movies. Let's hear it like it's the first time.

**"The Source is my shepherd. I lack nothing."**

Not *I have everything*. *I lack nothing.* There's a difference. He's not saying his life is full of stuff. He's saying nothing that *matters* is missing. And it starts with the relationship, not the inventory: *my shepherd*. Personal. Possessive. Mine.

David was a shepherd before he was anything else. He knew what the job was. Sheep can't find water on their own. They'll eat the same patch of grass until it's dirt. They panic. They wander. They can't fight, can't run fast, can't see well. They *need* somebody. So when David says *God is my shepherd*, he's saying: *I'm the sheep. I know exactly how helpless I am. And I have Someone.*

"He makes me lie down in green pastures."

*Makes* me. Sheep won't lie down when they're anxious, hungry, bothered by flies, or afraid of the flock. All four have to be handled before a sheep will rest. So when David says *He makes me lie down*, he's saying: *He dealt with everything that was keeping me up.*

"He leads me beside quiet waters."

Sheep won't drink from fast water — they're afraid of falling in. The shepherd finds the still pool or *makes* one, damming the stream with rocks. Quiet water isn't a coincidence. Somebody arranged it.

**"He restores my soul."**

*Restores* — same word as Psalm 19, *brings back*. A sheep on its back, legs in the air, can't get up on its own. It'll die there in hours. "Cast down," shepherds call it. The shepherd has to find it, roll it over, rub its legs until the blood comes back, hold it up until it can stand. That's what *restores my soul* means. He came and found you on your back and stood you up.

"He guides me on the right paths — for His name's sake."

*For His name's sake.* Not because you earned the good path. Because His reputation is tied to how His sheep turn out. He's not going to let you wander off — it would make Him look bad. Your safety is His honor.

Now the turn. The middle of the psalm. Everything before was *He*. Now it's *You*:

**"Even when I walk through the valley of deepest darkness — I will not be afraid of harm. Because You are with me."**

*Through.* Not *into*. Not *around*. *Through.* The valley has an exit. You're walking to it.

And look what changed: he stopped talking *about* God and started talking *to* Him. The green pastures were "He." The dark valley is "You." When it gets dark, the third person isn't enough anymore. You need face to face.

"Your rod and Your staff — they comfort me."

The rod is a club — for the wolf. The staff is a crook — for pulling you back from the edge, for lifting you out of the ravine. One's for what's attacking you. One's for what you're doing to yourself. Both of them *comfort*.

Now the scene shifts. No more pasture. A table:

**"You prepare a table before me — in the presence of my enemies."**

Right in front of them. They're still there. They didn't go away. And You're setting out the food anyway. Pulling out the chair. *Sit down. Eat. Let them watch.*

"You anoint my head with oil."

Honored guest treatment. In the ancient world you oiled the head of the person you were glad to see.

**"My cup overflows."**

Not *full*. Over. Past the rim. Running down the sides. More than you can hold.

And then the ending — two lines that carry more than they seem:

**"Surely goodness and loyal-love will pursue me all the days of my life."**

*Pursue.* Not "follow." The Hebrew is *radaf* — to chase, to hunt down. It's the word for how an enemy pursues you. David flips it: the thing chasing me down every day of my life isn't my enemies. It's *goodness*. It's *loyal-love*. They're hunting me. I can't outrun them.

**"And I will dwell in the house of the Source for the length of days."**

Home. Not visiting. *Dwelling.* Living there. The sheep that spent the whole psalm in fields and valleys ends up inside the house — because the shepherd, it turns out, was bringing him home the whole time.

Six verses. Green grass, dark valley, a table, a house. That's your life, in order, with Someone in it who knows exactly how sheep work.

You lack nothing.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* lo echsar *= I lack nothing / I shall not be in want. v. 3 —* yeshovev *= brings back/restores. v. 4 —* tzalmavet *= deep darkness/death-shadow;* lo ira ra *= I will not fear harm — ordinary human dread, correctly rendered (not* yirah*). v. 6 —* radaf *= pursue/chase, normally used of enemies; "follow" undersells it.* chesed *= loyal-love. "Forever" (KJV) is* l'orech yamim *= "for length of days" — extended life, and by implication beyond it.*
