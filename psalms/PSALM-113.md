# PSALM 113 — He Raises the Poor from the Dust

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 113:1–9. The first of the Egyptian Hallel — Psalms 113–118, sung at Passover. Jesus sang these the night before He died. Nine verses about a God who bends down.*

---

Passover night. The lamb's eaten. The cups are poured. And the family sings — *these* psalms. 113 and 114 before the meal. 115 through 118 after. The last thing Jesus sang with His disciples was Psalm 118. The first was *this*.

**"Praise the Source! Praise, you servants of the Source — praise the name of the Source."**

*Hallelujah.* Three *praises* in one verse. The Hallel — "praise" — earning its name.

**"Let the name of the Source be praised — both now and forevermore. From the rising of the sun to the place where it sets — the name of the Source is to be praised."**

*Now and forever. Sunrise to sunset.* Time and space — both covered. Every hour, every longitude. Psalm 19 — *nothing hides from its heat*. Here — *nothing's outside the praise*.

**"The Source is exalted over all the nations. His glory is above the heavens."**

*Above the heavens.* Psalm 8 — *You've set Your glory above the heavens.* He's *higher* than the highest thing.

**"Who is like the Source our God — the One who sits enthroned on high —"**

And then — the turn. The whole psalm is in the next line:

**"— who stoops down to look on the heavens and the earth?"**

*Stoops down.* The Hebrew is *ha-mashpili lir'ot* — *the One who lowers Himself to see*. He's *so high* that the *heavens themselves* are *below* Him — and He has to *bend* to look at them. Not at earth. At *heaven*. The sky is *down* from where He sits.

And He bends anyway.

That's the paradox the Hallel is built on. *Exalted over all the nations* — and *stoops*. Psalm 18 — *He bent the heavens and came down*. Psalm 40 — *He bent down to me and heard my cry*. The higher He is, the *further* the stoop. And He stoops *all the way*.

Now — what He does when He gets down there:

**"He raises the poor from the dust — and lifts the needy from the ash heap."**

*From the dust. From the ash heap.* The garbage pile outside the city. Where the lepers sat. Where Job sat. The lowest physical place a human could be. And God *reaches into it* and *lifts*.

*Raises. Lifts.* Two verbs. Same motion. *Up.*

**"— He seats them with princes — with the princes of His people."**

*With princes.* Not "gives them a meal and sends them off." *Seats them.* At the table. Next to royalty. The ash-heap person — *at the head table*. Psalm 23 — *You prepare a table before me.* Psalm 68 — *He sets the lonely in families.*

This is Hannah's song — 1 Samuel 2. *He raises the poor from the dust and lifts the needy from the ash heap. He seats them with princes.* Word for word. A barren woman who'd been mocked for years — praying it over a baby she thought she'd never have.

And it's *Mary's* song — Luke 1. *He has brought down rulers from their thrones — and lifted up the humble.* A teenage girl in a backwater town, pregnant with the One who'd sing this psalm at His last Passover.

**"He settles the childless woman in her home — as a happy mother of children."**

*The childless woman.* Hannah. Sarah. Rachel. Elizabeth. The ancient world's deepest shame — *barren*. And God *settles her* — gives her a *home* — makes her *a happy mother*. The Hebrew is *em ha-banim s'mechah* — *the mother of the children, rejoicing*. Psalm 68's *lonely in families* — with a *nursery*.

If you've been the one on the outside of the thing everyone else has — this verse. He *settles* you.

**"Praise the Source!"**

*Hallelujah.* Bookend.

Nine verses. Here's the shape: *Praise — now, forever, sunrise to sunset. He's above the heavens. Who's like Him — so high He has to STOOP to see the sky — and stoops all the way down to the ash heap — and lifts the poor out of it — and seats them with PRINCES — and gives the barren woman a home full of kids. Hallelujah.*

Jesus sang this the night before the cross. *He stoops down.* And then He went and did it — all the way to the ash heap, all the way to the dust — so He could seat you with princes.

Passover starts here. So does everything.
