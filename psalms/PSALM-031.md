# PSALM 31 — Into Your Hands

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 31:1–24. Jesus's last words came from verse 5.*

---

"In You, Source, I take refuge. Don't let me be ashamed — ever. In Your right-standing, get me out."

*Ever.* He's asking for a permanent thing. Not "don't let me be ashamed today." *Never.* Because he's staked everything on this refuge, and if it fails, there's nothing behind it.

"Turn Your ear to me. Rescue me fast. Be my rock of refuge — a fortress house to save me. Because You *are* my rock and my fortress. For Your name's sake — lead me. Guide me. Get me out of the net they've hidden for me. Because You're my stronghold."

Then the verse Jesus said with His last breath:

**"Into Your hand I commit my spirit."**

Luke 23:46. *Father, into Your hands I commit My spirit.* And then He stopped breathing.

That's what this line is for. It's the surrender. Not giving up — *handing over*. The Hebrew is *pakad* — to deposit, to entrust, like putting something in a vault. *Here's my spirit. You hold it. I can't anymore.*

You'll say it at the end. Learn to say it now.

"You have redeemed me, Source, God of truth. I hate the ones who guard worthless idols. But I — I trust in the Source."

**"I will rejoice and be glad in Your loyal-love — because You've seen my affliction. You've known my soul in its distresses."**

*Known my soul.* Not observed. *Known.* The word for intimate knowledge — the way you know a person you've lived with. He's been inside the trouble with you.

"You haven't handed me over to the enemy. You've set my feet in a wide-open place."

Now the lament — and it gets specific about what suffering does to a body:

"Be gracious to me, Source — I'm in trouble. My eye is wasting away with grief. My soul and my body too. My life is used up with sorrow. My years with groaning. My strength fails because of my guilt. My bones waste away."

*My strength fails because of my guilt.* He's not innocent in this one. Something he did is part of the weight. He says so.

"I'm a disgrace to all my enemies — even worse to my neighbors. A dread to my friends. People who see me in the street run from me. **I've been forgotten, like a dead man out of mind. I'm like a broken pot.**"

*Forgotten like a dead man.* The people who used to call don't. The friends cross the street. You're alive, but the world's already moved on like you're not. If you've been sick a long time, or in trouble a long time — you know this. Nobody sends a card for the second year.

"I hear the whispering of the crowd — terror all around. They scheme together against me. They plot to take my life."

And then — no explanation — the turn:

**"But I trust in You, Source. I say: 'You are my God.' My times are in Your hand."**

*My times.* The seasons. The calendar. When this ends. When the next thing starts. *In Your hand* — the same hand he committed his spirit to. He's not holding the schedule. He's given that up too.

"Rescue me from my enemies and my pursuers. **Make Your face shine on Your servant.** Save me in Your loyal-love."

*Make Your face shine.* The priestly blessing — Numbers 6. Every psalm David writes, he's asking for the face.

"Don't let me be ashamed — I've called on You. Let the mark-missers be ashamed. Let them be silenced in Sheol. Let the lying lips be shut — the ones speaking arrogantly against the one who does right, with pride and contempt."

Then the celebration of what God's got stored up:

**"How abundant is Your goodness — which You've stored up for those who stand in awe of You! Which You've worked out, in front of everyone, for those who take refuge in You!"**

*Stored up.* Like a warehouse. There's goodness on shelves with your name on it. It's not theoretical. It's inventory.

"In the shelter of Your presence You hide them from the schemes of men. You keep them safe in a shelter from accusing tongues."

*From accusing tongues.* Not just from swords. From *words*. God builds a room where the gossip can't get in.

"Blessed be the Source — He's shown me wonderful loyal-love in a besieged city."

*In a besieged city.* Surrounded. Cut off. Supplies running out. And *in there* — wonder. Not after the siege. During.

"I said in my panic: 'I've been cut off from Your eyes!' **But You heard the voice of my pleading when I cried to You.**"

He admits it — he panicked. Said *You can't see me anymore.* Wrong. He heard.

"Love the Source, all you loyal ones! The Source guards the faithful — and pays back the arrogant in full."

And the ending — the same instruction as Psalm 27:

**"Be strong. Let your heart be courageous — all you who wait for the Source."**

So: *Into Your hand I commit my spirit.* Say it before you have to. Then say the next one — *my times are in Your hand.* Both hands full. Yours empty. That's when you're finally safe.

---

*Note on translation: v. 5 —* b'yadcha afkid ruchi *= "into Your hand I deposit/entrust my spirit" — Luke 23:46. v. 7 —* yadata *= You have known (intimately). v. 12 — "like a broken vessel" =* k'chli oved *= a lost/perished pot. v. 15 —* ittotai *= my times/seasons. v. 17 — "Sheol" = the grave, silence — not hell. v. 19 — "them that fear thee" =* yere'echa *=* yirah *= those in awe of You. v. 22 —* b'chofzi *= in my panic/haste.*
