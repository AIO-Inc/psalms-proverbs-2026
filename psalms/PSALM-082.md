# PSALM 82 — You Are Gods — and You Will Die Like Men

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 82:1–8. Asaph. Eight verses. A courtroom scene — God putting the powerful on trial. Jesus quoted it to the men about to stone Him.*

---

Short. Strange. And Jesus reached for it when they picked up rocks.

**"God presides in the great assembly. He gives judgment among the 'gods.'"**

*The gods.* The Hebrew is *elohim* — the same word used for God Himself. So who are they? Two readings, both in the tradition: the *rulers* — judges, kings, the powerful who act like gods. Or the *spiritual powers* behind the nations. Either way — beings with *authority*, standing in a courtroom, and God is the one on the bench.

**"'How long will you judge unjustly — and show partiality to the wicked?'"**

*How long.* Now it's *God* saying it. Psalm 13, 74, 79 — humans asked God *how long*. Here God turns around and asks *the powerful*: *how long are YOU going to keep doing this?*

*Partiality to the wicked.* Tilting the scales toward the guy who can pay. That's the charge.

Now — the job description. What the powerful were *supposed* to do:

**"'Defend the weak and the fatherless. Uphold the cause of the poor and the oppressed. Rescue the weak and the needy. Deliver them from the hand of the wicked.'"**

Four verbs. *Defend. Uphold. Rescue. Deliver.* Four groups. *Weak. Fatherless. Poor. Oppressed.* That's the *entire* mandate for anyone with power in this psalm. Not "grow the economy." Not "expand the borders." *Protect the ones who can't protect themselves.*

Psalm 72's King did this. Psalm 82's gods didn't.

**"'They know nothing. They understand nothing. They walk around in darkness. All the foundations of the earth are shaken.'"**

*The foundations shake.* When the powerful stop protecting the weak — the *ground* goes. Psalm 11 — *when the foundations are torn down*. This is *why*. Injustice at the top isn't a policy failure. It's *structural*. The planet wobbles.

And then — the verdict. And it's the line Jesus quoted:

**"'I said: You are gods. You are all sons of the Most High.'"**

*You are gods.* God's acknowledging it. *You DO have authority. I gave it to you. You were meant to carry My image, exercise My justice, stand in My place for the ones under you.* That's what *gods* means here — delegated authority. Representatives.

**"'But you will die like mere men. You will fall like every other ruler.'"**

*But.* You were given a god's job. You did a tyrant's. So you get a *man's* ending. Same grave as the people you crushed.

That's the sentence. Not fire from heaven. Just — *mortality*. The gods find out they're dust.

Now — John 10. The crowd's about to stone Jesus for saying *I and the Father are one.* And He says: *Isn't it written in your Law, "I said you are gods"? If He called them gods — those to whom the word of God came — why are you angry that I say I'm the Son of God?* He's using Psalm 82 to say: *Scripture already calls humans gods when they carry God's word. So don't choke on Me.*

And the last verse — Asaph's prayer, after the trial:

**"Rise up, God — judge the earth. Because all the nations are Your inheritance."**

*All the nations.* Every one. The gods who failed — the ones that were supposed to represent Him in Egypt, in Assyria, in Babylon — they're *fired*. And God takes direct control. *Your inheritance.* All of it.

Eight verses. Here's the shape: *God's on the bench. The powerful are in the dock. How long will you tilt the scales? Your job was: defend the weak, the fatherless, the poor, the oppressed. You walked in darkness and the ground shook. You were gods — I gave you that. You'll die like men. Rise up — judge it all Yourself.*

If you've got any power over anyone — a kid, an employee, a congregation, a vote — you're in this courtroom. Four verbs. Four groups. That's the whole review.
