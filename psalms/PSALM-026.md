# PSALM 26 — Test Me

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 26:1–12.*

---

Psalm 25 was the man with the past. This is the man with the clean record — and he's just as bold.

"Judge me, Source — because I've walked with integrity. I've trusted in You. I haven't slipped."

*Judge me.* Most people spend their lives avoiding that word. David runs *toward* it. Because he's done the inventory and he knows where he stands.

**"Examine me, Source. Test me. Refine my kidneys and my heart."**

Kidneys — the ancient world's word for your deepest gut, the place the emotions come from. He's not saying *check my behavior*. He's saying *go in. Go all the way in. Test the part I can't fake.*

You can only pray this if you mean it. Do you?

"Because Your loyal-love is in front of my eyes. I've walked in Your truth."

Now the negatives — what he *hasn't* done:

"I haven't sat with worthless men. I haven't gone in with the hidden ones — the pretenders. I've hated the gathering of the harmful. I haven't sat with the mark-missers."

*The hidden ones.* People with something under the surface. The ones whose real agenda you find out about later. David says: *I don't sit at that table.* Not because he thinks he's better — because he knows what happens to you at that table. You become a hidden one too.

Who you sit with is who you become. Pick your table.

"I wash my hands in innocence. I go around Your altar, Source — to make my voice heard in thanksgiving, to tell about all Your wonders."

*Go around the altar.* Picture it — a man circling the place of sacrifice, not to bring one, but to *talk*. To thank. To tell stories. He's so full of what God's done he can't stand still.

**"Source, I love the house where You live. The place where Your glory dwells."**

*I love the house.* Not "I'm obligated to attend." *Love.* He wants to be where God is the way you want to be where your best friend is.

Then the ask, and it's about the *ending*:

"Don't gather my soul with the mark-missers. Don't take my life with men of blood — whose hands hold schemes, whose right hand is full of bribes."

*Don't gather me with them.* He's not afraid of dying. He's afraid of dying *in their company*. Of ending up in the pile with the ones who sold out. He wants to be counted with the right crowd at the finish.

**"But I — I walk in my integrity. Redeem me. Be gracious to me."**

Read those together. *I walk in integrity* — AND — *redeem me. Be gracious.* The clean-record man still asks for grace. Because integrity isn't perfection. It's *direction*. And even the one walking straight knows he didn't get there on his own.

**"My foot stands on level ground. In the assembly I will bless the Source."**

*Level ground.* Not slipping. Not the edge of a cliff. Flat, solid, room to stand. He started the psalm saying *I haven't slipped*. He ends it standing on a floor that doesn't tilt.

So here's the dare. Say it: *Test me. Go all the way in.* If you can't say it yet, that's fine — go back to Psalm 25. But if you can — if you've done the work and you know your table — say it. And then go stand on level ground.
