# PSALM 7 — He Falls in His Own Pit

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 7:1–17. David, falsely accused.*

---

Somebody's lying about you. Somebody's built a whole story about who you are, and it's not true, and people are believing it.

That's where this psalm lives.

"Source, my God, I take refuge in You. Save me from everyone hunting me. Or they'll rip me like a lion — tear me to pieces, and nobody will pull me out."

He's not being dramatic. When your reputation gets torn up, it *feels* like being eaten alive. He names the feeling.

Then he does something brave. He opens himself up for inspection:

"Source, my God — if I actually did this, if there's wrong in my hands, if I paid back a friend with evil, if I robbed someone for no reason — then let my enemy catch me. Let him trample my life into the dirt. Let him grind my honor into the dust."

Do you hear that? He's saying, *Check me. If I'm guilty, I accept the consequence.* That's not the prayer of a man with something to hide. Only someone who's done the honest inventory can pray that.

Do your inventory first. Then you get to pray the rest of it.

"Rise up in Your anger, Source. Stand against the rage of my enemies. Wake up for me — You've already commanded justice."

Wake up. He's poking God in the ribs. *You said You'd handle this. Handle it.*

"Let the whole assembly gather around You, and You sit above them on high. You judge the nations — judge me too. Judge according to my right-standing, according to my integrity."

He wants the audit. He *wants* it. Because he knows what it'll show.

"Bring the harm of the mark-missers to an end, but establish the one who does right — You, who examine hearts and minds, You, the just God."

Examine hearts. Not the gossip. Not the headline. The *heart*. That's the only court that matters, and it's the only one that gets it right every time.

"God is my shield. He saves the upright in heart. He's a just judge — He's ready every single day."

Every day. Not once a year at the big trial. Daily. He's on the bench right now.

Then the warning to the accuser, and it's one of the great pictures in all of Scripture:

"If a man won't turn, God sharpens His sword, bends His bow. He's prepared deadly weapons — arrows of fire."

But watch how the judgment actually lands:

"Look — he's pregnant with wickedness. He conceives trouble, and gives birth to lies. **He digs a pit, he hollows it out — and he falls into the hole he made.** His trouble comes back on his own head. His violence lands on his own skull."

That's it. That's how it works. God doesn't have to throw the lightning bolt. The liar builds his own trap. The schemer's scheme swallows him. The pit you dug for someone else has your name on it.

You don't have to dig a counter-pit. You just have to stay out of his.

"I will thank the Source according to His justice. I will sing to the name of the Source, the Most High."

Let them lie. Do your inventory. Come clean before the only Judge that counts. Then watch the man with the shovel fall in his own hole.

---

*Note on translation: v. 11 — "God is angry with the wicked every day" (KJV) is a mistranslation; Hebrew reads "God is a righteous judge every day" — the anger was added. v. 15–16 — the self-defeating pit is a recurring biblical principle (Prov 26:27, Eccl 10:8). "Righteousness" =* tzedek *= right-standing, straightness. "Wicked" =* rasha*. No* yirah *in this psalm.*
