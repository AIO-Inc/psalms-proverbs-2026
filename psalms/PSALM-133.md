# PSALM 133 — How Good It Is

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 133:1–3. Fourteenth Song of Ascent. Three verses. Attributed to David. The pilgrims have arrived — and the first thing they notice isn't the building. It's each other.*

---

Fourteen songs. The road from Kedar to the Temple gate. And now they're *in* — standing shoulder to shoulder with people from every tribe, every town, every family feud. And somebody looks around and says:

**"Look — how good and how pleasant it is — when brothers dwell together in unity."**

*Look.* *Hinneh* — *behold*. It's a *pointing* word. Like he's grabbing your sleeve. *Look at this.*

*Brothers dwelling together.* In the ancient world, that was a legal thing — Deuteronomy 25 — brothers who *stayed on the family land together* instead of splitting the inheritance and scattering. It meant nobody left. Nobody sold out. They *chose* to stay under one roof.

And *unity* — *yachad*. *Together.* As one. Not *agreeing on everything*. Being *in the same place, on purpose*.

You know how rare that is. Brothers fight. Genesis is *nothing but* brothers fighting — Cain, Ishmael and Isaac, Jacob and Esau, Joseph and the ten. Israel itself split — north and south — and never got back together. So when David says *how good, how pleasant* — he's not describing normal. He's describing a *miracle*. And the pilgrims singing it, standing next to people they'd never share a table with back home — they *knew*.

Then — two pictures. And they're strange ones. Not *a house*. Not *a family dinner*. *Oil* and *dew*.

**"It is like precious oil on the head — running down on the beard — running down on Aaron's beard — down on the collar of his robes."**

*Aaron's beard.* The pilgrims are standing at the Temple. They're watching *priests*. And the image is Aaron's *ordination* — Exodus 29, Leviticus 8 — the oil poured on the high priest's head. Not dabbed. *Poured.* So much it ran down his face, into his beard, onto his collar. Wasteful. Extravagant. Dripping.

That's what unity is like. It doesn't stay where you put it. It *runs down*. Starts at the head — and everyone under it gets wet. One person chooses peace — and it drips onto the next, and the next.

And it's *consecrating* oil. That oil made Aaron *holy* — set apart to stand before God. David's saying: *when brothers dwell together, that's not just nice. That's holy. That's ordination oil.* The unity *itself* is what sets you apart.

**"It is like the dew of Hermon — falling on the mountains of Zion."**

*Hermon.* The big mountain — way up north, snow-capped, nine thousand feet. Dew so heavy up there it soaks you through by morning. *Zion* — small, dry, down south. Hermon's dew doesn't reach Zion. Geographically — impossible. A hundred miles.

That's the *point*. Unity is *Hermon's dew on Zion*. Something from way up there — landing way down here. Not natural. A *gift*. You can't make dew. You can only wake up and find it fell.

And in a dry land — dew is *life*. Psalm 110 — *the dew of your youth*. Hosea 14 — *I will be like the dew to Israel*. No rain for months — and every morning, the dew keeps things alive. That's what a united community is in a dry world. The thing that keeps everyone breathing until the rain comes.

**"For there the Source bestows His blessing — even life forevermore."**

*There.* Where? *Where the brothers dwell together.* That's where the blessing lands. Not on the building. Not on the mountain. *There* — on the *togetherness*.

*Life forevermore.* The last words. Psalm 21 — *length of days forever*. Psalm 121, 125, 131 — *now and forevermore*. The Ascents keep landing on that word. And Psalm 133 says — *it's found where people stay together.*

Three verses. Here's the shape: *LOOK — how good it is when people who could split, don't. It's like ordination oil — poured, dripping, making everyone under it holy. It's like Hermon's dew on Zion — impossible, from above, life in a dry place. And THERE — right there — God puts His blessing. Life forever.*

Jesus prayed this psalm without quoting it — John 17 — *that they may be one, as We are one.* And the church's first description — Acts 2 — *all the believers were together, and had everything in common.* *Yachad.* The oil ran down. The dew fell.

Here's what to do today. One brother you've been dwelling *apart* from — a sibling, a friend, someone at church. Not the one who wronged you badly. The one where it's just *distance*. Close it. One text. One call. Because *there* — not at the altar, not on the mountaintop — *there* is where the blessing comes down.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 — *hinneh mah-tov u-mah-na'im* = "behold, how good and how pleasant"; *shevet achim gam-yachad* = "the dwelling of brothers also together" — *yachad* = in unity/as one; *shevet* echoes Deut 25:5 (brothers dwelling together on undivided inheritance). v. 2 — *shemen ha-tov* = "the good oil"; *yored* = descending/running down (×2, and again in v. 3 — three descents); *z'kan-Aharon* = Aaron's beard — Exod 29:7; 30:22–33; Lev 8:12; *pi middotav* = "the mouth/opening of his garments" = collar. v. 3 — *tal-Chermon* = dew of Hermon; *sheyored al-har'rei Tziyyon* = "that descends on the mountains of Zion" — geographically impossible, hence gift/grace imagery; *chayyim ad-ha-olam* = "life unto the age/forever." NT resonance: John 17:21–23; Acts 2:44–47; Eph 4:3. No *yirah* in this psalm.*
