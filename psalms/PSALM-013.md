# PSALM 13 — How Long?

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 13:1–6. Six verses. Four "how longs."*

---

This is the shortest, sharpest lament in the book. Read it when you're out of patience.

"How long, Source? Will You forget me forever?
How long will You hide Your face from me?
How long do I have to carry this in my soul, with grief in my heart every day?
How long is my enemy going to be on top?"

Four times. *How long. How long. How long. How long.*

He's not asking for information. He's not expecting a date. He's *pounding on the door*. And notice — he says "forget me forever" and "hide Your face" — the exact accusations the wicked man made in Psalm 10 about God not seeing. David's saying them now, to God's face, as a believer. Because sometimes the faithful feel exactly what the faithless assume.

You're allowed to say it. It's in the book.

Then he asks — and it's simple:

"Look at me. Answer me, Source my God. Light up my eyes — or I'm going to sleep the sleep of death."

*Light up my eyes.* The eyes go dim when hope goes. He's saying, *I'm fading. Turn the lights back on before they go out completely.*

"Or my enemy will say, 'I beat him.' My opponents will throw a party when I fall."

He gives God a reason — *Your reputation is tied to mine. If I go down, they'll say You couldn't hold me.* It's a fair argument. God accepts fair arguments.

And then — no transition, no explanation, no "and then God spoke." Just the turn. Verse five:

**"But I — I have trusted in Your loyal-love. My heart will celebrate in Your rescue."**

*I have trusted.* Past tense. He's not saying "I'm going to start trusting now that I feel better." He's saying: *the trust was underneath the whole time. Even while I was yelling how long.*

That's the secret of this psalm. The four "how longs" weren't a lack of faith. They were faith with its voice raised. You don't pound on a door you don't believe anyone's behind.

"I will sing to the Source — **because He has dealt well with me.**"

*Has* dealt well. Still past tense. He's not out of the situation. Nothing in the text says the enemy's gone. He's singing *in* it, because he's remembered what's already true.

Six verses. The whole shape of a hard season: the pounding, the asking, the turn, the song.

Say your how-longs. All four if you need them. Then say the "but I." It's been in you the whole time.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* ad anah *= "until when?" — the language of exhausted patience. v. 3 — "lighten mine eyes" =* ha'irah einai *= restore vitality/hope; dim eyes = dying. v. 5 —* batachti *= "I have trusted" — perfect tense, completed action. "Mercy/steadfast love" =* chesed *= loyal-love. No* yirah*.*
