# PSALM 80 — Restore Us

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 80:1–19. Asaph. The northern tribes have fallen — Assyria, 722 BC. A prayer built on one refrain, said three times, growing each time.*

---

The north is gone. Ephraim, Manasseh, Benjamin — the tribes named in this psalm — carried off. Asaph prays for what's left, and he does it with a refrain that *escalates*.

**"Hear us, Shepherd of Israel — You who lead Joseph like a flock. You who sit enthroned between the cherubim — shine forth."**

*Shepherd.* First word for God. Not King. Not Judge. The one who leads sheep — because the sheep have been scattered and he needs the *shepherd*, specifically.

*Shine forth.* Psalm 76 — *radiant with light*. He wants the face.

**"Before Ephraim, Benjamin, and Manasseh — awaken Your might. Come and save us."**

Three tribes. The ones Assyria took. He names them so God will.

Here's the refrain — the first time:

**"Restore us, God. Make Your face shine on us — that we may be saved."**

*Restore us.* The Hebrew is *hashivenu* — *turn us back*. *Bring us around.* It's the word for repentance — but Asaph's asking God to do the *turning*. Not "we'll turn." *You turn us.* Because they've tried and couldn't.

*Make Your face shine.* The priestly blessing — Numbers 6. Psalm 67. He's asking for the oldest blessing there is.

**"Source God of Armies — how long will Your anger smolder against the prayers of Your people?"**

*Against the PRAYERS.* Not against the sins — against the *prayers*. That's the horror: they're praying, and the prayers feel like they're hitting a wall of smoke.

**"You've fed us with the bread of tears. You've made us drink tears by the bowlful."**

*Bread of tears.* Psalm 42 — *my tears have been my food.* Now it's the whole nation. *By the bowlful* — the Hebrew is *shalish*, a large measure. Not a cup of tears. A *basin*.

**"You've made us a source of contention to our neighbors. Our enemies mock us."**

The refrain — second time — and watch it grow:

**"Restore us, God of Armies. Make Your face shine on us — that we may be saved."**

First time: *God.* Second time: *God of Armies.* He's adding a title. Getting more specific about Who he's talking to. Because the first ask didn't land, and he's turning up the address.

Now — the vine. This is the heart of the psalm:

**"You brought a vine out of Egypt. You drove out the nations and planted it. You cleared the ground for it — and it took root and filled the land. The mountains were covered with its shade — the mighty cedars with its branches. Its branches reached to the Sea — its shoots as far as the River."**

*A vine.* Israel as a grapevine. God *carried it* out of Egypt — like a gardener moving a plant. *Cleared the ground.* *Planted it.* And it *grew* — shade over the mountains, branches to the sea. It was *beautiful*. That's the memory.

Jesus picked this up — *I am the true vine.* John 15. He knew this psalm.

**"Why have You broken down its walls — so that all who pass by pick its grapes? Boars from the forest ravage it. Creatures of the field feed on it."**

*Broken down its walls.* The hedge around the vineyard — gone. Anyone walking by grabs a handful. Wild pigs trampling through. The vine God planted with His own hands — *unprotected*.

*Why?* He asks it. Doesn't answer it.

**"Return to us, God of Armies! Look down from heaven and see! Watch over this vine — the root Your right hand has planted — the son You've raised up for Yourself."**

*The root Your right hand planted.* He's reminding God: *this is YOUR planting. You put it here. You don't abandon what You planted.*

*The son You've raised up.* The vine becomes a *son*. Israel as God's child. And behind it — the Son who'd call Himself the vine.

**"Your vine is cut down. It's burned with fire. At Your rebuke Your people perish."**

*Cut down. Burned.* He's not softening it.

**"Let Your hand rest on the man at Your right hand — the son of man You've raised up for Yourself. Then we won't turn away from You. Revive us — and we'll call on Your name."**

*The man at Your right hand. The son of man.* Asaph's praying for the king — the one who represents the people. And the language is *exactly* what Jesus used for Himself: *son of man*, *at the right hand*. The psalm's reaching forward.

*Revive us — and we'll call.* He's honest about the order. *You revive first. THEN we'll call.* They can't even pray right until He does something.

And the refrain — third time, and it's the fullest:

**"Restore us, Source God of Armies. Make Your face shine on us — that we may be saved."**

*God.* Then *God of Armies.* Then *Source God of Armies.* Three refrains, three names, each one longer. He's not repeating himself. He's *climbing*. Every time the refrain comes around, he's got more of God's name in his mouth.

That's how you pray when the first ask doesn't land. You don't give up. You say it again with a *bigger name*.

Nineteen verses. Here's the shape: *Shepherd — shine. Restore us. How long against our PRAYERS? Bread of tears by the bowlful. Restore us, God of Armies. You brought a vine out of Egypt and it was beautiful — why did You break down the wall? Watch over what You planted. Rest Your hand on the son of man. Revive us and we'll call. Restore us, Source God of Armies.*

Say it three times. Add a name each time. Let the face shine.
