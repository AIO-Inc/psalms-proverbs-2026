# PSALM 3 — I Slept Anyway

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 3:1–8. Written by David while running from his own son, Absalom.*

---

Let me tell you where David was when he wrote this.

His own son had turned on him. Not a stranger. Not an enemy nation. His boy. Absalom had raised an army, taken the capital, and David was out in the wilderness with the people who were still loyal — and even *they* were saying, "It's over. God's done with him."

That's the setup. Now hear what he wrote.

"Look how many are against me. Look how many are rising up. Everybody's saying I'm finished — that there's no help for me anymore."

He doesn't pretend. He names it. That's the first move: you say out loud how bad it actually is.

Then he turns.

"But You. You're a shield around me. You're the One who lifts my head."

Picture that — a man whose head is hanging, and a hand under his chin, raising it back up. That's the image. Not a lightning bolt from heaven. A hand under the chin.

"I cried out, and He answered me from the holy mountain."

And then — this is the line, this is the whole psalm right here:

**"I lay down and slept. I woke up again, because the Source holds me up."**

He *slept.* Surrounded. Betrayed. Outnumbered. He put his head down on the ground in the wilderness and closed his eyes, because he had decided who was actually holding the situation together — and it wasn't him.

Do you understand how much strength it takes to sleep when everything's on fire? That's not weakness. That's the most defiant act of trust a human being can commit.

So now he can say it: "I won't be afraid of ten thousand people lined up against me." Not because ten thousand isn't a lot. Because ten thousand isn't the point.

"Rise up. Save me. You knock them in the teeth. You break the bite of the ones who missed the mark."

Rescue belongs to the Source. And His blessing is on His people — all of them, including the ones the crowd has already written off.

Tonight, whatever's surrounding you — lay down. Sleep anyway. You'll wake up. He's got the watch.

---

*Note on translation: "Selah" appears three times — a musical pause, "stop and let that land." Kept in the rhythm of the script as breath. "Wicked" (*rasha*) = habitual mark-missers. No* yirah *in this psalm; v. 6 "I will not be afraid" is* lo ira *— ordinary human dread, correctly rendered.*
