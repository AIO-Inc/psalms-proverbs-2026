# PSALM 40 — Out of the Mud

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 40:1–17. Half testimony, half new trouble — because that's how life actually runs.*

---

**"I waited — waited — for the Source. And He bent down to me and heard my cry."**

The Hebrew doubles the word. *Kavoh kiviti* — "waiting, I waited." Not a quick wait. The kind where you're still standing there after everyone else went home.

And then — *He bent down*. The word is *natah* — to incline, to lean over. Like a parent leaning down to hear a kid whisper. He had to *bend* to get to David's level.

**"He brought me up out of the roaring pit — out of the muddy clay. He set my feet on a rock. He made my steps firm."**

*The roaring pit.* Some translations say "horrible pit" — the Hebrew is *sha'on*, the sound of rushing water, chaos, noise. A hole where you can't hear yourself think. And *muddy clay* — the kind that grabs your legs, where every step you take to get out sinks you further. Anyone who's been in addiction knows that mud exactly. You move and you go deeper.

He didn't climb out. *He brought me up.* And then — *rock*. Something that doesn't give. First time in a long time the ground held.

**"He put a new song in my mouth — praise to our God. Many will see, and stand in awe, and trust in the Source."**

*Many will see.* Your rescue isn't private. People are watching what happens to the man who was in the mud. When he comes out singing, some of them are going to trust for the first time. Your story is someone else's door.

**"Happy is the man who makes the Source his trust — who doesn't turn to the proud or to those who fall away to lies."**

"You've done so many wonders, Source my God — so many plans for us. **There's nobody like You.** If I tried to declare them, tell them — there are too many to count."

*Too many to count.* Try it sometime. Sit down and list what He's done. You'll run out of paper before you run out of items.

Now — the sacrifice section. This one got quoted in Hebrews 10 as the words of Christ:

**"Sacrifice and offering You didn't want. You've opened my ears. Burnt offering and sin offering You didn't ask for. Then I said: 'Here I am — I've come. In the scroll it's written about me. To do Your will, my God — that's my delight. Your instruction is deep inside me.'"**

*You've opened my ears.* Literally — *You've dug ears for me*. Carved out the capacity to hear. God doesn't want the animal on the altar. He wants a person who *hears* and *shows up*. *Here I am.*

*To do Your will is my delight.* Not duty. *Delight.* The instruction isn't a fence — it's *inside*. Written on the inside walls.

**"I've proclaimed right-standing in the great assembly. Look — I didn't hold back my lips. You know that, Source. I didn't hide Your right-standing in my heart. I spoke of Your faithfulness and Your rescue. I didn't conceal Your loyal-love and truth from the great assembly."**

Five ways of saying *I told people*. He didn't keep the rescue to himself. Psalm 32 was about not hiding the *sin*. This is about not hiding the *salvation*. Both need to come out.

Now — verse 11. The turn. The testimony's done — and trouble's back:

**"You — Source — don't hold back Your compassion from me. Let Your loyal-love and truth guard me continually. Because evils surround me — too many to count. My guilt has overtaken me — I can't see. More than the hairs on my head. My heart has failed me."**

*Too many to count.* Same phrase as verse 5. His *wonders* — too many to count. My *troubles* — too many to count. Both true at once. That's how it is. The man who was pulled out of the mud is back in trouble by verse 12 — and he's not pretending otherwise.

"Be pleased, Source, to rescue me. Source — hurry to help me. Let the ones hunting my life to sweep it away be ashamed and disgraced together. Let the ones who want my harm be turned back in humiliation. Let the ones saying 'Ha! Ha!' at me be appalled at their own shame."

**"Let all who seek You rejoice and be glad in You. Let those who love Your rescue say continually: 'The Source is great!'"**

And then — the last verse. And it's the most honest ending in the first book:

**"But I am poor and needy. May the Master think of me.**
**You're my help and my rescue. My God — don't delay."**

*Poor and needy.* He started the psalm on a rock singing a new song. He ends it *poor and needy*, asking God to *think of him*. Not "rescue me" — *think of me*. Just — don't forget I'm here.

*Don't delay.* The last word.

Here's the shape of this psalm, and it's the shape of a real life: *I was in the mud. He pulled me out. I told everyone. And now I'm in trouble again, and I'm asking Him to hurry.* Both halves are true. The rescue was real. The new trouble is real. You don't have to pick one.

You were in the mud. He bent down. Tell people. And when the next thing comes — *don't delay* is a complete prayer.
