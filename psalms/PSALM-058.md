# PSALM 58 — Break Their Teeth

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 58:1–11. An imprecatory psalm against corrupt judges — the harshest language in the book so far. Don't soften it. Understand it.*

---

Fair warning: this one's violent. And it's *supposed* to be. Because it's about the people who were supposed to protect the innocent and instead sold them.

**"Do you really speak justice — you 'gods'? Do you judge the children of Adam with fairness?"**

*You gods.* Sarcastic. The judges thought of themselves as divine — above the law, untouchable. David calls them what they think they are and then asks the question they can't answer.

**"No — in your heart you work out wrongdoing. Your hands weigh out violence in the land."**

*Weigh out.* Like a merchant on a scale. They're measuring out injustice — calculating exactly how much they can get away with. Not passion. *Business.*

**"The wicked are estranged from the womb. From birth they've gone astray — speaking lies. Their venom is like the venom of a snake. Like a deaf cobra that stops its ear — that won't hear the voice of the charmer, however skilled."**

*A deaf cobra.* You can't reason with it. You can't charm it. The most skilled snake-handler in the world can't get through — because it's *chosen* not to hear. That's the corrupt judge. Not ignorant. *Deaf on purpose.*

Now — the imprecations. Here they come:

**"God — break their teeth in their mouth. Tear out the fangs of the young lions, Source."**

*Break their teeth.* Why teeth? Because that's what a predator uses. A lion with no teeth can still roar — it just can't *kill*. David's not asking for them to be erased. He's asking for them to be *disarmed*. Take away the thing they hurt people with.

That's what every prayer against injustice is actually asking: *stop the hand. Break the teeth. Make them unable.*

**"Let them melt away like water that runs off. When he aims his arrows — let them be blunted. Like a snail that dissolves as it moves. Like a stillborn child that never saw the sun."**

Five images of *disappearing*. Water running off. Blunted arrows. A snail melting into its own trail. A stillborn. Not conquered — *gone*. As if they'd never been.

**"Before your pots feel the heat of the thorns — green or burning — He'll sweep them away."**

Thorns burn fast and hot — you use them for kindling. Before the pot even gets warm, the fire's out. The wicked judge's whole reign — over before the water boils.

Now — here's the verse people can't handle:

**"The one who does right will rejoice when he sees the vengeance. He'll wash his feet in the blood of the wicked."**

Okay. Stop. Sit with that.

*Wash his feet in the blood.* That's an ancient war image — walking through a battlefield where the enemy's fallen. It's not literal for David and it's not literal for you. It means: *the victory will be total and you'll walk through it.*

But here's why it's in the Bible: because if you've been on the wrong end of a corrupt judge — if the system that was supposed to protect you *sold* you — there's a rage in you that's *righteous*. It's not sin. It's what a human being is supposed to feel when justice is perverted. And God gave you a psalm to *pour it into* so you don't pour it into a person.

That's what the imprecatory psalms are *for*. They're not commands. They're *containers*. You bring the rage here — the full, ugly, break-their-teeth rage — and you hand it to God. And you *don't* go break anyone's teeth yourself.

Psalm 37 said *don't get heated*. Psalm 58 says *when you are heated — bring it here*. Both are true. One is the goal. The other is the release valve.

And then — the last verse. The reason the whole thing was written:

**"And people will say: 'Surely there is a reward for the one who does right. Surely there is a God who judges on the earth.'"**

*Surely there is a God who judges on the earth.* That's the payoff. Not *I got my revenge*. *There is a God — and He judges — HERE.* Not just in the afterlife. *On the earth.* The corrupt judge who thought he was a god finds out there's a real one — and the people who'd started to wonder if justice was a fairy tale find out it isn't.

So — when the system sells you: say the whole psalm. Break their teeth. Melt them like snails. Let it out — *to God*. Then walk away with your own hands clean, because the One who judges on the earth has got it.
