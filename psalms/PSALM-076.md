# PSALM 76 — The Warriors Couldn't Lift Their Hands

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 76:1–12. Asaph. A victory song — probably Sennacherib's army dying outside Jerusalem overnight (2 Kings 19). The morning after.*

---

Psalm 74 was the temple in ashes. Psalm 75 was God saying *I choose the time.* Psalm 76 is *the time came.*

**"God is known in Judah. In Israel His name is great."**

*Known.* Not "believed in." *Known.* Because of what just happened. Everyone saw it.

**"His tent is in Salem. His dwelling place in Zion."**

*Salem.* The old name for Jerusalem — and it means *peace*. Same root as *shalom*. He lives in *Peace*.

**"There He broke the flashing arrows — the shields and the swords — the weapons of war."**

*There.* On the spot. Every weapon — broken. Psalm 46 — *He breaks the bow, He shatters the spear.* Now it's a *report*.

**"You are radiant with light — more majestic than mountains rich with game."**

*Radiant.* The Hebrew is *na'or* — illuminated, shining. After the darkness of 74 — *light*.

Now — what happened. And it's told strangely, because what happened *was* strange:

**"The stout-hearted were plundered. They slept their last sleep. Not one of the warriors could lift his hands."**

*Slept their last sleep.* 185,000 Assyrian soldiers went to bed outside the walls of Jerusalem. In the morning — dead. No battle. No siege engine. They just — didn't wake up.

*Not one of the warriors could lift his hands.* The strongest army in the world — and not a hand raised. Not because they were beaten. Because they were *gone*.

**"At Your rebuke, God of Jacob — both horse and rider lie stunned."**

*Stunned.* The Hebrew is *radam* — a deep sleep, a stupor. The horses too. The cavalry that was going to take the city — *asleep*.

**"You alone are to be held in awe. Who can stand before You when You are angry?"**

*Awe.* *Yirah.* Here's where it belongs — the morning after 185,000 men didn't wake up. Not fear of God hurting *you*. Awe at what He *did* without lifting a finger. The whole city walked out to the walls and looked at the silent camp and said — *You. Only You.*

**"From heaven You pronounced judgment. The land was afraid and grew quiet."**

*Grew quiet.* The earth itself went still. Psalm 46 — *be still*. Now the whole *land* is.

**"When You, God, rose up to judge — to save all the afflicted of the land."**

*To save the afflicted.* That's what the judgment was *for*. Not vengeance for its own sake. The people inside the walls — the ones who couldn't fight, the widows, the kids — *saved*. The army died so the afflicted could live.

Now — the verse that's hard to translate and worth the effort:

**"Surely even the wrath of man will praise You. The survivors of wrath — You gird on Yourself."**

Two readings, both true. First: *even human rage ends up praising You* — the Assyrians came to mock God and their corpses became the biggest sermon Jerusalem ever heard. The wrath *turned into* praise. Second: *the survivors of wrath — You wrap around Yourself* like a belt. The ones who lived through the night — He *wears* them. Keeps them close.

Either way — nothing gets wasted. Not even the enemy's rage.

**"Make vows to the Source your God — and fulfill them. Let all the neighboring lands bring gifts to the One to be held in awe."**

*Awe* again. Bring gifts to the One who makes armies sleep.

**"He breaks the spirit of rulers. He is held in awe by the kings of the earth."**

Third *yirah*. *The kings of the earth* — the men who thought they were the ones to be afraid of — *in awe*. They'd sent the army. They heard what happened to it.

Twelve verses. Here's the shape: *He's known. He lives in Peace. He broke every weapon on the spot. The warriors went to sleep and didn't wake up. Not one hand lifted. Horse and rider stunned. Only You are to be held in awe. You did it to save the afflicted. Even the enemy's rage ends up praising You. The kings are in awe now.*

When the army's outside your wall: go to bed. Psalm 3 — *I lay down and slept.* Psalm 75 — *I choose the time.* Psalm 76 — *the time came, and it was overnight.*

Sometimes God's answer to *how long* is *by morning*.
