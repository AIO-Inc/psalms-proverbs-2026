# PSALM 22 — My God, My God, Why?

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 22:1–31. The psalm Jesus quoted from the cross. Written a thousand years before crucifixion existed.*

---

Handle this one carefully. It's the darkest psalm in the first book — and it ends in the brightest place.

**"My God, my God — why have You abandoned me?"**

Jesus said this. Out loud. Nailed to the wood, in the dark, in front of his mother. If you've ever wondered whether you're allowed to say it — He already said it for you.

"Far from my rescue. Far from the words of my groaning. My God, I cry by day — You don't answer. By night — I get no rest."

No answer. Day and night. Not the silence of an empty universe — the silence of a Father who is *there* and *not speaking*. That's worse. And David writes it down.

Then — in the middle of the abandonment — he does the thing that keeps him alive:

"But You are holy. You sit enthroned on the praises of Israel. Our fathers trusted You. They trusted — and You delivered them. They cried to You — and they got out. They trusted You — and they weren't ashamed."

*Our fathers.* He goes back to the record. *You did it before. You did it for them.* When you can't feel God in the present, borrow someone else's past.

Then back down:

"But I'm a worm, not a man. Scorned by people. Despised by the crowd. Everyone who sees me mocks me. They sneer. They shake their heads: 'He trusted God — let God save him! Let Him rescue him, if He's so pleased with him.'"

*If He's so pleased with him.* Read Matthew 27:43 — the priests at the cross said almost exactly this. David wrote the script for his own descendant's execution and didn't know it.

Then the tenderest lines in the psalm:

"But You — You brought me out of the womb. You made me trust when I was at my mother's breast. From birth I was thrown onto You. From my mother's belly, You've been my God."

*Thrown onto You.* Like a newborn placed on a father's chest. He goes all the way back — before the pain, before the words, to the first moment. *You've been mine since before I could speak.*

"Don't be far from me. Trouble is near. There's no one to help."

Now the description — and every line of it happened on Golgotha:

"Bulls surround me. Strong bulls of Bashan close in. They open their mouths at me like ravening, roaring lions.

**I am poured out like water. All my bones are out of joint. My heart has turned to wax — it's melted inside me. My strength is dried up like a broken pot. My tongue sticks to my jaws.**

You've laid me in the dust of death."

Bones out of joint — what hanging by the arms does. Heart melting — the organ failure. Tongue stuck — dehydration. A broken pot — that's what a body sounds like when the breath goes.

"Dogs surround me. A gang of evildoers closes in.

**They've pierced my hands and my feet.**

I can count all my bones. They stare. They gloat. **They divide my clothes among them. They throw dice for my garment.**"

Pierced hands and feet. A thousand years before Rome invented the method. Dice for the clothes — John 19:24, the soldiers did exactly that.

You can't read this as coincidence. Somebody knew.

"But You, Source — don't be far! My strength — hurry, help me! Rescue my life from the sword. My one life from the power of the dog. Save me from the lion's mouth, from the horns of the wild oxen."

And then — verse 21 — mid-sentence, everything changes:

**"You have answered me."**

Three words in Hebrew. No explanation. No transition. The man who was poured out like water, whose bones were showing, whose hands were pierced — says *You answered.* Something happened between the horns of the oxen and the next breath.

Here's what happened next:

**"I will tell of Your name to my brothers. In the middle of the assembly I'll praise You."**

*My brothers.* He's not alone anymore. He's in a room full of people, telling them.

"You who stand in awe of the Source — praise Him! All you children of Jacob — glorify Him! Stand in awe of Him, all you children of Israel! Because He has not despised or detested the affliction of the afflicted. **He hasn't hidden His face from him. When he cried, He heard.**"

*Hasn't hidden His face.* The whole psalm started with *why have You abandoned me*. Now: *He never hid His face.* Both are in the same poem, by the same man. The abandonment was *real* and the presence was *real* and the second one was truer.

"From You comes my praise in the great assembly. I'll pay my vows before the ones who stand in awe of You. **The poor will eat and be satisfied.** The ones who seek Him will praise the Source. May your heart live forever!"

The poor eat. That's the first fruit of the rescue — a meal for the hungry.

Now it goes global:

**"All the ends of the earth will remember and turn to the Source. All the families of the nations will bow before You. Because the kingdom belongs to the Source. He rules over the nations."**

Every family. Every nation. *Remember and turn.* The man who was abandoned becomes the reason the whole world comes home.

"All the well-fed of the earth will eat and bow. All who go down to the dust will kneel before Him — the one who couldn't keep his own soul alive."

Rich and poor. Living and *dying* — "all who go down to the dust." The ones already in the ground kneel too. Death doesn't exempt you from the party.

"A seed will serve Him. It will be told of the Master to the next generation. They'll come and declare His right-standing to a people not yet born —

**He has done it."**

*He has done it.* The last words of the psalm. In Hebrew, *asah* — accomplished, finished, completed.

Jesus's last words, John 19:30: *It is finished.*

Same word. Same psalm. The one He started on the cross with "My God, my God, why" — He finished with its last line. He prayed the whole thing while He died, and the last thing He said was the last thing David wrote.

So — when you're in the first half, when the silence is total and the mockers are right there and your strength is a broken pot: keep going. The psalm doesn't end where you are. Verse 21 is coming. *You have answered me.* And the poor are going to eat, and the nations are going to turn, and a people not yet born are going to hear what He did.

He has done it.
