# PSALM 42 — As the Deer Pants

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 42:1–11. Opens Book Two. Written by the Sons of Korah — temple singers in exile, far from the place they sang. Psalms 42 and 43 are one poem with one refrain, sung three times.*

---

Book One was David. Book Two opens with a different voice — a temple musician who's been dragged far from the temple. He can't get to the one place his whole life was built around.

**"As a deer pants for streams of water — so my soul pants for You, God."**

*Pants.* Not "desires." Not "longs for." The sound a deer makes when it's been running from something and hasn't found water. The open-mouthed, sides-heaving, *I need this or I die* sound.

That's the soul he's describing. Not a pious wish. *Thirst.*

**"My soul thirsts for God — for the living God. When can I come and see the face of God?"**

*The living God.* Not an idea. Not a doctrine. *Living.* And — again — *the face*. David's word. Now the Korahites have picked it up. Everyone who's ever been close to God wants the same thing: not the blessings. The *face*.

*When can I come?* He can't. He's exiled. The question hangs.

**"My tears have been my food day and night — while they say to me all day: 'Where is your God?'"**

*Tears as food.* He's not eating. He's crying instead. And the people around him have the same taunt every day: *Where is He? You sang about Him your whole life — so where is He now?*

You've heard that voice. Maybe from other people. Maybe from inside.

"These things I remember — and I pour out my soul inside me: how I used to go with the crowd, leading them in procession to the house of God — with shouts of joy and thanksgiving, a festival throng."

*How I used to.* He remembers *leading* the parade. Being at the front. Voice raised, crowd behind him, the whole city celebrating. And now he's in a foreign land eating tears.

The memory of the good is what hurts most in the bad.

And now — the refrain. Read this carefully, because he says it three times across the two psalms, and it's the spine of the whole thing:

**"Why are you bowed down, my soul? Why are you in turmoil inside me?**
**Wait for God — because I will praise Him again — the rescue of His face."**

He's talking to *himself*. Asking his own soul *why*. Not accusing — *asking*. Like sitting down next to a crying friend: *hey — what's going on in there?*

And then — the instruction. *Wait. I will praise Him again.* Not "I feel like praising." *I will.* Future tense. A decision made in the dark about what's going to happen in the light.

**"My God — my soul is bowed down inside me. So I remember You — from the land of the Jordan, from the peaks of Hermon, from Mount Mizar."**

*I remember You.* When he can't get to the temple, he brings the temple to his memory. From wherever he is — Jordan, Hermon, some hill nobody's heard of — he points himself back.

**"Deep calls to deep at the roar of Your waterfalls. All Your waves and breakers have gone over me."**

*Deep calls to deep.* The deep in him — the thirst, the grief, the bottomless want — calling out to the deep in God. Abyss to abyss. He's saying: *the size of my emptiness matches the size of what You have. Only Your deep can fill my deep.*

*Your waves.* He's drowning — and he calls the waves *Yours*. Not the enemy's. Not fate's. Even the thing crushing him, he attributes to God — because he'd rather be crushed by God than saved by anything else.

**"By day the Source commands His loyal-love. At night His song is with me — a prayer to the God of my life."**

*His song is with me.* At night. In exile. He's the temple singer — and God's singing back. The music didn't stay in the building.

Then — raw:

"I say to God my Rock: 'Why have You forgotten me? Why do I go around in mourning, oppressed by the enemy?' Like a shattering in my bones — my enemies taunt me, saying to me all day: 'Where is your God?'"

*Shattering in my bones.* The taunt doesn't just hurt his feelings. It *breaks something*.

And the refrain again — same words, second time:

**"Why are you bowed down, my soul? Why are you in turmoil inside me?**
**Wait for God — because I will praise Him again — my rescue and my God."**

Notice the tiny change. First time: *the rescue of His face*. Now: *my rescue and my God*. Closer. Owned. Between the first refrain and the second, something shifted — not the circumstances. The *grip*.

Here's what this psalm gives you: permission to pant. Permission to eat tears. Permission to say *why have You forgotten me* to the God you're panting for. And then a tool — *talk to your own soul*. Sit down next to it. Ask it why. Tell it to wait. Say *I will praise Him again* before you feel it.

Then let deep call to deep.
