# PSALM 131 — Like a Weaned Child

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 131:1–3. Twelfth Song of Ascent. Three verses — one of the shortest in the book. Attributed to David. The quietest psalm in the Psalter, and one of the hardest to actually live.*

---

Psalm 130 was *out of the depths*. Psalm 131 is what the man looks like *after* he's been pulled out. And it's not triumphant. It's *small*.

Three verses. Read them slow.

**"Source — my heart is not proud. My eyes are not raised too high. I do not busy myself with great matters — or with things too wonderful for me."**

*Not proud.* The Hebrew is *lo-gavah libbi* — my heart is not *lifted up*. Psalm 101 — *the proud of heart I won't tolerate*. Psalm 18 — *You save the humble, You bring down the haughty eyes*. David's checked himself. *My heart's not up there.*

*Eyes not raised too high.* Psalm 121 — *I lift my eyes to the hills*. That was the right kind of lifting. This is the wrong kind — the eyes that look *down* on people, the eyes that think they see farther than they do.

*I do not busy myself with great matters.* The Hebrew — *lo-hillachti* — literally *I do not walk about in*. He doesn't *pace around* in the big questions. Not because they don't matter. Because they're *too wonderful for me* — *nifla'ot* — the same word as Psalm 139's *wonderfully made*. Things past his reach.

Here's what that means today. There's a kind of exhaustion that comes from carrying questions you were never asked to answer. *Why did that happen. What's going to happen. What's God doing over there.* You pace around in them at 2 a.m. And David says — *I stopped.* Not *I figured it out*. *I stopped walking in it.*

**"But I have calmed and quieted my soul — like a weaned child with its mother. Like a weaned child is my soul within me."**

*Calmed and quieted.* Two verbs. *Shiviti* — I have leveled, smoothed. *Domamti* — I have stilled. Psalm 62 — *my soul is silent before God*. Psalm 46 — *be still and know*. He *did* something. He put a hand on his own soul and *stilled it*.

And the image — *a weaned child with its mother.*

Not a *nursing* child. That's the thing everyone misses. A nursing child at its mother is *demanding* — rooting, crying, wanting the thing she has. A *weaned* child at its mother is different. It's done wanting the milk. It's just — *there*. Sitting on her lap. Leaning back. Not because it needs anything from her. Because it *wants to be near her*.

That's the picture. Not *give me*. Just *I'm here*.

You know how hard that is? To sit in God's presence and *not* ask for something? Not the healing, not the answer, not the fix. Just — *lean back*. That's the weaned child. Psalm 23 — *I lack nothing*. Psalm 73 — *Whom have I in heaven but You*. Psalm 131 — *I'm on the lap. That's enough.*

Weaning *hurts*, by the way. There's a fight before there's a rest. The child screams for what it used to get. And then — one day — it stops, and finds it likes just *being held*. If God's taken something from you that you used to run to — that's what this is. The scream comes first. Then the lap.

And the last verse — the one that turns a personal psalm into a road song:

**"Israel — put your hope in the Source, both now and forevermore."**

*Israel.* He's been talking about *his* soul. Now he turns to everyone on the road. *You too.* Psalm 130 ended the same way — *Israel, hope in the Source*. Psalm 131 picks it up — *now and forevermore* — Psalm 121's ending. The Ascents echo each other, step by step.

*Hope.* *Yachel* — wait, expect. Not *have all the answers*. *Wait.* The weaned child on the lap isn't figuring out the mother. It's just *waiting with* her.

Three verses. Here's the shape: *My heart's not lifted up. My eyes aren't looking down on anyone. I stopped pacing in the questions too big for me. I stilled my own soul — like a weaned child, not grabbing, just leaning back. And Israel — wait like that too. Now and forever.*

Here's your practice today. One thing you keep pacing around in — one question too wonderful for you. Set it down. Not solved. *Set down.* Then sit for two minutes and ask for nothing. Just be on the lap.

That's the whole psalm. It's short because it doesn't need to be long. Weaned children don't make speeches.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 — *lo-gavah libbi* = "my heart is not lifted up/exalted"; *lo-ramu einai* = "my eyes are not raised"; *lo-hillachti* = "I have not walked about in" — habitual pacing; *bi-g'dolot u-v'nifla'ot* = "in great things and in wonders" — *nifla'ot* is the same root as 139:14. v. 2 — *shiviti* = leveled/composed (some read "I have set/placed"); *domamti* = stilled/silenced, same root as 62:1, 5; *k'gamul alei immo* = "like a weaned one upon its mother" — *gamul* is specifically weaned, not nursing; the image is contentment without demand. Some MSS read *k'gamul alai* = "like a weaned child am I" — reading preserved in the note. v. 3 — *yachel* = wait/hope, same as 130:7; *me-atah v'ad-olam* = "from now and until forever," identical to 121:8 and 125:2. No *yirah* in this psalm.*
