# PSALM 146 — Do Not Put Your Trust in Princes

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 146:1–10. Ten verses. No author — David's voice is done; the book itself is singing now. First of the five closing Hallelujah psalms (146–150), each framed by "Hallelujah" at both ends. Politics, mortality, and a list of the people God actually shows up for.*

---

Five psalms left. Every one of them starts and ends with the same word — *Hallelujah*. *Hal'lu-Yah.* *Praise Yah.* Ten *Hallelujahs* in five psalms — the book's *closing bell*.

But Psalm 146 isn't just praise. It's *praise with a warning built in*.

**"Hallelujah! Praise the Source, my soul. I will praise the Source all my life. I will sing praise to my God as long as I live."**

*Praise the Source, my soul.* *Hal'li nafshi et-YHWH.* Psalm 103, 104 — *bless the Source, my soul*. Psalm 146 — *praise*. The singer's talking to *himself*. Telling his own soul what to do. Because the soul doesn't always want to.

*All my life. As long as I live.* *B'chayyai. B'odi.* *While I still have breath.* That's the deadline. Not *forever* — he's honest — *as long as there's an I to do it*.

And then — the warning. Right in the middle of the praise:

**"Do not put your trust in princes — in mortal men, who cannot save. When their spirit departs — they return to the ground. On that very day their plans come to nothing."**

*Do not put your trust in princes.* *Al-tivt'chu vi-n'divim.* *N'divim* — the *nobles*. The *generous* ones — literally. The people with power who *seem* to hand things out. Psalm 118 — *better to take refuge in the Source than to trust in princes*. Psalm 146 — *don't do it AT ALL*.

*Mortal men, who cannot save.* *B'ven-adam she-ein lo t'shuah.* *A son of Adam — in whom there's no salvation.* Not *little* salvation. *None.* Because of what comes next.

*When their spirit departs — they return to the ground.* *Tetze rucho — yashuv l'admato.* *His breath goes out — he goes back to his dirt.* Genesis 3 — *dust you are, to dust you'll return*. Psalm 104 — *You take away their breath, they die and return to the dust*. Psalm 146 — *and the PRINCE too*. The powerful guy — same dirt.

*On that very day their plans come to nothing.* *Ba-yom ha-hu avdu eshtonotav.* *That day — his plans PERISH.* All the strategy. All the promises. All the *vote for me and I'll*. The day he dies — *gone*. Every campaign is a mortality problem.

This isn't cynicism. It's *math*. Every leader you've ever trusted is going to be dirt. Some already are. So — *don't build on them*. Not because they're bad. Because they're *temporary*.

**"Happy is the one whose help is the God of Jacob — whose hope is in the Source his God."**

*Happy.* *Ashrei.* Psalm 1's word — *happy is the one*. Psalm 144 — *happy are the people*. Psalm 146 — *happy is the one whose help is the GOD OF JACOB*. Not the prince. The God of the guy who wrestled all night and limped away blessed.

Now — *why*. And it's a résumé. Nine lines. Each one *the Source does something*. Read them slow — because this is the list of who He *shows up for*:

**"The Maker of heaven and earth — the sea and everything in them — who remains faithful forever."**

*Maker of heaven and earth.* Psalm 121, 124, 134 — the Ascents' refrain. Psalm 146 — the closing Hallel picks it up. Compare: the *prince* returns to the ground. The *Maker* made the ground.

*Remains faithful forever.* *Ha-shomer emet l'olam.* *Keeps truth to the age.* Psalm 121 — *shomer* — the *keeper*. Psalm 146 — *keeps FAITHFULNESS*. He's guarding His own promise.

**"He upholds the cause of the oppressed."**

*Oseh mishpat la-ashukim.* *Does justice for the crushed.* Psalm 103 — *works righteousness and justice for the oppressed*. Psalm 146 — *first on the list*.

**"He gives food to the hungry."**

*Noten lechem la-r'evim.* Psalm 136 — *food to all flesh*. Psalm 145 — *You open Your hand*. Psalm 146 — *the hungry get bread*. Not the deserving. The *hungry*.

**"The Source sets prisoners free."**

*YHWH mattir asurim.* *Looses the bound.* Psalm 142 — *bring me out of my prison*. Psalm 146 — *He does that*.

**"The Source gives sight to the blind."**

*YHWH pokeach ivrim.* *Opens the blind.* Psalm 145 — *poteach* — *opens His hand*. Psalm 146 — *pokeach* — *opens eyes*. Same sound. Same God. Isaiah 35, 42 — *the eyes of the blind will be opened*. Luke 4 — Jesus reads Isaiah 61 in Nazareth — *recovery of sight to the blind* — and says *today this is fulfilled*. Psalm 146 was already on the list.

**"The Source lifts up those who are bowed down."**

*YHWH zokef k'fufim.* Psalm 145 — *lifts up all who are bowed down*. Psalm 146 — *same word*. *K'fufim* — the *bent*. Two psalms in a row. The editors wanted you to hear it twice — *He straightens the bent*.

**"The Source loves the righteous."**

*YHWH ohev tzaddikim.* Plain. *Loves.* Not *rewards*. *Loves.* Psalm 11 — *the Source is righteous, He loves justice*. Psalm 146 — *loves the ones who DO it*.

**"The Source watches over the foreigner — and sustains the fatherless and the widow."**

*The foreigner.* *Gerim.* The *stranger*. The immigrant. Deuteronomy 10 — *He defends the cause of the fatherless and the widow, and loves the foreigner*. Psalm 146 — *shomer gerim* — *keeps the strangers*. The keeper from Psalm 121 — *keeps the outsider* too. Exodus 22, Deuteronomy 24, Leviticus 19 — *you were foreigners in Egypt*. The three-word list — *stranger, orphan, widow* — is the Torah's *definition* of the unprotected. Psalm 146 — *He's their protection*.

That's nine. Oppressed, hungry, prisoners, blind, bent, righteous, foreigner, orphan, widow. Not one prince on the list. Not one king. Not one *n'div*. The résumé of God is *entirely* people the princes forgot.

**"But the way of the wicked He frustrates."**

*V'derech r'sha'im y'avvet.* *The road of the mark-missers He BENDS.* *Avvet* — *twists*. Makes crooked. The ones who *bend* others — Psalm 146 — *He bends their road*. Psalm 1 — *the way of the wicked perishes*. Psalm 146 — *gets twisted into a dead end*.

**"The Source reigns forever — your God, O Zion, for all generations. Hallelujah!"**

*The Source reigns forever.* *Yimloch YHWH l'olam.* The *prince* — back to the ground. The *Source* — *reigns forever*. That's the whole psalm in one contrast. Psalm 93, 96–99 — *the Source reigns*. Psalm 146 — *and the princes DON'T*.

*Your God, O Zion.* The last time the book says *Zion* in address. *Your* God. Not the empire's. *Yours.*

Ten verses. Here's the shape: *Hallelujah. Praise Him, my soul — while I've got breath. — Don't trust princes. They're dirt with a schedule. Their plans die when they do. — Happy is the one whose help is Jacob's God. The Maker. Faithful forever. Justice for the crushed. Bread for the hungry. Prisoners loose. Blind eyes open. Bent backs straight. Loves the righteous. Keeps the stranger, the orphan, the widow. Twists the wicked's road. — He reigns forever. Hallelujah.*

Here's what to do. Next time you catch yourself putting hope in a *prince* — a candidate, a boss, a leader, a *person who cannot save* — say verse four. *When their breath goes, they return to the ground.* Then read the list. Nine things. And ask — *am I on it?* If you're hungry, bent, stranger, grieving — you are. If you're not — the list is your *job description*. Go be the hands.

He opens the eyes. He straightens the back. He keeps the stranger. Hallelujah.

*Note on translation: First of the closing Hallel (146–150), each bracketed by *hal'lu-Yah*. v. 1–2 — *hal'li nafshi* = "praise, my soul"; *b'odi* = "while I still am." **v. 3 — *al-tivt'chu vi-n'divim* = "do not trust in nobles/princes" — *nadiv* = noble, lit. "generous/willing one"; *b'ven-adam she-ein lo t'shuah* = "in a son of man in whom there is no salvation" (cf. 118:8–9; 60:11).** v. 4 — *tetze rucho yashuv l'admato* = "his breath goes out, he returns to his ground" (Gen 3:19; cf. 104:29; Eccl 12:7); *eshtonotav* = his plans/thoughts (hapax; Aramaic loanword). v. 5 — *ashrei* = happy; *El Ya'akov* = God of Jacob. v. 6 — *oseh shamayim va-aretz* — Ascents formula (121:2; 124:8; 134:3); *ha-shomer emet l'olam* = "keeping truth/faithfulness forever." v. 7–9 — nine participial clauses; *ashukim* = oppressed; *asurim* = bound ones; *pokeach ivrim* = "opening the blind" (cf. Isa 35:5; 42:7; Luke 4:18; 7:22); *zokef k'fufim* = "straightening the bent" — identical to 145:14b; *gerim* = sojourners/foreigners; *yatom v'almanah* = orphan and widow — Torah triad (Exod 22:21–22; Deut 10:18; 24:17–21; 27:19). v. 9c — *y'avvet* = He bends/twists/makes crooked. v. 10 — *yimloch YHWH l'olam* = "the Source will reign forever" (cf. Exod 15:18; 93:1; 96:10; 97:1; 99:1); *Elohayich Tziyyon* = "your God, O Zion." No *yirah* in this psalm.*
