# PSALM 56 — You Keep My Tears in a Bottle

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 56:1–13. Written when the Philistines seized David in Gath — the same episode as Psalm 34, but this is the "during," not the "after."*

---

Psalm 34 was David *after* he escaped Gath — *taste and see*. Psalm 56 is David *in* Gath. Caught. Surrounded. Not sure he's getting out.

"Be gracious to me, God — because man tramples me. All day long the attacker presses me. My watchers trample me all day. Many fight against me from on high."

*All day long.* Twice. The pressure doesn't let up. There's no break, no shift change.

*My watchers.* He's being *observed*. Every move tracked. In an enemy city, recognized, with eyes on him constantly.

And then — the refrain. He says it twice, and it's the spine:

**"The day I'm afraid — I trust in You."**

Read that carefully. Not *I'm not afraid.* Not *I've conquered fear.* *The day I'm afraid* — on *that* day — *I trust.* Fear and trust in the same sentence, same breath. The fear doesn't cancel the trust. The trust doesn't cancel the fear. They ride together.

That's permission. You don't have to stop being afraid to start trusting. Do them both.

**"In God — whose word I praise — in God I trust. I will not be afraid. What can flesh do to me?"**

*What can flesh do to me?* It's not a boast. He's *in Gath*. Flesh can do plenty — flesh has him surrounded. He's asking the question anyway, because the answer, at the deepest level, is *nothing that lasts.*

"All day long they twist my words. All their plans are against me — for evil. They gather. They lurk. They watch my steps — waiting to take my life."

*Twist my words.* Everything he says gets spun. That's part of the trap — you can't even speak without it being used.

"Because of their wrongdoing — will they escape? In anger, God — bring the peoples down."

And then — the verse this psalm is loved for:

**"You've counted my wanderings. Put my tears in Your bottle. Aren't they in Your book?"**

*Counted my wanderings.* Every time he had to run. Every cave. Every night on the ground in a foreign country. *Counted.* Not one missed.

*Put my tears in Your bottle.* In the ancient world, mourners sometimes collected tears in small flasks — evidence of grief, proof of what was suffered. David's saying: *You keep mine. You have a bottle with my name on it. Every one I've cried is in there.*

*Aren't they in Your book?* The wanderings — counted. The tears — bottled. Both — *written down*. Nothing wasted. Nothing lost. Every night you cried alone, thinking nobody saw — it's in a book, and He's the author.

If you've cried where nobody could see — He saw. It's bottled. It's recorded. It *counts*.

"Then my enemies will turn back — on the day I call. This I know: God is for me."

*This I know.* Four words. *God is for me.* Not neutral. Not observing. *For.* On his side. In Gath, in the trap, with the watchers — *for me*.

And the refrain, second time — slightly changed:

**"In God — whose word I praise — in the Source, whose word I praise — in God I trust. I will not be afraid. What can man do to me?"**

First time it was *flesh*. Now it's *man*. He's gotten more specific — he's looked his captors in the face and called them what they are. *Man.* Not gods. Not fate. Just men. And men can only do so much.

**"Your vows are on me, God. I will pay You thank-offerings. Because You've rescued my soul from death. Haven't You kept my feet from stumbling — so I can walk before God in the light of the living?"**

*In the light of the living.* He's still in Gath when he says it. Still surrounded. And he's already thanking God for being alive — because he's decided how this ends before it's ended.

Here's the psalm: *I'm caught. They're watching. The day I'm afraid — I trust. You've counted every night I ran. You've bottled every tear. It's in Your book. This I know — God is for me. I'll walk in the light of the living.*

The day you're afraid — same day — trust. And know that the bottle's full and the book's open.

*Note on translation: v. 3 —* yom ira *= "the day I fear" — ordinary human dread (*yare*), correctly rendered; the point is fear and trust coexisting. v. 8 —* nodi *= my wandering/tossing;* simah dim'ati v'nodecha *= "put my tears in Your bottle/flask" — wordplay:* nod *(wandering) and* nod *(flask) are homographs. v. 9 —* zeh-yadati ki-Elohim li *= "this I know, that God is for me" — cf. Romans 8:31. v. 13 —* b'or ha-chayim *= in the light of the living/of life.*
