# PSALM 28 — Don't Be Silent to Me

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 28:1–9.*

---

"To You, Source, I call. My Rock — don't be deaf to me. **If You're silent to me, I'll be like the ones going down to the pit.**"

Here's what he's saying: *Your silence is the one thing I can't survive.* Not the enemies. Not the danger. If You don't answer — I'm as good as buried.

That's a bold thing to tell God. It's also the truest thing a person can say. The worst hell isn't fire. It's a phone that never rings.

"Hear the voice of my pleading when I cry to You for help — when I lift my hands toward Your holy inner room."

*Lift my hands.* Body language. The oldest prayer posture there is — palms up, empty, reaching. You don't need words for that one.

Now the ask about the wrong crowd — and it's specific:

"Don't drag me off with the harmful ones — the workers of wrong — **who speak peace to their neighbors while evil is in their hearts.**"

*Speak peace with evil in their hearts.* Same disease as Psalm 12's double heart. Smiling at you while planning against you. David's not asking for them to be punished here — he's asking *not to be lumped in with them*. Not to be sorted into the wrong pile when the sorting happens.

"Give them according to their work — according to the evil of what they do. Give them according to the deeds of their hands. Return to them what they've earned."

Notice — he's not asking for anything *extra*. Just: *give them what they built.* That's not vengeance. That's *accounting*. He's asking God to let the math work.

**"Because they don't pay attention to the works of the Source — to what His hands have made — He'll tear them down and not rebuild them."**

*They don't pay attention.* That's the root sin — not violence, not lying. *Inattention.* Walking past the sunrise, the newborn, the healed friend, and never once thinking *Someone did this.* Psalm 19 said the heavens pour out speech. These are the people who never listened.

Then — mid-psalm — the phone rings:

**"Blessed be the Source — because He has heard the voice of my pleading."**

*Has heard.* Past tense. Verse 1 was *don't be deaf*. Verse 6 is *He heard.* Five verses. That's how long the silence lasted.

**"The Source is my strength and my shield. My heart trusted in Him — and I was helped. So my heart celebrates, and with my song I thank Him."**

*I trusted — and I was helped.* Cause, effect. He's laying it out for you as a pattern: this is how it goes. Trust first, help second. The order matters. If you wait for the help to start trusting, you'll wait forever.

"The Source is strength to His people. He's the stronghold of rescue for His anointed."

And then — like Psalm 25 — he can't end with just himself:

**"Save Your people. Bless Your inheritance. Shepherd them and carry them forever."**

*Shepherd them and carry them.* Two verbs. Lead — and when they can't walk anymore, *carry*. That's the whole job description. Psalm 23 told you He's a shepherd. Psalm 28 tells you what a shepherd does when the sheep gives out: picks it up.

*Forever.* No expiration on the carrying.

So — when the silence is killing you: say so. *If You're silent, I'm as good as dead.* He can take it. And then lift your hands, and wait for verse 6.
