# PSALM 39 — A Handbreadth

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 39:1–13. A meditation on how short life is — and how to talk to God about it.*

---

"I said: I'll guard my ways so I don't miss the mark with my tongue. I'll keep a muzzle on my mouth while the mark-misser is in front of me."

*A muzzle.* He knows if he opens his mouth around this guy, he's going to say something he'll regret. So he shuts it. Deliberately.

**"I was silent — completely still. I held my peace — even from good. And my pain got worse."**

Here's the honest part. He kept quiet — and it *didn't help*. The silence backed up. Everything he wasn't saying to the wicked man, he wasn't saying to God either. And the pressure built.

"My heart was hot inside me. As I brooded, the fire burned. **Then I spoke — with my tongue:**"

*Then I spoke.* Not to the enemy — to God. That's the release valve. The muzzle stays on for the wrong audience. It comes off for the right One.

And what comes out is one of the most honest things a human has ever said to God:

**"Source — let me know my end. What's the measure of my days? Let me know how fleeting I am."**

*Let me know how fleeting I am.* He's not asking for a date. He's asking to *feel* it. To have the shortness of his life made real to him — because knowing you're temporary changes how you spend today.

**"Look — You've made my days a few handbreadths. My lifetime is as nothing in front of You. Every man standing is only breath."**

*A handbreadth.* Four fingers. The smallest unit of measurement they had. That's your life. Not a mile. Not a yard. *Four fingers.*

*Only breath.* The Hebrew is *hevel* — vapor, the fog that comes off your mouth on a cold morning and is gone before you finish looking at it. That's the word Ecclesiastes uses forty times. *Breath. Vapor. Gone.*

**"Man walks around as a shadow. He rushes around for nothing. He piles up — and doesn't know who'll gather it."**

*He piles up and doesn't know who gets it.* Every estate. Every hoard. You spend forty years stacking, and someone you may never meet divides it up in an afternoon.

Then the turn — and it's a question:

**"And now, Master — what am I waiting for? My hope is in You."**

*What am I waiting for?* If life is four fingers wide and I'm going to leave it all to strangers — what's the point? And the answer is *You*. Not the pile. Not the legacy. *You.* The only thing that doesn't evaporate.

"Rescue me from all my rebellions. Don't make me the mockery of fools."

**"I'm silent. I won't open my mouth. Because You've done this."**

The muzzle again — but different now. Before, it was silence *from* frustration. Now it's silence *of* acceptance. *You did this. I'm not going to argue.*

"Remove Your blow from me. I'm being finished off by the strike of Your hand. **With correction for guilt You discipline a man. You consume what he treasures like a moth.** Every man is only breath."

*Like a moth.* Quiet. Invisible. You open the closet and the coat you loved has holes in it. That's how what you treasure goes — not in an explosion. In a slow, silent eating.

*Every man is only breath.* Second time. He wants you to sit with it.

"Hear my prayer, Source. Listen to my cry. Don't be deaf to my tears. **Because I'm a guest with You — a temporary resident, like all my fathers.**"

*A guest.* The Hebrew is *ger* — a foreigner, a sojourner, someone passing through with no permanent address. *That's what I am here. Just visiting. Like everyone before me.*

But — *with You*. A guest *with You*. Not alone in a strange country. Staying at *His* place. Temporary — but hosted.

And then the ending, and it's not tidy:

**"Look away from me — so I can smile again, before I go and am no more."**

*Look away.* Not "come closer." *Look away.* Ease up. Let me have a little joy before the vapor's gone.

That's how it ends. Not resolved. A man asking for a few good days.

Here's what to do with this psalm: let it make you small. Four fingers. Vapor. A guest. Then — with all that pressing on you — ask the question: *what am I waiting for?* If the answer's a pile, you're going to hand it to a stranger. If the answer's *You* — then the handbreadth is enough.
