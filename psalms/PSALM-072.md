# PSALM 72 — The King Who Hears the Poor

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 72:1–20. A prayer for the king — attributed to Solomon, or for him. The last psalm of Book Two. And a portrait of a King no earthly throne has ever produced.*

---

This is what a king is *supposed* to be. Read it as a job description. Then read it as a prophecy. Both are true.

**"God — give the king Your justice. The king's son — Your right-standing."**

*Your* justice. Not his own. The first thing asked for the king isn't power or wealth or victory. It's *God's sense of right*, installed in the man on the throne.

**"Let him judge Your people with right-standing — Your poor with justice."**

*Your poor.* God's poor. The first group named. Not the nobles, not the army — *the poor*. They're who the justice is *for*.

"Let the mountains bring peace to the people — and the hills, right-standing."

*The mountains bring peace.* When the king rules right, even the *landscape* gets better. The land itself produces peace like a crop.

**"Let him defend the afflicted among the people. Let him save the children of the needy. Let him crush the oppressor."**

Three verbs. *Defend. Save. Crush.* Defend the hurt. Save the poor kids. And *crush* — the one word of violence — aimed at exactly one target: *the one who oppresses them.* The king's sword has one job.

**"Let them stand in awe of You as long as the sun endures — as long as the moon — through all generations."**

*Awe.* *Yirah.* As long as the sun's up. That's the timeline.

**"Let him be like rain falling on mown grass — like showers watering the earth."**

*Rain on mown grass.* The field's been cut. It's stubble. It looks dead. And the rain comes — and it *greens*. That's what this king does to a people who've been cut down. He's *rain*.

**"In his days let the one who does right flourish — and abundance of peace — until the moon is no more."**

*Until the moon is no more.* Not a term limit. *Forever.* No human king gets that. The psalm already knows it's describing Someone else.

**"Let him rule from sea to sea — from the River to the ends of the earth."**

*Ends of the earth.* Book Two's obsession — the *nations*, the whole map. The king isn't for one country.

"Let the desert tribes bow before him. Let his enemies lick the dust. Let the kings of Tarshish and the islands bring tribute. Let the kings of Sheba and Seba present gifts. **Let all kings bow to him. Let all nations serve him."**

*All kings. All nations.* The Magi brought gold and frankincense to a child in Bethlehem — and the church has read *Sheba's gifts* here ever since.

Now — the *reason*. Why does every nation bow to this king? Not because he conquered them. Because of this:

**"Because he rescues the needy when they cry — and the afflicted who has no helper."**

*Who has no helper.* The person with *nobody*. No lawyer, no advocate, no family, no money. *That's* who this king rescues. That's what makes the nations bow — they've never seen a king who bends down for the one with no one.

**"He has compassion on the weak and the needy. He saves the lives of the needy."**

*Compassion.* The Hebrew is *chus* — to pity, to spare, to feel it in the gut. A king who *feels it*.

**"He redeems their life from oppression and violence. Their blood is precious in his eyes."**

*Precious in his eyes.* The poor man's blood — the blood nobody else counts — this king counts it *precious*. The word is *yakar* — Psalm 36, *how precious is Your loyal-love*. Same weight. The king weighs the blood of the nobody at the same value God weighs His own love.

"Long may he live! Let the gold of Sheba be given to him. Let prayer be made for him continually. Let them bless him all day long."

**"Let there be abundance of grain in the land — on the tops of the mountains let it wave. Let its fruit flourish like Lebanon. Let people blossom in the cities like grass of the field."**

*Grain on the mountaintops.* Where nothing grows — a *harvest*. *People blossoming like grass.* Under this king, *humans* flourish like a crop after rain.

**"Let his name endure forever. Let his name continue as long as the sun. Let all nations be blessed in him. Let them call him happy."**

*All nations blessed in him.* Genesis 12:3 — Abraham's promise. *In you all nations will be blessed.* The king is where the promise lands.

Then — the doxology. Book Two closing:

**"Blessed be the Source God — the God of Israel — who alone does wonders. Blessed be His glorious name forever. Let the whole earth be filled with His glory. Amen and amen."**

*The whole earth filled with His glory.* Book Two started with a deer panting in exile (Psalm 42). It ends with the whole earth *full*.

And one more line — an editor's note, honest as anything in the Bible:

**"The prayers of David son of Jesse are ended."**

Not that David wrote no more — Books Three through Five have more. But *this collection* — the man who started with *why are You so far off* and ended praying for a King who hears the poor — this stretch is done.

Twenty verses. Here's the King: *God's justice, not his own. For the poor first. Defends, saves, crushes the oppressor. Rain on mown grass. Rules to the ends of the earth — because he rescues the one with no helper. Their blood is precious to him. Grain on mountaintops. All nations blessed in him.*

No throne has produced that. One manger did.
