# PSALM 35 — Fight My Fight

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 35:1–28. An imprecatory psalm — David asks God to take his side against people he once helped.*

---

Some psalms are hard to read because they're sad. This one's hard because it's *angry*. Let it be.

**"Contend, Source, with the ones contending with me. Fight the ones fighting me."**

*You take the fight.* That's the first request — not "give me strength to fight" but "*You* fight." David steps back and hands God the case.

"Take up shield and armor. Rise up to help me. Draw the spear and the javelin against my pursuers. **Say to my soul: 'I am your rescue.'**"

That middle line — *say to my soul*. Not "rescue me." *Tell me You will.* Sometimes you don't need the rescue yet. You need the sentence.

Then the request against the enemies — and it's not gentle:

"Let the ones who hunt my life be ashamed and disgraced. Let the ones plotting harm be turned back and humiliated. **Let them be like chaff in the wind — with the messenger of the Source driving them.** Let their road be dark and slippery — with the messenger of the Source chasing them."

Same angel who *camped around* David in Psalm 34 — now *chasing* the enemies. One messenger, two jobs.

"Because for no reason they hid their net for me. For no reason they dug a pit for my life. Let ruin come on them by surprise. Let the net they hid catch them. Let them fall into that same ruin."

*For no reason.* Twice. David hadn't wronged them. That's what makes this psalm burn — it's unprovoked.

"Then my soul will celebrate in the Source. I'll rejoice in His rescue. **My whole body will say: 'Who is like You, Source? You rescue the poor from the one too strong for him. The poor and needy from the one who robs him.'**"

*My whole body.* Every bone praising. Because the rescue is *physical* — the weak guy got saved from the strong guy.

Now — the part that hurts most:

"Violent witnesses stand up. They accuse me of things I don't know anything about. **They repay me evil for good — my soul is bereaved.**"

**"But I — when they were sick, I wore sackcloth. I humbled myself with fasting. My prayer came back to my own chest. I went around like it was my own brother, my own mother — bowed down, mourning."**

Do you see what he's saying? *These are people I sat with in the hospital.* He fasted for them. Grieved for them like family. And now they're the ones testifying against him.

That's the wound. Not enemies. *Former friends.* People you showed up for who didn't show up for you — who showed up *against* you.

"But when I stumbled, they gathered and celebrated. Strangers I didn't know gathered against me. They tore at me and wouldn't stop. Like godless mockers at a party, they ground their teeth at me."

Then he turns and asks God the question everyone in this situation asks:

**"Master — how long will You watch?"**

*Watch.* He's not accusing God of absence. He's accusing Him of *spectating*. Standing there while it happens. *You see this. Do something.*

"Rescue my soul from their destruction — my one life from the lions. **Then I'll thank You in the great assembly. Among a huge crowd I'll praise You.**"

The rescue always ends in a crowd. David never gets saved privately. He gets saved so he can *tell people*.

"Don't let them celebrate over me — the ones who are my enemies for no reason. The ones who hate me without cause — winking their eyes. **They don't speak peace.** Against the quiet ones of the land they cook up deceit."

*Against the quiet ones.* The people who weren't bothering anyone. The ones minding their business. That's who these predators target.

"They open their mouths wide against me. They say: 'Ha! Ha! We saw it with our own eyes!'"

**"You saw it too, Source. Don't be silent. Master — don't be far from me."**

*You saw it too.* Their "we saw it" — mockery. His "You saw it" — evidence. Same event. Two witnesses. Only one of them is the Judge.

"Wake up! Get up for my case, my God and my Master — for my cause. Judge me according to Your right-standing, Source my God. Don't let them celebrate. Don't let them say in their heart: 'Ha! Just what we wanted!' Don't let them say: 'We swallowed him.'"

"Let the ones celebrating my hurt be ashamed and disgraced together. Let the ones puffing themselves up over me be clothed in shame and humiliation."

And then — David flips the whole thing. Same words, other direction:

**"Let the ones who want my right-standing shout for joy and be glad! Let them say continually: 'The Source is great — who delights in the peace of His servant!'"**

*Who delights in the peace of His servant.* That's what God wants for you. *Peace.* He's *delighted* by it. The whole imprecation — all the anger, all the "let them be ashamed" — it's in service of one thing: *a servant at peace.*

**"My tongue will speak of Your right-standing — Your praise, all day long."**

So — when someone you showed up for turns on you: hand God the case. *You fight.* Tell Him how long. Tell Him You saw it too. And then stand back and let Him work — because what He's after isn't their destruction. It's your peace. That's what He delights in.
