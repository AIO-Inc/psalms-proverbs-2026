# PSALM 50 — I Don't Need Your Bulls

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 50:1–23. Written by Asaph. God as judge — putting His own people on trial, not for the sins you'd expect.*

---

This is a courtroom psalm. God's the judge. His *own people* are the defendants. And the charge isn't what you think.

**"The Mighty One — God — the Source has spoken. He summons the earth from sunrise to sunset. From Zion — perfect in beauty — God shines out."**

*Summons the earth.* Subpoena. Everyone. From where the sun comes up to where it goes down.

"Our God comes — He will not be silent. Fire devours in front of Him. A storm rages around Him. **He calls to the heavens above and to the earth — to judge His people.**"

*His people.* Not the pagans. Not the enemies. The ones who go to the temple. That's who's on trial.

**"Gather My loyal ones to Me — the ones who made a covenant with Me by sacrifice."**

They *did* make a covenant. They *did* bring sacrifices. They've been doing everything right, ritually. Watch what God says about it.

"The heavens declare His right-standing — because God Himself is judge."

Now — God speaks. First person. And He starts with the religious people:

**"Hear, My people — and I will speak. Israel — I will testify against you. I am God — your God."**

*Your God.* He's not disowning them. *Your* God. He's testifying *as theirs*. A Father in the witness stand about His own kids.

**"I'm not rebuking you for your sacrifices. Your burnt offerings are always in front of Me."**

*I'm not rebuking you for that.* You did the rituals. You showed up. Fine.

**"I won't take a bull from your house — or goats from your pens. Because every animal of the forest is Mine. The cattle on a thousand hills. I know every bird in the mountains. Everything moving in the field is Mine."**

Here's the correction. *You think you're giving Me something?* He owns the bull before it's on the altar. He owns the goat, the field the goat ate from, the hill the field is on, and the thousand hills behind it.

**"If I were hungry, I wouldn't tell you — because the world is Mine, and everything in it. Do I eat the flesh of bulls? Do I drink the blood of goats?"**

He's being *sarcastic*. *You think I'm hungry? You think your barbecue is feeding Me?* The whole sacrifice system was never about God needing something from them. They'd forgotten that. They'd turned it into a transaction — *I bring the bull, You bring the blessing.* God's saying: *that's not the deal. That was never the deal.*

So what does He want?

**"Offer God thanksgiving. Fulfill your vows to the Most High. Call on Me in the day of trouble — I will rescue you — and you will glorify Me."**

Three things. *Thank Me. Keep your word. Call on Me when it's bad.* That's it. Not bulls. *Gratitude, integrity, and dependence.* You can do all three with empty hands.

*Call on Me in the day of trouble — I will rescue you.* Read it as a promise, because it is. Not "if you've sacrificed enough." *Call — I rescue.* That's the whole exchange.

Now — the second group. The ones who *talk* religion:

**"But to the mark-misser God says: 'What right do you have to recite My decrees — to take My covenant on your lips? You hate discipline. You throw My words behind you.'"**

*You quote Me — and you hate what I say.* The Bible on the lips, the Bible in the trash. God's not impressed by people who can recite Him.

**"You see a thief — you run with him. You keep company with adulterers. You give your mouth to evil. Your tongue is harnessed to deceit. You sit and speak against your brother. You slander your own mother's son."**

Look at the list. Theft. Adultery. Lying. Slander — *of your own brother*. That's the indictment. Not "you didn't bring enough goats." *You hurt people while quoting Scripture.*

**"You did these things — and I kept silent. So you thought I was just like you."**

There it is. The most dangerous line in the psalm. *I was quiet — so you assumed I approved.* People do that. God doesn't strike immediately, and they conclude He's fine with it. He's not. He's *patient*. Those aren't the same thing.

**"I will rebuke you. I'll lay out the case in front of your eyes."**

And then — the summary. God's closing argument. And it's not about sacrifice at all:

**"Understand this — you who forget God — or I'll tear you apart and no one will rescue you.**
**The one who offers thanksgiving honors Me. And the one who sets his road right — I will show him the rescue of God."**

*Forget God.* That's the root sin — same as Psalm 9, Psalm 10. Not disbelief. *Forgetting.* Doing the ritual with no memory of Who it's for.

And the two things that *do* work: *thanksgiving* and *setting your road right*. Gratitude and direction. Not a bull in sight.

Here's the psalm in one line: *God doesn't need anything from you. He wants you to be grateful, to keep your word, to call Him when it's bad, and to stop hurting people while quoting His book.* That's the whole trial.

The bull was never the point. *You* were.
