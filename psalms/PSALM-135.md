# PSALM 135 — Their Idols Have Mouths But Cannot Speak

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 135:1–21. Twenty-one verses. The Ascents are done; this is the first of two great praise psalms that follow (135, 136). A mosaic — almost every line borrowed from another psalm or from Deuteronomy. Israel singing its own greatest hits, on purpose.*

---

Psalm 134 ended in the dark with a blessing. Psalm 135 turns the lights on. And it's *loud*.

**"Hallelujah! Praise the name of the Source — praise Him, you servants of the Source — you who stand in the house of the Source — in the courts of the house of our God."**

*You who stand in the house.* Psalm 134's night watch — still there. Now it's morning, and the whole crowd's back. *Praise.* *Hallelu* — three times in two verses.

**"Praise the Source — for the Source is good. Sing to His name — for it is pleasant. For the Source has chosen Jacob to be His own — Israel to be His treasured possession."**

*Chosen.* *Bachar.* Psalm 132 — *the Source has chosen Zion*. Here — *chosen Jacob*. Same word. Same act. He picked a hill *and* a people.

*Treasured possession.* *Segullah.* Exodus 19 — *you will be My treasured possession*. Deuteronomy 7 — *the Source chose you… because He loved you*. It's a word for *personal property* — the thing a king keeps in his own chest, not the state treasury. Not *useful*. *Treasured*.

**"I know that the Source is great — that our Master is greater than all gods."**

*I know.* First person. In a *crowd* psalm — one voice says *I know*. Psalm 20 — *now I know the Source saves His anointed*. Psalm 56 — *this I know: God is for me*. You can stand in a crowd of ten thousand and still have to know it *yourself*.

Now — the *evidence*. Three exhibits. Creation, Exodus, Conquest.

**"The Source does whatever pleases Him — in the heavens and on the earth — in the seas and all their depths. He makes clouds rise from the ends of the earth — He sends lightning with the rain — He brings out the wind from His storehouses."**

*Whatever pleases Him.* Psalm 115 — *our God is in heaven; He does whatever pleases Him*. Nobody's editing His to-do list.

*Wind from His storehouses.* Psalm 33 — *He puts the deep into storehouses*. Jeremiah 10 — *He brings out the wind from His storehouses*. God's got a *warehouse*. Weather is *inventory*. He decides what ships today.

**"He struck down the firstborn of Egypt — of people and animals alike. He sent His signs and wonders into your midst, Egypt — against Pharaoh and all his servants."**

Exodus. Psalm 78, 105, 106 — told it already. Psalm 135 tells it in *two verses*. Because by now — you *know* the story. This is the *headline*, not the article.

**"He struck down many nations — and killed mighty kings — Sihon king of the Amorites — Og king of Bashan — and all the kings of Canaan — and He gave their land as an inheritance — an inheritance to His people Israel."**

*Sihon. Og.* Numbers 21. The two kings east of the Jordan who tried to block the road. Psalm 136 will name them too. They're the *pattern* — two guys who stood in the way and aren't there anymore.

**"Your name, Source, endures forever — Your renown, Source, through all generations. For the Source will vindicate His people — and have compassion on His servants."**

*Vindicate.* *Yadin* — He will *judge for* them. Deuteronomy 32 — *the Source will vindicate His people and have compassion on His servants* — the psalmist is *quoting Moses' last song*. Word for word.

*Compassion.* *Yitnecham* — the same root as *comfort*. He *relents* toward them. Judges, then softens. Psalm 103 — *as a father has compassion*.

Now — the *other* exhibit. The comparison. And it's savage:

**"The idols of the nations are silver and gold — made by human hands. They have mouths — but cannot speak. Eyes — but cannot see. They have ears — but cannot hear — nor is there breath in their mouths."**

Psalm 115 — word for word. *Mouths that can't speak. Eyes that can't see.* And Psalm 135 adds one thing — *nor is there breath in their mouths*. *Ruach.* No spirit. No wind. The God who *brings the wind out of His storehouses* — versus a thing with *no breath in it at all*.

And then the line that'll haunt you:

**"Those who make them will be like them — and so will all who trust in them."**

*Like them.* You *become* what you worship. Make a god with no ears — you go deaf. Make a god with no eyes — you go blind. Make a god with no breath — you stop breathing. Psalm 115 said it first. Psalm 135 says it again because it's the *most important warning in the book*.

Look at what you trust. The account. The reputation. The person. The substance. Does it have breath in it? Can it *speak back*? Can it *see you*? If not — you're turning into it. Slowly. That's not a threat. It's a *diagnosis*.

**"House of Israel — bless the Source. House of Aaron — bless the Source. House of Levi — bless the Source. You who stand in awe of Him — bless the Source."**

*House of Levi.* Psalm 115 had Israel, Aaron, and *those in awe*. Psalm 135 adds *Levi* — the night watch from 134 gets their own line.

*You who stand in awe of Him.* *Yir'ei YHWH.* Not *fear*. *Awe.* The ones who *know* — verse five — *I know that the Source is great*. That knowing *is* the awe. And they're listed as a *fourth house* — alongside Israel, Aaron, Levi. Not a tribe. Not a bloodline. A *posture*. Anybody can be in that house.

**"Blessed be the Source from Zion — He who dwells in Jerusalem. Hallelujah!"**

*From Zion.* Psalm 134 — *the Source bless you from Zion*. Psalm 135 — *blessed be the Source from Zion*. The blessing goes both ways.

Twenty-one verses. Here's the shape: *Hallelujah — praise Him, you standing in the house. He CHOSE Jacob — treasured. I KNOW He's great. He does what He wants — weather from the warehouse. Egypt struck. Sihon, Og, Canaan — gone. He vindicates His people. — Meanwhile the idols: mouths, eyes, ears, no breath. And you become what you trust. — So bless Him: Israel, Aaron, Levi, and everybody in awe. From Zion. Hallelujah.*

Here's your check today. Name the thing you're trusting most right now. Then ask the four questions. Does it speak? See? Hear? *Breathe?* If it fails — you know what you're becoming. Turn around. The One with breath in His mouth is still choosing.

*Note on translation: v. 1–3 — *hallelu* ×3 + *hal'lu-yah* opening and closing = frame. v. 4 — *bachar* = chose (cf. 132:13); *segullah* = treasured/personal possession — Exod 19:5; Deut 7:6; 14:2; 26:18. v. 5 — *ani yadati* = "I know" — singular in a communal psalm. v. 6 — cf. 115:3. v. 7 — cf. Jer 10:13; 51:16; *otzrotav* = His storehouses (cf. 33:7). v. 8–9 — Exod 12:29; Ps 78:51; 105:36; 136:10. v. 10–12 — Num 21:21–35; cf. 136:17–22. v. 13 — Exod 3:15. v. 14 — direct quotation of Deut 32:36 — *yadin* = will judge/vindicate; *yitnecham* = will have compassion/relent. v. 15–18 — near-verbatim from 115:4–8, with v. 17 adding *af ein-yesh-ruach b'fihem* = "moreover there is no breath in their mouths." v. 18 — *k'mohem yihyu oseihem* = "like them will be their makers." v. 19–20 — four houses; 115:9–11 had three (Israel, Aaron, *yir'ei YHWH*); 135 adds Levi. v. 20 — ***yir'ei YHWH*** = "those in AWE of the Source" — *yirah* correction; KJV "ye that fear the LORD" — rendered as awe, the fourth "house," a posture not a lineage. v. 21 — cf. 134:3. *Yirah* in v. 20.*
