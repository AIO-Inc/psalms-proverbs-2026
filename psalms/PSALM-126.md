# PSALM 126 — Those Who Sow in Tears

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 126:1–6. Seventh Song of Ascent — the middle of the fifteen. Six verses. The return from exile — remembered as a dream, prayed for again, and pictured as a farmer crying into the furrows.*

---

The middle Ascent. Halfway up the hill. And the pilgrims look back at the biggest thing that ever happened to them.

**"When the Source restored the fortunes of Zion — we were like those who dreamed."**

*Like those who dreamed.* The exiles came home — 538 BC, Cyrus's decree, Ezra 1 — and it felt *unreal*. Psalm 73 said the wicked's prosperity is like a dream you wake up from. Psalm 126 says the *rescue* was like a dream you wake up *into*. Too good. You keep checking if it's real.

If you've ever gotten the call — the test was clear, the job came through, they're coming home — and stood there thinking *this can't be happening* — you were *like those who dreamed*.

**"Our mouths were filled with laughter — our tongues with songs of joy."**

*Filled with laughter.* The Hebrew is *yimale s'chok pinu* — *our mouth was FULL of laughter*. Not a chuckle. Full. Can't-stop. Sarah at ninety — *God has brought me laughter*. Psalm 30 — *You turned my mourning into dancing.* Psalm 126 — *into LAUGHING*.

**"Then it was said among the nations: 'The Source has done great things for them.'"**

*Among the nations.* Psalm 117's Gentiles — *watching*. And they said it. Not Israel — *the nations*. The outsiders looked at the exiles coming home and said *their God did that.* Psalm 67's *so that*. The blessing as *evidence*.

**"The Source has done great things for us — and we are filled with joy."**

*For us.* The nations said *for them*. Israel says *for US*. Same sentence. Owned.

Now — the turn. Because the return wasn't *finished*. They came home to rubble. Psalm 85 — *You restored us — restore us AGAIN.*

**"Restore our fortunes, Source — like streams in the Negev."**

*Like streams in the Negev.* The Negev's the southern desert. Bone dry. *Wadis* — riverbeds — empty eleven months a year. And then the rain comes — and *overnight* the dry channel is a *torrent*. Roaring. Nothing — then everything. That's the restoration they're asking for. Not gradual. *Sudden.* The Negev doesn't drizzle. It *floods*.

And then — the verses. The ones on every gravestone, every recovery wall, every hard-season card:

**"Those who sow in tears — will reap with songs of joy."**

*Sow in tears.*

Picture the farmer. It's planting season. And he's *crying*. Maybe the famine's been long. Maybe he's burying the last of the seed grain — the stuff the family could've *eaten* — into the ground, betting on a harvest that might not come. Every handful he throws is *food he's not eating now*. And the tears fall in the furrows with the seed.

*Will reap with songs of joy.* The Hebrew is *b'rinnah* — Psalm 30's *ringing shout*. The same field. The same man. Months later. *Singing.*

Here's what the verse *doesn't* say. It doesn't say *don't cry*. It doesn't say *the tears are weakness*. It says — *sow IN them*. Keep planting *while* crying. The tears aren't the opposite of the work. They're *part of it*. They go in the ground *with the seed*.

**"Those who go out weeping — carrying seed to sow — will return with songs of joy — carrying sheaves with them."**

*Go out weeping — return singing.* Same road. Two directions. Psalm 121 — *your going out and your coming in*. Here — the going out is *tears*. The coming in is *sheaves*.

*Carrying seed — carrying sheaves.* Same arms. Different load. What you carried *out* — small, precious, uncertain. What you carry *back* — heavy, golden, *more than you planted*. That's the math of the field. You don't get back what you put in. You get back *multiplied*.

Jesus — John 12:24 — *unless a grain of wheat falls to the ground and dies, it remains alone. But if it dies, it produces many seeds.* He was talking about *Himself*. Going out weeping — Gethsemane — carrying the seed. Coming back — three days later — with the *sheaves*. All of us.

Six verses. Here's the shape: *When He brought us home — it was like a dream. Our mouths were FULL of laughing. The nations said "look what He did for them." — Do it again — like a Negev flood. — Those who sow in TEARS reap in SONG. Go out crying with the seed. Come back singing with the harvest.*

Here's what to do with the middle Ascent: if you're crying — *keep planting*. Don't wait till the tears stop. They're supposed to go in the ground. The harvest isn't *despite* them. It's *from* them.

Sow. In the tears. The Negev's going to flood.
