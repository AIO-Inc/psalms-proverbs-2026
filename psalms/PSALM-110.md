# PSALM 110 — Sit at My Right Hand

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 110:1–7. David. Seven verses. The most-quoted psalm in the New Testament — Jesus used it to silence the Pharisees, Peter used it at Pentecost, Hebrews built five chapters on it. A King who's also a Priest — of an order older than Aaron.*

---

Seven verses. And the whole New Testament kept reaching for them.

**"The Source says to my Master: 'Sit at My right hand — until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet.'"**

Read the pronouns. *The Source* — YHWH — *says to my Master* — *adoni*, my lord. David — the *king* — is calling someone *his* lord. Who outranks David?

Jesus asked the Pharisees exactly that. Matthew 22. *If David calls him Lord — how is he David's son?* They couldn't answer. Because the answer is: *the Son of David who's also David's Lord.* Born after him. Ranked above him.

*Sit at My right hand.* The seat of honor. Not standing — *seated*. Work finished. Psalm 2 — *I have set My King on the hill.* Here — *sit.* Hebrews 1:3 — *He sat down at the right hand.* Hebrews 10:12 — the priests *stood* daily offering sacrifices that never finished. *This* one *sat*. Because it was done.

*Until I make your enemies a footstool.* God's the one doing the making. The King *sits*. The Father *works*. 1 Corinthians 15:25 — *He must reign until He has put all enemies under His feet.*

**"The Source will extend your mighty scepter from Zion. You will rule in the midst of your enemies."**

*In the midst of.* Not after they're gone. *Among* them. Psalm 23's table *in the presence of my enemies*. The King rules while they're still in the room.

**"Your troops will be willing on your day of battle. Arrayed in holy splendor — your young men will come to you like dew from the morning's womb."**

*Willing.* The Hebrew is *n'davot* — freewill offerings. Psalm 54's word. The army isn't drafted. They *volunteer*. They come because they *want* to.

*Like dew from the morning's womb.* Dew appears at dawn — silently, everywhere, out of nowhere. That's the army. Not marching in formation. *Appearing*. Fresh. Countless. Born from the morning.

And then — the verse Hebrews spent chapters on:

**"The Source has sworn — and will not change His mind: 'You are a priest forever — in the order of Melchizedek.'"**

*Sworn. Will not change His mind.* An *oath*. Psalm 89 — *I will not lie to David.* God doesn't swear casually.

*A priest forever.* Wait. Kings weren't priests. Priests weren't kings. Israel *separated* them — Saul lost his kingdom for offering a sacrifice. And here — God swears the King is *also* the Priest. *Forever.*

*In the order of Melchizedek.* Genesis 14. One scene. Abraham comes back from battle — and a man named Melchizedek — *king of Salem* and *priest of God Most High* — brings him bread and wine and blesses him. And Abraham *tithes* to him. Then he vanishes from the story.

King *and* priest. Of *Jerusalem* — *Salem*. Before Aaron. Before Levi. Before the Law. And *Abraham* — the father of everything — bows to *him*.

Hebrews 7 says: *that's the order Jesus belongs to.* Not Aaron's — which had to keep repeating. Melchizedek's — which had no beginning, no end, no successor. *A priest forever.* The One who brought bread and wine and blessed the father of the faith — and then showed up again with bread and wine in an upper room.

**"The Master is at your right hand. He will crush kings on the day of His wrath. He will judge the nations — heaping up the dead — and crushing the rulers of the whole earth."**

The warrior verses. The King who *sat* — also *fights*. Revelation 19 — the rider on the white horse. It's not one or the other. Seated *and* riding.

And the last verse — cryptic and worth the puzzle:

**"He will drink from a brook along the way — and so He will lift up His head."**

*Drink from a brook.* A soldier on the march — stopping at a stream. Human. Thirsty. Gideon's men drank from the brook before the battle. And the One who drinks — *lifts up His head*. Refreshed. Ready. The Priest-King who sits at the right hand — also *gets thirsty*. Also *stops for water*. John 4 — *give Me a drink*. John 19 — *I thirst*.

Seven verses. Here's the shape: *The Source says to David's Lord — SIT. Rule among your enemies. Your army volunteers — like dew. You're a Priest forever — Melchizedek's order — sworn, unchangeable. The Master at your right hand crushes kings. And He drinks from a brook and lifts His head.*

Jesus quoted this to shut the Pharisees up. Peter quoted it to open three thousand hearts. Hebrews quoted it to explain why there's no more sacrifice.

Here's what it means for you: *the One who sits at God's right hand is your Priest.* Not a priest who has to keep going back in. *Forever.* The bread and wine He brought Abraham — He brings you. And He *sat down* — because the work's done.

Your enemies aren't a footstool yet. That's fine. He's *sitting*. The Father's working. *Until.*
