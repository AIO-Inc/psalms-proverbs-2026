# PSALM 86 — Give Me an Undivided Heart

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 86:1–17. The only David psalm in Book Three. A prayer stitched together from other prayers — because when you're desperate, you use what you've got.*

---

David's back — for one psalm. And this one's a *quilt*. He borrows lines from Exodus, from his own earlier psalms, from the priestly blessings. Because when you're in trouble, you don't write new material. You grab what worked.

**"Hear me, Source, and answer me — because I'm poor and needy."**

Psalm 40, Psalm 70. *Poor and needy.* The argument that always works.

**"Guard my life — because I'm devoted to You. You are my God. Save Your servant who trusts in You."**

*Devoted.* The Hebrew is *chasid* — loyal one. Not "perfect." *Loyal.*

**"Have mercy on me, Master — because I call to You all day long. Bring joy to Your servant — because to You, Master, I lift up my soul."**

*Lift up my soul.* Psalm 25:1. He's quoting himself.

Now — the line he'll build the psalm around:

**"You are forgiving and good, Master — abounding in loyal-love to all who call to You."**

*Abounding.* Overflowing. Not rationed. To *all who call*. Not the qualified. The *callers*.

**"Hear my prayer, Source. Listen to my cry for mercy. When I'm in distress — I call to You — because You answer me."**

*Because You answer.* Not "hoping You'll answer." That's why he calls — *track record*.

Now — the middle section. And it's *theology*, in the middle of a plea:

**"Among the gods there is none like You, Master. No deeds can compare with Yours."**

*Among the gods.* Psalm 82's gods — the powers, the rulers, the things people worship. *None like You.*

**"All the nations You've made will come and worship before You, Master. They will bring glory to Your name."**

*All the nations.* Even David — in a personal, desperate prayer — can't stop talking about the nations. Book Two and Three drilled it in.

**"Because You are great — and You do marvelous deeds. You alone are God."**

*Alone.* One.

And then — the verse. The one to memorize from this psalm:

**"Teach me Your way, Source — so I can walk in Your truth. Give me an undivided heart — so I can stand in awe of Your name."**

*An undivided heart.* The Hebrew is *yached l'vavi* — *unite my heart*. Make it *one*. Psalm 12's double heart — *a heart and a heart*. David's asking for the opposite. *One heart. Pointed one direction. Not split between You and the thing I'm scared of, You and the thing I want, You and the version of me I show people.*

*So I can stand in awe.* Here's *yirah* — and look what it *requires*. You can't be in awe with a divided heart. Half of you's looking at God, half's looking at the exit. Awe needs the *whole* heart aimed at one thing. That's why he asks for unity *first*.

**"I will praise You, Master my God — with all my heart. I will glorify Your name forever."**

*All my heart.* The united one. Once it's one, he can give *all* of it.

**"Because great is Your loyal-love toward me. You've delivered me from the depths of Sheol."**

*Depths of Sheol.* The grave's bottom. He's been there. He's out.

Now — the trouble:

**"Arrogant men are attacking me, God. A band of ruthless men seeks my life. They have no regard for You."**

*No regard.* They don't factor God in. Psalm 54, Psalm 86 — same disease.

And then — the quote. The one from Exodus 34. God's own self-description, given to Moses on the mountain:

**"But You, Master — You are a compassionate and gracious God — slow to anger — abounding in loyal-love and faithfulness."**

*Compassionate. Gracious. Slow to anger. Abounding in loyal-love.* David's throwing God's *own words* back at Him. *This is what You said You were. Be that.* It's the boldest thing you can do in prayer — hold God to His own description. And it's exactly what He wants.

**"Turn to me and have mercy on me. Grant Your strength to Your servant. Save the son of Your servant woman."**

*The son of Your servant woman.* He's claiming *hereditary* loyalty. *My mother served You. I'm hers. I belong to the house.*

**"Give me a sign of Your goodness — so my enemies will see it and be put to shame — because You, Source, have helped me and comforted me."**

*A sign.* Something visible. Psalm 79 — *before our eyes*. He wants the enemies to *watch* the rescue.

*Comforted.* The word from Psalm 71 — *nacham*. Comfort, at the end.

Seventeen verses. Here's the shape: *I'm poor and needy. You're forgiving and abounding. Teach me Your way. UNITE my heart — so I can stand in awe. You pulled me from Sheol's bottom. You said You were compassionate, gracious, slow to anger — BE that. Give me a sign. You've comforted me.*

Pray for one heart. Not a better heart. *One.* Awe needs the whole thing.
