# PSALM 67 — Let Your Face Shine on Us — So That

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 67:1–7. Seven verses. A blessing that refuses to stay local.*

---

Short one. But it's got the biggest *so that* in the Psalms.

**"May God be gracious to us and bless us. May He make His face shine on us."**

That's the priestly blessing — Numbers 6. *The Lord bless you and keep you. The Lord make His face shine on you.* Every Israelite knew it. Every kid had it said over them.

But watch what the psalmist does with it. He adds two words:

**"— so that Your way may be known on the earth. Your rescue among all the nations."**

*So that.* The blessing isn't the endpoint. It's the *means*. *Bless us — so that they'll see.* The face shining on Israel is a *floodlight* — pointed at them so the rest of the world can see what God looks like.

That changes everything about how you ask for blessing. Not *bless me because I want it*. *Bless me — so that the people watching my life see You.*

**"Let the peoples thank You, God. Let ALL the peoples thank You."**

*All.* Every nation. The Korahites, the Asaphites, David — they keep saying it. Book Two of the Psalms is *relentless* about the nations. This wasn't supposed to be a private club.

**"Let the nations be glad and shout for joy — because You judge the peoples fairly. You guide the nations on the earth."**

*Judge fairly. Guide the nations.* Not "conquer." Not "punish." *Judge fairly* — so the ones who've never had a fair judge finally get one. *Guide* — the shepherd word. God shepherding *Egypt*. God shepherding *Babylon*. That's the vision.

**"Let the peoples thank You, God. Let ALL the peoples thank You."**

Refrain. Second time. Because he means it.

**"The earth has given its harvest. God — our God — blesses us."**

*The earth has given its harvest.* Psalm 65 again — the grain's in. The wagon spilled. And here's the connection: the *physical* blessing — food on the table — is *evidence*. When the nations see a people who are *fed*, who are *blessed*, whose God's face is *shining* on them — that's the sermon. Not the words. The *harvest*.

**"God blesses us — so that all the ends of the earth may stand in awe of Him."**

And there it is again. *So that.* The second one. Bookends. *Bless us so that Your way is known. Bless us so that the ends of the earth stand in awe.*

*Stand in awe.* *Yirah*. Not "fear Him." Not "cower before Him." *Be amazed by Him.* The whole point of God blessing His people is so the *far edges of the map* look up and go *wow*.

Seven verses. Two *so thats*. Here's the shape: *Shine on us — SO THAT they see. Let ALL the peoples thank You. You judge fairly. You shepherd nations. The harvest came in. Bless us — SO THAT the ends of the earth stand amazed.*

Here's what to do with it: next time you ask God for something — the raise, the healing, the house — add the two words. *So that.* Finish the sentence. *Bless me so that ____.* If you can't finish it — you might be asking for a floodlight to point at yourself.

He shines on you so *they* can see Him. That's the deal. It's a good one.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 — echoes Numbers 6:24–26 (the Aaronic blessing). v. 2 —* lada'at ba'aretz darkecha *= "that Your way may be known in the earth" — purpose clause. v. 4 —* tanchem *= You guide/lead (shepherding verb). v. 3, 5 — refrain:* yoducha amim Elohim, yoducha amim kulam*. v. 7 —* v'yir'u oto kol-afsei-aretz *=* yirah *= "and all the ends of the earth will stand in awe of Him" — second purpose clause, bookend with v. 2.*
