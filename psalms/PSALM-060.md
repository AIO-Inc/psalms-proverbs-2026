# PSALM 60 — Through God We Will Do Valiantly

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 60:1–12. A national lament after a military setback — probably a raid on Judah while David's army was fighting in the north (2 Samuel 8). Verses 5–12 reappear as Psalm 108.*

---

David's winning up north and getting hit at home. The army's in one place; the disaster's in another. He can't be everywhere. That's this psalm.

**"God — You've rejected us. You've broken through our defenses. You've been angry. Restore us!"**

He starts with the accusation *and* the request in the same breath. *You did this. Fix it.* No transition. Because when you've been trusting God and you get hit, that *is* the prayer — *You — and also — please.*

**"You've shaken the land. You've split it open. Heal its cracks — because it's tottering."**

*Split it open.* An earthquake image for a national disaster. The ground itself giving way. *Heal its cracks* — not "rebuild." *Heal.* Like a wound. The land's a body and it's been cut.

**"You've shown Your people hard things. You've made us drink wine that makes us stagger."**

*Wine that makes us stagger.* The cup of judgment — Psalm 75, Jeremiah 25. God poured something, and it wasn't the river of His delights (Psalm 36). It was the cup that makes you lose your footing.

David's not pretending it was someone else's hand. *You've shown us. You've made us drink.* He puts it on God — because putting it on God is the only way to ask God to change it.

**"You've given a banner to the ones who stand in awe of You — to rally to, away from the bow."**

Here's the first shift. *A banner.* A rallying point. Something you can see across a chaotic battlefield and run toward. Even in the defeat — there's a flag. And who gets it? *The ones in awe of You.* The wonder-keepers get the flagpole.

"Save with Your right hand — and answer us."

And then — God speaks. Directly. And what He says is a *land survey*:

**"God has spoken in His holiness: 'I will exult. I will divide Shechem. I will measure out the Valley of Succoth. Gilead is Mine. Manasseh is Mine. Ephraim is the helmet of My head. Judah is My scepter. Moab is My washbasin. On Edom I'll throw My sandal. Over Philistia I'll shout in triumph.'"**

Read what God's doing. He's *walking the property*. Shechem, Succoth, Gilead, Manasseh — *Mine, Mine, Mine*. Ephraim's His *helmet*. Judah's His *scepter*. The tribes aren't just territory — they're *His gear*. He wears them.

And the enemies? Moab is the *washbasin* — the bowl you rinse your feet in. Edom gets the *sandal* — you throw your shoe on what you own. Philistia gets the *shout* — the victory cry.

*I will exult.* God's not worried. He's *celebrating*. The land David sees as split open — God sees as *His*, surveyed and assigned, down to which tribe is His hat.

That's the turn. Not "God will fix it." *God already owns it — and He's happy about it.*

Now — David's response, and it's honest:

**"Who will bring me to the fortified city? Who will lead me to Edom?"**

*Who?* He still has to *go*. God's declared ownership — but the fortified city's still standing and Edom's still hostile. Faith doesn't skip the march.

**"Haven't You rejected us, God? Don't You go out with our armies anymore?"**

Still asking. He heard the survey — and he's *still* asking. Because a promise and a present reality can both be true, and you don't have to pretend the reality away to hold the promise.

"Give us help against the enemy — because human help is worthless."

*Worthless.* The Hebrew is *shav* — empty, vain, a breath. Every ally, every alliance — *vapor*. Same word for the horse in Psalm 33. Only one help that isn't hollow.

And the last verse — the reason this psalm gets sung again as 108:

**"Through God we will do valiantly. He will tread down our enemies."**

*Through God we will do valiantly.* *We* will. Not "God will do it while we watch." *We* fight — *through* Him. Both. The march is ours. The strength is His. The valor comes from the union.

Twelve verses. *You broke us — restore us. You made us stagger — but You gave us a flag. You walked the property and said Mine — but I still have to march. Human help is vapor — through You we'll do valiantly.*

When you're winning somewhere and getting hit somewhere else: say *You did this — and please*. Look for the banner. Remember Who owns the ground. And march.
