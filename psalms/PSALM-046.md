# PSALM 46 — Be Still and Know

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 46:1–11. Luther's "A Mighty Fortress" came from this. Sons of Korah.*

---

**"God is our refuge and strength — a help in trouble, found immediately."**

*Found immediately.* Most translations say "a very present help." The Hebrew is *nimtza m'od* — "found exceedingly," *right there when you reach*. Not on His way. Not "available by appointment." *Already there.* You turn around and He's standing behind you.

Now — the worst-case scenario. And the poet doesn't hold back:

**"So we will not fear — though the earth gives way, though the mountains topple into the heart of the sea. Though its waters roar and foam. Though the mountains shake at its swelling."**

*Though the earth gives way.* Not "though the job falls through." The *planet*. The ground itself. Mountains — the most permanent things anyone knew — sliding into the ocean. He's picked the biggest disaster he can imagine and said: *even then*.

Whatever your earth-giving-way is — the diagnosis, the divorce, the collapse — it's smaller than a mountain falling into the sea. And he's covered that one.

Then — the scene changes. From chaos to a city:

**"There is a river — its streams make glad the city of God, the holy dwelling of the Most High."**

Jerusalem has no river. Never did. That's the point. This is a city with a river *nobody can see* — a source of gladness that doesn't depend on geography. Ezekiel saw it coming out from under the temple. John saw it in Revelation 22 running down the middle of the street. It's the river you drink from when everything visible is falling apart.

**"God is in the middle of her — she will not be shaken. God helps her at the break of dawn."**

*Not be shaken.* The word from Psalm 15, 16, 21, 30, 36. The mountains shake. The city doesn't. Because of Who's *in the middle of her*.

*At the break of dawn.* The night's real. It lasts till dawn. But there's a dawn.

"Nations roared. Kingdoms toppled. **He raised His voice — the earth melted.**"

The same voice from Psalm 29. One word — and the thing you were terrified of turns to liquid.

**"The Source of Armies is with us. The God of Jacob is our fortress."**

*With us.* The refrain. Said twice in this psalm. *Immanu.* God-with-us. Immanuel.

Now the invitation:

**"Come — see the works of the Source. What devastation He's brought on the earth. He makes wars cease to the ends of the earth. He breaks the bow. He shatters the spear. He burns the shields with fire."**

*Makes wars cease.* Every weapon — bow, spear, shield — broken or burned. Not one side winning. *The war itself* ending. That's what He does when He raises His voice. Not conquest. *Disarmament.*

And then — the verse. The one on the coffee mugs, the one that means so much more than the mugs know:

**"Be still — and know that I am God.**
**I will be exalted among the nations. I will be exalted in the earth."**

*Be still.* The Hebrew is *harpu* — literally *let go. Drop your hands. Release your grip.* It's the word for a soldier lowering his weapon. Not "relax." *Stand down.* Stop fighting. Open your fists.

And it's addressed to *everyone* — to the raging nations *and* to you. *All of you — stop. Let go. And in the stillness, know.*

*Know that I am God.* You can't know it while you're swinging. Knowing requires the hands to be down.

*I will be exalted.* Not "help Me be exalted." *I will.* It's going to happen regardless. Your fighting isn't what makes Him win. Your stillness is what lets you *see* Him win.

**"The Source of Armies is with us. The God of Jacob is our fortress."**

Second time. The bookend.

So: the earth can give way. The mountains can go in the sea. There's a river you can't see. He's in the middle of the city. He breaks the bow. And He says — *drop your hands. Let go. Know.*

Be still. You don't have to hold it. He's found immediately.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* ezrah b'tzarot nimtza m'od *= "a help in troubles, found exceedingly/immediately." v. 2 —* lo nira *= we will not fear — ordinary human dread, correctly rendered (not* yirah*). v. 4 —* nahar *= river — Jerusalem has none; symbolic (cf. Ezek 47, Rev 22). v. 7, 11 —* YHWH tzeva'ot immanu *= "YHWH of Armies is with us" — root of "Immanuel." v. 10 —* harpu *= let go, drop, release, cease striving — the imperative to a combatant, not to a meditator.*
