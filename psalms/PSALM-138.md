# PSALM 138 — You Made Me Bold

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 138:1–8. Eight verses. Attributed to David — the first of eight final Davidic psalms (138–145) before the closing Hallelujahs. After the river of Babylon, a man stands up, faces the Temple, and says thank you — in front of the gods.*

---

Psalm 137 ended in silence by the river. Psalm 138 is the answer — not to Babylon, but to the *harp in the tree*. Somebody takes it back down.

**"I will give You thanks with all my heart. Before the gods — I will sing Your praise."**

*Before the gods.* *Neged elohim.* Some read it *before the angels*. Some read *before the judges*. But take it straight — *the gods*. The Babylonian pantheon. The idols of Psalm 115 and 135 with no breath in their mouths. David — or the exile singing in his voice — says *I'll sing to YOU. In front of THEM.*

That's the reversal of 137:4 — *how can we sing here?* Psalm 138 — *like this. To Him. In their faces.* The harp comes down from the poplar.

*With all my heart.* Psalm 9, 86, 111 — *whole heart*. Not the half that isn't scared.

**"I will bow down toward Your holy temple — and I will praise Your name — for Your loyal-love and Your faithfulness."**

*Toward Your holy temple.* In exile, Daniel prayed facing Jerusalem — Daniel 6. Solomon asked for exactly this — 1 Kings 8 — *when they pray toward this place from the land of their captivity, hear them*. You don't have to be *in* the house. You have to *face* it.

*Loyal-love and faithfulness.* *Chesed v'emet.* Psalm 136's word — twenty-six times. Now — once, from one man. It doesn't need twenty-six when it's *yours*.

**"For You have exalted Your word above all Your name."**

Hard verse. Hebrew's compressed. Best reading: *You've made Your promise even bigger than Your reputation.* Meaning — He'd rather keep His word than protect His image. Psalm 132 — *a sure oath He will not revoke*. God stakes His *name* on His *word*. That's why you can trust it.

**"When I called — You answered me. You made me bold — with strength in my soul."**

*When I called — You answered.* Psalm 120 — *I call, He answers*. Psalm 34 — *this poor man called*. Psalm 138 — *the day I called* — *b'yom karati* — same day. Not eventually.

*You made me bold.* *Tarhiveni.* The root is *rahav* — to be *wide*, *proud*, *emboldened*. Some translate it *You made me arrogant* — in the good sense. *You gave me nerve.* Psalm 18 — *the spacious place*. Psalm 119 — *I walk in freedom*. Here — the *width* is *inside*. Chest out. Shoulders back. *Strength in my soul.*

That's the gift after the river. Not answers. *Nerve.* The ability to stand in front of the gods and sing anyway.

**"May all the kings of the earth praise You, Source — when they hear what You have decreed. May they sing of the ways of the Source — for great is the glory of the Source."**

*The kings of the earth.* Psalm 2 — the kings plotting. Psalm 72, 96, 102, 117 — *all nations, all kings, worship*. David can't see them praising yet. He's saying *may they*. From exile. In front of gods that aren't God. Nerve.

**"Though the Source is high — He looks on the lowly. But the proud — He knows from afar."**

*High — but He looks on the lowly.* Psalm 113 — *He stoops down to look… He raises the poor from the dust*. Psalm 131 — *my heart is not lifted up*. The lowly — *shafal* — Psalm 136:23 — *He remembered us in our low estate*. Same word. The God who's *high* keeps His eyes *low*.

*The proud He knows from afar.* *Gavoah* — the *high* one. Psalm 131 — *my heart is not high*. The ones who lift themselves up — He *knows* them. *From a distance.* He doesn't come close. He doesn't need to. He can see them from where He is.

**"Though I walk in the midst of trouble — You preserve my life. You stretch out Your hand against the anger of my enemies — Your right hand saves me."**

*In the midst of trouble.* *B'kerev tzarah.* Not *after*. *In the middle of*. Psalm 23 — *through the valley*. Psalm 138 — *through the middle of the trouble* — and *You keep me alive*. *T'chayeni* — You *make me live*. Psalm 119 — *preserve my life according to Your word* — nine times. It's the exile's verb.

*Your right hand.* Psalm 16, 110, 118, 121 — the *right hand*. Strong side. The saving side.

And the last verse:

**"The Source will fulfill His purpose for me. Your loyal-love, Source, endures forever. Do not abandon the work of Your hands."**

*Will fulfill His purpose for me.* *Yigmor ba'adi* — He will *complete* on my behalf. Philippians 1:6 — *He who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion*. Paul got it from here. God doesn't leave projects half-built.

*Your loyal-love endures forever.* Psalm 136's refrain — once more, in one man's mouth, as a *statement*, not a chorus.

*Do not abandon the work of Your hands.* The last line. After all the boldness — a request. *Don't let go of me.* Because *I* am the work of Your hands. Psalm 139 is about to say *You knit me together*. Psalm 138 gets there first — *You made me. Don't quit on what You made.*

That's the tension the whole psalm holds. *You made me bold* — and — *please don't let go.* Both true. The nerve is real. The need is real. Boldness isn't the *absence* of *hold me*. It's what you get *when* He does.

Eight verses. Here's the shape: *I'll thank You — in front of the gods. Facing Your house. You keep Your word above Your name. I called — You answered — You made me BOLD. May the kings hear it. You're high — but You look low. I'm in the middle of the trouble — and You keep me breathing. You'll finish what You started. Loyal-love forever. Don't let go of Your work.*

Here's your practice. Whatever's *in front of you* today — the thing that acts like it's a god — pick up the harp. One line of thanks. *Out loud.* In its face. Then say the last verse — *don't abandon the work of Your hands.* Boldness and the plea, in the same breath. That's how David did it.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 — *neged elohim* = "before/in front of gods" — LXX reads "angels," Targum "judges"; retained as "gods" (foreign deities/idols), consistent with 135:15–18 and the exilic setting. v. 2 — *chasd'cha v'al-amittecha* = "Your loyal-love and Your truth/faithfulness"; *ki-higdalta al-kol-shimcha imratecha* = "for You have made great above all Your name Your word" — syntactically difficult; rendered as promise exalted above reputation. v. 3 — *b'yom karati* = "in the day I called"; *tarhiveni* = "You made me bold/wide/proud" (root *rahav* — cf. Isa 3:5; Song 6:5) — often smoothed to "strengthened"; rendered as "bold." v. 6 — *ram YHWH v'shafal yireh* = "high is the Source and the low He sees"; *gavoah* = the high/proud one; *mi-merchak y'yeda* = "from afar He knows." v. 7 — *b'kerev tzarah* = "in the midst of distress"; *t'chayeni* = You make me live/preserve me. v. 8 — *YHWH yigmor ba'adi* = "the Source will complete on my behalf" — cf. Phil 1:6; *ma'asei yadecha al-teref* = "the works of Your hands do not let drop/abandon." Note *yireh* in v. 6 is the verb *ra'ah* (to see), not *yirah* (awe) — no correction needed. No *yirah* in this psalm.*
