# PSALM 9 — He Doesn't Forget the Cry of the Poor

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 9:1–20. An acrostic — the first half of a poem that continues in Psalm 10.*

---

Start with gratitude. That's the rule. Before you complain, before you ask, you thank.

"I will thank You, Source, with my whole heart. I will tell about every wonderful thing You've done. I'll be glad and celebrate in You. I'll sing to Your name, Most High."

Whole heart. Not the polite half. All of it.

Now, *why* the gratitude? Because he's seen what happens:

"When my enemies turn back, they stumble and die in front of You. You've upheld my cause. You've sat on the throne and judged rightly."

He's not imagining this. He's *remembering* it. That's the trick — you've got a history with God. Go back and read it before you panic about the present.

"You've rebuked the nations. You've wiped out the mark-missers. You've erased their names forever. The enemy is finished — ruins forever. You uprooted their cities. Their memory is gone."

Empires that seemed permanent — gone. Names that were on every building — nobody remembers them. He's walked through the rubble of people who thought they'd last forever.

**"But the Source sits enthroned forever. He's established His throne for justice."**

They're rubble. He's on the throne. That's the contrast the whole psalm hangs on.

"He judges the world with right-standing. He governs the peoples with fairness."

Now the heart of it — and if you're broke, if you're being ground down, if nobody's listening — this is for you:

**"The Source is a stronghold for the oppressed. A stronghold in times of trouble."**

Not for the successful. Not for the well-connected. For the *crushed*. He's a fortress with a door that opens for the people every other fortress locks out.

"The ones who know Your name trust You — because You, Source, have never abandoned the ones who look for You."

Never. Not once. Search the record.

"Sing to the Source who lives in Zion. Tell the peoples what He's done. Because the One who avenges blood remembers. **He does not forget the cry of the afflicted.**"

Does not forget. Every unjust thing that happened to you — every time you were cheated, dismissed, walked over — it's *on file*. Not for your bitterness. For His justice.

Then David asks for himself: "Be gracious to me, Source. Look at what I'm suffering from the people who hate me. You're the One who lifts me up from the gates of death — so I can tell everyone at the gates of Zion about Your praise. So I can celebrate Your rescue."

Lifted from the gates of death *so that* he can testify. Your rescue isn't just for you. It's a story someone else is going to need.

"The nations have sunk into the pit they dug. Their own feet got caught in the net they hid."

Same principle as Psalm 7 — the trap catches the trapper.

"The mark-missers will turn back to Sheol — every nation that forgets God."

*Forgets.* That's the sin. Not disbelief — forgetting. Acting like He's not there.

**"But the needy will not be forgotten forever. The hope of the poor will not perish for all time."**

Read that one twice. The poor are not forgotten. Their hope does not die. That's a promise with God's name on it.

"Rise up, Source. Don't let mortal man prevail. Let the nations be judged before You. Put awe into them, Source — let the nations know they're only human."

*Only human.* Let the ones who forgot God remember they're made of dust. Not to crush them — to wake them up.

Give thanks. Remember your history. Stand in the fortress. The poor are not forgotten.

---

*Note on translation: v. 17 — "Sheol" = the grave, NOT hell. The wicked "return to the grave" = they die like everyone else; there is no torment described here. v. 20 — "put them in fear" =* morah *(from* yirah*) = awe, the shock of realizing one's mortality — not terror. "Wicked" =* rasha*. "Steadfast/righteousness" =* tzedek*.*
