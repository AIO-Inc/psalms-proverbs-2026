# PSALM 37 — Don't Get Heated

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 37:1–40. An acrostic wisdom psalm — David as an old man, telling you what he's learned about watching bad people win.*

---

David's old when he writes this. He says so — *I was young, now I'm old.* And the whole thing is one lesson, repeated in forty ways: *Don't lose your mind over people who are getting away with it.*

**"Don't get heated over the ones doing harm. Don't envy the ones doing wrong. Because like grass they'll quickly dry up. Like green plants they'll wither."**

*Don't get heated.* The Hebrew is *charah* — to burn, to get hot. That's what watching injustice does — you *burn*. And he's saying: *put it down. They're grass.* Green today. Brown in a week. You're burning over a lawn.

Now the positive — five verbs, and they're the spine of the psalm:

**"Trust in the Source and do good. Live in the land and feed on faithfulness."**

*Trust. Do. Live. Feed.* Don't just trust — *do good* while you're trusting. Don't just survive — *feed* on the faithfulness. Make it your diet.

**"Delight yourself in the Source — and He will give you the desires of your heart."**

Read the order. *Delight first.* When He's your delight, what your heart desires changes. And then He gives it — because you're finally wanting what's actually good for you.

**"Commit your road to the Source. Trust Him — and He will act."**

*Commit* — the Hebrew is *gol*, to roll. *Roll your road onto Him.* Like rolling a boulder off your chest onto someone who can carry it. Then — *He will act.* Not "He might." *Will.*

"He'll bring out your right-standing like the light — your justice like noon."

*Like noon.* Nothing hidden at noon. No shadows. Your vindication will be *obvious*.

**"Be still before the Source. Wait patiently for Him. Don't get heated over the one whose road is succeeding — over the man carrying out schemes."**

*Be still.* The word is *dom* — silence, stillness, *be quiet*. Stop arguing with the situation. Stop narrating. Just — still.

And there's the burn again. *Don't get heated over the one who's succeeding.* He knows it's the success that gets you. If they were failing, you wouldn't care. It's watching them *win* that burns.

**"Stop the anger. Abandon the rage. Don't get heated — it only leads to harm."**

Third time. *Don't get heated.* Because *you* get hurt. The anger doesn't touch them. It burns *you*.

"Because the ones doing harm will be cut off. But the ones who wait for the Source — **they will inherit the land.**"

**"A little while — and the mark-misser is gone. You'll look at his place — and he's not there. But the humble will inherit the land. They'll delight in abundant peace."**

Jesus quoted this — *blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth*. This is where He got it. Psalm 37:11. The ones who *don't get heated* — who wait, who stay still — they end up with everything.

"The mark-misser plots against the one doing right. Grinds his teeth at him. **The Master laughs at him** — because He sees his day coming."

Same laugh as Psalm 2. The plotting is *funny* to God. Because He's already read the ending.

"The mark-missers draw the sword, bend the bow — to bring down the poor and needy, to slaughter the upright. **Their sword will go into their own heart.** Their bows will break."

Psalm 7 again. The pit-digger falls in. The sword turns around.

**"Better the little of the one doing right than the abundance of many mark-missers."**

*Better the little.* A small clean life beats a big dirty one. Not eventually — *now*.

"The arms of the mark-missers will break. The Source holds up the ones doing right. **The Source knows the days of the blameless. Their inheritance will be forever.** They won't be ashamed in the evil time. In days of famine they'll be satisfied."

*Knows the days.* Every one. Counted. Held.

"But the mark-missers perish. The enemies of the Source — like the beauty of pastures — vanish. Like smoke, they vanish."

"The mark-misser borrows and doesn't pay back. **The one doing right is generous and gives.**"

That's the tell. Not what they believe — what they do with money. The taker vs. the giver.

Now the part every parent needs:

**"The steps of a man are made firm by the Source — He delights in his road. Though he falls, he won't be thrown down — because the Source holds his hand."**

*Though he falls.* He's going to fall. That's assumed. But — *holds his hand*. A child learning to walk. Falls happen. Face-plants don't, because someone's got the hand.

And then David gets personal:

**"I was young. Now I'm old. And I have never seen the one doing right abandoned — or his children begging for bread."**

*Never.* A whole lifetime of watching. He's telling you: *I checked. Across decades. The pattern holds.*

"All day long he's generous and lends. His children become a blessing."

**"Turn from harm and do good — and live forever. Because the Source loves justice. He won't abandon His loyal ones. They're guarded forever. But the children of the mark-missers are cut off."**

"The ones doing right will inherit the land — and live in it forever."

*Inherit the land* — third time. That's the promise he's hammering. *You get the land. Not them. Wait.*

**"The mouth of the one doing right speaks wisdom. His tongue speaks justice. The instruction of his God is in his heart — his steps won't slip."**

Now David tells you a story he saw with his own eyes:

"The mark-misser watches the one doing right. He hunts for a way to kill him. **The Source won't abandon him into his hand.** He won't let him be condemned when he's judged."

**"Wait for the Source. Keep His road. He'll lift you up to inherit the land — you'll watch the mark-missers get cut off."**

**"I saw a mark-misser — ruthless, spreading himself like a native tree in rich soil. Then he passed by — and look: he was gone. I searched for him. He couldn't be found."**

*I searched for him.* Went looking. The tree that was taking over the whole yard — *gone*. Not a stump. Not a trace. He's telling you: *the thing you're burning over right now — in a while, you'll go look for it and it won't be there.*

"Watch the blameless. Look at the upright. **The man of peace has a future.** But the rebels are destroyed together. The future of the mark-missers is cut off."

*The man of peace has a future.* The Hebrew is *acharit* — an *afterward*. An ending that's actually a continuing. The rebels have no *acharit*. The peaceful man does.

**"The rescue of the ones doing right is from the Source. He's their stronghold in the time of trouble. The Source helps them and delivers them. He delivers them from the mark-missers and saves them — because they took refuge in Him."**

Forty verses. Here's the whole thing: *Don't get heated. They're grass. Trust, do good, live, feed, delight, commit, be still, wait. You'll fall — He's got your hand. I'm old and I've watched — it always ends this way. The man of peace has a future.*

Put the fire down. Go inherit the land.
