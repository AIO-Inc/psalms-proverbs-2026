# PSALM 20 — Some Trust in Horses

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 20:1–9. A blessing spoken over the king before battle.*

---

This one's different. It's not David talking to God. It's the *people* talking to David — before he rides out. A whole nation laying hands on one man.

Read it as somebody speaking it over *you*.

"May the Source answer you in the day of trouble.
May the name of the God of Jacob set you high out of reach.
May He send you help from His holy place. May He support you from Zion."

*Set you high out of reach.* Like lifting a kid up over your head when the dog's barking. Not removed from the situation — just held above where it can bite.

"May He remember all your offerings. May He accept your sacrifices."

Everything you've laid down — the hours, the money, the things you gave up to do right. May He *remember*. None of it forgotten.

**"May He give you the desire of your heart. May He fulfill every one of your plans."**

Read that slow. *The desire of your heart.* Not "may He give you what's good for you." *What you actually want.* This is a blessing that assumes your heart's been made right — and if it has, then what you want is worth praying for.

"We will shout for joy at your rescue. In the name of our God we will raise the banner."

*Your* rescue — *our* joy. When you win, the whole community celebrates. Nobody fights alone.

"May the Source fulfill every one of your requests."

Then the speaker steps forward — the king, or the priest, or the one guy who's already sure:

**"Now I know that the Source saves His anointed. He answers him from His holy heaven with the saving strength of His right hand."**

*Now I know.* Not "I hope." Not "we'll see." *Know.* Somewhere in the middle of the blessing, the confidence landed.

And then the line the whole psalm is famous for:

**"Some trust in chariots. Some trust in horses. But we — we remember the name of the Source our God."**

Chariots and horses were the military tech of the day. The tanks. The air force. The thing that *should* decide who wins. And Israel was famously short on both — God had actually told them *not* to stockpile horses (Deuteronomy 17:16), because He wanted them to remember Who actually wins wars.

What are your chariots? The bank balance. The credentials. The connections. The plan B. The thing you'd point to if someone asked "why are you confident?" Nothing wrong with having them. Everything wrong with *trusting* them.

*We remember the name.* That's it. That's the whole military strategy. Not "we have no horses." *We know Who we belong to.*

"They collapse and fall. But we rise and stand firm."

The chariot people go down. The name-rememberers get up. Not because they're stronger — because what they're standing on doesn't move.

**"Source, save the king! Answer us when we call."**

That's the finish. A shout. The whole nation with one voice.

Here's what to do with this psalm: find somebody about to go into their battle — the interview, the surgery, the court date, the hard conversation — and *say it over them*. Out loud. "May He give you the desire of your heart. Some trust in horses. We remember the name."

Then, when it's your turn — let somebody say it over you.

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* yesagvecha *= set you inaccessibly high, out of reach. v. 4 —* ta'avat levavcha *= desire/longing of your heart — the blessing assumes a rightly ordered heart. v. 7 — Deuteronomy 17:16 forbids kings from multiplying horses; the contrast is theological, not tactical. "Anointed" =* mashiach*. No* yirah*.*
