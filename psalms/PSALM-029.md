# PSALM 29 — The Voice

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 29:1–11. A thunderstorm, from the Mediterranean to the desert, in eleven verses.*

---

Stand on a porch in a real storm sometime. The kind where the sky cracks open. That's this psalm.

"Give to the Source, you sons of the mighty — give to the Source glory and strength. Give to the Source the glory of His name. Bow to the Source in holy splendor."

He starts by calling the *angels* to worship. The heavenly beings. *You* bow first. Then the storm rolls in:

**"The voice of the Source is over the waters. The God of glory thunders. The Source is over the mighty waters."**

Seven times in this psalm — *the voice of the Source*. Seven. The number of completion. Every thunderclap is Him talking.

"The voice of the Source — in power. The voice of the Source — in majesty."

Now watch the storm move. It starts over the sea, then hits the coast:

**"The voice of the Source breaks the cedars. The Source shatters the cedars of Lebanon."**

Cedars of Lebanon — the biggest trees in that world. Hundred-foot giants that took centuries to grow. Kings built palaces out of them. And one word from God snaps them like kindling.

"He makes Lebanon skip like a calf. Sirion like a young wild ox."

*Skip like a calf.* The mountains — bouncing. He's not describing destruction. He's describing *play*. The mountain ranges jumping around like livestock in spring because the Voice went by.

"The voice of the Source strikes with flames of fire."

Lightning. Now the storm crosses the whole land and hits the desert on the other side:

"The voice of the Source shakes the wilderness. The Source shakes the wilderness of Kadesh."

Kadesh — the far southern edge. The storm went from the Mediterranean to the Sinai border. Coast to desert. Nothing was outside it.

**"The voice of the Source makes the deer give birth. It strips the forests bare."**

Those two in the same breath. *Makes the deer give birth* — the storm's so intense a pregnant doe drops her fawn from the shock. New life, terrified into existence. *And strips the forests bare.* Birth and destruction in one sentence. That's what real power looks like — it doesn't only break. It also *starts things*.

"And in His temple — everyone says: **Glory!**"

One word. That's all anybody can manage after that. Not a sermon. *Glory.*

Then the storm passes. And the last two verses are the calm:

**"The Source sat enthroned over the flood. The Source sits as King forever."**

*Over the flood.* Noah's flood — the word is *mabbul*, only used for that. The worst thing that ever happened to the earth, and He was *sitting* over it. Not scrambling. Not reacting. On a throne.

Whatever's flooding you — He's not underwater. He's on top of it, seated.

And then the ending — after all the thunder, all the cracking cedars, all the shaking wilderness:

**"The Source gives strength to His people. The Source blesses His people with peace."**

*Peace.* The last word after seven thunderclaps is *shalom*. The same Voice that snaps hundred-foot trees is the one that says *be at rest* to you.

That's the whole point. The power isn't the enemy. The power is *yours*. The One who shatters cedars is on your side. He spends the thunder on what threatens you, and the peace on you.

Stand on the porch. Watch the storm. Say the one word. Then go inside and sleep — the storm's Yours.

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* b'nei elim *= sons of the mighty / heavenly beings. v. 3–9 —* qol YHWH *= "voice of the LORD," seven occurrences. v. 6 —* yarkidem *= makes them skip/dance. v. 9 —* y'cholel ayalot *= causes the does to give birth (some read "oaks" — "does" is the primary sense). v. 10 —* mabbul *= the Flood, used only of Noah's deluge (Gen 6–11). v. 11 —* shalom *= peace, wholeness. No* yirah*.*
