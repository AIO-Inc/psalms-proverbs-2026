# PSALM 141 — The Lifting of My Hands as the Evening Sacrifice

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 141:1–10. Ten verses. Attributed to David. An evening prayer — the companion to Psalm 5's morning one. The middle verses are the most textually damaged in the Psalter; the beginning and end are crystal clear. Prayer as incense. Correction as kindness. Traps as the enemy's own grave.*

---

Psalm 140 ended — *the upright live with His face*. Psalm 141 is what that looks like at *sundown*. The day's over. The man's tired. And he prays before the smoke goes up.

**"Source — I call to You. Come quickly to me. Hear my voice when I call to You."**

*Come quickly.* *Chushah li.* Psalm 22, 38, 40, 70, 71 — *hurry*. David says it more than anyone. Not because God's slow. Because the *need* is fast.

**"May my prayer be set before You like incense — may the lifting of my hands be like the evening sacrifice."**

*Like incense.* *K'toret.* Exodus 30 — the incense altar, burned morning and evening, in front of the veil. The smoke went *up* and *in*. Revelation 5 and 8 — *golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints*. John got it from here. Your prayer *is* the smoke. It rises whether or not you feel it rising.

*The lifting of my hands as the evening sacrifice.* *Minchat-arev.* The grain offering at dusk. Psalm 134 — *lift your hands toward the sanctuary*. Psalm 141 — *the hands ARE the offering*. David's not at the Temple. He might be nowhere near it. And he says — *my hands going up — count that as the sacrifice*. That's the whole shift from Temple to *anywhere*. The body is the altar.

If you've got nothing to give tonight — lift your hands. That's the sacrifice. It counts.

Now — what he asks for. And it's not rescue. It's *restraint*:

**"Set a guard over my mouth, Source — keep watch over the door of my lips."**

*A guard over my mouth.* *Shomrah l'fi.* Psalm 121 — *He who KEEPS you*. Psalm 127 — *the WATCHMAN stays awake*. Psalm 141 — *put a watchman on my MOUTH*. The same guard who watches the city — station him at the *lips*. Psalm 39 — *I will put a muzzle on my mouth*. Psalm 141 — *You put it there. I can't hold it myself.*

*The door of my lips.* *Dal s'fatai.* Lips as a *door*. Things go *out*. Once out — gone. Psalm 140 — *viper venom on their lips*. Psalm 141 — *guard MINE*. He's just spent a psalm on their tongues. Now he's worried about his own.

**"Do not let my heart be drawn to what is evil — so that I take part in wicked deeds along with those who are evildoers. Let me not eat their delicacies."**

*Do not let my heart be drawn.* *Al-tat libbi.* Don't let it *tilt*. Not *I won't*. *Don't LET me.* Psalm 119 — *turn my heart toward Your statutes*. He knows the heart *leans*. He's asking God to hold the level.

*Let me not eat their delicacies.* *Man'ammeihem.* Their *tasty things*. Not their poison — their *treats*. The bribe that comes as dessert. The compromise that tastes good. Psalm 1 — *don't sit in their seat*. Psalm 141 — *don't eat at their table*. Because you'll *like* it. That's the danger.

Now — the verse that separates the wise from the proud:

**"Let a righteous man strike me — that is a kindness. Let him rebuke me — that is oil on my head. My head will not refuse it."**

*Let a righteous man strike me — that's loyal-love.* *Chesed.* Psalm 136's word — twenty-six times. Psalm 141 — *the correction that hurts is chesed too*. Proverbs 27 — *faithful are the wounds of a friend*. Proverbs 9 — *rebuke a wise man and he'll love you*.

*Oil on my head.* Psalm 23 — *You anoint my head with oil*. Psalm 133 — *oil running down Aaron's beard*. Psalm 141 — *the REBUKE is the oil*. The thing that stings is the anointing.

*My head will not refuse it.* *Al-yani roshi.* I won't *duck*. When the right person tells me the hard thing — I'll *hold still*. That's maturity. Not being right. Being *correctable*.

Here's the test. When was the last time somebody told you something true that you didn't want to hear — and you said *thank you*? If you can't remember — verse five's for you.

**"Yet my prayer is ever against the deeds of evildoers."**

*Still* — I pray against *their* deeds. Correction from the righteous — yes. Partnership with the wicked — no. Both at once.

Now — verses six and seven. The Hebrew here is *broken*. Scribes damaged it somewhere in the centuries, and nobody's fully sure what it says. Here's the best reading:

**"Their rulers will be thrown down from the cliffs — and the wicked will learn that my words were well spoken. They will say: 'As one plows and breaks up the earth — so our bones have been scattered at the mouth of the grave.'"**

*Thrown down from the cliffs.* Judgment. Psalm 137 — *dashed against the rock*. Psalm 141 — the *rulers* go off the *rock*.

*Our bones scattered at the mouth of Sheol.* Psalm 53 — *God scattered the bones*. Psalm 141 — *like plowed earth* — turned over, broken up, spread. The grave's *mouth* — Psalm 141 already talked about *my* mouth. Now — the *grave's*. Which mouth wins?

The verses are a mess. The *note* has the options. But the shape's clear: *the ones who set the table with delicacies end up scattered at the pit*.

**"But my eyes are fixed on You, Source, my Master. In You I take refuge — do not give me over to death."**

*But my eyes.* Psalm 123 — *my eyes look to You*. Psalm 25 — *my eyes are ever on the Source*. Psalm 141 — *fixed*. Whatever verses six and seven mean — *verse eight is where he's looking*.

*Do not give me over to death.* *Al-t'ar nafshi.* Don't *pour out* my soul. Isaiah 53 — *He poured out His soul to death*. David asks *not* to be poured. The Servant *chose* to be. That's the difference between a prayer and its answer.

**"Keep me from the snares they have laid for me — from the traps set by evildoers. Let the wicked fall into their own nets — while I pass by in safety."**

*Keep me.* *Shomreni.* Verse three — *guard my mouth*. Verse nine — *guard ME*. Same watchman. Mouth *and* whole man.

*Their own nets.* Psalm 7, 9, 35, 57, 140 — the pit-digger. Here — *the net-setter*. And the ending — *while I pass by*. *Ad-e'evor.* *Until I've walked past.* Not *destroy them*. *Let me get through*. The traps stay where they are. He just — *doesn't step in them*. Psalm 121 — *He won't let your foot slip*. Psalm 141 — *and I'll walk right past the trap*.

Ten verses. Here's the shape: *Come quickly — hear my voice. My prayer's incense. My hands are the evening sacrifice. — Guard my MOUTH. Don't let my heart tilt. Keep me from their dessert. Let the right man hit me — that's oil. I won't duck. — Their rulers off the cliff. Their bones at the grave's mouth. — But my eyes on You. Don't pour me out. Guard me from the traps — and let me walk past.*

Here's your practice tonight. Sundown. Lift your hands — that's the sacrifice. Then three requests, in order: *Guard my mouth. Don't let my heart tilt. Make me correctable.* Then — whoever set the trap — don't ask for their destruction. Ask to *walk past*.

The smoke's going up. It counts.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 — *chushah li* = hasten to me (cf. 22:19; 38:22; 40:13; 70:1, 5; 71:12). v. 2 — *k'toret* = incense (Exod 30:7–8; cf. Rev 5:8; 8:3–4); *mas'at kappai* = "the lifting of my palms"; *minchat-arev* = evening grain offering (Exod 29:38–41; 1 Kgs 18:36; Dan 9:21). v. 3 — *shomrah l'fi* = "a guard for my mouth" (cf. 39:1; Prov 13:3; Jas 3); *dal s'fatai* = "the door of my lips" — *dal* is a rare form of *delet* (door). v. 4 — *al-tat libbi* = "do not let my heart incline"; *man'ammeihem* = their delicacies/dainties (hapax). **v. 5 — *yehelmeni tzaddik chesed* = "let a righteous one strike me — [it is] *chesed*/loyal-love"; *shemen rosh al-yani roshi* = "oil of the head — let my head not refuse" — text uncertain; rendered as correction-as-anointing (cf. Prov 27:6; 9:8).** v. 6–7 — among the most corrupt verses in the Psalter; *nishm'tu bidei-sela shof'teihem* = "their judges were thrown down by the sides of the rock"; *k'mo foleach u-vokea ba-aretz* = "as one splitting and cleaving in the earth"; *nifz'ru atzameinu l'fi Sh'ol* = "our bones are scattered at the mouth of Sheol" — some MSS/LXX read "their bones"; both preserved. Sheol = the grave, never hell. v. 8 — *al-t'ar nafshi* = "do not pour out my soul" (cf. Isa 53:12). v. 9 — *shomreni* = keep/guard me — same root as v. 3. v. 10 — *ad-e'evor* = "until I pass over/by" — some read *yachad anochi ad-e'evor* = "I alone, until I pass by." No *yirah* in this psalm.*
