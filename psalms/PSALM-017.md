# PSALM 17 — Hide Me in the Shadow of Your Wings

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 17:1–15. A prayer for the night before a hard day.*

---

"Hear a right cause, Source. Pay attention to my cry. Listen to my prayer — it's coming from lips that don't lie."

He opens by saying *this is an honest prayer*. No angle. He's not trying to manipulate God. Just the straight ask.

"Let my verdict come from Your face. Let Your eyes see what's straight."

*From Your face.* Not from the crowd. Not from the people who've decided who he is. Straight from God, looking directly at him.

Then — like Psalm 7 — he invites the inspection:

"You've examined my heart. You've visited me at night. You've refined me and found nothing. I've made a decision: my mouth will not cross the line."

*Visited me at night.* The hours when nobody's watching, when you're alone with your thoughts. God's been there. Checked the heart while it was unguarded. And David's saying: *You didn't find what they say You'd find.*

"My steps have held to Your paths. My feet haven't slipped."

Now the ask — and here's where the language gets tender:

**"Keep me like the pupil of Your eye. Hide me in the shadow of Your wings."**

The pupil — the "little man" of the eye, the Hebrew says. The most protected part of your body. You blink without thinking to guard it. *Guard me like that — reflexively, instantly, before You even think about it.*

And the wings. A mother bird spreading over the nest when the hawk's circling. The chicks don't see the hawk. They see feathers and dark and warmth. *Hide me there.*

Then he describes the ones after him, and it's vivid:

"They've closed off their hearts with fat. Their mouths speak arrogance. They've tracked me down — now they surround me. Their eyes are set to throw me to the ground. Like a lion hungry to tear, like a young lion crouching in the brush."

*Closed their hearts with fat.* Comfortable. Insulated. Too well-fed to feel anything for anybody. That's what makes them dangerous — not their strength, their *numbness*.

"Rise up, Source! Confront him. Bring him down. Rescue my life from the mark-misser with Your sword."

And then a line that cuts:

"From men, by Your hand, Source — from men of this age, whose portion is in this life only."

*Whose portion is in this life only.* That's the diagnosis. Their whole inheritance is what they can grab here. When this life ends, they've got nothing. David said in Psalm 16: *You are my portion.* These men have chosen a different portion — and it comes with an expiration date.

"You fill their belly with what You've stored up. Their children have plenty. They leave the rest to their babies."

He's not saying they starve. He's saying: *they got what they wanted, and that's all they'll get.* Full bellies, full estates, and nothing that survives the funeral.

Then the last verse — the whole psalm was building to this:

**"But I — in right-standing I will see Your face. When I wake, I will be satisfied with Your likeness."**

*When I wake.* Two meanings, and David means both. Tomorrow morning, after this hard night — I'll wake up and see You. And *the* morning, the one after the last night — I'll wake up and see You.

*Satisfied with Your likeness.* The men with fat hearts got satisfied with a full belly. David gets satisfied with a *face*. Same word — *satisfied* — but only one of them lasts.

Tonight, before the hard day: *Keep me like the pupil of Your eye. Hide me in the shadow of Your wings.* Then close your eyes. You'll wake up satisfied.
