# PSALM 140 — Keep Me From the Hands of the Violent

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 140:1–13. Thirteen verses. Attributed to David. After the intimacy of 139, the danger of 140 — snakes' tongues, hidden traps, hot coals. An old-style David lament, with an imprecatory middle and a quiet ending. Paul quotes verse three in Romans 3.*

---

Psalm 139 — *You knit me, You know me, search me.* Psalm 140 — *and now there are people trying to unravel what You made.* The knitting has enemies.

**"Rescue me, Source, from evil men. Protect me from the violent — who devise evil plans in their hearts — and stir up war every day."**

*The violent.* *Ish chamasim* — *man of violences*. Plural. Not one act — a *pattern*. Psalm 11 — *the one who loves violence*. Psalm 18 — *the violent man*. Psalm 140 names him twice — verse one and verse four — because he's the *shape* of the threat.

*Stir up war every day.* Psalm 120 — *I am for peace — they're for war*. Psalm 140 — *every day*. It's their *hobby*. Some people don't want peace. They want the fight — because in the fight, they matter.

**"They make their tongues as sharp as a serpent's. The poison of vipers is on their lips."**

*A serpent's tongue.* Psalm 52 — *your tongue is a sharpened razor*. Psalm 57 — *tongues are sharp swords*. Psalm 58 — *venom like a snake*. Psalm 140 — the *viper*. *Achshuv* — an unknown snake species, appears only here. Something *specifically* poisonous.

And Paul — Romans 3:13 — *the poison of vipers is on their lips* — quoted word for word, right after Psalm 14 and Psalm 5. Paul's building his case that *everyone's* got the venom. Not just David's enemies. *Us.* Psalm 139 said *search me*. Romans 3 says *here's what He found.*

*A breath.*

**"Keep me safe, Source, from the hands of the violent — protect me from the wicked — who plan to trip my feet. The arrogant have hidden a snare for me. They have spread out the cords of their net — and have set traps for me along my path."**

*Trip my feet.* Psalm 121 — *He won't let your foot slip*. Psalm 140 — *they're TRYING to make it slip*. The keeper and the tripper — both working the same road.

*Snare. Net. Traps.* Psalm 9, 35, 57 — the pit-digger. Psalm 140 — three different trap words in two verses. *Pach* — the bird-snare. *Reshet* — the net. *Mokshim* — the trip-wires. They've got the whole hunting kit out. *For you.*

*A breath.*

Now — the turn. From describing them to *talking to God*:

**"I say to the Source: 'You are my God.' Hear, Source, my cry for mercy. Source, my Master — my strong deliverer — You shield my head in the day of battle."**

*I say to the Source: You are my God.* *Amarti la-YHWH Eli attah.* Psalm 16 — *I say to the Source: You are my Master*. Psalm 31 — *I say: You are my God*. Psalm 140 — *same words*. When the traps are laid — you *say it*. Out loud. *You are my God.* Not the trap. Not the viper. *You.*

*You shield my head in the day of battle.* *Sakkotah l'roshi.* You *covered* my head. Psalm 139 — *t'sukkeni* — *You knit me* — *same root*. The word that *wove* you in the womb — *covers your head* in the battle. The Maker is the *helmet*.

**"Do not grant the wicked their desires, Source. Do not let their plans succeed."**

*Their desires.* *Ma'avayei rasha.* What the mark-misser *craves*. Psalm 10 — *the desires of his heart*. Psalm 140 — *don't give it to him*. Simple prayer. Not *punish him*. *Don't let him get what he wants.* Sometimes that's enough.

*A breath.*

Now — the imprecatory part. Verses nine through eleven. Handle it like 137 — full strength, container, not command:

**"Those who surround me proudly rear their heads — may the mischief of their lips engulf them. May burning coals fall on them. May they be thrown into the fire — into miry pits — never to rise. May slanderers not be established in the land. May disaster hunt down the violent."**

*Mischief of their lips engulf them.* Psalm 7 — *the pit-digger falls in his pit*. Psalm 140 — *the liar drowns in his own lies*. Measure for measure. What comes out of the mouth comes back on the head.

*Burning coals.* Psalm 11 — *fire and sulfur*. Psalm 120 — *coals of the broom bush*. It's *hyperbole for total defeat* — the disarming of harm, not a wish for torture. David's not building a bonfire. He's *handing the rage up*. Psalm 137's container. Psalm 139 — *I hate them — SEARCH me.* Psalm 140 — *here's what I want to happen — You decide.*

*May disaster hunt down the violent.* *Y'dofenu* — *pursue him*. Psalm 23 — *goodness and loyal-love PURSUE me*. Same verb. Psalm 140 — *may disaster pursue HIM*. What chases you says who you are.

*A breath.*

And then — the quiet. After the coals — two verses. Almost whispered:

**"I know that the Source secures justice for the poor — and upholds the cause of the needy."**

*I know.* Psalm 135, 139 — *I know*. Not *I hope*. *Yadati.* After the trap and the viper and the coals — *I KNOW*. Psalm 9 — *the Source is a refuge for the oppressed*. Psalm 10 — *You hear the desire of the afflicted*. Psalm 140 — *I know He does the poor man's justice.* *Din ani.* He *litigates* for the one who can't afford a lawyer.

**"Surely the righteous will praise Your name — and the upright will live in Your presence."**

*The upright will live in Your presence.* *Yesh'vu y'sharim et-panecha.* They will *dwell with Your face*. Psalm 11 — *the upright will see His face*. Psalm 17 — *I'll see Your face when I wake*. Psalm 27 — *Your face, Source, I will seek*. Psalm 140 — *the upright SIT with it*. Not glimpse. *Live there.*

That's the last line. From *rescue me from the violent* to *the upright live in Your presence*. The violent are still out there. The traps are still set. And David's already *sitting with the face*. Because *I know*.

Thirteen verses. Here's the shape: *Rescue me — from the violent — who plan war every day. Viper tongues. Traps on the path — snare, net, trip-wire. — I SAY to the Source: You are my God. You covered my head. — Don't give them what they want. Let their lies drown them. Coals. Fire. Let disaster chase them. — I KNOW He does justice for the poor. The upright live with His face.*

Here's your practice. Whoever's got the traps out — say verse six. Out loud. *I say to the Source: You are my God.* Then say verse twelve — *I know*. Skip the coals if you can. Say them if you can't — to *Him*. Then go sit with the face.

He knit your head. He can cover it.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1, 4 — *ish chamasim* = "man of violences" — plural intensive; *rasha* = mark-misser. v. 2 — *yaguru milchamot* = "they stir up wars." v. 3 — *shan'nu l'shonam k'mo-nachash* = "they sharpen their tongue like a serpent"; *chamat achshuv* = "venom of a viper" — *achshuv* hapax (only here); **quoted Rom 3:13 (LXX form)**. v. 4 — *lidchot p'amai* = "to push/trip my steps." v. 5 — *pach* = snare; *chavalim* = cords; *reshet* = net; *mokshim* = traps — four hunting terms. v. 6 — *amarti la-YHWH Eli attah* = "I say to the Source: my God are You" — cf. 16:2; 31:14. v. 7 — *sakkotah l'roshi* = "You have covered my head" — root *sakak*, identical to 139:13 *t'sukkeni* ("You knit me") — deliberate echo, the Maker as helmet. v. 8 — *ma'avayei* = desires/cravings (hapax). v. 9–11 — imprecatory; *amal s'fateimo y'chasumo* = "the trouble of their lips — may it cover them" (reflexive judgment); *gechalim* = coals (cf. 11:6; 18:8, 12; 120:4); *mahamorot* = pits/watery depths (hapax); *y'dofenu* = may it pursue/hunt him — same verb as 23:6 (*yird'funi*). v. 12 — *yadati* = I know (Ketiv *yada'ta*, "You know" — Qere *yadati*; both preserved); *din ani* = judgment/justice for the poor. v. 13 — *yesh'vu y'sharim et-panecha* = "the upright will dwell with Your face." Selah after v. 3, 5, 8 — rendered as breath. No *yirah* in this psalm.*
