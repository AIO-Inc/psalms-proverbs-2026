# PSALM 63 — Better Than Life

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 63:1–11. David in the wilderness of Judah — dry country, no water, and the thirst becomes the prayer.*

---

He's in the desert. Actual desert — the Judean wilderness, where nothing grows and the ground cracks. And instead of praying *for* water, he prays *like* thirst.

**"God — You are my God. I seek You at dawn. My soul thirsts for You. My flesh longs for You — in a dry and weary land without water."**

*I seek You at dawn.* First thing. Before anything else. The Hebrew is *shachar* — the dawn itself, and the verb means to seek *early*, eagerly. He wakes up hunting.

*My flesh longs for You.* Not just the soul — the *body*. His skin wants God. That's what real thirst does — it's not an idea. It's a physical ache. He's saying his need for God is as physical as his need for water in a desert.

**"So I've seen You in the sanctuary — looking at Your strength and Your glory."**

He *remembers*. He's in the wilderness, but he's been in the temple. He knows what it looks like when God shows up. That memory is what he's drinking.

And then — the verse that's the center of everything:

**"Because Your loyal-love is better than life — my lips will praise You."**

*Better than life.* Not "better than my problems." Not "better than wealth." *Better than being alive.* If he had to choose between breathing and being loved by God — he'd pick the love. Because the love is what makes the breathing worth anything.

That's the most extreme thing a human can say about God, and David says it in a desert.

**"So I'll bless You as long as I live. In Your name I'll lift my hands."**

*Lift my hands.* Body language. Open. Reaching. Empty.

**"My soul is satisfied as with rich food. With joyful lips my mouth praises You."**

*Rich food.* The Hebrew is *chelev v'deshen* — fat and marrow. The best cuts. He's in a *desert* — starving — and he says his soul is *full of fat and marrow*. Because he found the thing that feeds deeper than food.

**"When I remember You on my bed — through the night watches I meditate on You."**

*On my bed. Night watches.* Can't sleep. Lying there in the dark. And instead of spiraling — he *meditates*. Turns God over in his mind. The insomnia becomes a prayer session.

If you're up at 3 a.m. — here's what to do with it.

**"Because You've been my help — in the shadow of Your wings I sing for joy."**

Wings again. Psalm 17, 36, 57, 61. He keeps going back under the feathers.

**"My soul clings to You. Your right hand holds me up."**

*Clings.* The Hebrew is *davak* — the word for how a husband and wife become one flesh in Genesis 2. *Glued.* Stuck fast. Won't let go.

*Your right hand holds me up.* And here's the thing — he's clinging, *and* God's holding. Both. It's not that David's grip is what saves him. It's that David's grip meets God's grip. Two hands. One holds tight; one holds *up*.

Now the enemies — briefly:

"The ones seeking to destroy my life will go down to the depths of the earth. They'll be given to the sword. They'll be food for jackals."

**"But the king will rejoice in God. Everyone who swears by Him will celebrate. The mouth of liars will be shut."**

*The mouth of liars — shut.* Silence for the ones who spoke against him. Song for the one who thirsted.

Eleven verses. Here's the shape: *I'm in the desert. I'm thirsty for You the way my body's thirsty for water. Your love is better than being alive. My soul is full of fat and marrow. I can't sleep so I think about You. I'm clinging; You're holding. The liars go quiet.*

Better than life. Say it in your desert. Then see what it does to the thirst.
