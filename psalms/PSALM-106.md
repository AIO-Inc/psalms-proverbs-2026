# PSALM 106 — Many Times He Delivered Them

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 106:1–48. Forty-eight verses. The other side of Psalm 105 — same history, told as confession. The last psalm of Book Four.*

---

Psalm 105 — *He remembered.* Psalm 106 — *we forgot.* Same events. Same God. Different subject in every sentence.

**"Praise the Source! Give thanks to the Source — because He is good. His loyal-love endures forever."**

*Hallelujah.* Then — the refrain that'll anchor Psalm 107, 118, and 136: *He is good — loyal-love forever.* The confession *opens* with praise. Because you can only confess safely to Someone whose love is *forever*.

**"Who can proclaim the mighty acts of the Source — or fully declare His praise? Happy are those who act justly — who always do what is right."**

**"Remember me, Source — when You show favor to Your people. Come to my aid when You save them — that I may enjoy the prosperity of Your chosen ones — share in the joy of Your nation — and join Your inheritance in giving praise."**

*Remember me.* One person. In the middle of a national confession. *Don't forget me when You fix this.*

And now — the confession. It starts with *we*:

**"We have sinned — even as our ancestors did. We have done wrong and acted wickedly."**

*We.* Not "they." The exiles owning the whole chain. Daniel 9 — *we have sinned*. Nehemiah 9. The generation in Babylon didn't say *our fathers did it.* They said *we*.

Then — the record. Eight failures. Watch them stack:

**One — the Red Sea.**

**"When our ancestors were in Egypt — they gave no thought to Your miracles. They did not remember Your many kindnesses — and they rebelled by the sea — the Red Sea."**

*Did not remember.* There it is. Psalm 105's hero — God's memory. Psalm 106's villain — *ours*. They were *at the sea*, watching it about to split — and complaining.

**"Yet He saved them for His name's sake — to make His mighty power known. He rebuked the Red Sea and it dried up. He led them through the depths as through a desert. He saved them from the hand of the foe. From the hand of the enemy He redeemed them. The waters covered their adversaries — not one of them survived. Then they believed His promises — and sang His praise."**

*Then they believed.* *After* the sea. For a minute.

**Two — the wilderness cravings.**

**"But they soon forgot what He had done — and did not wait for His plan. In the desert they gave in to their craving. In the wilderness they put God to the test. So He gave them what they asked for — but sent a wasting disease among them."**

*Soon forgot.* How soon? Three days. Exodus 15 to Exodus 16. *He gave them what they asked for.* Psalm 78's quail. Psalm 81's *I gave them over*. You can get what you want and lose what you need.

**Three — the mutiny.**

**"In the camp they grew envious of Moses — and of Aaron, who was consecrated to the Source. The earth opened up and swallowed Dathan. It buried the company of Abiram. Fire blazed among their followers. A flame consumed the wicked."**

Numbers 16. The revolt against leadership. The ground *ate* them.

**Four — the calf.**

**"At Horeb they made a calf — and worshiped an idol cast from metal. They exchanged their glorious God — for an image of a bull — which eats grass."**

*Which eats grass.* The psalmist's contempt. *You traded the One who split the sea for a thing that eats LAWN.* Romans 1:23 — *they exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images.*

**"They forgot the God who saved them — who had done great things in Egypt — miracles in the land of Ham — and awesome deeds by the Red Sea."**

*Forgot.* Second time.

**"So He said He would destroy them — had not Moses, His chosen one — stood in the breach before Him — to keep His wrath from destroying them."**

*Stood in the breach.* Exodus 32. Moses — *between* God and the people. Like a man stepping into a gap in the wall so the flood doesn't get through. One person standing where the wrath would've come. The psalm says that's what *saved* them. Hebrews 7 — *He always lives to intercede.* Moses was the sketch. Somebody else is the finished drawing.

**Five — the refusal.**

**"Then they despised the pleasant land. They did not believe His promise. They grumbled in their tents — and did not obey the Source. So He swore to them with uplifted hand — that He would make them fall in the wilderness — make their descendants fall among the nations — and scatter them throughout the lands."**

*Despised the pleasant land.* They got to the border — the land God swore to Abraham — and *didn't want it*. Too scary. Giants. Psalm 95 — *they shall never enter My rest.*

**Six — Baal of Peor.**

**"They yoked themselves to the Baal of Peor — and ate sacrifices offered to lifeless gods. They aroused the Source's anger by their wicked deeds — and a plague broke out among them. But Phinehas stood up and intervened — and the plague was checked. This was credited to him as right-standing — for endless generations to come."**

*Phinehas stood up.* Numbers 25. Another one standing in the breach.

**Seven — Meribah.**

**"By the waters of Meribah they angered the Source — and trouble came to Moses because of them. Because they rebelled against the Spirit of God — rash words came from Moses' lips."**

*Trouble came to Moses.* Even *Moses* broke. Numbers 20 — struck the rock instead of speaking to it. And didn't enter the land. The people's rebellion cost the *intercessor*.

**Eight — the land.**

**"They did not destroy the peoples as the Source had commanded them — but they mingled with the nations — and adopted their customs. They worshiped their idols — which became a snare to them. They sacrificed their sons and their daughters to false gods. They shed innocent blood — the blood of their sons and daughters — whom they sacrificed to the idols of Canaan — and the land was desecrated by their blood. They defiled themselves by what they did. By their deeds they prostituted themselves."**

*Sacrificed their sons and daughters.* The bottom. The people God carried out of Egypt on eagle's wings — burning their kids to Molech. The psalmist doesn't look away.

**"So the Source was angry with His people — and abhorred His inheritance. He gave them into the hands of the nations — and their foes ruled over them. Their enemies oppressed them — and subjected them to their power."**

Exile. Judges. Assyria. Babylon.

And then — the verse. The whole psalm in one line:

**"Many times He delivered them — but they were bent on rebellion — and they wasted away in their sin."**

*Many times.* The Hebrew is *p'amim rabbot* — *many, many times*. Eight failures listed. Eight rescues implied. Every fall — a *catch*. And still — *bent on rebellion*.

**"Yet He took note of their distress — when He heard their cry."**

*Yet.* Psalm 78 — *yet He was merciful*. Psalm 106 — *yet He took note*. Eight rebellions. One *yet*. And it wins.

**"For their sake He remembered His covenant — and out of His great love He relented. He caused all who held them captive — to show them mercy."**

*He remembered.* Psalm 105's word — in Psalm 106. Even the confession psalm can't get to the end without it. *They* forgot — twice, named. *He* remembered.

*Out of His great love He relented.* The Hebrew is *va-yinachem* — Psalm 90's *relent*, Psalm 71's *comfort*. He *changed course* — because of love.

Now — the exiles' prayer:

**"Save us, Source our God — and gather us from the nations — that we may give thanks to Your holy name — and glory in Your praise."**

*Gather us.* Psalm 147 — *He gathers the exiles*. They're asking.

And the Book Four doxology:

**"Praise be to the Source — the God of Israel — from everlasting to everlasting. Let all the people say: 'Amen!'**
**Praise the Source!"**

*Let all the people say Amen.* The whole congregation. Not just the psalmist. Everyone who just heard eight failures — *amen*. Because the *yet* held.

Forty-eight verses. Here's the shape: *Hallelujah — He's good — loyal-love forever. WE sinned — like our fathers. Red Sea — complained. Wilderness — craved. Camp — mutinied. Horeb — a bull that eats grass. Moses stood in the breach. Despised the land. Baal of Peor. Meribah — even Moses broke. Canaan — burned their kids. MANY TIMES He delivered them. Yet — He remembered. He relented. Gather us. Amen.*

Psalm 105 and 106 together are the whole Bible: *He remembered — we forgot — He remembered anyway.*

Book Four closes. Book Five opens with the four rescues — and the refrain that answers all eight failures.
