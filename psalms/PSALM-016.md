# PSALM 16 — You Will Not Abandon Me to the Grave

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 16:1–11. Quoted by Peter at Pentecost (Acts 2) as pointing to resurrection.*

---

This one's about contentment. Real contentment — not the fake kind you post about.

"Keep me, God — I take refuge in You. I said to the Source: 'You are my Master. I have no good thing apart from You.'"

*No good thing apart from You.* Not "You're the best thing." *Nothing good exists outside of You.* Every good thing you've ever had — the sunlight, the coffee, the friend, the laugh — it was all Him, delivered.

"The holy ones on the earth — they're the excellent ones. All my delight is in them."

He loves God's people. Not the institution. The *people*. Finds them excellent. When did you last say that about the folks in your congregation? Try it.

"The ones who run after another god multiply their sorrows. I won't pour out their blood offerings. I won't even take their names on my lips."

*Multiply their sorrows.* That's what a false god does. It doesn't just fail you — it *compounds* the failure. Every substitute you chase — money, approval, control, the substance, the person — adds another layer of grief. He's not going there.

Now the contentment section, and it's beautiful:

**"The Source is my allotted portion and my cup. You hold my lot secure."**

In ancient Israel, every family got a *portion* of land — their inheritance, their piece of the promise. David says: *You're my portion. You're the land I got. I don't need acreage — I've got You.*

**"The boundary lines have fallen for me in pleasant places. Yes, my inheritance is beautiful to me."**

*The boundary lines.* The limits of your life — where you were born, what you were given, the hand you were dealt. He looks at his and says: *pleasant. Beautiful.* Not because it's all been easy — he's the guy who wrote Psalm 6 with the wet pillow. Because he's decided to love the lot he was handed, with God in the middle of it.

"I'll bless the Source who counsels me. Even at night my inner self instructs me."

Even at night. The stuff God's teaching you sinks in while you sleep. Your gut catches up to what your head already heard.

**"I've set the Source in front of me continually. Because He's at my right hand, I will not be shaken."**

*Continually.* Not just Sunday. In the meeting. In traffic. In the argument. He's in front of you, and He's beside you, and so — same word as Psalm 15 — you don't get knocked off your feet.

"So my heart is glad and my glory rejoices. My body also rests secure."

Heart, glory (the inner self), body. All three. Gladness from the inside out. That's what contentment actually feels like.

And then the lines Peter stood up and quoted in front of three thousand people:

**"Because You will not abandon my soul to Sheol. You will not let Your loyal one see the pit."**

Not abandoned to the grave. This is one of the earliest whispers in the Bible that death isn't the end of the story. David didn't have the full picture. But he had the *conviction*: the One he'd made his portion wasn't going to leave him in the ground.

**"You'll show me the path of life. In Your presence — fullness of joy. At Your right hand — pleasures forevermore."**

*Fullness of joy.* The Hebrew is *soba* — satiety, the feeling after a great meal when there's nothing left to want. That's what His presence is. Full. Nothing missing.

You want contentment? Here's the recipe. Make Him your portion. Look at your boundary lines and call them pleasant. Set Him in front of you all day. And know — the grave isn't the end of the path.

---

*Note on translation: v. 10 — "Sheol" = the grave/realm of the dead — NOT hell. "Corruption/the pit" =* shachat *= the pit, decay. Peter (Acts 2:27) reads this as prophecy of resurrection. v. 11 —* soba semachot *= satiety of joys — completeness, nothing lacking. "Holy One" (v. 10) =* chasid *= loyal/devoted one. No* yirah*.*
