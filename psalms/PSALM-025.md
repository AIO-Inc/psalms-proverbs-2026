# PSALM 25 — Remember Not the Sins of My Youth

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 25:1–22. An acrostic — each verse begins with the next Hebrew letter. A prayer for people with a past.*

---

"To You, Source, I lift up my soul."

Remember Psalm 24 — *the one who hasn't lifted his soul to what's false*? Here's the alternative. Same verb. *I lift my soul to You.* You're going to lift it to something. Pick the real thing.

"My God, I trust in You. Don't let me be ashamed. Don't let my enemies celebrate over me. Nobody who waits for You will be ashamed — but the ones who betray for no reason, they'll be the ones ashamed."

*Nobody who waits.* Waiting is the hard part. He's saying the wait itself is the trust.

Now the request — and it's the request of a man who knows he doesn't know:

**"Show me Your ways, Source. Teach me Your paths. Lead me in Your truth and teach me — because You're the God who saves me. I wait for You all day long."**

*Teach me.* Twice. He's not asking God to fix his circumstances. He's asking God to fix his *understanding*. That's a different prayer, and a smarter one.

"Remember Your compassion, Source, and Your loyal-love — they're from forever."

Then the line this psalm is known for. If you've got a history — and you do — this one's yours:

**"Do not remember the sins of my youth or my rebellions. According to Your loyal-love, remember me — for the sake of Your goodness, Source."**

*The sins of my youth.* The stuff you did before you knew better. Before you got clean. Before you turned around. It's still there — in the memory, in the consequences, in the people who knew you then.

David says: *Don't remember that. Remember ME. And do it because of who YOU are, not because of who I was.*

Notice — he doesn't ask God to *forget*. He asks God to *remember differently*. To look at him through loyal-love instead of through the record. Same facts, different lens. That's what mercy actually is.

**"Good and straight is the Source. So He teaches the ones who miss the mark the right road."**

Wait. He *teaches the mark-missers*? Not punishes — *teaches*. The people who got it wrong are the ones He's *instructing*. Because good and straight isn't just what He is — it's what He does *to* you.

"He guides the humble in what's right. He teaches the humble His way."

*Humble* — the Hebrew is *anav*, the same root as Psalm 18's "Your gentleness made me great." God's gentleness lands on the gentle. Come low, and He comes to meet you there.

"All the paths of the Source are loyal-love and truth — for the ones who keep His covenant."

**"The secret of the Source is for those who stand in awe of Him. He shows them His covenant."**

*The secret.* The Hebrew is *sod* — the intimate counsel, the inside conversation. The stuff He tells His close friends. And who gets it? Not the smartest. Not the most religious. *The ones in awe.* Wonder is the password. Stand amazed and He'll tell you things.

"My eyes are always on the Source. He'll pull my feet out of the net."

Then the ask gets raw:

"Turn to me. Be gracious to me — because I'm alone and afflicted. The troubles of my heart have grown wide. Bring me out of my distress. **Look at my affliction and my pain. And carry away all my sins.**"

*Alone.* The Hebrew is *yachid* — the only one, the solitary. He's not surrounded by enemies in this psalm. He's just — alone. If you've been there, you know it's worse than surrounded.

And then — *carry away all my sins*. Not "overlook." *Carry away.* Pick them up and take them somewhere else. The word is *nasa* — to lift, to bear, to carry off. Same word used for what the scapegoat did. Somebody carries it away for you.

"Look how many enemies I have. How violently they hate me. Guard my soul and rescue me. Don't let me be ashamed — I've taken refuge in You. Let integrity and straightness protect me, because I've waited for You."

And then — the acrostic is done, every letter used — and David adds one more line, outside the pattern. Because he couldn't stop with just himself:

**"Redeem Israel, God — out of all its troubles."**

Not just me. *All of us.* He's a man with a past praying for a whole people with a past.

So — you with the youth you'd rather not remember: *Don't remember that. Remember me.* He's good and straight. He teaches the ones who missed. The secret's for the ones who stand amazed. And He carries it away.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* nasa nefesh *= lift the soul, direct contrast with Ps 24:4. v. 8 — "therefore will he teach sinners in the way" =* yoreh chata'im *— instruction, not punishment. v. 9, 12, 14 — "fear him" =* yere'av *=* yirah *= those who stand in awe;* sod *(v. 14) = intimate counsel, secret. v. 18 —* nasa l'chol chatotai *= "lift/carry away all my sins" — the scapegoat verb (Lev 16:22). v. 16 —* yachid *= alone, solitary, only one.*
