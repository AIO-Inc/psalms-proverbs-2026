# PSALM 34 — Taste and See

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 34:1–22. Written after David escaped from the Philistine king Achish by pretending to be insane (1 Samuel 21). An acrostic. And one of the most* yirah*-heavy psalms in the book — four times "fear of the Lord," every one of them awe.*

---

Here's the setup: David's on the run from Saul, flees to enemy territory, gets recognized, and the only way out is to drool on his beard and scratch on the gate like a madman until they throw him out. He gets away. And *then* he writes this — one of the most joyful psalms in the book.

Don't miss that. This came *out of* the humiliation. Not before it.

**"I will bless the Source at all times. His praise is always in my mouth."**

*At all times.* Including the drooling-on-the-gate times. That's the claim.

"My soul boasts in the Source. Let the humble hear — and be glad. **Make the Source great with me. Let's lift His name together.**"

He wants company. *Come do this with me.* Praise is better in a group.

**"I sought the Source — and He answered me. He rescued me from all my terrors."**

*All my terrors.* Plural. The guy pretending to be crazy at the Philistine gate was terrified. And every one of those terrors — gone.

**"They looked to Him and were radiant. Their faces were never ashamed."**

*Radiant.* Look at Him long enough and it shows on your face. Moses came down the mountain glowing. Same idea. You reflect what you stare at.

**"This poor man cried — and the Source heard. He saved him from all his troubles."**

*This poor man.* David's pointing at himself. Not "the king." Not "the anointed." *This poor man.* That's how he identifies at the gate — and that's who gets heard.

"The messenger of the Source camps around those who stand in awe of Him — and delivers them."

*Camps around.* Not visits. Sets up tents. Stays.

Now the invitation — the line this psalm is known for:

**"Taste and see that the Source is good. Happy is the one who takes refuge in Him."**

*Taste.* Not "believe." Not "understand." *Taste.* Put it in your mouth. You can't argue someone into knowing what honey's like — they have to eat it. He's saying: *stop debating. Try it.* One prayer. One day of trust. One act of doing right when it costs you. Taste, then decide.

**"Stand in awe of the Source, you His holy ones — because those who stand in awe of Him lack nothing."**

There it is. *Lack nothing* — the same words as Psalm 23. And what's the condition? *Awe.* The ones who stay amazed don't run out. Wonder is what keeps the well full.

"Young lions go hungry and starve. But the ones who seek the Source lack no good thing."

*Young lions* — the strongest, fastest predators alive — go hungry. The seekers don't. Strength isn't the variable. Seeking is.

Now David turns teacher:

**"Come, children — listen to me. I'll teach you the awe of the Source."**

This is the sentence that's been mistranslated for four hundred years — *I will teach you the fear of the Lord.* Generations of children have heard that and learned to be afraid of God. That was *never* what it meant. *Yirah.* Awe. Wonder. The gasp. He's saying: *Kids, sit down. I'm going to teach you how to be amazed.*

And here's the curriculum:

"Who wants a long life? Who wants to see good days? **Keep your tongue from evil. Keep your lips from speaking deceit. Turn from evil and do good. Seek peace — and chase it.**"

That's the awe class. Watch your mouth. Don't lie. Turn around. Do good. *Chase* peace — the word is *radaf*, the same pursuit word from Psalm 23. Hunt peace down like it owes you money.

Nothing in there about being afraid. Everything in there about how you *live*.

**"The eyes of the Source are on the ones who do right. His ears are open to their cry."**

Eyes and ears. Watching and listening. Full attention.

"The face of the Source is against the ones doing evil — to cut off their memory from the earth."

**"They cried — and the Source heard. He rescued them from all their troubles."**

Same as verse 6, now for everyone. Not just this poor man — *them*. Whoever cries.

And then — the verse that's held more people together than almost any other:

**"The Source is close to the broken-hearted. He saves the crushed in spirit."**

*Close.* Not "will eventually attend to." *Close.* Right there. The broken heart isn't a barrier to God — it's a magnet. He moves *toward* it.

*Crushed in spirit.* The word is *dakka* — pulverized, ground to powder. Not cracked. Not bruised. *Dust.* And He saves *that*.

"Many are the troubles of the one who does right — **but the Source rescues him from all of them.**"

*Many.* He doesn't lie to you. Doing right doesn't mean fewer troubles. It means Someone in every one of them.

**"He guards all his bones. Not one of them is broken."**

John 19:36 — the soldiers didn't break Jesus's legs. John says it was to fulfill this. Every bone accounted for.

"Evil will kill the evil one. The ones who hate the righteous will be condemned. **The Source redeems the soul of His servants. None who take refuge in Him will be condemned.**"

*None.* Not "few." *None.*

So: one poor man, drooling at a gate to save his life, gets away — and sits down and writes *taste and see that He is good* and *let me teach you awe*. That's what's possible on the other side of your worst day.

Taste. Chase peace. He's close to the broken.

---

*Note on translation: v. 7, 9 (x2), 11 —* yirah *appears four times, more than almost any psalm. "Fear of the LORD" in every case =* yirat YHWH *= awe, reverence, wonder — NEVER dread. v. 11 "I will teach you the fear of the LORD" is one of the most damaging mistranslations in the Psalter — the curriculum that follows (v. 12–14) is entirely ethical, not about fright. v. 8 —* ta'amu *= taste, experience directly. v. 14 —* radfehu *= pursue/chase it (peace). v. 18 —* nishberei lev *= broken of heart;* dakk'ei ruach *= crushed/pulverized of spirit. v. 20 — cf. John 19:36.*
