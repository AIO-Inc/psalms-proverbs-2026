# PSALM 38 — My Wounds Stink

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 38:1–22. A penitential psalm — sickness, guilt, abandonment, all at once.*

---

This is the ugliest psalm in the first forty. Not badly written — *ugly*, in what it describes. Sickness plus guilt plus loneliness. If you've had all three at once, this is yours.

"Source — don't correct me in Your anger. Don't discipline me while You're hot."

Same opening as Psalm 6. He's not saying *don't correct me*. He's saying *correct me like a Father, not like a judge.*

"Your arrows have gone into me. Your hand has come down on me. **There's no soundness in my body because of Your anger. No peace in my bones because of my missing-the-mark.**"

He owns it. *My* missing-the-mark. Whatever this illness is, he's connected it to something he did, and he's not dodging.

**"My guilt has gone over my head. Like a heavy load — too heavy for me."**

*Over my head.* Drowning. Not "I feel bad." *The water's above me.* And the weight — *too heavy for me*. He's admitting he can't carry it. That admission is the door.

Now — the body. He doesn't spare you:

**"My wounds stink and fester — because of my foolishness. I'm bent over. Bowed down completely. All day I go around in mourning. My insides are burning. There's no soundness in my body. I'm numb. Completely crushed. I roar from the groaning of my heart."**

*Stink and fester.* He wrote that in Scripture. Infected wounds. The smell of a body going wrong. *Numb* and *roaring* in the same breath — the deadness and the screaming, at once. That's what a long sickness does. You go flat and you go wild and there's no in-between.

And *because of my foolishness* — he's not saying God did this to him. He's saying *I did this to me, and now it's rotting.*

Then — the one calm line in the storm:

**"Master — all my longing is before You. My groaning isn't hidden from You."**

*All my longing is before You.* Not the words. Not the prayer. The *longing* — the wordless want underneath. You don't have to say it right. He reads the longing.

"My heart pounds. My strength has left me. **Even the light of my eyes — gone.**"

Same image as Psalm 13 — *light up my eyes*. His went out.

Now the loneliness:

**"My loved ones and my friends stand back from my wound. My relatives stand far off."**

*Stand back.* Not gone — *standing back*. They're in the room but they won't come close. The smell. The mess. The awkwardness. They love him, and they can't do it. If you've been the one in the bed, you know the exact distance they stand at.

"The ones hunting my life set traps. The ones wanting my harm speak of ruin. They meditate on deceit all day long."

**"But I — like a deaf man, I don't hear. Like a mute, I don't open my mouth. I'm like a man who doesn't hear — with no argument in his mouth."**

He's *not responding*. Not because he can't — because he's chosen not to. No comeback. No defense. Isaiah 53 says the same about the Servant — *like a sheep before its shearers, silent*. David's already living it. The rotting, mocked, abandoned man who *won't fire back*.

Why?

**"Because I wait for You, Source. You will answer, my Master and my God."**

That's why. He's not silent from weakness. He's silent because he's *waiting for a different Voice*. Why argue with the accusers when the Judge is about to speak?

"I said: 'Don't let them celebrate over me — the ones who exalt themselves when my foot slips.' **Because I'm ready to fall. My pain is in front of me continually.**"

*Ready to fall.* The Hebrew is *tzela* — on the verge of limping, about to go down. He's standing, but barely.

**"I confess my guilt. I'm anxious about my missing-the-mark."**

Confess. Same door Psalm 32 walked through. *When I kept silent, my bones wore out.* He's not keeping silent.

"My enemies are alive and strong. The ones who hate me for lies have multiplied. They repay good with evil. They accuse me because I pursue good."

And then — no resolution. No "and then God healed me." The psalm just — asks:

**"Don't abandon me, Source. My God — don't be far from me.**
**Hurry to help me. Master — my rescue."**

That's the end. Still sick. Still guilty. Still alone. Wounds still stinking. And he says: *Don't leave. Hurry.* And stops.

Here's why this psalm is in the book: because sometimes you don't get the turn. Sometimes the morning doesn't come by the last verse. And God wanted you to have a prayer for *that* — the one where you're in the middle and nothing's fixed and all you've got is *hurry*.

You can end there. He did. It's allowed.
