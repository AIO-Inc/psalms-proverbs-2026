# PSALM 4 — Lie Down in Peace

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 4:1–8. An evening psalm.*

---

This is the psalm for the end of a bad day.

"Answer me when I call, God of my right-standing. You've given me room before when I was boxed in. Do it again. Be gracious. Hear me."

Notice he's not asking for a miracle. He's asking for *room*. Breathing space. That thing that happens when the walls stop closing in.

Then he turns to the people who've been making his life miserable — and this is good, because you've got these people too:

"How long are you going to trade my dignity for shame? How long are you going to love empty things and chase lies?"

Some people fall in love with what's hollow. They chase what isn't there. You can't fix that for them. But you can know this:

"The Source has set apart the one who's loyal to Him. He hears me when I call."

Set apart. Marked. Known. You're not lost in the crowd of eight billion. He picked you out.

Now here's the advice section — and it's about anger, because bad days generate anger:

"Tremble — feel it fully — but don't miss the mark with it. Go to your bed and have it out with your own heart. Then be still."

He's not saying don't feel it. He's saying feel it *in bed*, with God, with your own soul — not out in the street with your fists. Let the anger come, talk to it, and then get quiet.

"Bring the offering of doing right. Trust the Source."

Everyone around you is saying, "Who's going to show us anything good? Where's the good in any of this?" The whole world is asking that right now.

And David answers: "Lift up the light of Your face on us."

That's the good. His face turned toward you. That's what everyone's actually looking for.

"You've put more gladness in my heart than they have when the harvest comes in and the wine is flowing."

More joy than the party. More joy than the payday. Not from stuff — from being *looked at* by the One who made you.

And then the last line, the one you say as your head hits the pillow:

**"In peace I will lie down, and I will sleep — because You alone, Source, make me dwell in safety."**

Not the locks on the door. Not the balance in the account. You alone.

Say it tonight. Mean it. Then close your eyes.

---

*Note on translation: v. 4 — often rendered "stand in awe and sin not"; Hebrew* rigzu *= tremble/be agitated — this is about anger, not reverence. Paul quotes it as "be angry and sin not" (Eph 4:26). "Sin" =* chata *= miss the mark. "Godly" (v. 3) =* chasid *= loyal, devoted, covenant-faithful.*
