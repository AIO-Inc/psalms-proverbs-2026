# PSALM 47 — Clap Your Hands

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 47:1–9. Nine verses of pure celebration. Sons of Korah.*

---

Psalm 46 said *be still*. Psalm 47 says *now get loud*. Both are right. Stillness first — then, when you've seen Him, you can't stay quiet.

**"Clap your hands, all you peoples! Shout to God with a ringing cry!"**

*All you peoples.* Not just Israel. Everybody. Every nation, every tribe — *clap*. The poet's inviting the whole planet to the party.

*A ringing cry.* The Hebrew is *rinnah* — the shout that happens at a wedding, a homecoming, a victory. Not polite applause. The noise a stadium makes.

**"Because the Source, the Most High, is awesome — the great King over all the earth."**

*Awesome.* There's *yirah* — the word that's been mistranslated as "terrible" and "to be feared" for centuries. Here it's *nora* — awe-inspiring, breathtaking. The King you look at and your jaw drops. Not the King you hide from. The King you *clap for*.

"He subdues peoples under us — nations under our feet. He chooses our inheritance for us — the pride of Jacob, whom He loved."

*Whom He loved.* Again. That's always the reason. Not "whom He selected for strategic purposes." *Loved.*

Now — the center of the psalm. Picture the Ark being carried up into the temple, and the crowd watching it go:

**"God has gone up with a shout — the Source with the sound of the trumpet!"**

*Gone up.* The ascension. God taking His seat. And the noise — a *shout*, a *trumpet* — it's a coronation, and everyone's invited to make noise.

**"Sing praises to God! Sing praises! Sing praises to our King! Sing praises!"**

Four times in one verse. *Zamru. Zamru. Zamru. Zamru.* Sing-sing-sing-sing. The poet's not being repetitive by accident. He's *drumming*. He wants you to feel the beat.

**"Because God is King of all the earth — sing praises with skill!"**

*With skill.* Same as Psalm 33. Don't just make noise. *Make it well.* Practice. Rehearse. He's worth your best — and *joy done skillfully* is a different thing than joy done sloppy.

**"God reigns over the nations. God sits on His holy throne."**

*Sits.* Seated. Not pacing. Not anxious. On the throne — the seat of someone whose work is done and whose rule is settled.

And then the last verse — and it's bigger than it looks:

**"The princes of the peoples gather — as the people of the God of Abraham.**
**Because the shields of the earth belong to God. He is greatly exalted."**

*The princes of the peoples gather as the people of the God of Abraham.* Read that slow. The *foreign* rulers — the nations, the outsiders, the ones who weren't part of the covenant — they gather, and they're counted *as Abraham's people*.

That's the promise God made Abraham in Genesis 12 — *through you, all the families of the earth will be blessed*. Psalm 47 is watching it happen. The whole world becoming family. Paul spent Romans and Galatians explaining it. The Korahites just *sang* it.

*The shields of the earth belong to God.* Every defense every nation ever built — His. Not because He seized them. Because they were always His, and the nations are finally figuring it out and *clapping*.

Nine verses. No lament. No enemy. No "how long." Just — *clap, shout, He's awesome, He's gone up, sing-sing-sing-sing, He sits, everyone's family.*

You need one of these. After 44's belly in the dirt and 46's earth giving way — you need a psalm that's *just* the party. Here it is.

Clap. Get loud. He's awesome — and that's the good kind.
