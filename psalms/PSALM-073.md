# PSALM 73 — I Almost Slipped

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 73:1–28. Book Three opens. Asaph — a temple musician — nearly loses his faith watching the wicked prosper. This is how he got it back.*

---

This is the most honest psalm about doubt in the whole book. A worship leader almost walked away. He tells you exactly why — and exactly what stopped him.

**"Surely God is good to Israel — to the pure in heart."**

He starts with the conclusion. The thing he *now* believes. Then he tells you how close he came to not believing it.

**"But as for me — my feet almost stumbled. My steps nearly slipped."**

*Almost. Nearly.* He was on the edge. Not "I had a hard week." *I almost went over.*

**"Because I envied the arrogant — when I saw the prosperity of the wicked."**

There's the trigger. *Envy.* He looked at the people who didn't care about God — and they were *winning*. And he wanted what they had.

Now — what he saw. And he's specific:

**"They have no pain. Their bodies are healthy and strong. They're free from the burdens common to man. They're not plagued like other people."**

*No pain.* The wicked aren't just rich — they're *comfortable*. No back trouble. No sleepless nights. The people who ignore God look like they got a better deal on the *body*.

**"So pride is their necklace. Violence is the clothing they wear."**

They *wear* it. Pride like jewelry. Cruelty like a jacket. On display.

**"Their eyes bulge with fat. The imaginations of their heart run wild. They mock. They speak with malice. From on high they threaten oppression. Their mouth claims heaven. Their tongue takes possession of the earth."**

*Their mouth claims heaven.* They talk like gods. And nothing stops them.

**"So the people turn to them — and drink up their words like water."**

The *crowd* follows them. The wicked have *fans*. Everyone's lining up to hear what the arrogant have to say.

**"They say: 'How would God know? Does the Most High have knowledge?'"**

Psalm 10, Psalm 14 — the practical atheism. *He doesn't see.*

**"Look — this is what the wicked are like: always at ease. Increasing in wealth."**

Now — Asaph's conclusion at the low point:

**"Surely I've kept my heart pure for nothing. I've washed my hands in innocence — for nothing."**

*For nothing.* The Hebrew is *rik* — empty, vain, in vain. He's saying: *I did everything right and it got me NOTHING. They did everything wrong and got EVERYTHING.* That's the exact sentence people say right before they walk out of the church for good.

**"All day long I've been plagued. Every morning brings new punishment."**

*He's* the one in pain. Not them. The worship leader has the back trouble and the sleepless nights.

And here's the moment. Watch how close it gets:

**"If I'd said: 'I'll speak like this' — I would have betrayed the generation of Your children."**

*If I'd said it out loud.* He's got the whole argument ready. *God doesn't pay. Righteousness is a scam. Look at them — look at me.* And he *almost* says it. Almost preaches it. And the one thing that stops him is — *the kids*. *Your children.* The next generation, who'd hear a worship leader say *it's all for nothing* — and believe him.

He kept his mouth shut. Not because he'd resolved it. Because he knew what his words would do to the people listening.

**"When I tried to understand all this — it was oppressive to me —"**

*Oppressive.* He couldn't think his way out. The more he thought, the worse it got.

**"— until I entered the sanctuary of God. Then I understood their end."**

*Until.* One word. *Until I went in.* Not "until I figured it out." Until he *entered the sanctuary*. He stopped thinking and went to where God was. And *there* — not in the argument, in the *presence* — the picture changed.

*Their end.* He'd been looking at their *middle*. The comfortable years. The bulging eyes and the fans. In the sanctuary he saw where the road *goes*.

**"Surely You place them on slippery ground. You throw them down to ruin. How suddenly they're destroyed — completely swept away by terrors. Like a dream when one awakes — so when You arise, Master — You despise them as fantasies."**

*Like a dream when you wake up.* All that prosperity — a *dream*. You wake up and it's not there. Not because it wasn't vivid — because it was never *real*.

Now — Asaph turns on himself, and it's brutal and it's honest:

**"When my heart was bitter — and I was pierced inside — I was senseless and ignorant. I was a brute beast before You."**

*A brute beast.* He's calling himself an animal. Not to grovel — because he sees now that the envy had made him *stupid*. Psalm 49's animals — no understanding. He'd become one.

And then — the turn. The verse that saved his faith:

**"Yet I am always with You. You hold my right hand."**

*Yet.* Even as a brute beast. Even bitter. Even about to betray the children. *I am always with You.* Not because he held on. Because *You hold my right hand.* Psalm 63 — two hands. He'd let go. God hadn't.

**"You guide me with Your counsel. Afterward You'll receive me into glory."**

*Afterward.* The word Psalm 37 used — *acharit*. The end. *Their* end was the slippery ground. *His* end is *glory*. Same road, different destination.

**"Whom have I in heaven but You? And on earth — I desire nothing besides You."**

He started envying what they had. He ends *wanting nothing they have*. Not because he got their stuff. Because he got *God*, and it turned out that was the thing he actually wanted the whole time.

**"My flesh and my heart may fail — but God is the rock of my heart and my portion forever."**

*My flesh may fail.* He's still the one with the back trouble. The wicked are still healthy. He's not pretending it flipped. But — *rock. Portion. Forever.* Psalm 16 — *You are my portion.* He's got the land they can't buy.

"Those who are far from You will perish. You destroy all who are unfaithful to You."

**"But as for me — it is good to be near God. I've made the Master — Source — my refuge. I will tell of all Your deeds."**

*It is good to be near God.* That's the whole psalm in seven words. Not "it pays." Not "it's fair." *It's good to be near.* The nearness *is* the reward — and once you know that, the wicked man's health and money look like what they are. A dream you're glad you woke up from.

Twenty-eight verses. Here's what saved Asaph: he kept his mouth shut for the kids' sake, he went into the sanctuary instead of thinking harder, he saw the end instead of the middle, he called himself a beast, and he found out God had never let go of his hand.

If you're almost slipping — go in. Not to figure it out. To be near. That's where the picture changes.
