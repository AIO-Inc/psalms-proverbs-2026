# PSALM 6 — The Bed Is Wet with Tears

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 6:1–10. The first of the penitential psalms.*

---

This is the one for when you've hit the bottom and you know some of it was your own fault.

"Don't correct me in anger. Don't discipline me while You're hot."

He's not saying "don't correct me." He knows he's got it coming. He's saying, *do it as a Father, not as a judge*. There's a difference, and every kid who's ever been in trouble knows it.

"Be gracious to me, Source — I'm wasting away. Heal me — my bones are shaking. My soul is terrified. And You — how long?"

*How long.* Two words, and every human who's ever suffered has said them. He doesn't get an answer. He just says them anyway.

"Turn back. Rescue my life. Save me — because of Your loyal-love."

Not because I deserve it. Because of *who You are*. That's the only argument that ever works, and it's the one that always works.

Then he gets real:

"There's no remembering You in death. Who's going to thank You from the grave?"

He's bargaining, honestly. *Keep me alive, I'm more useful to You breathing.* It's not theology. It's a man on the floor. And God lets him say it.

"I'm worn out from groaning. Every night I flood the bed. I've soaked the mattress with tears. My eyes are wasted from grief — they've gotten old from all the people against me."

Every night. The bed is wet. Read that again and let yourself be seen in it. If you've been there, you know: it's not one bad night. It's a season where the pillow never dries.

He's written it down. Three thousand years ago. So you'd know you weren't the first.

And then — the turn. No explanation of what changed. Just:

"Get away from me, all of you doing harm — **because the Source has heard the sound of my weeping.**"

Heard the weeping. Not the prayer — he barely had one. The *weeping*. The sound before the words. He heard *that*.

"The Source has heard my plea. The Source receives my prayer."

Past tense. Present tense. Done deal.

"All my enemies will be ashamed and thoroughly terrified. They'll turn back — suddenly, shamed."

The ones who counted him out? They're about to be very surprised.

Here's what this psalm gives you: permission. Permission to flood the bed. Permission to say "how long." Permission to bargain and beg and not have it together. And then a promise — that the weeping itself is a prayer, and it gets heard.

You don't have to stop crying to be heard. The crying *is* the being heard.

---

*Note on translation: v. 1 — "rebuke/chasten" =* yakach / yasar *— corrective discipline, a parent's teaching, not punitive wrath. v. 5 — "Sheol" = the grave, the realm of the dead — NOT hell. David has no concept of a fiery torment; Sheol is simply where the dead are silent. v. 10 —* bosh *= ashamed, disappointed in expectations. No* yirah *here; v. 3* bahal *and v. 10* bahal *= alarmed, dismayed — human panic, correctly rendered.*
