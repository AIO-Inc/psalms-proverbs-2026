# PSALM 52 — A Green Olive Tree

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 52:1–9. Written after Doeg the Edomite told Saul that the priest Ahimelech had helped David — and Saul had eighty-five priests slaughtered for it (1 Samuel 22).*

---

Here's the backstory. David, on the run, stopped at a sanctuary and the priest gave him bread and a sword. A man named Doeg saw it. Doeg went to Saul and reported it. Saul ordered every priest killed. Eighty-five men. Their families too. One informant, one report — and a whole town of priests was gone.

David wrote this about Doeg.

**"Why do you boast about evil, you mighty man? The loyal-love of God lasts all day."**

*You mighty man.* Sarcasm. Doeg's a snitch who got people killed by talking. That's not mighty. And David sets it against the thing that actually lasts — *God's loyal-love, all day long.* Your evil had one afternoon. His love has every hour.

**"Your tongue plots destruction — like a sharpened razor, you worker of deceit."**

*A sharpened razor.* Doeg didn't hold the sword. He held a *tongue*. And it cut deeper than any blade in the room. Words did this. One report.

If you've ever been on the receiving end of someone's report — the coworker who went to the boss, the family member who went to the group, the friend who repeated what you said in confidence — you know the razor.

**"You love evil more than good. Lying more than speaking right. You love every devouring word — you deceitful tongue."**

*You love it.* Three times — *love*. Doeg didn't inform reluctantly. He *enjoyed* it. There are people who get a rush from the report, from being the one who knew, from watching what happens when they talk. David names that.

Now — the verdict:

**"God will tear you down forever. He'll snatch you up and rip you from your tent. He'll uproot you from the land of the living."**

*Uproot.* Watch that word. He's about to use the opposite image for himself.

**"The ones who do right will see — and stand in awe — and laugh at him: 'Look — the man who wouldn't make God his stronghold — who trusted in the abundance of his riches — who was strong in his own destruction.'"**

*Strong in his own destruction.* Doeg was powerful — at wrecking things. That was his strength. And a strength like that eventually turns around.

*Stand in awe — and laugh.* Both. The awe is at God's justice. The laugh is at how *obvious* it was — the man who trusted in himself, ending exactly how you'd expect.

And then — the turn. David's picture of himself. The opposite of *uprooted*:

**"But I — I'm like a green olive tree in the house of God. I trust in the loyal-love of God — forever and ever."**

*A green olive tree.* Olive trees live a *thousand years*. They're slow. They're not flashy. They don't grow fast. But they *don't die*. You can cut one down and it comes back from the root. They get gnarled and twisted and keep producing oil for centuries after the man who planted them is dust.

*In the house of God.* Planted *there*. In the courtyard. Roots down into the foundation of the one building that doesn't fall.

Doeg — the razor tongue, the mighty man — *uprooted*. David — the one running for his life, who got eighty-five priests killed by accepting bread — *planted*. The one who trusts himself gets torn out. The one who trusts loyal-love gets a thousand years.

**"I will thank You forever — because You've done it. I'll wait for Your name — because it's good — in front of Your loyal ones."**

*Because You've done it.* Past tense. He's still on the run when he writes this. Doeg's still alive. Saul's still king. And he says *You've done it.* Because the olive tree knows what it is before anyone else can see the fruit.

So: when the razor tongue cuts you — when someone's report costs you more than you can count — don't pick up a razor. Get planted. Green olive tree, house of God, loyal-love forever. Let him boast. You'll still be there in a thousand years.
