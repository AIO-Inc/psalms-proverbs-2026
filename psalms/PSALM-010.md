# PSALM 10 — Why Are You Standing So Far Away?

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 10:1–18. The second half of Psalm 9's acrostic — the complaint after the confidence.*

---

Psalm 9 was the confidence. This is the morning after, when the confidence feels like a memory.

"Why, Source, do You stand so far off? Why do You hide Yourself in times of trouble?"

He just spent a whole psalm saying God never abandons. Now he's asking why God's hiding. Both are in the Bible. Back to back. Because both are true to how it *feels*, and God wanted you to know you're allowed to say the second one too.

Then he describes the guy who's getting away with it. And you know this guy:

"In his arrogance the mark-misser hunts the poor. He gets caught in the very schemes he cooks up — but not yet. Right now he's bragging about his cravings. He blesses the greedy. He spits on the Source."

"In his pride he says, 'Nobody's going to hold me accountable.' Everything he thinks is: *There is no God.*"

He doesn't say it out loud. He *lives* it. Every decision he makes assumes nobody's watching. That's the real atheism — not the philosophical kind, the practical kind.

"His ways are always successful. Your judgments are way above him — out of sight. He sneers at everyone in his way. He says to himself, 'I'll never be shaken. Nothing bad will ever happen to me.'"

He's *winning*. That's what makes this psalm hard. The guy's not suffering. He's thriving.

"His mouth is full of curses, lies, threats. Trouble and evil are under his tongue. He waits in ambush near the villages. He murders the innocent in secret. His eyes stalk the helpless. He crouches like a lion in the brush — waits to pounce on the poor, drags them off in his net."

"He tells himself: 'God has forgotten. He's hidden His face. He'll never see.'"

There it is again — *forgotten*. He's betting everything on God not remembering. Psalm 9 just told you: God does not forget the cry of the afflicted. This man is making the worst bet in the universe.

Now David stands up:

"Rise up, Source! Lift Your hand, God! Don't forget the helpless!"

"Why does the mark-misser get to spit on God and say to himself, 'You'll never hold me accountable'?"

And then the answer — the turn — and it's not a thunderclap. It's a simple correction of the lie:

**"But You DO see. You see the trouble and the grief. You take it in Your hand."**

You *do* see. The whole psalm was built on "God doesn't see." This one line demolishes it. He sees. He *takes it in His hand* — He picks up your grief like a thing He's going to do something with.

"The helpless commits himself to You. You are the helper of the fatherless."

The orphan has a Father. The one with nobody has Someone.

"Break the arm of the mark-misser. Call the evil one to account for his wickedness — until there's none left to find."

Not "punish him for fun." *Break the arm* — stop the hand that's doing the harm. Stop the reaching, the grabbing, the dragging-off. Make him unable to hurt anyone else.

**"The Source is King forever and ever. The nations will vanish from His land."**

The guy who says "I'll never be shaken" — he vanishes. The King who seemed far off — He's forever.

"Source, You've heard the desire of the humble. You strengthen their heart. You listen. You do justice for the orphan and the oppressed — **so that mortal man, made of earth, will never terrorize again.**"

That's the finish line. Not vengeance. *Never again.* A world where dust doesn't get to bully dust.

So when it feels like He's standing far off — say so. He wrote it down so you'd know you could. Then remember: He sees. He's picking it up. The bully's clock is running.

---

*Note on translation: v. 4 — "There is no God" is the wicked man's operating assumption, not a philosophical claim. v. 14 —* ra'ah *= You see; "take it in Your hand" =* natan b'yad *= to receive/take hold of. v. 15 — "break the arm" = disable the capacity to harm. v. 18 — "man of the earth" =* enosh min ha'aretz *= mortal, dust-made. No* yirah *in this psalm — v. 18* aroz *= to terrorize/cause dread, describing what the oppressor does, correctly rendered.*
