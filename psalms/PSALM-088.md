# PSALM 88 — Darkness Is My Closest Friend

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 88:1–18. Heman the Ezrahite. The darkest psalm in the book. It does not turn. It does not resolve. It ends in the dark — and that's why it's here.*

---

Every psalm so far has had a turn. The *but I*. The *yet*. The morning after the weeping. This one doesn't.

Read it anyway. Especially if you're in it.

**"Source — God who saves me — day and night I cry out to You."**

*God who saves me.* He starts with the right name. He still believes it. That's the only hopeful line in the psalm, and it's the *first* one. Everything after is descent.

**"May my prayer come before You. Turn Your ear to my cry."**

**"I am overwhelmed with troubles — and my life draws near to death. I am counted among those who go down to the pit. I am like one without strength."**

*Counted among the dead.* He's not dying. He's *already listed*. Somebody's written his name on the wrong roll.

**"I am set apart with the dead — like the slain who lie in the grave — whom You remember no more — who are cut off from Your care."**

*Whom You remember no more.* That's the terror. Not the pain — the *forgetting*. Psalm 78 said *He remembered they were flesh*. Heman feels like he's on the *other* list — the *forgotten* one.

**"You have put me in the lowest pit — in the darkest depths."**

*You.* Not the enemy. Not fate. *You put me here.*

**"Your wrath lies heavily on me. You have overwhelmed me with all Your waves."**

*Your waves.* Psalm 42 said the same — *all Your waves and breakers have gone over me*. But 42 had *deep calls to deep* and a refrain. Heman just has the waves.

**"You have taken from me my closest friends. You have made me repulsive to them. I am confined and cannot escape."**

*Repulsive.* People can't stand to be near him. Psalm 38's *my friends stand back* — but worse. They didn't just step away. They *recoiled*.

*Cannot escape.* No window. Psalm 55's dove wanted to fly. Heman can't even want that.

**"My eyes are dim with grief."**

Psalm 13 — *light up my eyes*. His went out.

**"I call to You, Source, every day. I spread out my hands to You."**

*Every day.* He hasn't stopped. That matters. The man in the lowest pit with dim eyes and no friends — *still praying daily*. He's not writing this from outside the faith. He's writing it from the bottom of it.

Then — the questions. The same ones Psalm 6 asked, Psalm 30 asked — *what good am I to You dead?*

**"Do You show Your wonders to the dead? Do those who are dead rise up and praise You? Is Your love declared in the grave — Your faithfulness in Destruction? Are Your wonders known in the place of darkness — or Your righteous deeds in the land of oblivion?"**

Five questions. All the same question. *Can dust praise You?* It's the bargain from Psalm 30 — *keep me alive, I'm useful breathing*. But Psalm 30 got an answer. Heman doesn't.

**"But I cry to You for help, Source. In the morning my prayer comes before You."**

*In the morning.* Psalm 59 — *the dogs come at evening, I sing in the morning*. Heman's got the morning prayer. He just doesn't have the song.

**"Why, Source, do You reject me — and hide Your face from me?"**

*Why.* Not *how long*. *Why.* The question with no calendar.

**"From my youth I have suffered and been close to death. I have borne Your terrors and am in despair."**

*From my youth.* This isn't a season. It's his *life*. Some people get a hard year. Heman got a hard *existence*. If that's you — chronic illness, chronic pain, the depression that never fully lifts — this is your psalm. He's been in it since he was a kid.

**"Your wrath has swept over me. Your terrors have destroyed me. All day long they surround me like a flood. They have completely engulfed me."**

**"You have taken from me friend and neighbor —"**

And the last line. The last line of the darkest psalm in the Bible:

**"— darkness is my closest friend."**

That's it. That's how it ends. No *but*. No *yet*. No *I will praise Him again*. The last word in Hebrew is *machshach* — darkness. The psalm goes into the dark and *stays there*.

Now — why is this in the book?

Because somebody needed it. Somebody in every generation is Heman. And if every psalm ended in the sunrise — that person would read the Bible and conclude *this isn't for me. Everyone in here gets the turn. I don't.* Psalm 88 says: *you're in here too. Your night without a morning is in the canon. God kept it.*

And here's the other thing. Heman wrote it *to God*. Every line. *Source — God who saves me*. He didn't stop talking. He didn't write a note and leave. He *addressed* the darkness *to the One he couldn't see*. The prayer with no answer is still a prayer. The dim eyes are still *turned toward* something.

That's faith at the bottom. Not the feeling. The *direction*.

And — one more. The heading says *Heman the Ezrahite*. 1 Chronicles 25 lists him — a temple musician. A *worship leader*. The man who wrote *darkness is my closest friend* — led singing in the house of God. He didn't get fired for this psalm. It got *added to the hymnal*.

You can lead worship from the bottom of the pit. He did.

Eighteen verses. No turn. Here's the shape: *God who saves me — I'm counted with the dead — You put me here — my friends recoil — I pray every day anyway — can dust praise You? — why? — I've been here since I was a kid — darkness is my closest friend.*

If that's you tonight — you don't have to fake the turn. He didn't. Say it to God anyway. Address the dark to Him. That's enough. That's *in the book*.

And read Psalm 89 tomorrow. Not tonight. Tomorrow.

*Note on translation: v. 1 —* Elohei y'shuati *= God of my salvation — the only positive divine title in the psalm. v. 5 —* asher lo z'chartam od *= "whom You remember no more." v. 6 —* b'vor tachtiyot *= in the pit of the lowest places. v. 8 —* shitani to'evot lamo *= "You have made me an abomination to them." v. 11 —* avaddon *= Destruction/Abaddon — the realm of the dead. v. 12 —* eretz n'shiyah *= land of forgetfulness/oblivion. v. 15 —* mi-no'ar *= from youth. v. 18 —* m'yuda'ai machshach *= "my acquaintances — darkness" — the final word. Sheol/pit/Abaddon throughout = the grave, not hell. No* yirah*.*
