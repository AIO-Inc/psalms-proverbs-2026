# PSALM 5 — Morning Watch

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 5:1–12. A morning psalm — the companion to Psalm 4.*

---

Psalm 4 was for lying down. This one's for getting up.

"Hear my words, Source. Understand my groaning."

Groaning. Not polished prayer. The sound you make before the coffee — the sigh, the unformed thing. He hears *that*. You don't have to have your words together to be heard.

"Listen to the sound of my cry, my King and my God. Because it's to You I pray."

Then the line that makes this a morning psalm:

**"In the morning You hear my voice. In the morning I lay it all out before You and I watch."**

Lay it out. The Hebrew is what you'd do with an offering — arrange it on the altar, piece by piece. Your day, your worries, the meeting you're dreading, the person you don't want to see. Set each one down. Then *watch*. Keep your eyes open for what He does with it.

Now the hard part. David gets honest about the kind of people he's dealing with:

"You take no pleasure in doing-wrong. Evil doesn't get to bunk with You. The arrogant don't get to stand in Your presence. You can't stand people who do harm for a living. You destroy liars — the bloodthirsty, the deceitful, You reject them."

Strong. But notice — it's not "God hates people." It's God rejecting *the way of harm*. The lying, the violence, the arrogance — those things cannot share a room with Him. Not because He's squeamish. Because they are the opposite of what He is.

Then David pivots back to himself:

"But me — through Your overwhelming loyal-love I get to come into Your house. I bow toward Your holy place in awe of You."

*Awe*. Not fear. The word is *yirah* — the feeling of standing at the edge of the Grand Canyon. Too big to take in. That's how he walks into the presence of God. Wide-eyed.

"Lead me in Your right way, because of the ones watching me. Make the road straight in front of me."

Because of the ones watching. Your kids are watching. Your coworkers are watching. The people who want you to fail are watching. Walk straight for *their* sake.

The people trying to take him down — "there's nothing in their mouth you can trust, their insides are ruin, their throat is an open grave, their tongue is smooth." You know the type. All surface, no floor.

"Let them fall by their own plans." Not by David's hand. By the weight of their own schemes.

And then the ending — the morning benediction:

"But let everyone who takes refuge in You be glad. Let them sing forever. Spread Your protection over them, so the ones who love Your name can rejoice in You."

**"Because You bless the one who does right, Source. You surround him with favor like a shield."**

Surrounded. Not one shield in front — favor on every side. That's how you walk out the door this morning.

Lay it out. Watch. Walk straight. You're surrounded.

---

*Note on translation: v. 3 —* arak *= arrange, set in order (as an offering). v. 7 —* yirah *= awe, reverence, NOT fear. v. 7 "steadfast love" =* chesed *= loyal-love, covenant faithfulness. "Wicked" (*rasha*) = habitual mark-missers.*
