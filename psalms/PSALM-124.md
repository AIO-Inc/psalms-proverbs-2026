# PSALM 124 — If the Source Had Not Been on Our Side

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 124:1–8. Fifth Song of Ascent. David. Eight verses. A looking-back psalm — "here's what would've happened." And a bird that got out of the net.*

---

Psalm 123 was *eyes on the hand — until*. Psalm 124 is *the hand moved*. And the pilgrims look back at what almost happened.

**"If the Source had not been on our side — let Israel say —"**

*If.* The Hebrew is *lulei* — *if not*. Psalm 94:17 — *unless the Source had helped me, I'd be in the grave.* The whole psalm hangs on that word.

*Let Israel say.* Psalm 118 — *let Israel say, let Aaron say*. It's a *call-and-response*. The leader says *if the Source had not been on our side* — and the *crowd* says it back. Because everyone walking up that road has their own *if*.

**"— if the Source had not been on our side — when people attacked us — when their anger flared against us — they would have swallowed us alive."**

*Swallowed alive.* The Hebrew is *chayim b'la'unu*. Whole. Not killed first. *Alive* — down the throat. Numbers 16 — the earth *swallowed* Korah. Jonah — the fish. The image of being *consumed by something bigger* while you're still kicking.

**"The flood would have engulfed us. The torrent would have swept over us. The raging waters would have swept us away."**

Three water images. *Flood. Torrent. Raging waters.* Psalm 18, 42, 69, 93 — the chaos waters. Psalm 69 — *the waters have come up to my neck.* Psalm 124 — *they WOULD have. They didn't.*

That's the shape of this psalm. Every verse is *would have*. Conditional. What *didn't* happen. The pilgrims are standing on dry ground describing the drowning they escaped.

**"Praise be to the Source — who has not let us be torn by their teeth."**

*Torn by their teeth.* Psalm 57 — *my soul is among lions*. Psalm 58 — *break their teeth*. The predator image. And — *not let us*. The jaws closed on air.

And then — the verse. The image the whole psalm was heading toward:

**"We have escaped like a bird from the fowler's snare. The snare has been broken — and we have escaped."**

*Like a bird from the snare.*

Psalm 91 — *He will save you from the fowler's snare.* Psalm 124 — *He DID.* Picture the bird. It's *in* the net. Wings tangled. The trapper's coming. And then — *the snare breaks*. Not "the bird wriggled free." *The snare broke.* Something the bird didn't do — happened to the net.

*We have escaped.* Twice. *Nimlatnu — nimlatnu.* Once before the snare broke — once after. Like the bird can't stop saying it. *I'm out. I'm OUT.*

If you've been in the net — the addiction, the debt, the relationship, the diagnosis — and something *broke the net* that you didn't have the strength to break — you know the double *escaped*. You say it twice because once doesn't cover it.

**"Our help is in the name of the Source — the Maker of heaven and earth."**

Psalm 121's last word. *The Maker.* Psalm 124's last word — same. The Songs of Ascent keep landing on it. The One who *made* the sky is the One who *broke* the net.

Eight verses. Here's the shape: *IF the Source hadn't been on our side — say it back — they'd have swallowed us alive. The flood would've taken us. — Praise Him — the teeth closed on nothing. We escaped like a bird from the snare — the snare BROKE — we escaped. Our help is the Maker.*

Here's what to do with Psalm 124: sit down and finish the sentence. *If the Source had not been on my side — ___.* Say what *would* have happened. Out loud. The drowning. The teeth. The net. Then say *lulei* — *if not*. And say *escaped* twice.

The snare's broken. You're out. That's not luck. That's *Who was on your side*.
