# PSALM 79 — How Long, Source? Forever?

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Psalm 79:1–13. Asaph. Jerusalem in ruins, bodies unburied. A companion to Psalm 74 — and the source of a line the church has prayed for two thousand years.*

---

Psalm 74 was the temple burned. Psalm 79 is the *city*. And the bodies.

**"God — the nations have invaded Your inheritance. They've defiled Your holy temple. They've reduced Jerusalem to rubble."**

*Your* inheritance. *Your* temple. Asaph keeps putting *Your* in front of everything — because it's the only leverage he's got. *They didn't do this to us. They did it to YOURS.*

**"They've left the dead bodies of Your servants as food for the birds of the sky — the flesh of Your own people for the animals of the wild. They've poured out blood like water all around Jerusalem — and there is no one to bury the dead."**

*No one to bury the dead.* In that world, an unburied body was the final indignity. Worse than dying. And there's *no one left* to do it. Everyone who would have is dead too, or gone.

He's writing this while the birds are still working.

**"We're objects of contempt to our neighbors — of scorn and derision to those around us."**

The surrounding nations — *laughing*. The people of God became the joke of the region.

And then — the question. Psalm 74's question. Psalm 13's question. The one that's been asked in every book so far:

**"How long, Source? Will You be angry forever? How long will Your jealousy burn like fire?"**

*Forever?* He doesn't ask "how many years." He asks if there's an *end*. Because standing in the rubble with the birds overhead — you can't see one.

**"Pour out Your wrath on the nations that don't acknowledge You — on the kingdoms that don't call on Your name — because they've devoured Jacob — and devastated his homeland."**

*Pour it on them.* Not us. *Them.* Asaph knows Israel sinned — Psalm 78 just spent seventy-two verses on it. But the nations that *did this* — they weren't acting as God's instrument out of righteousness. They enjoyed it. He wants the cup (Psalm 75) passed to the right table.

Now — the confession. Brief, but it's there:

**"Don't hold against us the sins of our fathers. May Your mercy come quickly to meet us — because we're in desperate need."**

*The sins of our fathers.* He's not pretending. Israel earned the exile — Psalm 78 made that unarguable. But the generation standing in the rubble — some of them were *born* into this. He's asking God not to make the kids pay for the parents. *Come quickly. We're desperate.*

And here's the line:

**"Help us, God our Savior — for the glory of Your name. Deliver us and forgive our sins — for Your name's sake."**

*For Your name's sake.* Twice. Not *we deserve*. Not even *we're sorry*, though they are. *Your reputation.* If God's people stay in the rubble with birds on the bodies — what does that say about God? Asaph's arguing for God's honor as the reason to save them.

**"Why should the nations say: 'Where is their God?'"**

Psalm 42 — *where is your God?* The taunt. Now it's the whole world asking.

**"Before our eyes — make known among the nations that You avenge the outpoured blood of Your servants."**

*Before our eyes.* He wants to *see* it. Not read about it later. Watch it happen.

**"May the groans of the prisoners come before You. With Your strong arm — preserve those condemned to die."**

*The groans of the prisoners.* The ones carried off to Babylon. Chained. He can hear them from Jerusalem. He wants God to.

**"Pay back into the laps of our neighbors seven times the contempt they've hurled at You, Master."**

*Seven times.* Full measure. Overflowing. Same as the vengeance they poured out.

And the ending — after all that:

**"Then we — Your people, the sheep of Your pasture — will praise You forever. From generation to generation — we will proclaim Your praise."**

*The sheep of Your pasture.* Psalm 74's opening. Psalm 23. Even in the rubble with birds on the bodies — they're *sheep*. Still His. Still in the pasture, even if the pasture's on fire.

*From generation to generation.* Psalm 78's chain. The kids will hear about *this* too — the rubble *and* the rescue.

Thirteen verses. Here's the shape: *They wrecked Your city. The bodies are unburied. The neighbors are laughing. How long — forever? Pour the cup on the ones who did it. Don't make the kids pay for the fathers. Save us for YOUR name's sake — or they'll ask where You are. Hear the prisoners groan. And we'll praise You — the sheep — forever.*

When the sacred thing is rubble and the bodies aren't buried: say *Yours*. Say *how long*. Confess what's true. Then make it about His name — because His name is the one argument He can't refuse.
