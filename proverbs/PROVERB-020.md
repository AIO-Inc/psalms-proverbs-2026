# PROVERB 20 — Who Can Find a Faithful Man?

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 20:1–30. Eleventh sayings chapter — 30 two-liners. Clustered by theme (every verse present): wine and the sluggard, the heart no one can see (20:5, 20:9, 20:27), the mouth and honest weights (repeated), the king, the young and the old, and hasty vows. No yirah verse (20:2's "dread of a king" is eimah). Companion: Prov 11:1, 16:11; Ps 51:5; 1 Kgs 3 (Solomon's ear); Prov 16:31.*

---

Chapter twenty opens with a drink.

**"Wine is a mocker, strong drink a brawler, and whoever staggers under it isn't wise."**

Letz hayyayin — wine is a letz, the third-grade fool, the one who sneers and can't be corrected. Proverbs personifies the bottle as that guy. And shogeh — staggers, the same verb chapter five used for being drunk on your own wife. You can stagger on love or stagger on liquor. One of them mocks you. Chapter twenty-three will spend a whole passage on this; here it's one stone, thrown early.

First handful — the heart nobody sees. This is the chapter's spine. Verse five: **"Counsel in a man's heart is deep water, but a man of understanding draws it out."** Mayim amuqqim — chapter eighteen's deep waters. What's inside a person is a well. Most people never get to the bottom of themselves. And the wise person is yidlennah — the one who draws — lowers the bucket, patiently, into someone else, and brings up what they couldn't reach alone. That's what a real conversation is.

Verse six: **"Many a man proclaims his own loyal-love, but a faithful man — who can find?"**

Ish emunim mi yimtza. Everyone says they're chesed. Everyone advertises loyalty. And the actual faithful person — find one. The father's not cynical. He's counting. And the count is low. Chapter thirty-one will ask the same question about a woman of strength — "who can find?" — with the same word. The rarest thing in the book is someone who is what they say.

Verse nine: **"Who can say, 'I have made my heart clean; I am pure from my missing-the-mark'?"** Nobody. The book leaves the question hanging. Psalm 51 — "in sin my mother conceived me" — Psalm 130 — "if You kept a record of wrongs, who could stand?" Proverbs gives the same answer by not answering. That's not despair. It's honesty as the entry fee. You can't start the work if you think you're done.

Verse twenty-seven: **"The breath of a man is the lamp of God, searching all the inner chambers of the belly."** Nishmat adam — the breath, the Genesis 2 breath God blew into the dust. That breath in you is God's lamp. He's not searching you from outside with a flashlight. He gave you the light on the inside, and it's already in every room. Chapter fifteen — Sheol lies open before Him. Chapter twenty — so do you, and the lamp is your own breath.

Verse twenty-four: **"A man's steps are from God — how then can a man understand his own way?"** Chapter sixteen, verse nine. You don't even know where your feet are going. Verse twelve: **"The hearing ear and the seeing eye — God made them both."** Your senses are gifts. What you notice, you were given the equipment to notice. Use it.

Second handful — the mouth and the market. Verse ten: **"Differing weights and differing measures — God detests them both."** Verse twenty-three says it again: "Differing weights are detestable to God, and false scales are not good." Even va'even — a stone and a stone. Two stones in the bag: one for buying, one for selling. Chapter eleven, chapter sixteen, chapter twenty — twice in one chapter. To'evah, the idolatry drawer. The book won't let go of this. Rigged commerce gets more repetitions than adultery.

Verse fourteen: **"'Bad, bad,' says the buyer — then goes off and boasts."** That's haggling. He tells the seller it's junk, gets the price down, then brags about the deal. Observation, not command — but you're meant to laugh and then recognize yourself. Verse seventeen: **"Bread gained by deceit is sweet to a man, but afterward his mouth is full of gravel."** Chatzatz — grit. Chapter nine's stolen water, chapter five's honey to wormwood. The stolen thing tastes great going in.

Verse fifteen: **"There is gold and abundant rubies, but lips of knowledge are a precious vessel."** Verse nineteen: **"A gossip reveals secrets, so don't associate with one who opens his lips wide."** Poteh s'fatav — the wide-lipped one, chapter thirteen. Verse twenty: **"Whoever curses his father or mother — his lamp will go out in deep darkness."** B'ishon choshech — in the pupil of darkness, the black center. Chapter twenty-seven's lamp, extinguished. Verse eleven: **"Even a child is known by his actions, whether his conduct is pure and right."** You can tell early. The deeds are the tell, not the words. Verse three: **"It's honor for a man to keep away from strife, but every fool breaks out."** Yitgalla — chapter eighteen's isolated man. The fool picks fights; the honored man walks past them.

Verse twenty-five: **"It's a snare for a man to say rashly, 'It's holy!' and only afterward to consider his vows."** Yala qodesh — blurts out "consecrated!" — pledges something to God in the heat of the moment and then thinks about it. Don't promise heaven anything on a rush. Ecclesiastes 5 — better not vow than vow and not pay. Verse twenty-two: **"Don't say, 'I'll repay evil'; wait for God and He'll rescue you."** Qavveh la-YHWH — Psalm 27's wait, Psalm 40's waiting-I-waited. Revenge is a debt you don't have to collect. It's His.

Third handful — the sluggard and the field. Verse four: **"The sluggard doesn't plow in autumn; at harvest he looks and there's nothing."** Me-choref — from the cold season. It's cold, he doesn't want to go out, so he doesn't plow. Then he asks at harvest — v'sha'al, he goes and inquires — and there's nothing to find. Chapter six's ant, inverted. He didn't know what season it was. Verse thirteen: **"Don't love sleep or you'll become poor; open your eyes and you'll be satisfied with bread."** P'qach einecha — open your eyes. Chapter twelve gave you seeing eyes. Use them before noon.

Verses sixteen and twenty-one: **"Take the garment of one who puts up security for a stranger; hold it as collateral for the foreigner."** — the co-signer's cloak gets seized; fourth time on this. "An inheritance gained hastily at the beginning won't be blessed at the end." M'vohelet — grabbed in a rush. Chapter thirteen's vapor money.

Fourth handful — the king. Verse two: **"The dread of a king is like a lion's roar; whoever provokes him forfeits his life."** Eimat melech — the terror a king inspires. That's a genuine fear-word, and it's about a human, not God. Note it: the book uses eimah for the dread of power and yirah for awe of God. Different words. Different things. English merged them.

Verse eight: **"A king who sits on the throne of judgment winnows all evil with his eyes."** M'zareh — sifts, like chaff from grain, Psalm 1. Verse twenty-six: **"A wise king winnows the wicked and drives the wheel over them."** Same verb — the threshing wheel, the cart that separates grain from straw. Justice as agriculture. Verse eighteen: **"Plans are established by counsel; make war with guidance."** Chapter eleven, fifteen — get advisors. Verse twenty-eight: **"Loyal-love and truth guard the king, and by loyal-love his throne is upheld."** Chesed ve'emet — the Exodus 34 pair, chapter sixteen's atoning pair. The throne stands on the same two words God's character stands on. Not on the roar.

And the last two, young and old. Verse twenty-nine: **"The glory of young men is their strength, and the splendor of old men is gray hair."** Tif'eret bachurim kocham — the young man's beauty is his power; hadar z'qenim seivah — the old man's majesty is the gray. Chapter sixteen, verse thirty-one — the crown of splendor. Two different ornaments for two different seasons. Neither is better. Don't try to wear the wrong one. And verse thirty, the hard last line: "Blows that wound cleanse away evil, and strokes the inner chambers of the belly."

The text is rough there. What it seems to say: sometimes the thing that hurts is the thing that cleans. Chadrei-vaten — the inner rooms of the belly — chapter eighteen's phrase for where gossip lands, chapter twenty-seven's rooms the lamp searches. The wound goes to the same depth as the light. That's not a rod verse — it's not about a parent. It's about life. The thing that hit you hardest went to the room that needed cleaning. You know that's true. You wish it weren't.

Here's the shape. Wine is a mocker; don't stagger on it. Everyone advertises loyalty — find one who is it. Nobody can say "my heart is clean," and that honesty is the entry fee. Your own breath is God's lamp inside you. Two stones in the bag, twice — God files it with the idols. Don't blurt "holy!" and think later. Dread of a king is eimah; awe of God is yirah; English lost the difference. And the wound that went deepest cleaned the room that needed it.

Today: lower the bucket into one person — draw out the deep water instead of skimming. Then check your bag for a second stone. You know which deal it's for.

---

*Note on translation: No yirah in this chapter. 20:2 eimat melech — "the fear of a king" (KJV) — eimah = terror/dread, a genuine fear-word applied to human power; correctly rendered as dread and explicitly distinguished from yirat YHWH. 20:1 letz hayyayin = wine is a mocker; shogeh = staggers (5:19–20 verb). 20:5 mayim amuqqim = deep waters (18:4); yidlennah = draws it up (well-bucket verb). 20:6 ish emunim mi yimtza = a man of faithfulnesses, who can find — cf. 31:10 eshet chayil mi yimtza. 20:9 unanswered rhetorical question; cf. Ps 51:5, 130:3, 1 Kgs 8:46. 20:10, 20:23 even va'even, eifah v'eifah = a stone and a stone, a measure and a measure — to'evah (11:1, 16:11). 20:14 haggling saying — descriptive. 20:16 = 27:13 (near-verbatim); Ketiv nochrim, Qere nochriyah. 20:17 chatzatz = gravel. 20:20 b'ishon choshech — Ketiv b'ishon, Qere be'eshun; "pupil/blackest part of darkness." 20:22 qavveh la-YHWH = wait for God (Ps 27:14, 40:1). 20:24 = 16:9 theme. 20:25 yala qodesh = rashly utters "holy" — hasty consecration vow (Eccl 5:4–5). 20:26 ofan = threshing wheel. 20:27 nishmat adam ner YHWH = the breath of man is the lamp of God (Gen 2:7 n'shamah); chadrei-vaten = inner chambers of the belly (18:8, 20:30). 20:28 chesed ve'emet = loyal-love and truth (Exod 34:6; 16:6). 20:29 tif'eret / hadar = glory / majesty; seivah = gray hair (16:31). 20:30 chabburot petza tamruq b'ra — MT is difficult ("bruising wounds are a scouring of evil"); Ketiv/Qere variants; LXX differs; rendered to preserve the wound-cleanses sense, and noted as not a parental-discipline verse.*
