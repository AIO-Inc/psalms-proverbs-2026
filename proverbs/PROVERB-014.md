# PROVERB 14 — Awe Is a Fortress

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 14:1–35. Fifth sayings chapter and the longest yet — 35 two-liners. Clustered by theme (every verse present): the house, the road that seems right, the heart nobody else can see, the poor and the neighbor, nations and kings, and two yirah verses in a row (14:26–27) plus 14:2 and 14:16. Companion: Prov 9 (two houses); Ps 46 (fortress); Ps 34:11–14.*

---

Chapter fourteen opens with the two women from chapter nine, one more time, in a single line: "Wisdom builds her house, but Folly tears it down with her own hands."

B'yadeha — with her own hands. Nobody demolishes the fool's house. She does it herself, board by board, and calls it living. Hold that, because the chapter comes back to the house at the end.

First handful — the road that feels right. Verse twelve, and it's one of the most quoted lines in the book — so quoted that it shows up again word-for-word at 16:25: "There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the ways of death."

Yashar lifnei ish — straight, in front of a man. It doesn't say the road looks wrong and he takes it anyway. It says it looks straight. It feels right. That's the horror. Sincerity isn't a compass. You can be completely convinced and completely lost. Chapter twelve said the fool's way is right in his own eyes — here's what happens at the end of that road.

Verse eight: **"The wisdom of the prudent is to understand his way, but the folly of fools is deceit."** Mirmah — the fool isn't just wrong, he's self-deceiving. Verse fifteen: **"The naive believes everything, but the prudent considers his steps."** Peti — the first-grade fool, the one who can still learn. His problem is credulity. He believes the road because it told him it was straight. Verse eighteen: **"The naive inherit folly, but the prudent are crowned with knowledge."**

Verse six: **"A mocker seeks wisdom and doesn't find it, but knowledge comes easy to the discerning."** Letz — he seeks, and can't find, because the sneer is in the way. Verse seven: **"Leave the presence of a fool — you won't find knowledge on his lips."** Lech mi-neged — walk away. Sometimes the wise move is to physically leave the room. Verse nine: **"Fools mock at guilt, but among the upright is favor."** They joke about the thing that should stop them. Verse twenty-four: **"The crown of the wise is their wealth; the folly of fools is folly."** That second line's almost a shrug — folly just produces more folly. It doesn't turn into anything. Second handful — the heart nobody sees. Verse ten: **"The heart knows its own bitterness, and no stranger shares its joy."**

Sit with that. There's a room in you no one else can enter. Your bitterness is yours; your joy is yours. This isn't loneliness as a complaint — it's just true, and Proverbs says it plain. Which is why verse thirteen follows: "Even in laughter the heart may ache, and the end of joy is grief." The father knows people laugh at parties and drive home crying. He's not shaming it. He's naming it.

Verse thirty: **"A tranquil heart is life to the body, but envy is rot in the bones."** Marpe — the healing word — a healing heart. And qin'ah, jealousy, is rakav, bone-rot, chapter twelve's word for the shameful wife. Envy rots you from the frame out.

Verse twenty-nine: **"Whoever is slow to anger has great understanding, but the quick-tempered exalts folly."** Erech apayim — long of nostrils. Slow to flare. It's the phrase God uses about Himself in Exodus 34. Verse seventeen: **"A quick-tempered man does foolish things, and a man of schemes is hated."** Q'tzar apayim — short of nostrils. Two nose-lengths. Pick yours.

Verse thirty-three: **"Wisdom rests in the heart of the discerning, but among fools it's made known."** Wisdom rests — tanuach — in the wise. It sits quiet. Fools broadcast whatever they've got.

Third handful — the poor and the neighbor. This chapter has a conscience. Verse twenty: **"The poor is hated even by his own neighbor, but the rich has many friends."** That's not approval. That's observation — and it's damning. Verse twenty-one follows immediately: "Whoever despises his neighbor misses the mark, but happy is the one who is kind to the poor." Ashrei — the Psalm 1 word.

Verse thirty-one, and this is the one: "Whoever oppresses the poor insults his Maker, but whoever is kind to the needy honors Him."

Cheref osehu — reproaches the One who made him. The poor man is God's handiwork. Grind him and you've spit on the Craftsman. Feed him and you've honored the Craftsman. Jesus said it in Matthew 25 — "whatever you did to the least of these, you did to Me." He had this verse. Verse thirty-two: **"The wicked is thrust down by his own evil, but the right-standing has refuge even in death."** B'moto — in his death. Even there. Another whisper past the grave, like 12:28.

Verse twenty-two: **"Do those who plot evil not go astray? But loyal-love and truth are for those who plan good."** Chesed v'emet — God's pair. Verse twenty-five: **"A truthful witness saves lives, but a deceitful one breathes lies."** Verse five, same: "A faithful witness does not lie, but a false witness breathes out lies." Yafiach k'zavim — exhales lies, chapter twelve's breath-word inverted. Some people lie the way they breathe.

Fourth handful — work and money. Verse four: **"Where there are no oxen the stall is clean, but abundant harvest comes by the strength of the ox."** You want a clean barn? Don't have oxen. You want food? Get used to the mess. Every real thing is messy. Verse twenty-three: **"In all toil there is profit, but mere talk leads only to poverty."** D'var s'fatayim — lip-words. Talking about it isn't doing it. Verse eleven: **"The house of the wicked is destroyed, but the tent of the upright flourishes."** Yafriach — blossoms. The good man's tent — the temporary thing — outlasts the wicked man's house. Verse nineteen: **"Evil men bow before the good, and the wicked at the gates of the right-standing."** Verse fourteen: **"The backslider in heart gets what his ways deserve, and the good man his."** Verse three: **"In the mouth of a fool is a rod for his pride, but the lips of the wise protect them."** Choter — a shoot, a rod — his own mouth is the stick he gets hit with.

Fifth handful — the nation. Verse twenty-eight: **"In a multitude of people is a king's glory, but without people a prince is ruined."** Verse thirty-five: **"A king favors a wise servant, but his wrath is on the one who acts shamefully."** And verse thirty-four, the one carved on courthouses: "Right-standing exalts a nation, but missing-the-mark is a disgrace to any people." Chesed l'umim chattat — the Hebrew literally says sin is the chesed — the "loyal-love," the shame-ironic sense — of nations. It's dark wordplay. What a nation loves faithfully, if it's sin, is its disgrace.

And the awe verses. Four of them. Verse two: **"Whoever walks uprightly stands in AWE of God, but the crooked in his ways despises Him."** Y're YHWH — the one in awe walks straight. The opposite isn't "the fearless" — it's bozehu, the one who despises Him. Awe versus contempt. Not fear versus courage.

Verse sixteen: **"The wise one is in awe and turns from evil, but the fool is reckless and confident."** Chacham yare — the wise one reveres. Here it's absolute — no object stated — and the contrast is the fool who's mit'abber u-boteach, storming through and sure of himself. Awe is the thing that makes a wise person check. Not panic. Pause. The fool has no pause.

And then verses twenty-six and twenty-seven, back to back:

**"In the AWE of God there is strong confidence, and His children will have a refuge."**

"The AWE of God is a fountain of life, turning one away from the snares of death." Read verse twenty-six as the King James has it — "in the fear of the LORD is strong confidence." Fear is confidence? That's a contradiction. But awe is confidence — the person who has stood at the canyon and been made small by it walks away steadier, not shakier. Mivtach-oz — a stronghold of trust. Psalm 46's fortress. And it's inheritable — l'vanav, for his children. Your awe becomes your kids' refuge. The house that Wisdom built in verse one — this is what it's made of.

Verse twenty-seven takes chapter thirteen's line about wise teaching — a fountain of life turning from the snares of death — and swaps in yirat YHWH as the subject. So awe of God and the teaching of the wise are the same fountain. And ask yourself: is fear a fountain of life? Fear is a drain. Awe is the spring. The Hebrew is *consistent* every single time; only the English breaks it.

Here's the shape. Folly tears down her own house with her own hands. There's a road that feels straight and ends in death — sincerity is not a compass. Your heart has a room nobody enters; guard it from envy, which rots the bones. Grinding the poor insults the Maker. And awe — four times — is upright walking, the wise man's pause, a fortress your children inherit, and a fountain of life.

Today: find the road that feels straight — the one you're most sure about — and ask one wise person to check the map. Then do one kind thing for someone poor. You'll be honoring the Craftsman.

*Note on translation: 14:2 y're YHWH — "feareth the LORD" (KJV) — rendered "stands in AWE"; antithesis is bozehu (despises Him), so the contrast is awe vs. contempt. 14:16 chacham yare — absolute use, no object — "the wise man is in awe/reverent"; contrast mit'abber u-boteach = rages/storms through and is confident. 14:26 b'yirat YHWH mivtach-oz — "in the fear of the LORD is strong confidence" (KJV) — the awe reading resolves the apparent contradiction (fear ≠ confidence; awe → steadiness); l'vanav = for his children — inherited refuge. 14:27 yirat YHWH m'qor chayyim — identical structure to 13:14 with torat chacham swapped for yirat YHWH; the substitution equates awe and wise teaching; "fountain of life" cf. 5:18, 10:11. 14:1 b'yadeha = with her own hands. 14:12 verbatim at 16:25. 14:10 marrat nafsho = bitterness of his soul. 14:17 / 14:29 q'tzar apayim / erech apayim = short/long of nostrils — the Exod 34:6 divine self-description. 14:30 lev marpe = healing heart; qin'ah = envy; rakav = rot (12:4). 14:31 cheref osehu = reproaches his Maker; echoed Matt 25:40, 45. 14:32 b'moto — MT "in his death"; LXX reads b'tummo "in his integrity" — MT retained (cf. 12:28). 14:34 chesed l'umim chattat — chesed here in its rare negative sense (shame/reproach, cf. Lev 20:17); wordplay noted. 14:24 second line is tautological in MT (ivvelet k'silim ivvelet); some emend to liv'yat "garland" — MT kept. Yirah in this chapter: 14:2, 14:16, 14:26, 14:27.*
