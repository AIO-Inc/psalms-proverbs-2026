# PROVERB 6 — Go Watch the Ant

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 6:1–35. Four short units: co-signing a stranger's debt (6:1–5), the sluggard and the ant (6:6–11), the troublemaker and the seven things God hates (6:12–19), and the third strange-woman lecture — adultery as fire in your lap (6:20–35).*

---

Chapter six is four pieces of hard practical advice back to back. Money. Work. Character. Sex. The father is covering the four places grown men wreck their lives.

Piece one, verse one: **"My son, if you've put up security for your neighbor, if you've shaken hands on a stranger's debt — you've been snared by your own words, caught by the words of your mouth."**

Co-signing. That's what this is. You guaranteed someone else's loan. And the father doesn't say "that was wicked." He says "that was a trap, and you walked in." So verses three through five: **"Do this, my son, and free yourself, since you've fallen into your neighbor's hand: go, humble yourself, press your neighbor. Don't sleep. Don't let your eyelids close. Free yourself like a gazelle from the hand, like a bird from the fowler's hand."**

Urgency. Don't sleep on it. Go tonight. Get your name off the paper. This isn't about generosity — Proverbs is huge on generosity. It's about the difference between giving what you have and pledging what you don't. One is love. The other is a snare with your name in it.

Piece two, verse six, and it's the most famous animal in the book: "Go to the ant, you sluggard. Watch her ways and get wise."

Atzel. Sluggard. Proverbs has a whole running character with this name, and he's funny — until you recognize him. Verses seven and eight: **"She has no commander, no officer, no ruler — yet she prepares her food in summer and gathers her supplies at harvest."**

Nobody's yelling at the ant. Nobody's standing over her with a clipboard. She just knows what season it is and acts accordingly. That's the definition of wisdom in this book — knowing the time and doing the thing the time requires, without being told. Verses nine through eleven: **"How long will you lie there, sluggard? When will you get up from your sleep? A little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to rest — and your poverty comes like a highwayman, your want like an armed man."**

"A little." Hear the word. That's the sluggard's entire vocabulary. Just a little more. Five more minutes. I'll start Monday. The father says: poverty doesn't announce itself. It doesn't knock. It comes like a mugger — fast, from behind, when you weren't looking, and it takes everything. You didn't lose your future in one big decision. You lost it in a thousand "a little"s.

Piece three, verse twelve: **"A worthless man, a man of wrong, walks with a crooked mouth — winking his eyes, shuffling his feet, pointing his fingers — with perversity in his heart, plotting evil, always sowing strife."**

That's a body-language profile. The wink, the shuffle, the point. He's the guy who talks out of the side of his mouth. Who's always signaling something to someone else in the room. Verse fifteen: **"So suddenly disaster comes on him; in a moment he's broken, past healing."** The man who lived on angles gets no angle when it counts.

Then verses sixteen through nineteen, a numbered saying, and Proverbs loves these: "Six things God hates — seven are detestable to Him: proud eyes, a lying tongue, hands that shed innocent blood, a heart that plots wicked schemes, feet quick to run to evil, a false witness who breathes lies, and one who sows strife among brothers."

Look at the list. It's another body walk — eyes, tongue, hands, heart, feet. Chapter four gave you the body guarded by wisdom. Here's the body run by the opposite. And notice what's at the top and the bottom: proud eyes first, and sowing strife among brothers last — the seventh, the emphasized one. God's most-hated thing on this list isn't murder. It's the guy who splits up the family. The one who walks into a room of brothers and leaves it a room of factions. Remember who does that professionally next time someone tries to turn you against your own people.

Piece four, verse twenty, and it's the third time the father has warned about this, so he clearly thinks it matters. "My son, keep your father's command and don't abandon your mother's teaching. Tie them on your heart always; bind them around your neck. When you walk they'll lead you; when you sleep they'll watch over you; when you wake they'll talk to you." Then verse twenty-three: **"For the command is a lamp and the teaching is a light, and the corrections of training are the way of life."** Psalm 119:105 — Your word is a lamp to my feet. And there's musar again — "the corrections of training" — tochachot musar — that's the coaching that keeps you alive. Not the beating. The feedback.

Verse twenty-four: what's the lamp for? "To keep you from the evil woman, from the smooth tongue of the stranger. Don't desire her beauty in your heart; don't let her capture you with her eyelids." He's honest again — she's beautiful. Her eyelids — the look. The father isn't pretending she's plain.

Verse twenty-six: **"For a prostitute costs a loaf of bread, but another man's wife hunts down a precious life."** He's drawing a line. He's not endorsing the first — but he's saying the second is categorically more dangerous. The stranger in this chapter is a married woman. She's not just wrong; she's someone else's, and someone is going to come looking.

Then the fire, verses twenty-seven and twenty-eight: **"Can a man scoop fire into his lap and his clothes not burn? Can he walk on hot coals and his feet not be scorched? So is the one who goes in to his neighbor's wife — no one who touches her goes unpunished."**

You can't hold fire and stay unburned. That's not a rule God invented to spoil the fun. That's just what fire is. Adultery burns the man who carries it. Not because heaven strikes — because he's holding fire.

Verses thirty through thirty-five, and this is where the father gets very specific about why. "People don't despise a thief who steals when he's starving — but if he's caught he pays back sevenfold, he gives everything in his house." Even the sympathetic thief pays. "But the one who commits adultery with a woman has no sense; he destroys his own life. Wounds and disgrace he'll get, and his shame will never be wiped away." Never — lo timacheh.

And then the reason it's different from theft, verses thirty-four and thirty-five: **"For jealousy enrages a husband, and he'll show no mercy on the day of vengeance. He won't accept any payment; he'll refuse it however large the bribe."** A thief can pay restitution. There is no restitution for this. You can't write a check that makes a betrayed husband whole. Some debts have no currency.

Here's the shape. Don't pledge what you don't have — and if you did, don't sleep till you're out. Watch the ant: no boss, no clipboard, she just knows what season it is. God's most-hated sin on the list is splitting up brothers. And adultery isn't just a sin — it's fire in your lap, and it's a debt no payment can clear. Today: pick your piece. Debt, sloth, strife, or fire. One of the four has your name on it right now. Go to the ant on it — no commander, no officer. Just do the thing the season requires.

*Note on translation: 6:1 aravta = stand surety / co-sign; the warning is against pledging what one does not own, not against generosity (cf. 11:15, 17:18, 22:26). 6:6 atzel = sluggard — a recurring Proverbs character (10:26, 13:4, 15:19, 19:24, 20:4, 24:30, 26:13–16). 6:11 k'mhallech — like a traveler/highwayman; ish magen = armed man. 6:12 adam beliyya'al = worthless man. 6:16–19 a "graded numerical saying" (x / x+1), the seventh item carrying the emphasis; shole'ach medanim bein achim = sows strife among brothers. 6:20 torat immecha = your mother's teaching. 6:23 tochachot musar — KJV "reproofs of instruction" — rendered "corrections of training." 6:24 MT eshet ra (evil woman); LXX reads eshet re'a (neighbor's wife), which matches 6:29 — both readings noted. 6:26 the Hebrew is compressed; "costs a loaf of bread" follows the majority reading. 6:29 lo yinnaqeh = will not go unpunished/clean. 6:33 lo timacheh = will not be wiped away. 6:35 kofer = ransom/payment. No yirah in this chapter.*
