# PROVERB 16 — The Lot Is in His Lap

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 16:1–33. Seventh sayings chapter — 33 two-liners. This is the center of the Solomonic collection and it opens with a run of nine straight "God" sayings (16:1–9), then pivots to kings (16:10–15). Clustered by theme (every verse present): God and human plans, the king, pride and the slow to anger, the gray head, the mouth, and the one yirah verse (16:6). Companion: Prov 3:5–6; 14:12 (repeated verbatim at 16:25); Ps 37:23; Ps 90.*

---

Chapter sixteen is the hinge of Proverbs. It's near the exact middle of the book, and it starts with God nine times in a row — the most concentrated block of God's name in the whole collection. Then it hands off to kings. Then it comes down to you. Read it as three floors.

Top floor — God and your plans. Verse one: **"To man belong the plans of the heart, but from God comes the answer of the tongue."** You plan. You lay it out. And when you open your mouth to say it, what comes out is His. Verse nine says it back: "A man's heart plans his way, but God establishes his steps." Yachin tza'ado — He sets the footstep. You draw the map; He decides where your foot actually lands. Psalm 37:23 — "the steps of a good man are established by God." Same verb.

Verse two: **"All a man's ways are pure in his own eyes, but God weighs the spirits."** Chapter fourteen's road that seems right, now with the correction: tochen ruchot — He puts the spirits on the scale. Chapter eleven's honest weight, applied to you. Verse three: **"Roll your works onto God, and your plans will be established."** Gol — roll, Psalm 37:5's verb. You don't hand it over carefully. You roll it off you like a boulder.

Verse four: **"God has made everything for its purpose — even the wicked for the day of trouble."** Nothing's outside the plan. Not even the man ruining your week. Verse five: **"Everyone proud of heart is detestable to God; be sure of it — he won't go unpunished."** To'evah — the idolatry word. Pride gets filed with the idols because pride is one.

Verse six, the yirah verse, and it's the strangest one so far:

"By loyal-love and truth wrongdoing is atoned for, and by the AWE of God a man turns from evil." Two halves. First: b'chesed ve'emet y'chuppar avon — by loyal-love and faithfulness iniquity is covered. That's kaphar — the Day of Atonement verb. Proverbs is saying that God's own character words — chesed and emet, the Exodus 34 pair — are what do the covering. Not your effort. His loyalty. Second half: b'yirat YHWH sur me-ra — by awe of God, turning from evil. So here's the sequence: His loyal-love covers the wrong; your awe turns you away from doing it again. Forgiveness first, awe second. That's Psalm 130:4 exactly — "there is forgiveness with You, so that You may be held in awe." The KJV inverts it to "that Thou mayest be feared," as if the forgiveness were a threat. It's not. Being forgiven produces awe. Awe produces turning. That's the engine. You've heard it in 3:7, 8:13, 14:16 — awe turns you from evil every time it's mentioned. Fear makes you hide the evil. Awe makes you leave it.

Verse seven: **"When a man's ways please God, He makes even his enemies be at peace with him."** Verse eight: **"Better a little with right-standing than great income without justice."** Chapter fifteen's "better a little" again — with awe there, with right-standing here. Same little. Same enough.

Middle floor — the king. Verse ten: **"An inspired verdict is on the lips of a king; his mouth doesn't betray justice."** Qesem — literally a divination, an oracle. The ideal king's judgment carries something beyond himself. Verse eleven interrupts: "Honest scales and balances are God's; all the weights in the bag are His work." The market stones — chapter eleven — belong to God. Every weight in a merchant's bag is His property. Fraud is theft from Him.

Verses twelve through fifteen: **"Doing evil is detestable to kings, because a throne is established by right-standing. Right lips are a king's delight; he loves the one who speaks straight. A king's wrath is a messenger of death, but a wise man will appease it. In the light of a king's face is life; his favor is like a cloud of spring rain."** Malqosh — the late rain that makes the harvest. The good ruler's approval is weather — it makes things grow. And his wrath is a messenger — mal'achei mavet. That's not a flattering picture of kings. It's a realistic one: power is dangerous, so be wise around it.

Ground floor — you. Verse sixteen: **"How much better to get wisdom than gold — to get understanding is to be chosen over silver."** Chapter two, three, eight — the silver keeps losing. Verse seventeen: **"The highway of the upright turns away from evil; whoever guards his way guards his soul."** M'sillat — the built-up road, chapter fifteen's word. Verse twenty: **"Whoever attends to a matter finds good; whoever trusts in God — happy is he."** Ashrei. Verses eighteen and nineteen, the pride pair: "Pride goes before destruction, and a haughty spirit before a fall. Better to be lowly in spirit with the humble than to divide plunder with the proud." Lifnei shever ga'on — before the break, pride. You've heard it as "pride goeth before a fall" — it's this verse, and it's destruction, shever, the shattering word from chapter fifteen. Pride is the precursor. If you feel it rising, the break is already on the calendar.

Verse thirty-two, and this is the one to keep:

**"Better the slow to anger than a warrior, and the one who rules his spirit than the one who takes a city."**

Erech apayim — long-nostriled — again, the God-word. And the comparison: the man who can hold his own temper has done something harder than sacking a city. Israel had war heroes. Proverbs says the guy who didn't blow up at dinner outranks them.

Verse thirty-one: **"Gray hair is a crown of splendor; it's found in the way of right-standing."** Ateret tif'eret — a crown of beauty. The gray head isn't a decline. It's the medal. Chapter three, four — the jewelry keeps upgrading. This one you grow. Verse twenty-two: **"Understanding is a fountain of life to its owner, but the training of fools is folly."** Verse twenty-six: **"The worker's appetite works for him; his hunger drives him on."** Hunger is a tool. Verse twenty-one: **"The wise of heart is called discerning, and sweet lips increase persuasion."** Meteq s'fatayim — sweetness of lips. Verse twenty-three: **"The heart of the wise instructs his mouth and adds persuasion to his lips."** The heart teaches the mouth. Chapter four — heart first, mouth downstream. Verse twenty-four: **"Pleasant words are a honeycomb — sweet to the soul and healing to the bones."** Marpe la'atzem — healing to the bone. Chapter fifteen fattened them; here they're healed. The book is sure that good words are physical medicine.

Now the dark cluster, twenty-seven through thirty: "A worthless man digs up evil, and on his lips is like a scorching fire. A twisted man spreads strife, and a whisperer separates close friends. A violent man entices his neighbor and leads him on a path that isn't good. Whoever winks his eye plots perversity; whoever purses his lips has finished evil." Chapter six's body-language profile — the wink, the pursed lip. And nirgan — the whisperer — mafrid alluf, separates the alluf, the intimate, the close friend. Chapter six's seventh sin. God's most-hated thing: the one who splits people who were close.

Verse twenty-five, and you've heard this one word for word: "There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the ways of death." It's 14:12, exactly. The editors put it in twice. When a book that prizes economy repeats itself verbatim, it's because it wants the line to land twice. Sincerity is not a compass. Twice.

And verse thirty-three, the last line:

**"The lot is cast into the lap, but its every decision is from God."**

Goral — the lot, the dice, the thing you throw when you don't know. They threw lots for land, for temple duty, for who'd go first. The whole point of a lot is that nobody controls it. And Proverbs says: kol-mishpato — the entire verdict of it — is His. The roll you can't control, He controls. That's the chapter's bookend. Verse one: you plan, He answers. Verse thirty-three: you throw, He decides. Everything in between is how to live in a world where the dice aren't yours.

Here's the shape. Three floors. Top: you plan, you weigh yourself pure, you roll your works onto Him — and His loyal-love covers the wrong so your awe can turn you from it. Middle: kings are weather and messengers of death; be wise near power. Ground: pride is the precursor to the break, ruling your temper beats taking a city, the gray head is a medal, and the same road that feels right ends in death — twice. And the lot is in His lap.

Today: name the dice you've been trying to control — the roll that isn't yours. Roll it onto Him instead. Then go rule your spirit in one conversation. That's the city worth taking.

*Note on translation: 16:6 u-v'yirat YHWH sur me-ra — "by the fear of the LORD men depart from evil" (KJV) — rendered "by the AWE of God a man turns from evil"; the first half b'chesed ve'emet y'chuppar avon uses kaphar (atone/cover), making the verse's logic forgiveness → awe → turning, exactly parallel to Ps 130:4 and the reverse of the KJV's implicit threat. Every prior awe verse (3:7, 8:13, 14:16) also pairs awe with sur me-ra. 16:1 ma'aneh lashon = answer of the tongue. 16:2 tochen ruchot = weighs spirits. 16:3 gol = roll (Ps 37:5). 16:5, 16:12 to'evah = detestable (idolatry register). 16:9 yachin tza'ado = establishes his step (Ps 37:23). 16:10 qesem = divination/oracle — used positively of the ideal king's verdict. 16:11 avnei-kis = the stones of the bag — all weights are God's ma'aseh. 16:14 mal'achei mavet = messengers of death. 16:15 malqosh = spring/late rain. 16:18 lifnei shever ga'on — source of "pride goeth before a fall"; shever = shattering. 16:19 sh'fal-ruach = lowly of spirit. 16:20 ashrei = happy. 16:21, 16:23 meteq s'fatayim / leqach = sweetness of lips / persuasion. 16:22 musar evilim ivvelet = the training of fools is folly. 16:24 tzuf-d'vash = honeycomb; marpe la'atzem = healing to the bone (15:30). 16:25 = 14:12 verbatim. 16:28 nirgan = whisperer; mafrid alluf = separates the intimate friend (cf. 6:19, 17:9). 16:30 otzeh einav / qotzetz s'fatav = shuts his eyes / compresses his lips — body-language of plotting (6:13). 16:32 erech apayim = long of nostrils (Exod 34:6); moshel b'rucho = rules his spirit. 16:33 goral = lot; kol-mishpato = its whole judgment/decision. Yirah in this chapter: 16:6.*
