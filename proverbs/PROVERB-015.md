# PROVERB 15 — A Soft Answer

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 15:1–33. Sixth sayings chapter — 33 two-liners, and the most God-saturated so far (God is named nine times). Clustered by theme (every verse present): the mouth, the eyes of God, the cheerful heart, correction, and the two yirah verses — 15:16 (better a little with awe) and 15:33 (awe is the training of wisdom, the chapter's last word). Companion: Prov 1:7; 9:10; Ps 34; Ps 139.*

---

Chapter fifteen opens with the single most useful sentence in the book for anyone who has ever been in an argument.

**"A soft answer turns away wrath, but a painful word stirs up anger."**

Ma'aneh rach — a tender answer. Not a weak one. Not a doormat. Soft — the way you'd handle something that might break. And yashiv chemah — it turns back the heat. You've watched it. Someone comes in hot, you go quiet and gentle, and they don't know what to do with it. The fire needs fuel and you didn't hand it any. And the other side — d'var etzev — a word that hurts — ya'aleh-af, makes the nostril flare. Chapter fourteen's nose-length, again.

First handful — the mouth. This chapter is thick with it. Verse two: **"The tongue of the wise dispenses knowledge, but the mouth of fools pours out folly."** Yabbia — gushes. The fool's mouth is a broken hydrant. Verse four: **"A healing tongue is a tree of life, but a twisted one breaks the spirit."** Marpe lashon — a tongue of healing. Fourth tree of life in the book. Chapter twelve said the wise tongue heals; now it is the tree. And the twisted tongue — shever b'ruach — breaks the spirit. That's what a cruel word does. It doesn't wound the body. It cracks the thing underneath.

Verse seven: **"The lips of the wise spread knowledge, but the heart of fools isn't right."** Verse fourteen: **"The heart of the discerning seeks knowledge, but the mouth of fools feeds on folly."** Yir'eh — grazes. Fools graze on stupidity. It's their pasture. Verse twenty-three: **"A man finds joy in giving an apt reply — and a word at the right time, how good it is!"** Davar b'itto — a word in its season. Timing is half of wisdom. Verse twenty-six: **"God detests the thoughts of the wicked, but pleasant words are pure."** Imrei-noam — words of pleasantness. They're tahor, clean, the temple word. Verse twenty-eight: **"The heart of the right-standing weighs how to answer, but the mouth of the wicked pours out evil."** Yehgeh la'anot — meditates on the answer. He thinks first. The wicked mouth is the hydrant again — yabbia, same word as verse two. Two chapters running on the same theme: the wise one pauses; the fool has no pause.

Second handful — the eyes of God. Verse three: **"The eyes of God are in every place, watching the evil and the good."** Not surveillance. Tzofot — the word for *a* watchman on a wall. He's keeping watch. Psalm 139 — where can I go from Your presence? Same fact, turned to comfort there and to accountability here. It's the same eyes.

Verse eleven: **"Sheol and Abaddon lie open before God — how much more the hearts of men!"** Sheol va'avaddon — the grave and the place of ruin. Not hell. The deepest, darkest, most hidden places that exist — and God sees to the bottom of them. So your heart isn't hiding anything. Verse eight: **"The sacrifice of the wicked is detestable to God, but the prayer of the upright is His delight."** To'evah / ratzon — the pair from chapter eleven. You can't buy Him off with a ritual. He wants the prayer of the straight man, not the offering of the crooked one. Verse nine: **"God detests the way of the wicked, but He loves the one who pursues right-standing."** Ye'ehav — loves. Not tolerates. Verse twenty-nine: **"God is far from the wicked, but He hears the prayer of the right-standing."** Verse twenty-five: **"God tears down the house of the proud, but He maintains the widow's boundary."** G'vul almanah — the widow's property line. God personally defends the fence of the woman nobody else defends.

Third handful — the cheerful heart. Verse thirteen: **"A glad heart makes a cheerful face, but by heartache the spirit is crushed."** Ruach n'khe'ah — a beaten-down spirit. It shows. You can see the heart on the face; the mask never quite works.

Verse fifteen: **"All the days of the afflicted are bad, but a good heart is a continual feast."** Mishteh tamid — a constant banquet. Chapter nine's feast, made portable. You can carry Wisdom's table around in your chest and eat at it all day. Verse thirty: **"Bright eyes gladden the heart; good news puts fat on the bones."** M'or einayim — the light of the eyes. Psalm 13, Psalm 19 — light up my eyes. Someone else's lit eyes do it to you. And good news — sh'muah tovah — fattens the bones. Chapter fourteen said envy rots them. Good news is the antidote — it feeds the skeleton.

Verse sixteen, first yirah verse:

"Better a little with the AWE of God than great treasure with turmoil in it." Verse seventeen right behind it: "Better a meal of vegetables where love is than a fattened ox with hatred." Two "better than"s. A small house with awe in it beats a mansion with m'humah — chaos, panic. A salad with people who love you beats steak with people who don't. Notice what the awe verse is paired with: love at the table. Awe and love are the same kind of thing — the thing that makes a small room enough. Fear doesn't do that. Fear makes every room feel too small.

Fourth handful — correction. Verse five: **"A fool despises his father's training, but whoever heeds correction is prudent."** Musar / tochachat. Verse ten: **"Harsh training is for the one who leaves the path; whoever hates correction will die."** Musar ra — severe training. When you leave the path, the coaching gets harder — not because God's angry, because the ditch is steeper. Verse twelve: **"A mocker doesn't love being corrected; he won't go to the wise."** Letz, again. He won't go. It's a refusal, not an inability.

Verse thirty-one: **"The ear that listens to life-giving correction will lodge among the wise."** Tochachat chayyim — correction that gives life. And talin — lodges overnight, the guest-word from Psalm 30. Take correction and you get to stay in Wisdom's house. Verse thirty-two: **"Whoever ignores training despises his own soul, but whoever heeds correction gains heart."** Ignoring coaching isn't self-love. It's self-contempt. You're saying your own soul isn't worth the trouble of getting better.

Verse twenty: **"A wise son gladdens his father, but a dull man despises his mother."** Verse twenty-one: **"Folly is joy to the one lacking heart, but a man of understanding walks straight."** Verse twenty-two: **"Plans fail without counsel, but with many advisors they succeed."** Chapter eleven, verse fourteen — get advisors, plural. Verse nineteen: **"The way of the sluggard is a hedge of thorns, but the path of the upright is a highway."** The sluggard's road isn't just slow — it's overgrown, because he never cleared it. Verse six: **"In the house of the right-standing is much treasure, but the income of the wicked brings trouble."** Verse eighteen: **"A hot-tempered man stirs up strife, but the slow to anger calms a quarrel."** Erech apayim — long-nostriled, the God-word from Exodus 34. Verse twenty-seven: **"Whoever is greedy for gain troubles his own house, but whoever hates bribes will live."** Verse twenty-four: **"The path of life leads upward for the wise, so he turns away from Sheol below."** L'ma'lah — upward. Away from the grave. Not from hell — from the grave, the same place verse eleven said God can see into.

And verse thirty-three, the last line of the chapter, and it's a bookend to the whole first half of the book:

"The AWE of God is the training of wisdom, and before honor comes humility." Yirat YHWH musar chochmah. Awe of God is the musar — the training, the coaching — of wisdom. Chapter one: awe is the beginning. Chapter nine: awe is the beginning. Chapter fifteen: awe is the training. It's not just the first step. It's the whole gym. Every rep of wisdom is a rep of awe. And the second line tells you what the training produces: lifnei kavod anavah — before honor, humility. The one who stands at the canyon and lets himself be made small gets lifted. Chapter three, verse thirty-four — grace to the humble. Chapter eighteen and twenty-two will say it again. Awe → humility → honor. That's the sequence. Fear → hiding → shame is the other sequence, and it's the one the mistranslation accidentally teaches.

Here's the shape. A soft answer turns away wrath — the fire needs fuel you didn't hand it. The healing tongue is a tree of life; the twisted one breaks the spirit. God's eyes see to the floor of the grave, so your heart isn't hidden. A glad heart is a continual feast; good news fattens the bones. Better a little with awe than a lot with chaos. Take correction and you get to lodge with the wise. And awe is the training — before honor comes humility.

Today: the next time someone comes in hot, go soft. Not weak — soft. Watch what happens when the fire gets no fuel. That's awe doing a rep.
