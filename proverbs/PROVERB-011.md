# PROVERB 11 — Honest Scales

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 11:1–31. Second chapter of Solomon's sayings — 31 two-liners, heavy on the right-standing vs. wicked antithesis. Clustered by theme (every verse present): honest dealing, the city and its people, generosity vs. hoarding, the mouth again, and the "tree of life" that reappears at 11:30. No yirah verse; the fear-word at 11:23 is human expectation. Companion: Ps 15; Ps 37; Prov 3:18.*

---

Chapter eleven opens with a shopkeeper's tool. "False scales are detestable to God, but an honest weight is His delight."

Even sh'lemah — a whole stone, a full weight. In the market you weighed grain against a stone. A crooked seller filed the stone down. God says: that's to'evah — detestable, the word reserved for idols and abominations. Rigging the scale gets filed in the same drawer as worshipping Molech. That's how seriously heaven takes fair dealing.

Let's pick up the stones.

First handful — how you deal. Verse three: **"The integrity of the upright guides them, but the crookedness of the treacherous destroys them."** Verse five: **"The right-standing of the blameless makes his path straight, but the wicked falls by his own wickedness."** Verse six: **"The right-standing of the upright rescues them, but the treacherous are trapped by their own craving."** Three in a row saying the same thing: your character is your GPS. Crooked people don't get caught so much as they fall over — their own crookedness is the tripwire. The pit-digger, again.

Verse eighteen: **"The wicked earns a deceptive wage, but the one who sows right-standing gets a real reward."** Sheker — the wage is a lie. It looks like money. It doesn't spend. Verse twenty: **"God detests the twisted of heart, but the blameless in their way are His delight."** Same word pair as verse one — to'evah and ratzon. Twisted heart, twisted scale: same offense.

Verse twenty-seven: **"Whoever seeks good finds favor, but whoever hunts for evil — it comes to him."** You get what you go looking for. Verse thirty-one: **"If the right-standing are repaid on earth, how much more the wicked and the mark-misser."** Peter quotes the Greek of this — "if the righteous is scarcely saved, what will become of the ungodly?" Both directions pay out. Here, not just later. That's Psalm 58:11 — there is a God who judges in the earth. Second handful — pride and humility. Verse two: **"Pride comes, then shame comes; but with the humble is wisdom."** Zadon — arrogance walks in the door and shame walks in right behind it. They travel together. Verse twelve: **"Whoever despises his neighbor lacks heart, but a man of understanding keeps quiet."** Verse twenty-two, and the father is being funny: "Like a gold ring in a pig's snout is a beautiful woman without discretion." The jewelry's real. The pig's real. The two don't go together. Beauty without judgment isn't ugly — it's wasted.

Third handful — the city. Proverbs cares about the neighborhood, not just the individual soul. Verse ten: **"When the right-standing prosper the city rejoices; when the wicked perish there's shouting."** Verse eleven: **"By the blessing of the upright a city is lifted up, but by the mouth of the wicked it's torn down."** Good people are infrastructure. A town with honest men in it is a better town — everybody feels it, even the ones who don't know why.

Verse fourteen: **"Without guidance a people falls, but in many counselors there's safety."** Get advisors. Plural. Verse twenty-six: **"The people curse the one who hoards grain, but a blessing is on the head of the one who sells it."** That's the man who sits on food in a famine waiting for the price to go up. The whole town curses him. Contrast with the one who opens the storehouse. Proverbs has an economics, and it's not "whatever the market will bear."

Verses eight and nine: **"The right-standing is rescued from trouble, and the wicked walks into it instead. With his mouth the godless destroys his neighbor, but by knowledge the right-standing are rescued."** Verse twenty-nine: **"Whoever brings trouble on his own house inherits the wind, and the fool becomes servant to the wise of heart."** Yinchal ruach — inherits wind. That's the estate the troublemaker leaves. Hosea used the same phrase: "they sow the wind and reap the whirlwind."

Fourth handful — generosity. This is the chapter's heart, and it runs against every instinct.

Verse twenty-four: **"One gives freely and gains more; another withholds what's owed and comes to poverty."** Verse twenty-five: **"The generous soul is made fat, and whoever waters will himself be watered."** Nefesh b'rachah — a soul of blessing. The one who pours out gets poured into. It's not a formula for getting rich. It's a description of how the water table works. Watering makes wells. Hoarding makes deserts. Verse twenty-eight: **"Whoever trusts in his riches will fall, but the right-standing will flourish like a green leaf."** Psalm 1's tree by the water, Psalm 52's olive. Ka-aleh — like foliage. Green because it's connected, not because it's stockpiled.

Verse four: **"Wealth is worthless on the day of wrath, but right-standing rescues from death."** Verse seven: **"When a wicked man dies his hope perishes, and the expectation of the strong comes to nothing."** Verse twenty-three: **"The desire of the right-standing is only good; the hope of the wicked is wrath."** Tiqvah — hope, expectation. Not fear. The wicked man's forward-looking — the thing he's counting on — turns out to be wrath. He was banking on the wrong future.

Verse fifteen, a repeat from chapter six: "Whoever puts up security for a stranger will suffer, but whoever hates shaking hands on it is safe." Don't co-sign. Verse sixteen: **"A gracious woman gets honor, but the ruthless get riches."** Aritzim — the violent. They do get rich. The father doesn't deny it. He just notes what they don't get. Verse seventeen: **"A kind man does good to his own soul, but the cruel man harms his own flesh."** Kindness is self-care. Cruelty is self-harm that happens to have a target.

Fifth handful — the mouth. Verse thirteen: **"A gossip reveals a secret, but the faithful of spirit keeps a matter covered."** M'chasseh — covers. Chapter ten's word: love covers. The trustworthy person is the one your secret is safe with. Verse twenty-one: **"Be sure of it — the wicked won't go unpunished, but the offspring of the right-standing escape."** Verse nineteen: **"Steadfast right-standing leads to life, but the one who chases evil chases his own death."**

And verse thirty, the one to walk away with.

**"The fruit of the right-standing is a tree of life, and the wise one takes hold of souls."**

Chapter three said Wisdom is a tree of life. Now the fruit of a good man's life is a tree of life — meaning other people get to eat off him. And the second half — loqeach n'fashot — the old Bibles said "winneth souls," and the phrase got turned into a slogan. But hear the Hebrew plain: the wise person takes hold of people. Gathers them. Draws them in. Not by argument — by being edible. You've met that person. You leave them fed. That's a tree of life walking around.

Here's the shape. Honest scales — God files a rigged weight next to idolatry. Your character is your GPS; the crooked trip over themselves. Good people are infrastructure — the whole city is better with them in it. Pour out and you'll be poured into; hoard and inherit wind. And the good man's life is a tree other people eat from.

Today: check your scale. Somewhere you're weighing something — time, money, credit, blame — with a stone you filed down. Put the whole stone back. Then find one person and be edible.

*Note on translation: No yirah in this chapter. 11:23 tiqvat r'sha'im evrah — "the expectation of the wicked is wrath" — tiqvah = hope/expectation, not fear; some translations import "fear" here, wrongly. 11:1 moz'nei mirmah / even sh'lemah = deceitful scales / whole stone; to'evah (detestable) is the idolatry word (Deut 7:25, 18:12), here applied to commercial fraud; the to'evah / ratzon pair recurs at 11:20. 11:2 zadon = arrogance. 11:16 aritzim = the violent/ruthless — "strong men" (KJV) understates; LXX has a longer text here, MT followed. 11:18 sheker = deceptive/lying wage. 11:22 nezem zahav b'af chazir — gold ring in a pig's snout. 11:24 mi-yosher = "more than is right / what is owed" — the withholder keeps what he should have released. 11:25 nefesh b'rachah = soul of blessing; marveh = one who waters. 11:26 bar = grain — anti-hoarding in famine. 11:28 ka-aleh = like foliage/a green leaf (cf. Ps 1:3, 52:8). 11:29 yinchal ruach = inherits wind (cf. Hos 8:7). 11:30 etz chayyim = tree of life (3:18); loqeach n'fashot — "winneth souls" (KJV) — literally "takes/gathers souls"; LXX reads "the souls of transgressors are cut off," a different text — MT followed; the evangelistic slogan reading is not wrong but is narrower than the Hebrew, which describes the drawing power of a wise life. 11:31 quoted (via LXX) at 1 Pet 4:18.*
