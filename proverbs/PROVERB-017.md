# PROVERB 17 — A Friend at All Times

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 17:1–28. Eighth sayings chapter — 28 two-liners, looser in form than 10–15 (fewer strict antitheses, more observations). Clustered by theme (every verse present): the household and friendship, the crucible, fools and the mouth, bribes and justice, and the two closing sayings on silence. No yirah verse. Companion: Prov 15:16–17; 16:28; Ps 66:10 (refining).*

---

Chapter seventeen opens at the dinner table, the same table as chapter fifteen: "Better a dry crust with quiet than a house full of feasting with strife."

Pat charevah — a dry piece. Shalvah — tranquility. Stale bread and peace beats a banquet with a fight going. Three "better a little"s now — with awe (15:16), with love (15:17), with quiet (17:1). The book keeps telling you what makes a small room enough.

First handful — the household and friendship. Verse two: **"A wise servant will rule over a shameful son and share the inheritance with the brothers."** Blood doesn't guarantee the estate. Wisdom does. Verse six: **"Grandchildren are the crown of the aged, and the glory of children is their fathers."** Ateret — crown, again, chapter sixteen's gray-head word. The old get crowned with grandkids. Verse twenty-one: **"He who fathers a fool gets grief; the father of a dull one has no joy."** Verse twenty-five: **"A foolish son is a grief to his father and bitterness to her who bore him."** The father says it twice because he's watched it. Nothing in the book hurts like this line, and he doesn't dress it up.

Verse nine: **"Whoever covers an offense seeks love, but whoever repeats a matter separates close friends."** M'chasseh pesha — covers the wrong, chapter ten's love-that-covers. And the other side: mafrid alluf — separates the intimate — chapter sixteen's whisperer. The person who keeps bringing it up isn't seeking truth. He's seeking distance.

Verse thirteen: **"Whoever returns evil for good — evil won't leave his house."** Verse fourteen: **"Starting a quarrel is like opening a floodgate; drop the matter before the fight breaks out."** Poter mayim — releasing water. Once the dam's open you don't get to decide how much comes out. So lifnei hitgalla ha-riv n'tosh — before the quarrel bares itself, drop it. The fight you didn't start is the one you win.

Verse seventeen, and this is the one the chapter is named for: "A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for adversity."

B'chol-et — at every time. Not when it's convenient. Not when you're fun. Every. That's the definition. And ach l'tzarah yivvaled — a brother is born for the tight place. The whole point of having one is the day everything closes in. If they're not there for the tight place, they were a contact, not a brother. Chapter eighteen will say it sharper — there's a friend who sticks closer than a brother.

Verse eighteen, the co-signing warning, third time: "A man lacking heart shakes hands and puts up security in front of his neighbor." Verse nineteen: **"Whoever loves transgression loves strife; whoever makes his gate high invites a break."** Magbiah pitcho — the tall door. The man who builds his entrance too big to make a statement is asking for shever — the shattering.

Second handful — the crucible. Verse three: **"The crucible is for silver and the furnace for gold, but God tests hearts."**

Matzref / kur — the refiner's pot, the furnace. Psalm 66 — "You tested us like silver." Psalm 11 — bachan, the assayer's word. The heat isn't punishment. It's assay. The refiner heats the metal to find out what it's worth and to get the junk to float. When your life goes into the furnace, the question isn't "what did I do wrong?" It's "what is He finding in here?" Verse twenty-two: **"A glad heart is good medicine, but a crushed spirit dries up the bones."** Gehah — a cure, healing. Chapter fifteen's continual feast is pharmacy. And the crushed spirit — ruach n'khe'ah — chapter fifteen's word — dries the bone. The book keeps a running medical chart: envy rots the bones, good news fattens them, kind words heal them, a crushed spirit dries them. Your skeleton is keeping score of your inner life.

Third handful — fools. Verse ten: **"A rebuke goes deeper into a discerning man than a hundred blows into a fool."** Techat g'arah — a rebuke sinks in. One word to a wise man does more than a hundred lashes to a fool. There's your rod commentary from chapter thirteen, for free — the book itself says beating a fool doesn't work. Verse twelve: **"Better to meet a bear robbed of her cubs than a fool in his folly."** The father would rather run into a grieving mother bear than a fool who's committed to an idea. Think about who that's describing. Verse sixteen: **"What use is money in a fool's hand to buy wisdom, when he has no heart?"** He'd pay for the course and not attend. Verse twenty-four: **"Wisdom is in front of the face of the discerning, but the eyes of a fool are on the ends of the earth."** B'qtzeh-eretz — the far horizon. The fool's always looking over there. The wise man is looking at what's in front of him. Verse twenty-six: **"Fining the right-standing is not good, nor striking nobles for their integrity."** Verse seven: **"Fine speech doesn't fit a fool; how much less lying lips a ruler."** Verse eleven: **"An evil man seeks only rebellion; a cruel messenger will be sent against him."** Verse twenty: **"The twisted of heart finds no good, and the one with a perverse tongue falls into trouble."** Verse four: **"An evildoer listens to wicked lips; a liar gives ear to a destructive tongue."** You hear what you are. The liar has an ear for lies. Verse five: **"Whoever mocks the poor insults his Maker; whoever rejoices at calamity won't go unpunished."** Chapter fourteen, verse thirty-one — again. Cheref osehu. Mock the poor, mock the Craftsman. Twice now.

Fourth handful — bribes and justice. Verse eight: **"A bribe is a charm in the eyes of its owner; wherever he turns, he prospers."** That's not endorsement — it's diagnosis. The briber thinks it's a magic stone. Verse twenty-three: **"The wicked takes a bribe from the fold of the garment to bend the paths of justice."** Me-cheq — from the bosom, the inside pocket. The under-the-table exchange, described three thousand years before there were tables. Verse fifteen: **"Acquitting the guilty and condemning the innocent — God detests both."** To'evah — the idolatry word, chapter eleven's drawer. A rigged verdict either direction gets filed with the idols.

And the two on silence. Verse twenty-seven: **"Whoever restrains his words has knowledge, and the cool of spirit is a man of understanding."** Q'ar-ruach — cool of spirit. Not cold — cool. The person who doesn't heat up. Chapter fourteen's long nostril, chapter fifteen's soft answer, now as a temperature.

And verse twenty-eight, the last line, and it's funny on purpose:

**"Even a fool who keeps silent is counted wise; when he shuts his lips, he's discerning."**

Read it straight: the fool can pass for wise just by not talking. That's how powerful silence is. It works even for people who don't deserve it. The proverb's making a joke — but the joke is a mercy: if you know you're not sharp today, you have a move. Shut it. Chapter ten said fewer words, fewer misses. Chapter seventeen says the floor is zero, and zero works.

Here's the shape. Dry crust with quiet beats a feast with fighting. Grandkids are the crown. Cover the offense or split the friendship. A friend loves at every time and a brother is born for the tight place. The furnace is assay, not punishment. One rebuke to a wise man beats a hundred blows to a fool — the book knows beating doesn't work. Bribes come from the inside pocket, and God detests a rigged verdict either way. And a fool who shuts up passes for wise.

Today: find the one who was born for your tight place and tell them so. Then, in one conversation where you'd normally be the smartest voice, try being the quiet one. See if you pass.

*Note on translation: No yirah in this chapter. 17:1 pat charevah v'shalvah = a dry crust and quiet. 17:3 matzref / kur = crucible / furnace; bochen libbot = tests hearts (Ps 11:5, 66:10 bachan). 17:5 cheref osehu = reproaches his Maker (= 14:31). 17:6 ateret = crown (16:31). 17:8 even-chen = stone of favor/charm — the bribe as talisman, descriptive not approving. 17:9 m'chasseh pesha = covers transgression (10:12); mafrid alluf = separates the intimate friend (16:28). 17:10 techat g'arah = a rebuke descends/sinks in; me-hakkot k'sil me'ah = than a hundred blows to a fool — the book's own testimony that striking fools is ineffective (cf. 13:24 note). 17:12 dov shakkul = a bear bereaved of cubs. 17:14 poter mayim = one releasing water. 17:15 to'evah = detestable (idolatry register, 11:1). 17:17 b'chol-et = at every time; ach l'tzarah yivvaled = a brother is born for distress. 17:18 co-signing (6:1, 11:15, 22:26). 17:19 magbiah pitcho = raises his doorway high; shever = breaking. 17:22 gehah = healing/cure (hapax); ruach n'khe'ah = crushed spirit (15:13); t'yabbesh-garem = dries the bone — the bone-chart: 12:4, 14:30, 15:30, 16:24. 17:23 me-cheq = from the bosom-fold. 17:24 b'qtzeh-eretz = at the ends of the earth. 17:27 q'ar-ruach — Ketiv "precious of spirit," Qere q'ar "cool of spirit" — Qere followed. 17:28 the fool-passes-for-wise saying is deliberate irony; cf. 10:19.*
