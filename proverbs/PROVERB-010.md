# PROVERB 10 — The Mouth Is the Tell

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 10:1–32. First chapter of "the proverbs of Solomon" proper (10:1–22:16) — 32 two-line sayings, mostly right-standing vs. wicked, wise vs. dull. The lectures are over; from here the book speaks in single punches. Clustered here by theme (every verse present): work and wealth, the mouth, the two ways, and the one yirah verse (10:27). Companion: Ps 1; Ps 37.*

---

Nine chapters of lecture. Now the father stops explaining and starts firing. From here to chapter twenty-two, Proverbs is one-liners — two beats each, and each one designed to stick in your head and go off later. You don't read these like a story. You read them like a bag of stones and pick up the one you need today.

Verse one, the title: "The proverbs of Solomon." Then the first stone: "A wise son makes a father glad; a dull son is his mother's grief." That's the frame for the whole collection. Every saying that follows is a father hoping for the first half.

Let's pick up the stones by what they're for.

First handful — work and wealth. Verse two: **"Treasures gained by wickedness don't profit, but right-standing rescues from death."** Verse three: **"God doesn't let the right-standing go hungry, but He thwarts the craving of the wicked."** Verse four: **"A slack hand makes poor, but a diligent hand makes rich."** Verse five: **"The one who gathers in summer is a wise son; the one who sleeps at harvest is a shameful son."**

The ant from chapter six, said in one line. Know what season it is. Verse fifteen: **"A rich man's wealth is his strong city; the ruin of the poor is their poverty."** That's just observation — the father isn't pretending money doesn't matter. Verse sixteen sharpens it: "The wage of the right-standing leads to life; the income of the wicked leads to missing the mark." Same paycheck, different destination. Verse twenty-two: **"The blessing of God makes rich, and hard toil adds nothing to it."** Hear the balance — verse four says work hard, verse twenty-two says work can't replace blessing. Both true. Both stones in the bag.

Verse twenty-six, and it's the funniest line in the chapter: "Like vinegar to the teeth and smoke to the eyes, so is the sluggard to the one who sends him." You've had that employee. You've been that employee. Sending the sluggard on an errand is like getting smoke in your eyes — it's not that he's evil, it's that he stings.

Second handful — the mouth. This is where chapter ten spends most of its stones, and it's not an accident. Chapter four told you to guard the heart because everything flows from it; this chapter watches what flows.

Verse eight: **"The wise of heart receives commands, but the babbling fool comes to ruin."** Verse ten repeats the second half: "the babbling fool comes to ruin" — twice in three verses, because the father means it. Evil s'fatayim — the fool of lips. The one who never stops talking.

Verse eleven: **"The mouth of the right-standing is a fountain of life, but the mouth of the wicked conceals violence."** Fountain — m'qor. That's chapter five's word for your own well. A good man's mouth waters people. Verse thirteen: **"On the lips of the discerning wisdom is found, but a rod for the back of the one lacking heart."** Verse fourteen: **"The wise store up knowledge, but the mouth of a fool is near ruin."** Store up — the treasure word from chapter two. The wise bank what they learn. The fool spends it as fast as it comes in, out loud.

Verse eighteen: **"Whoever hides hatred has lying lips, and whoever spreads slander is a dull fool."** Both sides of the coin — the smiler with hate underneath, and the loudmouth who says the hate out loud. Both wrong. Verse nineteen, and this one should be framed: "When words are many, missing the mark isn't lacking — but the one who restrains his lips is wise." The more you talk, the more you miss. Statistically. Just by volume. The wise man wins by saying less.

Verse twenty: **"The tongue of the right-standing is choice silver; the heart of the wicked is worth little."** Notice: tongue matched to heart. What comes out the mouth is a direct read on what's in the control room. Verse twenty-one: **"The lips of the right-standing feed many, but fools die for lack of heart."** Feed. A good man's words are food. People leave a conversation with him fuller than they came.

Verses thirty-one and thirty-two close the chapter on it: "The mouth of the right-standing produces wisdom, but the twisted tongue will be cut off. The lips of the right-standing know what's fitting, but the mouth of the wicked knows only what's twisted." Ratzon — what's fitting, what's acceptable, what lands well. The wise man knows what the moment needs. The fool knows only one setting.

So the mouth is the tell. You want to know what's in a man's heart? Don't ask him. Listen for ten minutes. Third handful — the two ways. Psalm 1 said it. Chapter four said it. Now it's in stones.

Verse six: **"Blessings crown the head of the right-standing, but the mouth of the wicked conceals violence."** Verse seven: **"The memory of the right-standing is a blessing, but the name of the wicked will rot."** That's the long game — what people say at your funeral versus your name turning into a bad smell.

Verse nine: **"The one who walks with integrity walks securely, but the one who twists his ways will be found out."** Yivadea — will be known. Not necessarily caught by a cop. Known. Twisted paths always eventually show.

Verse twelve — and Peter and James both quote this one: "Hatred stirs up strife, but love covers all wrongs." Ahavah t'chasseh. Love covers. Not ignores — covers, the way you cover a wound. 1 Peter 4:8 — "love covers a multitude of sins." James 5:20 — "covers a multitude of sins." They got it here.

Verse seventeen: **"Whoever heeds training is on the path of life, but whoever ignores correction leads others astray."** Musar and tochachat. Coaching and feedback. Ignore them, and it's not just you who wanders — you take people with you.

Verses twenty-three through twenty-five: **"Doing wrong is like a game to a dull fool, but wisdom to a man of understanding. What the wicked dreads will come on him; what the right-standing desire will be granted. When the storm passes, the wicked are gone — but the right-standing are a foundation forever."** Psalm 1's chaff in the wind, said in three lines. And look at verse twenty-four — m'gorat rasha — the wicked man's dread. That's real fear. The father uses the fear word here, for the wicked, correctly. The wicked dread, and the thing they dread comes.

Verses twenty-eight through thirty: **"The hope of the right-standing is joy, but the expectation of the wicked comes to nothing. The way of God is a stronghold to the one with integrity, but ruin to those who do wrong. The right-standing will never be uprooted, but the wicked will not remain in the land."** Lo-yimot — will not be shaken. There's your Psalms thread — 15, 16, 21, 30, 46, 55, 62, 112, 125 — now in Proverbs. And "the land" — chapter two's promise, Psalm 37's inheritance.

And the one yirah stone. Verse twenty-seven:

"The AWE of God adds days, but the years of the wicked are cut short." Your Bible says "the fear of the LORD prolongeth days." Think about that for one second as fear. Chronic fear shortens your life. Cortisol, blood pressure, sleepless nights. Fear does not add days. Awe does. Awe slows your breathing. Awe makes you stand still and look up. Chapter three said awe is healing to your body. This verse says it's longevity. The physiology of the Hebrew is *right*; the physiology of the King James is backwards.

And verse twenty-four gave us the contrast in the same chapter — m'gorah, the wicked man's dread. Two different words. One adds years; the other eats them. English gave both of them the same name.

Here's the shape. From here on the book fires stones, not lectures — pick up the one you need. Work like the ant, but know work can't replace blessing. The mouth is the tell: a fountain or a fool, and the fewer words the fewer misses. Two ways — one is a foundation forever and one rots. And awe — not fear — adds days.

Today: count your words. Just today. Every time you're about to say the second sentence, ask if the first one was enough. Verse nineteen is a math problem, and you can win it by subtraction.

*Note on translation: 10:27 yirat YHWH tosif yamim — "the fear of the LORD prolongeth days" (KJV) — rendered "the AWE of God adds days"; the health/longevity claim (cf. 3:7–8) is physiologically coherent only under awe, not dread. 10:24 m'gorat rasha = the dread of the wicked — genuine fear (magor root), correctly rendered as dread; the chapter contrasts yirah (v. 27) and m'gorah (v. 24). 10:1 m'shalim = proverbs; the superscription marks the start of the Solomonic sayings collection (10:1–22:16, 375 sayings = the numerical value of Sh'lomoh). 10:8, 10:10 evil s'fatayim = fool of lips / babbling fool; 10:10b duplicates 10:8b in MT (LXX has a different second line: "he who reproves boldly makes peace") — MT followed. 10:11 m'qor chayyim = fountain of life (cf. 5:18). 10:12 ahavah t'chasseh kol-p'sha'im = love covers all transgressions — quoted at 1 Pet 4:8, James 5:20. 10:13 shevet = rod — here for the back of the chasar-lev; see 13:24 note for the shevet discussion. 10:14 yitz'p'nu = store up (treasure vocabulary, 2:1, 2:7). 10:17 musar / tochachat = training / correction. 10:19 lo-yechdal pasha = missing the mark does not cease. 10:22 etzev = toil/pain — "hard toil adds nothing" (alt.: "He adds no sorrow with it") — both readings viable; the first followed. 10:26 atzel = sluggard. 10:30 lo-yimot = will not be shaken/uprooted — the Psalms mot thread; eretz = the land. 10:32 ratzon = what is fitting/acceptable. Yirah in this chapter: 10:27.*
