# PROVERB 9 — Two Dinner Invitations

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 9:1–18. The close of the first section (chs. 1–9): two banquets, side by side. Lady Wisdom builds a house and sends servants to invite the naive (9:1–6); a middle block on how mockers and wise men each receive correction, with the thesis verse restated (9:7–12); and Lady Folly, sitting at her own door, using the same words to invite the same people (9:13–18). Companion: Prov 1:7; Ps 111:10; Ps 23:5 (the table).*

---

Nine chapters of the father at the kitchen table, and this is how he ends: two women, two houses, two dinner parties, same guest list. You're on both lists. You have to pick one.

**"Wisdom has built her house; she has hewn her seven pillars."**

Seven pillars. Complete. Solid. Chapter eight told you she was there when God laid the foundations of the earth — of course she can build a house. And it's not a shack. It's got columns.

Verse two: **"She has slaughtered her meat, mixed her wine, and set her table."** This is a real feast. Meat — that was rare. Mixed wine — spiced, prepared, not just poured. The table is set. Psalm 23 — "You set a table before me." Wisdom did the same.

Verse three: **"She has sent out her servant girls; she calls from the highest places of the city."** She's sending invitations. She's not waiting for you to find her. And she's using the high ground — the place where a voice carries.

And here's what she says, verse four: **"Whoever is naive, let him turn in here! To the one lacking heart, she says —"**

Stop. She's inviting the peti. The naive one. And the chasar lev — the heart-empty kid from chapter seven, the one who followed the stranger like an ox. Wisdom's guest list isn't the wise. It's the dumb ones who could still learn. She's specifically inviting the people who've already messed up. That's who the meat is for.

Verse five: **"Come, eat my bread and drink the wine I've mixed. Leave your naivety and live; walk in the way of understanding."**

Eat. Drink. Live. Isaiah 55 says it — "come, buy wine and milk without money." Jesus's parables are full of this — the king who sends servants to invite everyone to the wedding feast, and the ones who come are the ones from the streets. That's this. This is the first draft of the great banquet.

Then verses seven through twelve, a middle block that seems like a detour but isn't. It's about who can receive the invitation.

**"Whoever corrects a mocker gets shame for himself; whoever rebukes a wicked man gets a bruise. Don't correct a mocker or he'll hate you; correct a wise man and he'll love you."**

The letz. The mocker. The third grade of fool, the one who's made sneering a lifestyle. The father is honest: you can't reach him. You correct him, he hates you for it. Wisdom's servants are out there shouting, but the mocker isn't heart-empty — he's heart-armored. The invitation bounces off.

But: "Correct a wise man and he'll love you." That's the tell. The wise man isn't the one who never needs correcting. He's the one who thanks you for it. Verse nine: **"Give to a wise man and he'll get wiser; teach a right-standing man and he'll add to his learning."** Chapter one said this. The wise get wiser because they keep taking the reps.

And then verse ten. The thesis, one more time, as the section closes.

**"The beginning of wisdom is the AWE of God, and knowledge of the Holy One is understanding."**

Chapter one, verse seven opened the book with it. Chapter nine, verse ten closes the first section with it. It's bookends. Reshit chochmah yirat YHWH. And it's the same line as Psalm 111:10, word for word. Your Bible says "fear." It's awe. Nine chapters in, you've now seen what it does: it heals your bones, it makes you hate evil, it can't sit in a room with pride, it's a choice, and it's where you end up when you dig. Nothing in that list is fear. That's awe. That's the whole project of this book — making sure that one word is right, because everything else in Proverbs is built on it.

Verse eleven: **"For by me your days will be many, and years of life will be added to you."** Verse twelve: **"If you're wise, you're wise for yourself; if you mock, you alone will bear it."** Nobody can be wise for you. And nobody else pays for your mocking. This is the one decision that's completely yours.

Now verse thirteen, and the other house.

"The woman Folly is loud; she's naive and knows nothing." Eshet k'silut. Lady Folly. The stranger from chapters two, five, six, and seven — now given her formal name and set up as Wisdom's mirror. And note: she's naive too. P'tayyut. She's not a mastermind. She's as lost as the people she's inviting. The blind leading the blind to dinner.

Verse fourteen: **"She sits at the door of her house, on a seat at the high places of the city —"** The same high places Wisdom called from in verse three. Same elevation. Same acoustics. "— calling to those who pass by, who are going straight on their way —"

Ha-m'yashrim orchotam. The ones going straight. She's not going after people already headed her way. She's going after the ones walking the right road, trying to pull them off it. That's who she wants. Not the lost — the ones who are almost home.

And verse sixteen — listen to this. "Whoever is naive, let him turn in here! To the one lacking heart, she says —"

Word for word. Verse four and verse sixteen are identical in Hebrew. Wisdom's invitation and Folly's invitation are the same sentence. Same words, same target, same volume, same corner. The father has been telling you for nine chapters that the counterfeit uses the original's script. Here he just prints them next to each other so you can see it.

So what's the difference? The menu. Verse seventeen: **"Stolen water is sweet, and bread eaten in secret is pleasant."**

That's Folly's feast. Stolen water. Secret bread. Wisdom slaughtered meat and mixed wine and set the table in the open. Folly's got water — the cheapest thing there is — and it's only good because it's stolen. Bread that's only pleasant because it's hidden. The whole appeal is the sneaking. Take away the secrecy and it's tap water and a stale roll.

Verse eighteen, last line of the whole section: "But he doesn't know that the dead are there, that her guests are in the depths of Sheol."

V'lo yada. He doesn't know. The ox again — same words as 7:23. Her guests. She has guests too. They just aren't at the table. They're in the depths of the grave. Every man who ever took the stolen water is downstairs. That's the seating chart. Not hell — the grave. Silence. The place where the ones who chose the secret bread went quiet.

Here's the shape. Two houses, two women, two dinners, one guest list — and it's you. Wisdom built with seven pillars and served meat and wine in the open. Folly sat at her door and offered stolen water. They used the same invitation. The only difference is the menu and where the guests end up. And the hinge in the middle, one more time, bookending the whole first section: awe of God is where wisdom starts. Not fear. Awe.

Today: you're going to get both invitations before sundown. Same words. Check the menu. If it's only good because it's secret — that's the wrong house.

*Note on translation: 9:10 t'chillat chochmah yirat YHWH — "the fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom" (KJV) — rendered "the beginning of wisdom is the AWE of God." This bookends 1:7 (reshit da'at) and is verbatim Ps 111:10; the first nine chapters open and close on the yirah thesis. T'chillah (9:10) and reshit (1:7) are near-synonyms for "beginning." Da'at q'doshim = knowledge of the Holy One (plural of majesty). 9:1 chatzvah amudeha shiv'ah = hewn her seven pillars — completeness. 9:2 mas'chah yeinah = mixed/spiced her wine. 9:4 and 9:16 are identical in the MT — mi-peti yasur hennah, chasar-lev am'rah lo — the parallel is intentional and is the chapter's central device. 9:5 the banquet-invitation is echoed at Isa 55:1–2, Matt 22:1–10, Luke 14:16–24. 9:7–8 letz = mocker (third fool-grade, incorrigible). 9:8 hochach = correct/rebuke. 9:13 eshet k'silut = Lady Folly (from kesil); p'tayyut = naivety — Folly is herself naive. 9:15 ha-m'yashrim orchotam = those making their paths straight — she targets the ones on the right road. 9:17 mayim g'nuvim = stolen water; lechem s'tarim = bread of secrets. 9:18 r'fa'im = the dead/shades; imqei Sheol — "the depths of hell" (KJV) — rendered depths of the grave; v'lo yada repeats 7:23. LXX adds several verses after 9:12 and 9:18 (not in MT); MT followed. Yirah in this chapter: 9:10.*
