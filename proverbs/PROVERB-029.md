# PROVERB 29 — Where There Is No Vision

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 29:1–27. Close of Hezekiah's collection (25–29) and of the Solomonic material altogether. 27 sayings on rulers and justice, the fool and the angry man, children and servants, and the much-quoted 29:18 (which doesn't say what the poster says). Contains the last rod verse (29:15) and a human fear-word at 29:25 (cherdat adam — the trembling before people, rendered as fear and contrasted with trust). No yirah verse. Companion: Prov 13:24, 22:15, 23:13; 1 Sam 3:1; Ps 118:6; Prov 21:1.*

---

Chapter twenty-nine is the last of Solomon's sayings — after this, two outsiders and a mother finish the book. So the scribes close with a summary of everything the collection cared about: rulers, the poor, the fool, the child, the mouth, and the one thing you should be afraid of and the one thing you shouldn't.

**"A man often corrected who stiffens his neck will be broken suddenly, beyond healing."**

Ish tochachot maqsheh-oref. A man of many corrections — he's been told, and told — who hardens his neck. Chapter twenty-eight's hardened heart; here it's the neck. The ox that won't take the yoke. And ein marpe — no healing. The medicine word, chapter three, four, twelve, fifteen, sixteen — withdrawn. The correction was the medicine. He refused every dose. Now there isn't one.

First handful — rulers and the poor. Verse two: **"When the right-standing increase, the people rejoice; when the wicked rule, the people groan."** Chapter twenty-eight — full street, empty street. Here — cheering, groaning. Verse four: **"By justice a king gives a land stability, but one who takes bribes tears it down."** Ish t'rumot — a man of offerings, the temple word, twisted — he treats bribes like sacrifices owed to him. Verse twelve: **"If a ruler listens to lies, all his officials become wicked."** The staff becomes what the boss rewards. Verse fourteen: **"A king who judges the poor faithfully — his throne will be established forever."** L'ad — forever. The permanence of a government is indexed to how it treats the dal, the weak.

Verse seven: **"The right-standing knows the rights of the poor; the wicked doesn't understand such knowledge."** Yodea tzaddiq din dallim. He knows their case — he's read their file. Chapter twelve — knows the soul of his animal. Chapter twenty-seven — knows the faces of his sheep. The right-standing man knows the poor. Not pities. Knows. Verse thirteen: **"The poor man and the oppressor meet together; God gives light to the eyes of both."** Chapter twenty-two, verse two — rich and poor have one Maker. Here: the oppressor and his victim both see by the same sun. God lit both their eyes. Verse twenty-six: **"Many seek a ruler's favor, but justice for a man comes from God."** Don't lobby the governor. Mishpat-ish me-YHWH. The verdict that matters is filed upstairs.

Verse sixteen: **"When the wicked increase, transgression increases, but the right-standing will see their downfall."** Verse twenty-seven: **"An unjust man is detestable to the right-standing, and one whose way is straight is detestable to the wicked."** To'evah both ways. The last line of the whole Solomonic collection is mutual revulsion — the good man and the crooked man each find the other's way unbearable. That's not hatred; it's incompatibility. Chapter twenty-nine wants you to know which side of that you're on before the outsiders start talking.

Second handful — the fool and the angry man. Verse nine: **"If a wise man goes to court with a fool, the fool rages and laughs, and there's no quiet."** V'ragaz v'sachaq — he storms and laughs. There's no nachat — no rest. You can't get a settlement from someone who alternates between fury and mockery. Verse eleven: **"A fool gives full vent to his anger, but a wise man holds it back."** Kol-rucho yotzi — brings out his whole spirit. All of it, every time. The wise man y'shabb'chennah — quiets it — the word for *stilling* a storm. Verse twenty: **"Do you see a man hasty in his words? There's more hope for a fool than for him."** Chapter twenty-six, verse twelve, with speech swapped for self-regard. The fast-talker is beneath the fool. Verse twenty-two: **"An angry man stirs up strife, and a wrathful man is full of transgression."** Ba'al chemah — master of heat. Chapter twenty-two — don't befriend him; you'll catch it.

Verse eight: **"Mockers set a city aflame, but the wise turn away wrath."** Yafichu qiryah — they blow on the city, fan it. The letz is an arsonist with his breath. Chapter twenty-six's man adding wood. And the wise yashivu af — turn back the nostril, chapter fifteen's soft answer at city scale. Verse ten: **"Bloodthirsty men hate the blameless and seek the life of the upright."** Verse twenty-four: **"The partner of a thief hates his own life; he hears the oath and says nothing."** He hears the court's summons and stays silent — Leviticus 5 — and the silence is his confession. Verse six: **"An evil man is snared by his own wrong, but the right-standing sings and rejoices."** Yarun v'sameach — he shouts and is glad. The pit-digger, one more time — and the good man, singing past the pit.

Third handful — children and servants. Verse three: **"A man who loves wisdom makes his father glad, but a companion of prostitutes wastes his wealth."** Verse seventeen: **"Train your son and he'll give you rest; he'll bring delight to your soul."** Yasser bincha vi-nichecha — coach your son and he'll give you rest — nuach, the Sabbath verb. The payoff of musar is the parent gets to rest. Verse twenty-one: **"Whoever pampers his servant from childhood will find him a son in the end."** The Hebrew's disputed — "a son" or "ungrateful" — but the shape is: how you raise the one under you determines what he becomes. Verse nineteen: **"A servant isn't corrected by words alone; he understands but doesn't respond."** Some people understand and don't move. Words aren't reaching them — not because they're stupid; because they've decided not to answer.

And verse fifteen, the last rod verse in the book:

**"The rod and correction give wisdom, but a child left to himself brings shame to his mother."**

Shevet v'tochachat — the rod and correction — yitten chochmah, give wisdom. Hear the pairing. The rod isn't alone. It's with tochachat — the reasoning, the explanation, the feedback. Chapter thirteen's shepherd's rod plus chapter one's correction — the whole musar toolkit. And what's the alternative the verse warns about? Not "the un-hit child." Na'ar m'shullach — a child sent off, let loose, abandoned to himself. The opposite of the rod isn't gentleness. It's neglect. Every rod verse in this book — thirteen, twenty-two, twenty-three, twenty-nine — is aimed at the absent father, not the gentle one. The shepherd who doesn't show up is the villain. And the shame lands on immo — his mother. She's the one who's left holding what the absent shepherd wouldn't. Read all four rod verses together and the book's message is: be there, early, steadily, with the guiding rod and the explaining word — and never let it become the thing that destroys him. Chapter nineteen wrote the guardrail. Chapter three showed you how God does it. That's the whole doctrine. Anyone who pulls one verse out of it to justify a belt has read a quarter of the book.

Fourth handful — the mouth, the poor, the flatterer. Verse five: **"A man who flatters his neighbor spreads a net for his feet."** Machaliq — smooths. Chapter twenty-six's smooth mouth engineering a fall. Verse twenty-three: **"A man's pride brings him low, but the lowly in spirit gains honor."** Sh'fal-ruach yitmoch kavod — the low of spirit grasps honor. Chapter fifteen, sixteen, eighteen, twenty-two — before honor, humility. Fifth time. The scribes are hammering.

And the two famous ones — eighteen and twenty-five.

Verse eighteen: **"Where there is no vision, the people are unrestrained; but happy is the one who keeps the teaching."**

You've seen "where there is no vision, the people perish" on a thousand leadership posters. That's not what it says. Chazon — vision — is a prophetic word: the revelation, the word from God, the thing Samuel received in 1 Samuel 3 when "the word of God was rare." It's not a mission statement. And yippara am — the people don't perish; they go loose, run wild, get unrestrained — same root as chapter one's "you ignored all my advice." No word from God, and everybody does whatever they want. The corrective is the second line, which the poster always cuts: shomer torah ashrehu — happy is the one who keeps the teaching. The verse isn't about corporate strategy. It's about revelation and obedience — when God's word isn't heard, a society throws off its bridle. Fix the poster.

Verse twenty-five: **"The fear of man lays a snare, but whoever trusts in God will be set high."**

Cherdat adam. The trembling before people — charad, the quake word, Isaac trembling, the earth shaking. This is a fear word, and I'm rendering it as fear — because this is what the book wants you afraid of being afraid of. Not God. People. What they'll think. What they'll say. Whether they'll approve. That trembling is yitten moqesh — a trap-setter. And the way out isn't more courage — it's boteach ba-YHWH, trusting God, and you get y'suggav — set high, the tower-word from chapter eighteen. Psalm 118:6 — "God is with me; I won't fear — what can man do to me?" Twenty-nine chapters after "the awe of God is the beginning," the book finally names the wrong fear by its real name. Awe of God, up. Dread of people, out. The English gave both the same word and made it impossible to hear the difference. The Hebrew never confused them for a second.

Here's the shape. Stiffen the neck against every correction and the medicine gets withdrawn. A throne lasts as long as it judges the poor faithfully. The fool storms and laughs; the wise man stills the storm. Coach your son and rest. The rod is with correction, and its enemy is the absent father, not the gentle one. "No vision" is no word from God, and the people don't perish — they run wild. And the fear the book wants gone is the trembling before people; the trust it wants is in God, who sets you high.

Today: name the person you've been trembling before — the one whose opinion is running your decisions. Then say Psalm 118:6 out loud. That's the last thing Solomon's collection wanted you to know before the outsiders speak.

*Note on translation: No yirah in this chapter. 29:25 cherdat adam yitten moqesh — "the fear of man bringeth a snare" (KJV) — charad/cheradah = trembling/quaking — a genuine dread word, correctly rendered as fear; its object is adam (people), and the antithesis is boteach ba-YHWH (trust in God); this is the book's explicit contrast between the wrong fear (of people) and the right posture toward God (trust/awe) — the distinction the English "fear of the LORD" erases; cf. Ps 118:6, 56:4, 27:1. 29:18 b'ein chazon yippara am — "where there is no vision, the people perish" (KJV) — chazon = prophetic revelation (1 Sam 3:1, Isa 1:1), not strategic vision; yippara = are let loose/unrestrained (para, cf. 1:25, 8:33, 13:18, 15:32), not "perish"; second line shomer torah ashrehu = happy is the keeper of the teaching. 29:1 ish tochachot maqsheh-oref = a man of rebukes stiffening his neck; ein marpe = no healing. 29:4 ish t'rumot = man of offerings (bribes as tribute). 29:6 yarun v'sameach = sings and rejoices. 29:7 yodea tzaddiq din dallim = the righteous knows the case of the poor. 29:8 yafichu qiryah = blow upon/inflame a city. 29:9 v'ragaz v'sachaq = rages and laughs; ein nachat = no rest. 29:11 kol-rucho yotzi = brings out all his spirit; y'shabb'chennah = stills it. 29:13 me'ir einei sh'neihem = gives light to the eyes of both (22:2). 29:15 shevet v'tochachat yitten chochmah = rod AND correction give wisdom — the pairing is in the Hebrew; na'ar m'shullach = a child sent away/let loose — the antithesis of the rod is neglect, not gentleness; mevish immo = shames his mother; see 13:24, 22:15, 23:13–14 and guardrails 17:10, 19:18, 19:25, 22:8, 27:22. 29:17 yasser bincha vi-nichecha = train your son and he will give you rest (nuach). 29:21 manon — hapax; "son/heir" or "ungrateful/grief" — disputed; both noted. 29:23 sh'fal-ruach yitmoch kavod = the low of spirit grasps honor (15:33, 16:19, 18:12, 22:4). 29:24 the heard oath (Lev 5:1). 29:26 mishpat-ish me-YHWH = a man's judgment is from God. 29:27 to'evah both directions — mutual revulsion closes the Solomonic collection.*
