# PROVERB 27 — Iron Sharpens Iron

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 27:1–27. Hezekiah's collection continued — 27 sayings, several of them among the most quoted in the book (27:1, 27:6, 27:17, 27:19), closing with an unusual eight-verse pastoral on flocks and fields (27:23–27). Clustered by theme (every verse present): tomorrow and praise, friends and wounds, the far-off brother, iron and faces, the heart's hunger, and the shepherd's economy. No yirah verse. Companion: James 4:13–14; Ps 141:5; Prov 18:24; Ps 23.*

---

Chapter twenty-seven is about friends — the real kind, the kind that hurt you on purpose because they love you — and it ends, strangely, in a sheep pasture. Stay for the pasture. It's the point.

First handful — tomorrow and praise. Verse one: **"Don't boast about tomorrow, for you don't know what a day will bring forth."** Mah-yeled yom — what a day will give birth to. Tomorrow is pregnant and you don't know with what. James 4 — "you don't know what tomorrow will bring; you're a mist." He got it here. Verse two: **"Let another praise you, and not your own mouth; a stranger, and not your own lips."** If it's true, someone else will say it. If only you're saying it, check. Verse twenty-one: **"The crucible for silver and the furnace for gold, and a man is tested by his praise."** Chapter seventeen's furnace — but here the heat is praise. L'fi mahalalo — according to the praise he gets, or according to what he praises. Both readings work. What you praise reveals you; how you handle being praised refines you. Fame is a furnace. Watch what floats.

Verses three and four: **"A stone is heavy and sand is weighty, but a fool's provocation is heavier than both. Wrath is cruel and anger is a flood, but who can stand before jealousy?"** Qin'ah — jealousy, chapter fourteen's bone-rot. Rage is a flood you can see coming. Jealousy just stands there and nobody can face it down.

Second handful — friends and wounds. Verse five: **"Better open correction than hidden love."** Tochachat m'gullah me-ahavah m'suttaret. A rebuke out loud beats love that never says anything. If you love someone and never tell them the hard thing, the love is hidden — from them, and from use.

Verse six: **"Faithful are the wounds of a friend, but the kisses of an enemy are profuse."**

Ne'emanim pitz'ei ohev. The wounds of one who loves you are faithful — the emunah word, God's own reliability word. The friend who hurts you can be trusted. And the enemy's kisses are na'tarot — many, lavish, overflowing. Judas kissed. Chapter twenty-six's silver dross on a clay pot. The one who only ever makes you feel good is not necessarily your friend. The one who told you the thing you didn't want to hear — Psalm 141, "let the righteous strike me, it's a kindness" — that's the faithful wound.

Verse nine: **"Oil and perfume make the heart glad, and the sweetness of a friend comes from his earnest counsel."** Meteq re'a me-atzat-nafesh — the sweetness of a friend is from soul-counsel. Not from agreement. From advice that costs him something to give. Verse ten: **"Don't forsake your friend or your father's friend, and don't go to your brother's house in the day of your disaster — better a neighbor nearby than a brother far away."** Tov shachen qarov me-ach rachoq. Chapter seventeen: a brother is born for adversity. Chapter eighteen: a friend who cleaves closer than a brother. Chapter twenty-seven: the one who's actually there. Proximity is a virtue. Blood far away is theory. The neighbor next door is practice. Keep your father's friends — they knew you before you knew yourself.

Verse fourteen: **"Whoever blesses his neighbor with a loud voice early in the morning — it'll be counted as a curse to him."** Ba-boqer hashkem — at dawn. The scribes are being funny again. The loud early blesser. Even a blessing has a volume and a time.

Third handful — iron and faces. Verse seventeen:

**"Iron sharpens iron, and one man sharpens the face of his friend."**

Barzel b'varzel yachad, v'ish yachad p'nei-re'ehu. You've heard it on a poster. Hear what it means. Iron on iron — that's friction. Sparks. Noise. You don't sharpen a blade by petting it. And the second half isn't "sharpens his friend" — it's the face of his friend. P'nei. The countenance. The expression. A real friend changes how you look — sharpens the edge of your face, wakes you up. That's verse six's wound and verse nine's costly counsel, as metallurgy. Find the iron in your life. Stop only spending time with the people who leave you dull.

Verse nineteen: **"As water reflects the face, so a man's heart reflects the man."** Ha-adam la-adam — the heart of a man to a man. You see your face in still water; you see yourself in another person's heart. That's why friends are mirrors and why the wrong friend shows you a distorted face. Chapter thirteen — walk with the wise and become wise. You're being reflected all day. Pick the water.

Fourth handful — the heart's hunger. Verse seven: **"A full soul tramples honeycomb, but to a hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet."** Satiety makes you kick the honey. Hunger makes you eat the bitter. Both are distortions. Know which one you're in before you judge the meal. Verse twenty: **"Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied, and the eyes of man are never satisfied."** Lo tisba'nah. The grave never says "enough." Neither do your eyes. That's the diagnosis of every ad you've ever seen. Not hell — the grave, chapter fifteen's word — the appetite that only stops at death. Verse twelve: **"The prudent sees danger and hides; the naive keep going and pay for it."** Chapter twenty-two's line repeated — the peti just walks on. Verse thirteen: **"Take the garment of one who puts up security for a stranger; hold it as collateral for the foreign woman."** The co-signer's cloak — chapter twenty. Sixth time on this. The scribes really didn't want you to co-sign.

Verse eight: **"Like a bird that strays from its nest is a man who strays from his home."** Noded mi-m'qomo — wanders from his place. Chapter eighteen's isolated man. You have a place. Wandering from it isn't freedom; it's a bird with no nest at dusk. Verse eleven: **"Be wise, my son, and make my heart glad, so I can answer whoever reproaches me."** The teacher's own reputation is riding on the student. Verses fifteen and sixteen: **"A continual dripping on a rainy day and a quarrelsome woman are alike; restraining her is like restraining the wind or grasping oil with the right hand."** Chapter nineteen's delef tored — the drip you can't find. And here's the twist — you can't restrain it. Strife in the house isn't managed by clamping. Chapter twenty-six — you starve a fire; you don't grab it. Verse eighteen: **"Whoever tends a fig tree eats its fruit, and whoever guards his master is honored."** Shomer — the keeper, Psalm 121. Tend the tree; guard the man. Same faithfulness, two objects. Verse twenty-two: **"Though you grind a fool in a mortar with a pestle among the grain, his folly won't leave him."** You can pulverize him and the folly stays. Chapter seventeen — a hundred blows. Same lesson: force doesn't reach a fool.

And the pasture — verses twenty-three through twenty-seven.

**"Know well the condition of your flocks; give attention to your herds. For riches don't last forever, and does a crown endure to every generation? When the hay is removed and new growth appears and the grass of the hills is gathered — the lambs will provide your clothing, and the goats the price of a field. There'll be goats' milk enough for your food, food for your household, and life for your servant girls."**

Eight verses of farming at the end of a chapter about friendship. Why? Yadoa teda p'nei tzonecha — know — know knowingly — the faces of your sheep. Same word as verse seventeen. You sharpen your friend's face; you know your sheep's faces. Chapter twelve — the good man knows the soul of his animal. This is the scribes saying: real wealth isn't the gold that grows wings — chapter twenty-three — it's the living things you've tended. The flock feeds you every year. The crown doesn't pass to every generation. And look at the end of the list — chayyim l'na'arotecha — life for your servant girls. The household economy goes all the way down. When you tend the flock, the lowest person in the house eats. That's what the scribes wanted the last word of the friendship chapter to be: tend what's alive and everyone gets fed.

Here's the shape. Tomorrow is pregnant and you don't know with what. Let someone else praise you; praise is a furnace. The wounds of a friend are faithful; the enemy's kisses are many. The neighbor who's there beats the brother who isn't. Iron sharpens iron — and it sharpens the face. The grave and your eyes never say "enough." Force never reaches a fool. And know the faces of your sheep — tend what's alive, and the servant girl eats.

Today: thank the friend whose wound was faithful — the one who told you the thing. Then go check the faces of your flock — whatever's alive and depending on you — and make sure the last person in the house is fed.

*Note on translation: No yirah in this chapter. 27:1 mah-yeled yom = what a day gives birth to → James 4:13–14. 27:4 qin'ah = jealousy (14:30). 27:5 tochachat m'gullah me-ahavah m'suttaret = open rebuke [is better] than hidden love. 27:6 ne'emanim pitz'ei ohev = faithful are the wounds of a lover/friend; na'tarot = profuse/abundant (hapax; some read "perverse"); cf. Ps 141:5. 27:9 meteq re'a me-atzat-nafesh — MT compressed; "the sweetness of a friend [comes] from counsel of the soul"; LXX differs. 27:10 tov shachen qarov me-ach rachoq = better a near neighbor than a far brother. 27:13 ≈ 20:16. 27:14 ba-boqer hashkem = early in the morning. 27:15 ≈ 19:13 (delef tored); 27:16 tzofneha tzafan-ruach = restraining her is restraining wind; shemen y'mino yiqra — "oil [that] his right hand encounters/grasps" — difficult; sense: ungraspable. 27:17 barzel b'varzel yachad v'ish yachad p'nei-re'ehu = iron with iron sharpens, and a man sharpens the FACE of his friend — p'nei is in the Hebrew and usually dropped in English. 27:19 ka-mayim ha-panim la-panim ken lev-ha-adam la-adam = as water face to face, so the heart of man to man — mirror principle. 27:20 Sheol va'avaddon — "hell and destruction" (KJV) — the grave and ruin (15:11); lo tisba'nah = not satisfied. 27:21 l'fi mahalalo = according to his praise — either received or given; both noted. 27:22 mortar-and-pestle: force cannot extract folly (cf. 17:10). 27:23 yadoa teda p'nei tzonecha = know surely the FACES of your flock — echoes v. 17 p'nei; cf. 12:10. 27:24 nezer = crown/diadem. 27:27 chayyim l'na'arotecha = life for your maidservants — the household economy reaches its lowest member.*
