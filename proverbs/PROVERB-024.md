# PROVERB 24 — Seven Times Down, Seven Times Up

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 24:1–34. Close of the Thirty Sayings (24:1–22, sayings 20–30) plus a short appendix, "these also are sayings of the wise" (24:23–34), ending with the sluggard's field. Clustered by theme (every verse present): envy and the wicked, the house built by wisdom, rescue the ones being dragged to death, honey and the falling righteous, don't gloat, the king, and the overgrown vineyard. Yirah at 24:21. Companion: Ps 37; Ps 73; Prov 6:6–11; Micah 7:8.*

---

Chapter twenty-four finishes the Thirty Sayings and then adds a coda. The teacher's last big theme is how to hold steady while the wicked seem to be winning — the Psalm 37 problem, the Psalm 73 problem — and he answers it with the most encouraging line in the book.

Saying twenty — don't envy them. Verses one and two: **"Don't envy evil men or desire to be with them, for their hearts plot violence and their lips talk trouble."** Chapter twenty-three, verse seventeen — same command. The teacher says it twice in eight verses because the envy is that sticky. You look at what the cheater has and something in you goes I want that. He's saying: look at what's in him. You don't want that.

Verses nineteen and twenty say it a third time: "Don't get heated over evildoers, and don't envy the wicked — for the evil man has no future, and the lamp of the wicked will be snuffed out." Al-titchar — don't burn — Psalm 37's word, three times in that psalm, now here. Ein acharit la-ra — no afterward for the wicked. Chapter twenty-three said your hope has a future. Here: his doesn't. And his lamp — chapter thirteen, twenty, twenty-one — goes out. Every time you burn about the wicked man's success, you're standing in the light of a lamp that's about to be yid'ach — snuffed.

Saying twenty-one — the house. Verses three and four: **"By wisdom a house is built, by understanding it's established, and by knowledge its rooms are filled with all precious and pleasant riches."** Chapter nine — Wisdom built her house. Chapter fourteen — Folly tears hers down with her own hands. Here: your house. Three words — chochmah, t'vunah, da'at — the same three God used to build the universe in chapter three, verses nineteen and twenty. Build your household with the tools He built the cosmos with. It'll hold. Verses five and six: **"A wise man is strong; a man of knowledge grows in power. For by wise guidance you wage war, and victory comes through many counselors."** Chapter eleven, fifteen, twenty — plural advisors. Chapter twenty-one — the win is God's. Bring counsel; expect Him to deliver it.

Saying twenty-two — the fool at the gate. Verse seven: **"Wisdom is too high for a fool; at the gate he doesn't open his mouth."** Evil — the thick-headed one. When the real decisions get made — the city gate, the council — he's got nothing. Verses eight and nine: **"Whoever plots evil will be called a schemer. The scheme of folly is missing-the-mark, and the mocker is detestable to men."** To'evah l'adam — the letz is detestable to people. Not just God. Everyone hates the sneerer.

Saying twenty-three — rescue. Verse ten: **"If you falter in the day of trouble, your strength is small."** Tzar kochecha — your strength is narrow. Then verses eleven and twelve, and these are the ones that get people out of their chairs:

**"Rescue those being led away to death; hold back those staggering toward the slaughter. If you say, 'Look, we didn't know about this' — doesn't He who weighs hearts perceive it? Doesn't He who guards your soul know it? And He'll repay each man according to his deeds."**

Hatzel l'quchim la-mavet. Rescue the ones being taken to death. Not the ones who've asked. The ones being dragged. Chapter seven's ox going to slaughter — here, the book tells you to grab the ox. And then it strikes the excuse dead before you can use it: lo-yadanu zeh — "we didn't know." He who weighs hearts — chapter sixteen, twenty-one — He knows whether you knew. This verse has been read at every abolition meeting, every rescue, every time someone had to decide whether to look. It's the Bible's answer to I didn't want to get involved.

Saying twenty-four — honey and the future. Verses thirteen and fourteen: **"My son, eat honey, for it's good; the honeycomb is sweet to your taste. Know that wisdom is like that to your soul; if you find it, there's a future, and your hope won't be cut off."** Yesh acharit — there's an afterward. The exact phrase from 23:18. Wisdom tastes like honey and has a future. The stranger's honey turns to wormwood — chapter five. This honey stays sweet.

Saying twenty-five — the one to walk away with. Verses fifteen and sixteen: **"Don't lie in wait like a wicked man against the dwelling of the right-standing; don't destroy his resting place. For the right-standing falls seven times and rises again, but the wicked stumble in calamity."**

Sheva yippol tzaddiq va-qam. Seven times the right-standing man falls. Not "might fall." Falls. Seven — the complete number. He falls all the way, as many times as it takes to count as thoroughly fallen. And va-qam — and he gets up. That's the difference. Not that the good man doesn't fall. That he rises. The wicked man yikkash'lu b'ra'ah — stumbles in the trouble and stays there. The falling isn't the tell. The getting up is. Micah 7:8 — "though I fall, I will rise." Psalm 37:24 — "though he fall, he won't be thrown down." Every single person in recovery, every person who's blown it more than once and still shown up the next day — this is your verse. Seven times down. Seven times up. That's not failure. That's what the book calls right-standing.

Saying twenty-six — don't gloat. Verses seventeen and eighteen: **"Don't rejoice when your enemy falls; don't let your heart be glad when he stumbles — or God will see it and be displeased, and turn His anger away from him."** That last clause is sharp: gloat over your enemy and God might stop judging him. Because now you're the problem. Chapter twenty-five will say feed him. Here: at minimum, don't dance.

Saying twenty-seven — the awe verse. Verses twenty-one and twenty-two:

**"My son, be in AWE of God and the king; don't associate with rebels — for their calamity rises suddenly, and who knows the ruin both can bring?"**

Y'ra et-YHWH b'ni va-melech. Revere God, and the king. Now, here's where the awe reading actually does work the fear reading can't: you can't stand in canyon-awe of a human king the way you do of God — but you can respect him. The single verb yare is stretching across two objects, and its core meaning — take seriously, hold in regard — covers both, while "be terrified of" fits neither God (the whole book has said so) nor a legitimate ruler. Peter says it: "honor the emperor; revere God." Two levels of one posture. And the shonim — the rebels, the ones who change, who overthrow — their end comes fast. Don't get in bed with the coup.

The appendix — "these also are from the wise." Verse twenty-three. Verses twenty-three through twenty-five: **"Partiality in judging is not good. Whoever says to the guilty, 'You're innocent' — peoples will curse him, nations denounce him. But those who convict the guilty will have delight, and a good blessing will come on them."** Chapter seventeen, verse fifteen — rigged verdicts in the idolatry drawer. Verse twenty-six: **"He who gives an honest answer kisses the lips."** S'fatayim yishaq — an honest answer is a kiss. Truth is affection. Verses twenty-eight and twenty-nine: **"Don't testify against your neighbor without cause — would you deceive with your lips? Don't say, 'I'll do to him as he did to me; I'll pay him back.'"** Chapter twenty, verse twenty-two. The revenge ledger isn't yours. Verse twenty-seven: **"Prepare your work outside and get your fields ready; afterward, build your house."** Income first, then the house. Order of operations.

And then the last saying, verses thirty through thirty-four, and it's a story: "I passed by the field of a sluggard, by the vineyard of a man lacking heart — and look, it was all overgrown with thorns, the ground covered with nettles, its stone wall broken down. I looked, I took it to heart; I saw, and received training from it."

Laqachti musar — I took coaching from it. From a field. The wise man walks past a wreck and learns from it. He doesn't sneer, he doesn't pity — he gets educated. And here's what he learned — the same four lines as chapter six, verses ten and eleven, word for word: "A little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to rest — and your poverty comes like a highwayman, your want like an armed man."

The teacher walked past a vineyard and heard the ant proverb come true. That's how these sayings work. You hear them, and then one day you walk past the field and see one.

Here's the shape. Don't envy the wicked — three times — his lamp's about to go out and he has no afterward. Build your house with the three tools God built the cosmos with. Rescue the ones being dragged to death, and don't say you didn't know. Wisdom is honey that stays sweet and has a future. The right-standing falls seven times and gets up — the rising is the tell. Don't gloat. Hold God and the king in the regard each deserves. Truth is a kiss. And take coaching from the overgrown field.

Today: if you're down — third time, fifth time, seventh time — the verse already counted you and already wrote your next move. Va-qam. Get up. Then go find someone being dragged, and grab the rope.

*Note on translation: 24:21 y'ra et-YHWH b'ni va-melech — "fear thou the LORD and the king" (KJV) — rendered "be in AWE of / revere"; one verb, two objects — the shared sense is regard/reverence, graded (cf. 1 Pet 2:17 phobeisthe ton theon, ton basilea timate); shonim = those who change/rebel (some read "those who differ"). 24:1 ≈ 23:17, 24:19 — triple prohibition on envying the wicked; 24:19 al-titchar = do not get heated (Ps 37:1, 7, 8); ein acharit la-ra = no future for the evil; yid'ach = is snuffed (13:9, 20:20). 24:3 chochmah / t'vunah / da'at — the creation triad of 3:19–20. 24:7 ramot = too high (Ketiv/Qere ra'mot). 24:9 to'evah l'adam = detestable to men. 24:11 hatzel l'quchim la-mavet = rescue those being taken to death; matim la-heregh = staggering to the slaughter (cf. 7:22). 24:12 tochen libbot = weigher of hearts (16:2, 21:2); notzer nafsh'cha = guardian of your soul. 24:14 yesh acharit = there is a future (= 23:18). 24:16 sheva yippol tzaddiq va-qam = seven times the righteous falls and rises — the falling is real and complete (seven), the rising is the distinguishing mark; cf. Ps 37:24, Mic 7:8. 24:18 turning wrath from the enemy when you gloat — the gloater becomes the offender. 24:23 gam-elleh la-chachamim = these also are for/of the wise — appendix marker. 24:26 s'fatayim yishaq = kisses lips — honest answer as affection. 24:29 = 20:22 principle. 24:32 laqachti musar = I took training [from it]. 24:33–34 = 6:10–11 verbatim. Yirah in this chapter: 24:21.*
