# PROVERB 25 — Coals on His Head

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 25:1–28. Opening of the second Solomonic collection (25–29), "copied by the men of Hezekiah, king of Judah" — sayings preserved and edited two centuries after Solomon, during the revival of 2 Kgs 18–20. This chapter is heavy on similes ("like… so…") rather than antitheses. Clustered by theme (every verse present): God and king, court manners, the neighbor and the lawsuit, the mouth as fruit and weather, feeding your enemy (25:21–22, quoted in Romans 12), and self-rule. No yirah verse. Companion: Rom 12:20; Matt 5:44; Prov 16:32; Ps 141:5.*

---

New collection. Verse one tells you who saved it: "These also are proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied."

Hezekiah — the king who tore down the idols, reopened the temple, and watched God turn back the Assyrian army overnight; Psalm 46, Psalm 76, Isaiah 37. In the middle of that revival, his scribes went into the archives and copied out the old wisdom. Reform always includes recovering the text. What you're about to hear survived because a king thought it was worth the ink.

First handful — God and king. Verse two: **"It is the glory of God to conceal a matter, and the glory of kings to search a matter out."** K'vod Elohim haster davar. God's honor is in the hiding. He keeps things in the dark — not from meanness, from majesty; you don't get to see everything. And the king's honor is in digging. That's the human job: search out what He left buried. Chapter two's silver-mining, from the throne. Verse three: **"As the heavens are high and the earth is deep, so the heart of kings is unsearchable."** Even the king's heart, chapter twenty-one's irrigation ditch, has a floor you can't see.

Verses four and five: **"Remove the dross from silver, and out comes a vessel for the smith. Remove the wicked from the king's presence, and his throne is established in right-standing."** The furnace again — chapter seventeen. You don't fix bad silver by adding. You remove. Same with a court. Verses six and seven: **"Don't exalt yourself in the king's presence or stand in the place of the great; it's better to be told 'come up here' than to be humbled before a noble."** Jesus told this exact parable — take the low seat at the wedding, so the host says "friend, come up higher." Luke 14. He was quoting Hezekiah's scribes. Second handful — the neighbor and the lawsuit. Verse seven's tail and verses eight through ten: **"What your eyes have seen, don't rush to bring to court — for what will you do in the end when your neighbor puts you to shame? Argue your case with your neighbor himself, and don't reveal another's secret — or whoever hears it will shame you, and your bad reputation will never leave."** Riv'cha riv et-re'echa — take the fight to the neighbor, not to the judge. Matthew 18 — go to your brother alone first. And don't win the case by leaking someone's secret; that stain doesn't come out.

Verse seventeen: **"Let your foot be seldom in your neighbor's house, or he'll get his fill of you and hate you."** Hoqar ragl'cha — make your foot rare, precious. Don't wear out your welcome. Verse eighteen: **"Like a club, a sword, and a sharp arrow is a man who testifies falsely against his neighbor."** Three weapons. Verse nineteen: **"Like a bad tooth and a foot out of joint is trusting a treacherous man in the day of trouble."** You lean on him and he gives. You bite down and it hurts. Verse twenty: **"Like one who takes off a garment on a cold day, like vinegar on soda, is one who sings songs to a heavy heart."** Shar ba-shirim al lev-ra. Cheerful songs to a grieving heart — that's taking their coat off in the cold. Don't do it. Sit with them. Chapter fourteen — the heart knows its own bitterness. Don't sing at it.

Third handful — the mouth as fruit and weather. Verse eleven: **"A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in settings of silver."** Davar davur al-ofnav — a word spoken on its wheels, rolling exactly right. Chapter fifteen's word in season. Verse twelve: **"Like a gold ring or an ornament of fine gold is a wise reprover to a listening ear."** Mochiach — the one who corrects you — is jewelry, if you've got the ear. Verse fifteen: **"With patience a ruler can be persuaded, and a soft tongue breaks a bone."** Lashon rakkah tishbor-garem. A soft tongue — chapter fifteen's soft answer — breaks bone. Not a hard tongue. The soft one. Water cuts stone.

Verse thirteen: **"Like the cold of snow at harvest time is a faithful messenger to those who send him; he refreshes his master's soul."** Verse fourteen: **"Like clouds and wind without rain is a man who boasts of a gift he doesn't give."** The whole sky promises and nothing falls. Verse twenty-three: **"The north wind brings rain, and a backbiting tongue, angry looks."** L'shon sater — a tongue of secrets, gossip. It changes the weather in the room. Verse twenty-five: **"Like cold water to a weary soul is good news from a far country."** Chapter fifteen — good news fattens the bones. Here it's water to someone parched. Be the far-country letter to somebody. Verse twenty-six: **"Like a muddied spring and a polluted well is a right-standing man who gives way before the wicked."** Mat — who totters, the mot word. When the good man is shaken, the town's water goes bad. Everybody drinks from him. Fourth handful — honey and self-rule. Verse sixteen: **"Have you found honey? Eat only what you need, or you'll have too much and vomit it."** Verse twenty-seven: **"It's not good to eat too much honey, nor to seek honor upon honor."** Chapter twenty-four said eat honey, it's good. Chapter twenty-five says: only enough. Even the good thing has a dose. And the second half — cheqer k'vodam kavod — hunting glory after glory is too much honey. Praise-seeking is a sugar problem.

Verse twenty-eight, the last line: "Like a city broken into, without walls, is a man who has no rule over his spirit."

Ein ma'tzar l'rucho — no restraint on his spirit. Chapter sixteen, verse thirty-two — ruling your spirit beats taking a city. Here's the negative: the man without self-rule is the taken city. Walls down. Anyone walks in. Every impulse is an invader with the gate open.

And the center — verses twenty-one and twenty-two.

**"If your enemy is hungry, give him bread to eat; if he's thirsty, give him water to drink — for you'll heap burning coals on his head, and God will reward you."**

Paul quotes this word for word in Romans 12 and then adds his own gloss — "don't be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good." Jesus said love your enemies, pray for your persecutors. Both of them are standing on Hezekiah's scribes. Now — the coals. People have read it as revenge in disguise: be nice so he feels terrible. But there's an old Egyptian ritual where a penitent carried a pan of live coals on his head as a sign of repentance — and either way, the point is that your kindness does something to him. It burns. Not his skin — his certainty. He came at you expecting a fight and got a sandwich, and now he has to rethink everything. That's the coals. The bread is disarming. Chapter twenty-four said don't gloat when he falls. Chapter twenty-five says feed him. And the reward isn't his gratitude — va-YHWH y'shallem-lach — the God pays it.

Here's the shape. Hezekiah's men copied this out mid-revival. God's glory is in hiding; the king's is in digging. Take the low seat. Take the fight to the neighbor, not the court. Don't sing at a grieving heart. A soft tongue breaks bone. Good news from far away is cold water. Even honey has a dose. The man with no self-rule is a city with the wall down. And feed your enemy — the coals are the rethinking you just forced on him.

Today: find your hungry enemy — the person who's been coming at you — and hand him bread. Actual or otherwise. Then walk away and let the coals do their work. You don't have to watch.

*Note on translation: No yirah in this chapter. 25:1 he'tiqu = copied/transcribed — the Hezekian scribal collection (c. 715–686 BCE); cf. 2 Kgs 18–20, 2 Chr 29–32. 25:2 k'vod Elohim haster davar = the glory of God is to hide a matter; chaqor davar = to search out. 25:6–7 → Luke 14:7–11. 25:9 riv'cha riv et-re'echa = plead your case with your neighbor (→ Matt 18:15). 25:11 davar davur al-ofnav — "fitly spoken"; ofnav is obscure — "on its wheels / in its circumstances" — sense: exactly right. 25:12 mochiach = reprover/corrector. 25:15 lashon rakkah tishbor-garem = a soft tongue breaks bone (cf. 15:1). 25:17 hoqar ragl'cha = make your foot rare/precious. 25:20 shar ba-shirim al lev-ra = one singing songs to a bad/heavy heart; first line (garment on a cold day) has textual issues; LXX shorter; MT followed. 25:21–22 quoted verbatim Rom 12:20 (LXX); gechalim atah choteh al-rosho = you heap coals on his head — the Egyptian penitence-ritual reading (coals carried on the head as contrition) is one option; the majority sense is shame/remorse induced by kindness, not covert vengeance; va-YHWH y'shallem-lach = and God will repay you. 25:23 l'shon sater = tongue of secrecy. 25:24 = 21:9 verbatim (quarrelsome woman/roof corner — see 21:9 note on strife, not gender). 25:26 mat = tottering (the mot root). 25:27 cheqer k'vodam kavod — MT difficult; "searching out their glory is glory" — rendered per the honey parallel as excess honor-seeking; alt. emendations exist. 25:28 ein ma'tzar l'rucho = no restraint to his spirit (cf. 16:32).*
