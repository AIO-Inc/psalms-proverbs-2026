# PROVERB 12 — A Good Man Is Kind to His Donkey

*Motivational script in today's vernacular. Source: Hebrew text, Proverbs 12:1–28. Third sayings chapter — 28 two-liners. Clustered by theme (every verse present): loving correction, the household, animals and land, the mouth (the chapter's biggest cluster), and diligence. No yirah verse. Companion: Prov 10:19; Prov 4:23; Ps 15.*

---

Chapter twelve starts with a diagnostic question: how do you take criticism?

**"Whoever loves training loves knowledge, but whoever hates correction is stupid."**

Ba'ar — that's the word. Not kesil, not evil — ba'ar, brutish, an ox. The father is saying: the one thing that separates a person from livestock is the ability to hear "you got that wrong" and be grateful. Love musar — the coaching. Chapter nine said the wise man loves you for correcting him. Here it's the entry test. Fail it and you're grazing.

Verse fifteen makes the same cut: "The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, but the wise one listens to advice." The fool isn't the one who's wrong. Everyone's wrong sometimes. The fool is the one who can't imagine he's wrong.

First handful — the household. Verse four: **"A woman of strength is her husband's crown, but a shameful one is like rot in his bones."** Eshet chayil — hold that phrase. It's the title of the woman in chapter thirty-one, the acrostic at the end of the whole book. Strength. Valor. The military word. Not "virtuous" the way the old Bibles soften it — chayil is what you call a soldier. She's a crown; he wears her in public and stands taller. The opposite — rakav — is bone-rot. Internal, invisible, structural.

Verse seven: **"The wicked are overthrown and gone, but the house of the right-standing stands."** Verse three: **"No one is established by wickedness, but the root of the right-standing can't be moved."** Bal-yimot — won't be shaken. Psalms thread. Verse twelve: **"The wicked covets the catch of evil men, but the root of the right-standing yields fruit."** Root, root, root. The good house isn't upright because it's tall. It's upright because it's deep. Second handful — animals and land. Verse ten, and this is the one the chapter is named for: "The right-standing man knows the soul of his animal, but the compassion of the wicked is cruel."

Yodea nefesh b'hemto. He knows the soul of his beast. Proverbs measures a man by how he treats the creature that can't talk back. The donkey. The dog. The people under him who can't complain. And then the twist in the second line: the wicked man's compassion — rachamei — is cruel. Even when he's being nice, it lands like a blow. Because kindness from a heart that doesn't know you is just a different shape of using you.

Verse eleven: **"Whoever works his land will have plenty of bread, but whoever chases empty things lacks heart."** M'radef reiqim — chases empties. Vapor projects. Get-rich schemes. Work the ground in front of you. Verse nine: **"Better to be lightly regarded and have a servant than to play the big man and have no bread."** Better to be modest and solvent than look rich and be hungry.

Verses twenty-four and twenty-seven: **"The hand of the diligent will rule, but the slack hand ends up in forced labor. The slack one doesn't even roast his game, but the diligent man's wealth is precious."** The sluggard caught the deer and then didn't cook it. He got the hard part done and quit at the easy part. You know that pattern.

Third handful — the mouth. This chapter has more mouth-sayings than any yet. Chapter ten said the mouth is the tell. Chapter twelve zooms in.

Verse six: **"The words of the wicked lie in wait for blood, but the mouth of the upright rescues."** Words as an ambush. Verse thirteen: **"The evil man is trapped by the wrongdoing of his lips, but the right-standing escapes trouble."** Your mouth can be the trap you fall into. Verse fourteen: **"From the fruit of his mouth a man is filled with good, and the work of a man's hands comes back to him."** What you say feeds you — good or bad, you eat your own words.

Verses seventeen through nineteen: **"Whoever breathes truth tells what's right, but a false witness, deceit. Some speak rashly like a sword thrust, but the tongue of the wise brings healing. Truthful lips endure forever, but a lying tongue lasts only a moment."**

Yafiach emunah — breathes faithfulness. Truth as exhale. It's just what comes out when he opens his mouth, no effort. And the sword — madqarot charev — stab wounds. Some people talk and you bleed. The wise tongue is marpe — healing, the medicine word from chapters three and four. Same organ, opposite instrument. Scalpel or shiv. And the durability line — truth lasts la'ad, forever; a lie lasts ad-argiah, the blink of an eye. Lies are fast. They work immediately. That's their whole appeal. But they have the shelf life of milk.

Verse twenty-two: **"Lying lips are detestable to God, but those who act faithfully are His delight."** To'evah again — the idolatry word from 11:1. Rigged scales, twisted hearts, lying lips. Same drawer.

Verse twenty-three: **"A prudent man conceals knowledge, but the heart of fools proclaims folly."** The wise man knows things he doesn't say. Not hiding — restraint. Chapter ten, verse nineteen: fewer words, fewer misses. Verse sixteen: **"A fool's irritation is known at once, but the prudent man covers an insult."** Koseh qalon — covers the shame. Somebody insults him and it doesn't show. Not because he didn't feel it. Because he doesn't let his face run his mouth. Verse eighteen's sword-talker is the one whose every irritation is immediately public.

Verses twenty-five and twenty-six: **"Anxiety in a man's heart weighs him down, but a good word lifts him up. The right-standing man guides his neighbor, but the way of the wicked leads them astray."** D'agah — anxiety, dread, the weight. And the remedy isn't a program. It's davar tov — one good word. You've been that heavy. Someone said one sentence and you could stand up. Be the one who says it.

Fourth handful — the two ways, briefly. Verse two: **"A good man obtains favor from God, but the schemer He condemns."** Verse five: **"The plans of the right-standing are just; the counsels of the wicked are deceit."** Verse eight: **"A man is praised according to his good sense, but the twisted of heart is despised."** Verses twenty and twenty-one: **"Deceit is in the heart of those who plot evil, but counselors of peace have joy. No harm befalls the right-standing, but the wicked are filled with trouble."**

And the last verse, twenty-eight, and it's a whisper: "In the path of right-standing is life, and the way of its path is no-death."

Al-mavet. No-death. The Hebrew is *strange* there — most translations fix it to "there is no death" or "immortality." Leave it strange. It's the same al-mut that appeared in Psalm 48:14 — "He will guide us to the edge and beyond." The right-standing path doesn't just add days. Somewhere down it, death isn't there.

Here's the shape. Loving correction is the line between a man and an ox. A woman of strength — the soldier word — is a crown. The good man knows the soul of his donkey, and the wicked man's kindness is cruel. The mouth is a scalpel or a shiv; truth lasts forever and lies last a blink; one good word lifts a heavy man. And the path ends in no-death.

Today: find your donkey — the person or creature under you who can't talk back — and know their soul for a minute. Then say one good word to someone who's carrying weight.

---

*Note on translation: No yirah in this chapter. 12:1 musar / tochachat = training / correction; ba'ar = brutish, ox-like — a fourth fool-word, harsher than kesil. 12:3, 12:7 bal-yimot = will not be shaken — Psalms mot thread. 12:4 eshet chayil — "virtuous woman" (KJV) — chayil = strength/valor/military might (Ruth 3:11, Prov 31:10); rendered "woman of strength"; rakav = rot (bone-rot). 12:9 niqleh v'eved lo = lightly regarded but with a servant — the Hebrew is compressed; some read "and works for himself"; majority followed. 12:10 yodea nefesh b'hemto = knows the soul of his animal; rachamei r'sha'im achzari = the compassions of the wicked are cruel. 12:11 m'radef reiqim = pursues empty things. 12:16 koseh qalon = covers insult/shame (cf. 10:12, 11:13 kasah). 12:17 yafiach emunah = breathes out faithfulness/truth. 12:18 madqarot charev = sword-stabs; marpe = healing (3:8, 4:22). 12:19 la'ad / ad-argiah = forever / for a blink. 12:22 to'evah = detestable (idolatry register; 11:1, 11:20). 12:25 d'agah = anxiety/dread — a worry word, not yirah; davar tov = a good word. 12:27 lo-yacharoch = does not roast/catch — the verb is uncertain; "roast" follows most. 12:28 MT al-mavet — "no death"; many emend to el-mavet ("to death," making the second line negative); MT retained as an early immortality whisper, cf. Ps 48:14 al-mut and Ps 16:10.*
